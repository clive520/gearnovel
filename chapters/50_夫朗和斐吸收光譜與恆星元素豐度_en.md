### Section II: Kirchhoff's Laws of Spectroscopy and Blackbody Foundations

They stood silent and immutable, like ancient runes carved into the bones of radiant light, each shadow bearing microphysical messages from the stellar interior.

"Here lie the pillars of classical spectroscopy—Kirchhoff's Laws of Spectroscopy," <u>Vivi</u> observed, tracing the luminous rainbow background with her finger to illustrate the foundational physics. "The First Law dictates that a luminous solid, liquid, or sufficiently dense gas under high pressure emits a continuous spectrum across all wavelengths, uninterrupted by gaps. The core and deep photosphere of the companion star burn at temperatures approaching ten thousand Kelvin; the intense thermal motions and incessant collisions of its plasma yield powerful blackbody radiation, weaving the vibrant canvas before us."

The young scholar projected Planck's blackbody radiation formula, superimposing a smooth, graceful energy distribution curve across the rainbow ribbon:
$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}} - 1}$$
"The peak emission wavelength settles neatly at five hundred and fifty nanometers. In accordance with Wien's Displacement Law, $\lambda_{\text{max}} T = b$, the effective surface temperature of the companion star stands at approximately five thousand two hundred and sixty Kelvin—a classic late-type yellow dwarf. Its radiant continuum aligns seamlessly with theoretical blackbody curves."

"Yet when this continuous radiation filters through the cooler stellar atmosphere, Kirchhoff's Third Law works its wonder," <u>Dawn</u> continued, indicating the stark dark lines punctuating the colored background. "A cool, diffuse gas under low pressure absorbs precisely those wavelengths of light which it would emit if heated. Photons corresponding to discrete quantum transitions are absorbed by gas atoms in the cooler outer envelope and re-emitted in random directions, drastically attenuating the photon flux along our line of sight and casting dark absorption lines across the continuous spectrum."

"Kirchhoff's Second Law governs the complementary emission spectra—a hot, thin gas in isolation emits only discrete, bright emission lines," <u>Zephyr</u> added, observing the profound intensity depressions on the screen. "Thus, absorption lines do not represent the death of light, but rather quantum checkpoints established by microscopic atoms in the stellar canopy. $$E_n = -\frac{m_e e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2} \frac{1}{n^2} = -\frac{13.6\text{ eV}}{n^2}, \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$

### Section IV: Spectroscopic Binary Doppler Shifts and Radial Velocity Solutions

Even as the scholars marveled at the pristine atomic signatures, a subtle dynamical shift perturbed the observed spectrum. "The lines are splitting! And they exhibit periodic Doppler drift!" <u>Zephyr</u> cried out, pressing his eye to the micrometer comparator as his pupils narrowed. "A double-lined spectroscopic binary (SB2) undergoing Doppler shifts!" <u>Vivi</u> exclaimed, her voice thrilling with excitement as she invoked the orbital dynamics solver. "According to classical wave mechanics, when a radiating source possesses a radial velocity $v_r$ relative to an observer, the observed frequency and wavelength undergo a Doppler shift. In the non-relativistic regime where $v_r \ll c$, the wavelength displacement follows a pristine proportionality:
$$\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_0} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{obs}} - \lambda_0}{\lambda_0} = \frac{v_r}{c}$$
where recession marks a redshift ($\Delta\lambda > 0$), while approach manifests as a blueshift ($\Delta\lambda < 0$)!"

<u>Dawn</u> computed the relative displacements of the twin components: "The primary star's absorption features shift redward by $\Delta\lambda_1 = +0.018\text{ nm}$, yielding a line-of-sight radial recession velocity of:
$$v_{r,1} = c \cdot \frac{+0.018\text{ nm}}{656.28\text{ nm}} \approx +8.23\text{ km/s}$$
Meanwhile, the secondary companion's emergent absorption lines shift blueward by $\Delta\lambda_2 = -0.054\text{ nm}$, indicating an approach velocity of:
$$v_{r,2} = c \cdot \frac{-0.054\text{ nm}}{656.28\text{ nm}} \approx -24.68\text{ km/s}$$"

"The twin stars are positioned at their quadrature orbital phase!" <u>Zephyr</u> deduced, tracing two concentric orbital ellipses upon the chart. "Because the stars revolve about their common barycenter under conservation of momentum, the ratio of their orbital velocities must equal the inverse ratio of their masses:
$$\frac{M_1}{M_2} = \frac{|v_{r,2}|}{|v_{r,1}|} = \frac{24.68}{8.23} \approx 3.00$$

### Section V: Absorption Line Equivalent Widths and Saha Ionization Abundances

Having unlocked the orbital kinematics of the binary pair, the scholars turned their attention to the deeper chemical puzzle: what anomalous matter resided within the companion to provoke such unprecedented gravitational perturbations?

<u>Vivi</u> engaged the spectral line profile analysis suite. On the display, an individual dark line was magnified horizontally, revealing its true geometric contour—a Voigt profile with a central depression and broad absorption wings, synthesized from Gaussian thermal Doppler broadening and Lorentzian natural and collisional damping. While the line center was heavily saturated, the broad damping wings faithfully chronicled the photospheric gas density and microturbulent velocity.

"To quantify elemental abundances within stellar atmospheres, we cannot rely merely on central line depth; we must evaluate the Equivalent Width $W_\lambda$," <u>Vivi</u> declared, writing down the formulation:
$$W_\lambda = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{I_\lambda}{I_c} \right) d\lambda$$
where $I_\lambda$ denotes residual intensity within the line profile and $I_c$ represents the unattenuated continuum intensity. The physical equivalent width signifies the wavelength breadth of a rectangular, completely saturated black absorption band possessing equivalent absorbed energy, measured in Angstroms ($\text{Å}$) or picometers ($\text{pm}$). Exempt from instrumental broadening artifacts, it represents one of the few truly invariant observables in stellar spectroscopy.

"In the linear regime of the curve of growth for optically thin atmospheres, the equivalent width scales directly with the column density $N_j$ of the absorbing species in the lower energy level," <u>Dawn</u> explained, referencing the atomic transition data tables. "However, line strength does not directly reflect total elemental abundance. At temperatures exceeding five thousand Kelvin, vast fractions of atoms are ionized by violent thermal collisions or inhabit excited states unable to foster Balmer transitions."

"This demands the application of the Saha Ionization Equation, formulated by Meghnad Saha!" <u>Dawn</u> wrote upon the board, invoking the grand thermodynamic bridge linking microscopic atomic states, quantum mechanics, and astrophysics:

where $N_i$ and $N_{i+1}$ represent the number densities of successive ionization stages, $P_e$ is electron pressure in the photosphere, $\chi_i$ is ionization energy, and $g_i$ denotes the partition functions.
