# 第一章：失竊的二十四小時 (Chapter 1: The Stolen Twenty-Four Hours)

## 一、不存在的星期三 (I. The Wednesday That Never Was)

When the morning study bell rang across Class 6-1 at Lu-Yang Elementary, twelve-year-old Cheng Hao sat staring blankly at his pencil case.

His pencil case was unlike anyone else's—a custom metal tin he had modified with his own hands. Embedded on its side were two tiny spur gears salvaged from an old, discarded calculator. With a light flick of a brass spring latch, the tin lid slid smoothly open to both sides like the doors of a vintage train car, accompanied by a crisp, satisfying *click-clack*.

Usually by this hour, Cheng Hao would have already fished out his trusty miniature screwdriver from the bottom of his backpack, eager to tinker with his collection of clockwork gears and brass wires. But today, he hadn't even touched his favorite tool.

An inexplicable, eerie sensation—like a cold beetle creeping along his spine—was slowly inching upward.

"Hey, Jiang Jiang," Cheng Hao whispered, tapping his pencil eraser against the shoulder of the boy sitting in front of him. "What day is today?"

Jiang Jiang, sporting a buzz cut and a mouth half-stuffed with a pineapple bun, rolled his eyes and mumbled, "Did you sleep through your alarm or something? It's written right there on the blackboard."

Cheng Hao looked up.

In the upper-right corner of the classroom's sleek, newly installed smart LCD blackboard, smooth lines of crisp white digital text glowed faintly:

> **October 17, 2026**  
> **Thursday | Morning Temp: 21°C**  
> **Class Monitors on Duty: Cheng Hao, Ye Yijie**

Cheng Hao's pupils snapped tight.

"Thursday?" His voice pitched up half an octave without him realizing it. "Wait a minute... wasn't yesterday Tuesday? We were playing soccer on the field yesterday afternoon! I vividly remember taking a ball straight to the bridge of my nose and getting sent to the nurse's office with a nosebleed... Wasn't that yesterday?!"

"What kind of daydream are you stuck in?" Jiang Jiang chuckled through a mouthful of crumbs. "Soccer on Tuesday was two days ago! Yesterday was Wednesday. Regular school day."

"Yesterday was Wednesday?" Cheng Hao's heart rate spiked. He leaned closer, locking eyes with his friend. "Then tell me: what class did we have for third period yesterday?"

"Yesterday's third period..." The grin on Jiang Jiang's face froze mid-chew.

Cheng Hao saw it clearly—a fleeting, unnatural glitch fluttered across Jiang Jiang's ordinary eyes, as if a paused frame had seized a television screen.

It took three full, agonizing seconds before Jiang Jiang blinked, his expression snapping back to nonchalant ease. "Well... science class, obviously. The teacher went over plant photosynthesis, and... and magnets, or whatever. Man, who keeps track of every single lesson? School is school!"

"Then what color shirt was the science teacher wearing? What did we have for school lunch yesterday?" Cheng Hao pressed, his pulse hammering in his throat.

Irritated, Jiang Jiang scratched his messy hair. "Dude, what is your problem today?! Who counts how many stalks of bok choy they ate at lunch? As long as you're full, who cares!"

Jiang Jiang turned back around, ignoring him.

Cheng Hao glanced around the room. Two dozen classmates were either copying down homework assignments, chattering about comics, or yawning lazily. Everyone appeared completely normal on the surface. Yet, with closer scrutiny, it became chillingly apparent: not a single kid in the room could recall one concrete, specific detail from the previous day in under three seconds.

It was as if an immense, invisible eraser had wiped clean twenty-four solid hours from every brain in the class, leaving behind nothing but a hazy, fabricated smear of "vague impressions."

## 二、空白的筆記本 (II. The Blank Notebook)

Just then, a pair of light, hurried footsteps halted beside Cheng Hao's desk.

He looked up and found class president Ye Yijie standing over him.

Yijie wore her glossy black hair in a meticulous, immaculate high ponytail, framed by thin wire-rimmed glasses perched on her nose. At Lu-Yang Elementary, she was renowned as the undisputed "Logic Prodigy"—she could recite the entire periodic table in under five minutes, mapped her daily schedule down to the exact minute, and even logged the precise milliliters of water she drank each day.

Yet right now, Yijie's face was as stark white as a freshly pressed sheet of printer paper.

"Cheng Hao, outside. Now," she whispered, her tone strung with a taut urgency he had never heard before.

Cheng Hao stood up immediately, following her brisk stride out of the bustling classroom to the quiet corner beside the hallway water fountain.

Yijie scanned the corridor, ensuring no patrol teachers or cleaning androids were approaching. Taking a deep breath, she pulled out a thick, black leather-bound notebook clutched to her chest and held it open before Cheng Hao's eyes.

It was her daily bullet journal—the one item that never left her side. Cheng Hao looked down at the most recent entries.

*October 14 (Monday)*: Neat, print-perfect handwriting filled the page with actionable checklists, each accompanied by a crisp checkmark on the right.  
*October 15 (Tuesday)*: Equally dense, featuring even a hand-drawn diagram illustrating the precise trajectory of Jiang Jiang's mishit soccer ball.  
Then came the next page.

At the very top, written in flawless royal-blue fountain pen ink, sat the header:  
`Wednesday, October 16, 2026`.

Yet beneath the date, across the entire lined sheet—  
**It was entirely blank.**

Not a single scribble. Not even an accidental stray dot of ink. It was so immaculate it made Cheng Hao's skin prickle.

"Do you see it?" Yijie's voice trembled with a rare fracture of composure. "I have kept a daily log since first grade. Rain or shine, whether burning with a fever or traveling abroad, I have never missed a single entry. If we truly sat in class and went through a normal school day yesterday, it would be mathematically impossible for this page to be void of a single stroke."

"You don't remember yesterday either?" Cheng Hao whispered urgently.

"It isn't a matter of 'forgetting,'" Yijie pushed her glasses up, her gaze piercingly sharp. "The data simply does not exist in my brain. I tried tracing yesterday's breakfast, the route I took to school, even the hour I fell asleep... Cheng Hao, my mind is blank static, like a cassette tape scrubbed by an industrial electromagnet."

"I knew it..." Cheng Hao muttered. "The whole school skipped yesterday. And except for the two of us, everyone thinks jumping straight to Thursday is completely natural!"

"No, it isn't just the school," Yijie pointed through the corridor window toward the distant rooftops of Gear Town. "On my way here this morning, I asked the bakery lady. She insisted the shop was open as usual yesterday. But when I asked how many of her signature croissants she sold, she froze for five full seconds before brushing me off with a vague excuse. The entire town's memory... has been tampered with."

## 三、爺爺的幽靈護目鏡 (III. Grandpa's Ghost Goggles)

That revelation sent a cold shiver racing down Cheng Hao's back.

"Wait a second," Cheng Hao gasped, his eyes widening as a sudden memory hit him. He reached down and yanked open his backpack zipper. "Yijie, look at this."

From the deepest pocket of his bag, he pulled out an unusual bundle wrapped in a thick towel.

Unwrapping the cloth, he revealed a pair of vintage aviator-style goggles. The frames were forged from weathered, deep-toned brass, their rims fitted with miniature mechanical gears and microscopic dials. Instead of regular clear glass, the twin oval lenses were crafted from a mysterious, iridescent purple crystal that shimmered with an oily, rainbow sheen in the morning light.

"My grandfather left these behind," Cheng Hao murmured under his breath.

His grandfather had once been Gear Town's most legendary chief mechanical engineer—until he vanished mysteriously three years ago, leaving an attic crammed with dozens of wooden crates filled with cryptic blueprints and unfinished clockwork devices.

"At seven o'clock this morning, a piercing, high-pitched hum woke me up in the attic," Cheng Hao explained. "These goggles, buried at the very bottom of an iron chest, were glowing and emitting heat all by themselves. I knew something was wrong, so I smuggled them to school."

Yijie leaned in, examining the microscopic vernier scales etched along the brass rims with a furrowed brow. "These aren't ordinary aviator goggles. Both temples are equipped with miniature optical spectrometers and polarized prism chips... Have you tried putting them on?"

"Not yet," Cheng Hao swallowed hard. "Wearing something like this in homeroom would draw way too much attention."

"Try them now," Yijie said decisively, stepping sideways to block the view of the corridor. "Hurry."

Cheng Hao nodded. His hands trembled slightly as he lifted the cool brass frames. Taking a deep breath, he pulled the leather strap over his head and slid the lenses down over his eyes—

In a heartbeat, his entire universe shattered.

The plain, mundane school hallway—with its bland cream-colored tiles and pale walls—instantly lost its ordinary facade.

The space before his eyes was inundated with a dizzying, three-dimensional grid of glowing cyan vector lines!

The white plaster on the walls appeared semi-transparent. Through the purple lenses, Cheng Hao could clearly see the plumbing, electrical conduits, and... hundreds of heavy, jet-black data cables that had no business existing inside an elementary school! Those conduits pulsed with deep purple light like the veins of an underground leviathan, snaking through the concrete and converging toward the principal's office at the far end of the hall.

Even more bizarre: glowing purple footprints hovered above the tiled floor, marching in an erratic line straight through the locked metal door of the janitor's closet.

"Sweet gears of steam..." Cheng Hao gasped, his knees buckling under a sudden wave of vertigo.

"Cheng Hao? What do you see?" Yijie asked, catching his elbow as she noticed the color drain from his face.

"You really can't see it?" Cheng Hao pointed frantically into midair. "Right there! Suspended right above the lintel of the principal's office!"

Yijie slipped off her wire glasses, rubbed her eyes, and shook her head. "There's only the wooden school motto plaque."

"No!" Cheng Hao's voice shook. "It's a floating, semi-transparent crimson terminal window!"

In Cheng Hao's polarized vision, a massive alert dialog box hung suspended above the doorframe, pulsing rapidly with alarming warning signs:

> `[CRITICAL ALERT: TIMELINE PATCH v4.04 INSTALLED]`  
> `[ANOMALY CODE: WEDNESDAY SYSTEM LOG QUARANTINED TO MAINFRAME]`  
> `[UNAUTHORIZED MEMORY RESIDUE SCAN IN PROGRESS...]`

And right beneath that flashing warning box, an extended sequence of luminous digital glyphs was branded directly onto the hallway wall!

## 四、牆上的加密警報 (IV. The Encrypted Warning on the Wall)

"Quick, Yijie, pen and paper!" Cheng Hao grabbed her sleeve. "There's glowing text on the wall—a string of numbers! They're fading fast!"

Without hesitation, Yijie flipped open her notebook and uncapped her fountain pen. "Read them to me!"

Cheng Hao stared intently at the shimmering purple glyphs through the crystal lenses, reading at top speed:

"`04`... space... `15`... `14`... `20`!"  
"Next group: `04`... `18`... `09`... `14`... `11`!"  
"Final group: `13`... `09`... `12`... `11`!"

Yijie's fountain pen flew across the paper with razor precision, leaving a crisp trail of ink:

```
04 - 15 - 14 - 20
04 - 18 - 09 - 14 - 11
13 - 09 - 12 - 11
```

The moment Cheng Hao spoke the last syllable, the glowing numbers on the wall evaporated like droplets on a hot boiler, vanishing without a trace.

"Did you get them all?" Cheng Hao panted, pushing the heavy goggles up onto his forehead. In an instant, the hallway reverted to its boring, sunlit everyday appearance.

"Every single one," Yijie replied, studying the three clusters of digits. The morning sun reflected sharply off her glasses.

Her index finger tapped against the leather edge—her trademark habit whenever her mind hit overdrive.

"These aren't random numbers," Yijie analyzed coolly. "No value exceeds 26, and they are arranged in paired two-digit blocks... Cheng Hao, this is the classic A1Z26 substitution cipher! Each number corresponds to the ordinal position of an English letter."

Underneath the numbers, Yijie quickly jotted down their alphabetical equivalents:

* **Group 1:**
  * 04 = **D**
  * 15 = **O**
  * 14 = **N**
  * 20 = **T**
  * ➜ **DONT**

* **Group 2:**
  * 04 = **D**
  * 18 = **R**
  * 09 = **I**
  * 14 = **N**
  * 11 = **K**
  * ➜ **DRINK**

* **Group 3:**
  * 13 = **M**
  * 09 = **I**
  * 12 = **L**
  * 11 = **K**
  * ➜ **MILK**

Together, the deciphered words formed a complete, urgent sentence.

Yijie's breath caught in her throat.

"**DONT DRINK MILK**..." Cheng Hao read the words aloud, his voice turning dry as dust. "Don't... drink the milk?"

"Milk?" Yijie's head snapped up. "Every morning after second period, the school hands out free bottles of—"

"—the 'Smart-Brain High-Calcium Milk' sponsored by Gear Town Dairy Labs!" Cheng Hao finished her sentence, his face turning ghostly pale.

## 五、致命的鈴聲 (V. The Deadly Chime)

***CLANG—CLANG—CHIME—***

The very instant the words left Cheng Hao's mouth, the high-gain loudspeakers mounted along the corridor rafters screeched to life.

It was not the gentle, melodic chime that normally signaled morning recess. It was a sharp, grating, synthesized screech laced with high-frequency electrical static.

"Attention, all sixth-grade students," the vice principal's voice came through the intercom, calm to the point of being robotic. "Today's nutritional supplement has been delivered to each homeroom. In accordance with the Smart Health Monitoring Initiative, all students must consume their milk within five minutes. Discarding or removing bottles from the classroom is strictly prohibited."

Simultaneously, the heavy rumble of tank treads echoed from the far end of the corridor.

A sealed, stainless-steel insulated cart was being pushed toward Class 6-1 by a cylindrical, barrel-bodied campus cleaning patrol robot. Stacked neatly upon the shelves were dozens of glass bottles of fresh milk, their plastic caps marked with faint cyan smart-chip labels.

Inside the classroom, cheerful shouts broke out as kids swarmed the cart as they did every morning, cracking the seals with laughter.

Cheng Hao frantically yanked the Ghost Goggles back over his eyes.

A single glance nearly froze the marrow in his bones.

Under the ultraviolet spectrum of the goggles, tiny, luminous speckles of metallic silver floated inside every single bottle of milk—completely invisible to the naked eye! And suspended directly in the center of the classroom ceiling, an inverted funnel-shaped black transmitter had been installed, beaming continuous micro-pulses directly down upon the bottles!

Inside the room, his best friend Jiang Jiang had already peeled back the plastic seal. He tilted his head back, bringing the icy bottle to his lips—

"Jiang Jiang! Stop! DON'T DRINK IT!"

Unable to hold back for another second, Cheng Hao screamed at the top of his lungs and bolted toward the classroom doorway!

The instant his shout tore through the air, the cleaning patrol robot in the hallway lurched to a violent stop.

Its optical scanner, which had been glowing with a peaceful green status light, snapped shut with a mechanical *clack*—and blazed alive in a furious, blood-red combat protocol!

At the same time, in the lower-right corner of Cheng Hao's goggles, a cascade of crimson system warnings erupted across the lens:

> `[WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED SPECTRUM DETECTED]`  
> `[ALERT: ILLEGAL OBSERVER IDENTIFIED]`  
> `[TARGET LOCKED: CHENG HAO, CLASS 6-1, LU-YANG ELEMENTARY]`  
> `[EXECUTING FORCED MEMORY OVERWRITE——PURGE PROTOCOL ENGAGED!]`

Down the corridor, three cleaning robots pivoted on their treads simultaneously. Steel tracks shrieked against the polished ceramic floor as they extended heavy pneumatic capture claws, charging full throttle straight toward Cheng Hao!
