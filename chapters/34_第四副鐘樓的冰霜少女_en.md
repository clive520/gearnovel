# Chapter 2: The Frost Maiden of the Fourth Auxiliary Tower

## I. The Mist Crag and the Ancient Clock Tower in Dawn Fog

At six o'clock in the morning, pale golden light spilled like crushed gold across the crystalline canyon suspended high above the sea of clouds.

The two-seater aerial wind-skiff glided effortlessly within the rising thermal updrafts three thousand meters above the abyss. Its deep-blue solar sails drank in the crisp morning breeze, the microscopic wind sensors along the fabric vibrating with a high-pitched, cello-like resonance.

Seated in the copilot chair, <u>Dawn</u> gripped the polished brass safety railing with both hands, gazing down at the rolling sea of clouds tumbling beneath her feet like white foam. The headwind tousled the soft, wavy curls near her temples, sweeping away the lingering shadows of doubt and sorrow that had weighed upon her heart through the night.

Curled in her lap, <u>Lulu</u>'s delicate titanium ears spun rapidly in synchronized circles. The miniature magnetic resonance probes inside her ear canals clicked rhythmically—*tick-tack, tick-tack*—guiding the vessel through dense ribbons of humid, chilling fog.

"Right ahead! Look at that cliff jutting out above the cloud ocean!" At the helm beside her, <u>Zephyr</u> turned his head, a warm, sunlit smile brightening his face as he pointed toward a colossal black silhouette looming through the mist.

It was Mist Crag, jutting proudly from the eastern perimeter of Lumina Port. Perched precariously atop the obsidian precipice stood a monumental structure steeped in centuries of history: the Fourth Auxiliary Clock Tower of Tian-Shu.

Erected during the dawn of Lumina Port's founding, this clock tower was older than any modern spire in the city. Its stone facade was reinforced by heavy bronze rivets and draped in deep-green verdigris. Four massive celestial gear wheels, each ten meters across, ground together in slow, stately revolutions driven by the mountain winds. Though most modern astronomical observation gear had long migrated to the Academy's central citadel, within the ancient iron bones of this auxiliary tower still pulsed the low, resonant heartbeat of primal Astra Crystals.

*Whoosh—hiss—*

<u>Zephyr</u> deftly pulled back on the yaw lever and deployed the pneumatic airbrakes. The wind-skiff slid smoothly alongside the weathered copper mooring platform.

"Go on in alone, <u>Dawn</u>," <u>Zephyr</u> said, handing her a spare barometric transducer, his eyes full of gentle trust and encouragement. "The observatory logs indicate that <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> never tolerates strangers lingering during her calculations. I'll stay here on the platform to service the anemometer coils. Call out if you need anything."

"Thank you, <u>Zephyr</u>," <u>Dawn</u> said, taking a steadying breath and nodding resolutely. She secured her mother's brass tuning wrench into her leather belt, scooped up the curious <u>Lulu</u>, and pushed open the heavy, chilled bronze portal of the tower.

## II. The Solitary Equations on the Slate

The interior of the clock tower was vast, echoing, and colder than she had imagined.

The air smelled of aged slate chalk, cold machine grease, and a sharp, invigorating fragrance of peppermint ink.

Spanning the twenty-meter-high vaulted chamber, the stone walls that once showcased clockwork mechanisms were entirely lined with colossal dark-green slate blackboards. From ceiling to floor, they were covered in dense, breathtakingly complex chalk equations that looked like star charts: Fourier spectral transforms, Maxwell curl operators, optical polarization tensor matrices, and line upon line of damping decay differentials violently crossed out with red chalk.

Standing before the largest chalkboard at the center of the hall was a slender, solitary girl.

She wore a tailored silver-and-white uniform of the Tian-Shu Science Academy, the high collar fastened neatly to the very top button. Her long, ink-black hair was pulled back into a crisp, high ponytail bound by a silver alloy ring, swaying slightly with every stroke of her arm. The girl held a stub of white chalk, furiously calculating a monstrously complex formulation of optical chromatic dispersion.

It was the thirteen-year-old prodigy—<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>, known to the world as <u>Vivi</u>.

Yet on the long copper workbench beside her lay a mechanical prototype that had seized into total, lifeless paralysis. It was a miniature optical chronometer combining a dodecahedral rotating prism with a hair-thin brass escapement. Severe abrasion gouges scored the teeth where the pinions had locked, and tiny flecks of brass dust twisted under brutal torque were scattered across the bench.

*Snap!*

The chalk in <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s grip snapped under intense pressure, the broken half tumbling to the stone floor and shattering into white powder.

The girl froze. Her slender, fragile shoulders trembled faintly as her obsidian eyes—dark as a winter midnight—stared relentlessly at the dead machine before her. Her fingers clenched the hem of her lab coat, her teeth biting hard into her bloodless lower lip.

"Why... why is the mathematical matrix flawless, yet the physical escapement seizes up solid on the third cycle?" she whispered to herself, her voice cracked and hoarse from dozens of sleepless hours. "Could Father... truly be right? That calculations without a soul will never be more than worthless academic garbage..."

At that moment, a soft, clean metallic scuff broke the cavernous silence.

*Whir—clack—*

<u>Lulu</u> slipped down from <u>Dawn</u>'s arms, her velvet-padded titanium paws padding softly across the flagstones in a crisp, rhythmic cadence.

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stiffened as if struck by lightning and whipped around like a cornered deer! When she saw that the intruder was merely a civilian girl in an apron stained with watch oil, an old brass wrench hanging at her hip, the vulnerability on her face instantly froze behind a mask of cutting arrogance and guarded coldness.

"Who gave you permission to trespass into the Tian-Shu Fourth Auxiliary Tower?" <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> said icily, her eyes sweeping over <u>Dawn</u>'s scuffed artisan gloves and the white linen bandage wrapped around her finger. Her voice dripped with arctic frost: "If you came here like <u>Renault</u> and the rest of those pretentious fools to inspect my 'failed exhibit,' you can get out this instant."

## III. The 0.03 Millimeter Breath

Faced with this razor-sharp hostility, <u>Dawn</u> did not retreat, nor did she take offense.

Having spent her childhood at Dawn Hall greeting all manner of customers alongside her grandfather, she knew better than anyone that the coldest, hardest armor often shielded a heart that had just been burned to the core.

"I didn't come to mock you," <u>Dawn</u> said, her tone gentle yet immovable, stepping steadily toward the cluttered metal workbench. "I came because I saw the thermal compensation equation you carved on the copper pillar, and I crossed the entire gorge just to find you."

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s brows knit together, letting out a bitter, mocking laugh: "Understand my equations? Civilian workshops don't even know what a Taylor series expansion is. What could you possibly understand?"

"I understand that your formulas are breathtakingly beautiful, but I also see that your machine is suffocating," <u>Dawn</u> replied, meeting <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s piercing gaze calmly.

"Suffocating? What an absurd, unscientific emotional metaphor," <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> scoffed, lifting her chin. "Every gear engagement angle was computed to six decimal places. The torque balance satisfies a closed loop. Mechanical error is mathematically impossible!"

"There is no error in your mathematics, but real metal does not live on a frictionless blackboard in a zero-gravity vacuum."

<u>Dawn</u> gently unrolled her canvas tool wrap, retrieving a thin set of polished brass feeler gauges and her mother's mother-of-pearl tuning wrench.

Under <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s stunned, suspicious gaze, <u>Dawn</u> delicately slipped a gauge blade no thicker than a cicada's wing into the clearance gap between the seized rotating prism spindle and its ruby bearing sleeve.

"Look right here," <u>Dawn</u> said, tapping the outer collar of the bearing lightly with her fingernail. "The humidity in the Academy's temperature-controlled cleanrooms is thirty percent. But here on Mist Crag at dawn, atmospheric humidity is seventy-eight percent. When brass absorbs moisture and frictional heat builds up, thermal expansion and surface oxidation swell the arbor by exactly zero point zero three millimeters."

<u>Dawn</u> looked up into <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s changing expression: "You designed the bearing clearance with zero tolerance. Your formulas sought absolute mathematical perfection, but you left no room for the metal to breathe between hot and cold. It isn't a dead piece of iron; it's simply choking because you held it too tightly."

With that, <u>Dawn</u> fitted her mother's tuning wrench onto the arbor and reversed the retaining collar by exactly one-quarter of a turn. From her apron, she pulled a small glass vial of Dawn Hall's lavender-scented synthetic watch oil, using a microscopic needle to place a single glistening droplet directly into the balance jewel cup.

"<u>Lulu</u>, provide an initial oscillation pulse."

"Yip!" The mechanical fox deployed her micro-probe and flicked the prism escapement ever so gently.

*Click... clack... tick... tock... tick... tock!*

The dormant, seized mechanism shuddered violently for a heartbeat, then burst into an unimaginably smooth, fluid, and joyful mechanical cadence!

The dodecahedral rotating prism spun like a glittering top. Morning sunlight, streaming through the stained glass of the high arched windows, struck the faceted crystal and shattered into millions of dancing, dazzling rainbows across the chalkboards and cold stone walls!

## IV. The Breakthrough of Thermal Dispersion and Refraction

In an instant, the vast chamber of the Fourth Auxiliary Tower was illuminated by a cathedral of swirling rainbows!

The melodious, crystal-clear chime of gears interlocking echoed through the vaulted hall like an exuberant metal symphony.

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stood rooted to the spot, chalk dust smudging her pale cheek, entirely oblivious to the world around her. The very contraption that her father, Director <u>Lin Yan</u>, had condemned as "hopeless academic trash," and that the entire Academy Council had ridiculed as blackboard delusion, was spinning with ethereal grace in the hands of this civilian girl in an apron—radiating a wonder she had never even dared to dream of!

"It's moving... it's really ticking..." <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s eyes welled with moisture, the fierce pride and silent grief she had harbored for months threatening to overflow in tears.

Sniffling quickly, she forced back the rising tide in her chest. She stepped forward, her eyes devouring the pulsing hairspring: "Zero point zero three millimeters of tolerance... and a surface tension lubrication film. I... I derived all the differential geometry, but I never once felt the thermal impedance of metal with my bare hands."

"Yet without your peerless isochronous equations, my hands alone could never have stabilized this prism at microsecond rotational speed," <u>Dawn</u> said with a bright, earnest smile.

She reached into her satchel and placed the fractured No. 7 mainspring onto the workbench: "Look, this is my failure. Yesterday morning at Dawn Hall, I attempted to charge the core spring of my Star-Wish Orb. But every time I reached the eighth turn, frictional heat generated runaway thermal elasticity decay, seizing the barrel and shattering the alloy. I lacked the advanced calculus to solve it, and could only watch the metal snap in my hands."

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stared at the micro-fractures along the broken spring. The keen instincts of a mathematical and optical prodigy ignited within her like an explosion of fireworks!

"This is localized shear failure from coupled Hooke's strain and thermal expansion!" <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> wiped the chalk from her cheek, her dark eyes flashing with manic excitement. "Your barrel uses a single alloy with an uncompensated thermal diffusion coefficient. If... if we fuse a bimetallic strip of Invar alloy with negative thermal expansion onto the spring spine, and introduce Snell's Law of Refraction to dynamically anneal the core via polarized light—"

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> seized her broken chalk, whirled toward the blackboard, and began writing like an orchestra conductor swept up in inspiration:

```
[Bimetallic Active Thermal Compensation & Snell Polarization Annealing]:

1. Bimetallic Thermal Strain Self-Cancellation Equation:
   ΔL_comp = L₀ · (α_brass - α_invar) · ΔT
   When (α_brass - α_invar) exactly matches spring decay rate (1/k)·(dk/dT):
   The stored elastic torque remains constant: τ(T) ≡ τ₀! No more violent fractures!

2. Snell's Law of Refraction Dynamic Phase Shifting:
   n₁ · sin(θ₁) = n₂ · sin(θ₂)
   Separating excess infrared heat through the dodecahedral prism,
   boosting optical-thermal efficiency by 400%!

[Core Decryption Key]:
Cipher Sequence: [ 22 - 09 - 22 - 09 ]
Decoded Text: V - I - V - I ➜ VIVI!
```

"VIVI..." <u>Dawn</u> whispered, looking at the decrypted letters at the bottom of the blackboard, turning to her in delight: "That's your nickname, isn't it? <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>—Vivi!"

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s cheeks flushed a soft pink. She awkwardly turned her gaze aside, but a rare, genuine curve softened the corners of her lips: "My mother used to call me that when I was small... Nobody has called me that since she passed away."

## V. The Starlight Pact

At that moment, the heavy bronze door creaked open a fraction.

"Excuse me, master engineers. Would you like a warm drink to chase off the morning chill?"

<u>Zephyr</u> stepped in, holding his steaming thermos of honey pomelo tea, his face aglow with an open, affectionate smile.

He set two spotless porcelain cups onto the workbench and poured the golden, fragrant tea to the brim. The sweet citrus warmth instantly banished the lingering frost of the cavernous hall.

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stared at the steaming cup offered to her, momentary confusion softening her gaze. In the Academy, everyone treated her either as an intimidating rival or an unfeeling prodigy. No one had ever handed her a cup of sweet, steaming tea on a cold dawn.

She cradled the porcelain in her palms and took a small sip. The soothing sweetness flowed down her throat and straight into her chest, melting the fortress she had spent years building around her heart.

"The preliminary contest is in twelve days," <u>Dawn</u> said, holding her own tea, her amber eyes blazing with an unyielding fire. "The Academy examiners and <u>Renault</u> sneer that civilian artisans are crude and ignorant, while your father and the council condemn your brilliance as soulless theory. If we hit walls fighting alone, why don't we fight together?"

<u>Dawn</u> extended her right hand toward <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>—a hand calloused, wrapped in linen, yet radiant and warm in the morning light:

"My tactile intuition and feeling for metal, paired with your peerless optics and mathematical matrix. Together, we will build the most flawless, living Star-Wish Orb this city has ever seen. We'll show everyone who ever doubted us just how brightly two girls can shine!"

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> looked into the girl's earnest eyes.

In the rainbow glow cast by the spinning prism, <u>Dawn</u>'s gaze was crystal clear, untainted by deceit—holding only pure devotion to her craft and unreserved trust in a partner.

Taking a deep breath, <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> set down her cup. She reached out her pale, slender hand and clasped <u>Dawn</u>'s warm palm firmly!

"It's a deal," <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> smiled—a bright, dazzling smile that reached her eyes for the first time in years. "I'll recalculate every curvature and Snell optical track. We are going to win this contest."

Beside them, <u>Lulu</u> let out a joyous series of mechanical chirps, and <u>Zephyr</u> laughed with genuine delight.

The morning sun burst through the morning mist, illuminating the spinning prism in an ocean of gold and azure. Two solitary stars, once wandering alone in the void, had locked into the same orbital gear train, ready to ignite a storm of starlight across all of Lumina Port!
