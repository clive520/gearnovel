# Chapter 3: The Secret Furnace of Dawn Hall

## I. The White Coat in Artisan Alley

At three in the afternoon, mild golden sunlight filtered through thin clouds, washing over the undulating cobblestones of the Old Quarter.

Among the bustling throng of Artisan Alley, a slender figure in a tailored silver-white trench coat stood out conspicuously. It was the thirteen-year-old prodigy of the Tian-Shu Science Academy—<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>, known to her inner circle as <u>Vivi</u>.

This was the first time in her thirteen years that <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> had ever set foot in the civilian Old Quarter.

Her entire childhood had been strictly, almost clinical confined within the soaring cleanrooms, echoing marble corridors, and austere libraries of the Academy. Her father, Director <u>Lin Yan</u>, had always warned her in his measured, icy voice: "The streets of common artisans are choked with foul machine grease, uncalibrated black-market scrap, and meaningless squalor. It is a mire that any true scholar seeking mathematical truth must avoid."

Under her father's rigid discipline, <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> had been trained to view emotion as an algorithmic disturbance and the games of her peers as worthless squandering of intellect.

Yet as she ventured down this winding, mist-draped cobblestone lane alone, she was greeted by an atmosphere of vibrant, unpretentious warmth she had never encountered in her father's silent manor.

The air was rich with the fragrance of fresh-baked cinnamon buns, the bitter tang of molten pine rosin, and the warm, clean steam vented from neighborhood boilers. Inside an open-air blacksmith shop, burly smiths stripped to the waist hammered glowing iron pistons to a cheerful, march-like cadence. At a nearby corner, little girls with braided pigtails were giggling in delight as they chased a clockwork dragonfly made of carved wood and brass links, its translucent wings fluttering with astonishing, lifelike grace.

Those clockwork wings bore visible file marks, lacking the gilded sheen of academy laboratory models, yet they danced through the sunbeams with unmistakable, joyful vitality.

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> paused involuntarily, watching the clockwork dragonfly weave through the air, her knuckles tightening around her leather satchel packed with holographic analytical terminals and Invar annealing blueprints. Her immaculate silver-trimmed coat felt starkly alien against the sea of faded canvas aprons. Her pulse fluttered with nervous hesitation, her footsteps uncharacteristically timid.

"<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>! Over here! We're right here!"

A clear, ringing voice brimming with pure joy broke through the bustling street.

Standing beneath the weathered bronze gear signboard of "Dawn Hall" at the far end of the lane was <u>Dawn</u>, sporting a faded navy artisan apron and waving enthusiastically from the stone steps. Perched on her shoulder, the foldable mechanical fox <u>Lulu</u> reared up on its hind legs, its sleek metallic tail spinning like an excited propeller while chiming a melodic cascade of cheerful chimes.

Beholding <u>Dawn</u>'s open, sunlit smile—brighter and warmer than the afternoon sun itself—the knot of tension in <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s chest melted away. Upon the prodigy's guarded, porcelain face blossomed a soft, radiant smile of heartfelt relief.

## II. Grandpa's Osmanthus Cake and the Warm Hearth

"Welcome to Dawn Hall! Come on inside, the wind is picking up!" <u>Dawn</u> jogged down the steps, naturally taking <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s cool hand and guiding her across the threshold.

Stepping through the solid oak door, an intoxicating bouquet of natural beeswax, dried lavender watchmaker oil, warm brass filings, and sweet fruitwood charcoal smoke gently enveloped <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>.

The workshop interior was far deeper and cozier than it appeared from the street. Across soaring mahogany tiers stood hundreds of historical timepieces: mantel clocks etched with the celestial zodiac orbits, glazed hourglasses regulated by miniature hydraulic escapements, and longcase grandfather clocks whose swinging bronze pendulums maintained a serene, steady cadence like spring rain tapping against windowpanes.

Beside a round redwood table in the inner chamber, an elderly gentleman with silver hair and thick bifocal jeweler's spectacles smiled warmly at them.

"Grandpa, this is <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>, whom I told you all about!" <u>Dawn</u> announced with brimming pride, as if introducing a national treasure. "The top optical and celestial scholar in all of Lumina Port, ten times sharper at calculus than the Academy proctors!"

The old master was <u>Master Cai</u> Xiu-Cheng, the venerable proprietor of Dawn Hall. Chuckling softly, the elder set aside his apron and lifted a steaming blue-and-white porcelain dish from the hearth with his weathered, gentle hands:

"Eat while it's hot, child. I harvested these golden osmanthus blossoms from our backyard tree this morning, steamed with wild linden honey into fresh rice cakes. <u>Dawn</u> told me the Academy works young minds like grinding mills. Staring at cold ink all day exhausts the soul; a clever head needs warm, honest sweetness to recharge."

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> received the warm porcelain plate with both hands, momentary vulnerability catching in her throat.

In her father's grand residence, where conversations were strictly limited to examination metrics and biting criticisms, she had never known such unreserved, unconditional warmth from an elder. The neatly sliced rice cake was translucent, flecked with golden petals, radiating an aura of comforting sweetness.

She took a delicate bite. The soft, chewy pastry blossomed with wild honey and floral fragrance across her palate. That honeyed warmth flowed down her throat and into her chest, steadily dissolving the exhaustion, loneliness, and pressure that had weighed on her for years, causing her eyelashes to shimmer with unshed tears.

"Thank you, <u>Master Cai</u>... It is truly, wonderfully delicious," <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> murmured softly, her customary aloofness completely replaced by genuine tenderness.

<u>Master Cai</u> sat down, adjusting his spectacles to study the Invar bimetallic annealing schematics spread across the table, nodding in profound admiration:

"These calculations possess a rare soul. Many scholars and craftsmen mistakenly treat each other as enemies—academics look down on artisans as crude grease-monkeys who lack theory, while artisans mock academics as ivory-tower daydreamers who never touched raw metal. But having spent sixty years repairing clocks, I know the truth: theory is the pointer, while craft is the gear. Only when they mesh with perfect teeth can the heart of time beat with true greatness."

Jingling the heavy ring of brass keys at his belt, the old master gestured toward the archway leading down to the cellar: "Go on down, children. I've already opened the geothermal steam preheater in the forge. Go and hammer out the living heart of your dreams!"

## III. The Underground Forge and Invar Bimetallic Annealing

Descending a spiral stairway carved out of solid granite, the three companions—and one mechanical fox whose whiskers twitched with excitement—entered Dawn Hall's deepest sanctum: the subterranean forge.

The chamber was spacious, dry, and lined with heat-resistant obsidian blocks. Directly connected to the natural geothermal rift beneath Lumina Port, the room was suffused with a steady, comforting warmth.

At the chamber's center stood an imposing composite smelting furnace. Reinforced with heavy copper rivets, its thick quartz observation window revealed an interior glowing with the deep, gem-like crimson of clean geothermal fire.

"This forge was built by the founding masters of Dawn Hall," <u>Dawn</u> explained briskly, fastening her heavy leather gauntlets and grasping the lever of the brass air bellows. "The inner brickwork is coated with pulverized Astra Crystal. As long as the geothermal steam valves remain steady, we can maintain furnace temperatures within half a degree of precision."

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> quickly shed her trench coat for sleek fireproof overalls. Raising her wrist, she activated her holographic analytical terminal.

*Hum—*

A pale-blue three-dimensional projection blossomed above the hearth, streaming real-time convective heat gradients, infrared emission spectra, and thermal impedance curves.

"Our materials are prepared to the highest standard," <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> said, opening her padded flight case to display two mirror-polished alloy bars:

"On the left is high-purity Invar alloy from the Academy's specialized stores: thirty-six percent nickel, sixty-four percent iron. Its miraculous property stems from spontaneous volumetric magnetostriction within the electron lattice, which almost perfectly cancels out thermal lattice expansion, resulting in an expansion coefficient of barely one point two parts per million. On the right is Dawn Hall's heritage cold-rolled spring brass, expanding at nineteen parts per million."

"Our goal is to fuse these two metals of opposing physical natures into a unified composite strip under extreme heat and tonnage," <u>Dawn</u> said, her amber eyes burning with fierce determination.

"Exactly!" <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> gestured to the atomic diffusion matrix on her holographic display. "According to our boundary lattice migration equations, we cannot heat them haphazardly. The forge temperature must be locked precisely at the Curie point of Invar—two hundred and thirty degrees Celsius (230°C)! At this exact threshold, Invar's ferromagnetic state undergoes a transition, minimizing boundary interface energy and maximizing atomic diffusion with the brass!"

"Air dampers at forty-five percent! Geothermal steam pressure up to three point eight atmospheres!" <u>Dawn</u> ordered with seasoned authority, using her heavy tongs to slide both alloy bars into the cherry-red center of the flames.

<u>Lulu</u> vaulted onto the overhead pressure manifold, deploying her fiber-optic thermal sensor into the lavender quenching tank. The fox's titanium ears twitched fifteen times a second, tracking the micro-convective currents of every milliliter of quenching oil.

The firelight illuminated the determined faces of both thirteen-year-old girls. Sweat dampened their bangs, and the radiant heat flushed their cheeks, yet neither showed the slightest sign of retreating—bound together by an incandescent, shared conviction!

## IV. The Curie Temperature and Bimetallic Deflection

"Attention! Core temperature at two hundred twenty-nine point eight... two hundred thirty degrees exact! Lattice interface resonance peaked!" <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> shouted over the roar of the fire.

"Now! Strike!"

Planting her boots firmly against the forge platform, <u>Dawn</u> threw her full weight into the reduction lever of the hundred-and-twenty-kilogram gravity rolling press!

*CLANG————BOOM!!!!*

A thunderous, stone-rattling boom echoed through the underground forge!

Under the immense tonnage at the Curie transition threshold, the atoms of Invar and brass locked together like intermeshing gear teeth! A brilliant cascade of golden sparks showered from the die perimeter like shooting stars, illuminating every black stone brick in blinding splendor!

"Out of the fire! Quench!"

Before the sparks had settled, <u>Dawn</u>'s tongs were already a blur of motion, retrieving the glowing half-millimeter-thick composite ribbon in an elegant arc and plunging it directly into <u>Lulu</u>'s lavender oil bath!

*HISSSSSSSSSS—!*

A glorious plume of fragrant lavender-scented steam billowed upwards, condensing into warm droplets against the stone ceiling.

"Cooling rate at thirty-five degrees per second! Oil temperature stabilizing at twenty degrees! Microstructure converted to uniform martensite and resilient austenite!" <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> announced, her voice shaking with excitement.

Moments later, <u>Dawn</u> carefully retrieved the slender bimetallic ribbon, gleaming with dual sheens of deep indigo and polished gold. Wiping it clean with soft chamois, she mounted it beneath the micrometer gauge on the testing bench.

<u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> rushed to the chalkboard and chalked out Timoshenko's classic bimetallic bending theorem:

```

Curvature Radius Reciprocal:
```

"It worked..." <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stared at the nearly flat torque line across sixty degrees of thermal shock, awe shining in her dark eyes. "It didn't seize, nor did it lose torque! When the heat climbs, the differential expansion forces the spring to flex inward, creating the exact clearance required for the barrel!"

"This is our spring—forged with our own hands!" <u>Dawn</u> cheered with exuberant joy, throwing her arms around the slender scholar in a fierce embrace!

Caught off guard, <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> stiffened for a fraction of a second. But this time, she did not pull away. Feeling <u>Dawn</u>'s warm, honest heartbeat and pure joy, the fortress around <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s heart crumbled completely. She raised her arms and held <u>Dawn</u> tightly, soot smudging her cheek.

"Thank you, <u>Dawn</u>," <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u> whispered, resting her forehead on <u>Dawn</u>'s shoulder, a tear of release slipping down her cheek, her face aglow with genuine happiness.

## V. Shadows Beyond the Window and the First Spark

As the two girls finished assembling the core mechanism into the rotating chassis, light, familiar footsteps echoed down the stone stairway.

<u>Zephyr</u>, dressed in a navy flight jacket, hurried down the steps. He carried two reinforced storm lanterns, his handsome face unusually grave.

"<u>Dawn</u>, <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>, how did the test go?" <u>Zephyr</u> asked urgently.

"Beyond our wildest dreams!" <u>Dawn</u> beamed, proudly holding up the finished spring barrel and giving the arbor a gentle flick. "Look! The Invar bimetallic spring and dodecahedral prism mesh flawlessly. The torque output is rock-solid—just like a healthy, beating heart!"

<u>Zephyr</u> looked at the mechanism shimmering with delicate rainbows and twin metallic hues, deep admiration flashing in his eyes. But then his voice lowered in warning:

"I just came from the aerial flight docks. <u>Renault</u> noticed your absence from the differential geometry defense this afternoon, <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>. He filed a formal grievance with his uncle—the Academy Disciplinary Elder, accusing you of trafficking restricted Invar alloy to civilian workshops."

"<u>Renault</u>..." <u>Lin-Yi-Jie</u>'s brow furrowed, a scoff of disdain curling her lips. "He never changes. Unable to solve thermal equations himself, he resorts to family influence and council politics to stir trouble."

"There's more," <u>Zephyr</u> added gravely. "The dockworkers report that <u>Renault</u>'s family foundries shipped a three-hundred-and-fifty-kilogram steam-driven celestial colossus from the western rifts this afternoon. It mounts six tandem hydraulic rams, intended to dominate the preliminary contest through sheer mass and horsepower."

"Mass does not equal precision, and brute force can never conquer the laws of time," <u>Dawn</u> replied, chin lifted high, her amber eyes blazing with serene, unbreakable resolve. "A true clockmaker never wrestles against machinery with brute strength; we listen to it, guide it, and breathe alongside it."

The three young companions gathered around the workbench, their hands resting together over the golden machine.

Beneath their palms, the Star-Wish Orb—born of ancient artisanal warmth and cutting-edge theoretical calculus—glowed with gentle, steady light. Its rhythmic pulse resonated like the heartbeat of the earth before daybreak.

Outside, the stars of Lumina Port blazed in infinite splendor. The gathering shadows of rivalry and prejudice could no longer deter these three young souls from racing together toward the dawn of victory!
