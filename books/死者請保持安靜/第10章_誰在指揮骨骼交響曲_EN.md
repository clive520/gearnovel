# Chapter 10: Who Conducts the Bone Symphony?

At 4:40 in the afternoon, only three hours and twenty minutes remained before the curtain rose upon the mortal performance at eight o'clock sharp.

Inside the opulent Director's Suite on the top floor of the Grand Theatre, offering a panoramic vantage over the turbid expanse of the Binjiang, heavy crimson velvet drapes whipped wildly in the gale. The air was saturated with antique cigars, aged single-malt whisky, and a faint, secretive tang of clockwork lubricant tucked deep within the rosewood appointments.

The man seated in the carved leather armchair was approaching sixty, tall and lean, an immaculate mane of silver-white hair cascading over his shoulders, dressed in a bespoke handmade tailcoat suit. He was the internationally revered maestro, Lifetime Artistic Director, and Chief Conductor of the Binjiang Philharmonic Orchestra—He Lingfeng.

"Gentlemen, you are saying... Ye Chi is dead, and the subterranean ventilation plenum of this theatre is packed with enough liquid chemical explosive to send every soul here to heaven in three seconds flat?" He Lingfeng turned the gold signet ring on his ring finger with languid elegance, his eyes as impassive as stagnant water, not even a brow twitching.

"Director He, it appears your psychological fortitude is remarkably superior to that of your dehydrated first concertmaster downstairs." Striding forward with one hand in his trench coat pocket, Old Zhou slammed his forcefully muted pink thermos onto the costly rosewood desktop with a heavy thud. "Or perhaps you knew all along that beneath tonight's auditorium seats lay this *Chaconne* orchestrated by the dead?"

He Lingfeng lifted a crystal tumbler, sipping the amber spirit leisurely: "Officer, I have conducted 3,700 symphonies in my career. In Carnegie Hall, in the Musikverein of Vienna, I have witnessed snapped strings, smashed instruments, and musicians suffering massive myocardial infarctions mid-measure. The stage is a colosseum of merciless precision; panic only ever produces sour notes."

"Sour notes?" Standing by the bookshelves, Pei Yi'an let out a soft, freezing chuckle.

He had not taken a seat; instead, he paced before the mahogany library like an unfeeling inspection instrument. His clinical gaze swept past framed photographs with heads of state and musical legends, locking with surgical exactitude onto a microscopic flesh-toned silver hook tucked behind He Lingfeng's right ear.

"Director He, your right auditory nerve has undergone complete necrosis; you rely entirely on a Swiss-manufactured high-precision bone-conduction hearing aid." Hands thrust in his trench coat pockets, Pei Yi'an pivoted, his gaze sharp as a surgical scalpel. "The core gear train of this hearing aid operates on dual-axis micro-hairsprings—a patented design derived directly from Maurice Stein's 1934 'Brass Micro-Resonance Otoscope' custom-built for high-concession elites."

In that split second, the knuckles of He Lingfeng's hand cradling the tumbler locked with rigid, momentary paralysis.

"You possess a formidable grasp of mechanical engineering, young pathologist." He Lingfeng raised his heavy eyelids, his gaze dark as midnight.

"I hold zero interest in mechanical engineering; I am interested solely in human pathology." Pei Yi'an strode back to the desk, planting both hands firmly on the polished wood and leaning forward, his towering clinical presence instantly suffocating the desktop. "Thirty years ago, the youngest Associate Chief of Otolaryngology at the Municipal Hospital was secretly scrubbed from the registry and vanished off the face of the earth on the very night Dr. Lin Qingyuan was murdered—all because he refused to falsify 'auditory restoration metrics' on Lu Chongshan's illicit neuroblocking drug trials."

Pei Yi'an stared into He Lingfeng's clouded yet cavernous eyes, peeling away the final veneer syllable by syllable:

"The young surgeon whose records were wiped clean was never named He Lingfeng; his birth name was Lin Qinghuai—the paternal half-brother of Dr. Lin Qingyuan, and the biological uncle of the late Lin Huaiyuan!"

"What?!" Old Zhou shuddered violently, his right palm snapping instinctively down onto his service sidearm. "Director He... you're Lin Qingyuan's younger brother?! Then all of this... this entire string of serial butcheries, is a blood vendetta orchestrated by you?!"

"Old Zhou, stand down." Pei Yi'an didn't even turn his head, his tone flat as ice. "He is not the Ferryman. If he were the maestro conducting tonight's massacre, the bone-conduction apparatus anchored behind his right ear would not have emitted three abnormal high-frequency feedback squeals exceeding eighty decibels in the last sixty seconds."

Extending his gloved right hand, Pei Yi'an reached out like lightning toward He Lingfeng's ear, his index finger depressing a rice-grain-sized micro-switch concealed behind the hook!

*Screeech—*

A blast of harsh electronic feedback erupted from the hearing aid's micro-transducer, followed immediately by a chilled, impeccably cultured voice processed through multi-tier frequency modulators:

*"Dr. Lin Qinghuai, or rather, the exalted Maestro He. Your time grows exceedingly short. One hundred and ninety minutes remain until eight o'clock tonight. If you fail to mount the podium and conduct Wen Xueqing with that finger-bone bow, your daughter studying at the Conservatoire de Genève will, within the very same second, receive a human cardiac specimen sutured in pure silver wire."*

The transmission severed abruptly in a static burst.

Old Zhou sucked in a harsh breath, blue veins bulging along his temples: "Bastard! That son of a bitch is using Director He's daughter to force him onto that stage as an executioner's puppet!"

"Lin Qingyuan was my elder brother; he guarded the sacred oath of medicine until his final breath." He Lingfeng closed his eyes as two muddy tears tracked down his deeply furrowed cheeks, aging twenty years in the span of a heartbeat. "Thirty years ago I fled to Europe, assumed a pseudonym, and turned to conducting, believing the sanctity of music could wash clean the humiliation and blood the Lu family inflicted upon our bloodline. But the demon never released his grip... he tracked me down, forcing me to serve as the living metronome for this slaughter!"

"Wen Xueqing's forearm flexors are locked in violent spasms; she can't play the Chaconne to save her life." Old Zhou paced in frantic circles. "EOD stated that if the music halts for over three seconds, the chemical gas beneath 1,200 seats will detonate into a fuel-air inferno! Canceling the concert means death, yet letting the curtain rise means death all the same!"

"She doesn't need to cancel, nor does she need to complete the entire composition."

Turning around, Pei Yi'an calmly unzipped his forensic field kit.

From the interior foam slots, he extracted a precision micro-syringe brimming with clear liquid and a sealed sterile pack of ten-centimeter medical acupuncture needles.

"Wen Xueqing's trauma is classic post-traumatic psychogenic hypertonia, not organic neuropathology." A pure, crystalline flame of intellect ignited behind Pei Yi'an's spectacles. "Utilizing high-purity mecobalamin paired with local micro-dose neural blockade, I can abolish the pathological tremors of her radial and median nerves in thirty seconds, restoring at least two hours of flawless kinetic muscle memory."

"And what happens after that?" Old Zhou's eyes widened. "Even if she executes the opening movements, when the piece ends, do you expect the explosives to magically vanish?!"

"The complete Chaconne spans thirteen to fifteen minutes, comprising sixty-four breathtakingly intricate variations." Calibrating the needle against the light, Pei Yi'an expelled a single translucent droplet from the tip. "And at the master ventilation dampers, EOD requires precisely fifteen minutes to saturate the conduits with dry ice and short-circuit the electrical firing arcs."

Turning slightly, Pei Yi'an addressed the ashen He Lingfeng and the battle-hardened Old Zhou:

"There will be no hysteria on that stage tonight. Wen Xueqing will bow her instrument, Maestro He will command the podium, while Old Zhou and I..."

Pei Yi'an nudged his spectacles, his thin lips curling into a blade-sharp, lethally calm smile:

"Before the final cadence descends in the thirteenth minute, we will follow the reverse telemetry of that transmitter, and drag the Ferryman holding the detonator straight out of the auditorium shadows!"
