# Chapter 4: The Old Shipyard in the Rain

At 3:30 a.m., the industrial wasteland on Binjiang's north bank lay submerged in dead silence and torrential rain.

Once the city's most bustling hub, the "East River Shipyard" now retained only dozens of rusted portal gantry cranes towering like iron behemoths, standing mutely beneath low-hanging leaden clouds like a colonnade of scaffolds discarded by time.

During that suffocating midnight interrogation at headquarters, though Hanhai Group Chairman Lu Chongshan had hidden behind an army of attorneys to maintain strict silence regarding the thirty-year-old clinical trial records, his pale lips and trembling fingertips had long betrayed the collapse deep within his soul. Furthermore, Su Tang's trace analysis of the Polaroid print had pinpointed the fatal breakthrough—flaking red-lead anti-corrosive primer on the background metal plates, the specific rivet pitch, and freshwater barnacle spores sampled from Lin Huaiyuan's soles all converged on this shipyard, abandoned for twelve long years.

The aged Santana police cruiser crunched through gravel and pooling rainwater, idling to a halt outside the rusted chain-link fence of Dry Dock No. 2.

"My replacement thermos—ten bucks at an all-night bodega, cheap piece of junk." Old Zhou kicked open the car door, clutching in his right hand a double-walled tin jug stamped with a bright-red double-happiness character, an unlit cigarette dangling from his lips as he groaned in agony. "Lacks all heft, and its thermal insulation is downright miserable. Little Pei, if another madman tries to ram us with an SUV tonight, chucking this thing probably won't even scratch his paint."

"From the perspective of materials mechanics, thin-walled low-carbon sheet steel undergoes severe plastic deformation upon impact, yielding an energy absorption rate far inferior to austenitic stainless steel." Unfurling a long-handled black umbrella, Pei Yi'an stepped across the muddy ruts, his snowy surgical latex gloves starkly prominent against the gloom. "However, Deputy Captain Zhou, rather than lamenting your teaware, I strongly advise regulating your respiration—the atmospheric chloride ion concentration here reaches twenty milligrams per cubic meter. Chronic exposure accelerates pulmonary mucosal fibrosis."

"Forget it. Even ghosts wouldn't visit this godforsaken swamp, yet here the two of us are, sniffing rust like a pair of drowned rats." Despite his irreverent banter, Old Zhou unholstered his service revolver with the fluid grace of a stalking panther, the hammer clicking back softly into the downpour.

Wading through ankle-deep slurry, the two descended along the mottled iron staircase of the dry dock.

The cavernous dock plunged over ten meters down, flanking them with towering concrete retaining walls encrusted in barnacles and moss. Rain cascaded from above like a waterfall, turning the entire basin into a booming abyss of steel echoes.

The snowy beam of the tactical flashlight cut through dense sheets of rain, sweeping across the dark until it locked onto a suspended sheet-metal dispatch cabin at the innermost recess of the dock.

The cabin's iron door hung slightly ajar, a fresh pry mark scarring the latch.

Pei Yi'an nudged the iron door open with a gloved finger. With a dry, screeching grating of rusted hinges, the interior air surged out to meet them.

Neither the brackish tang of the river nor the reek of stale engine grease awaited them.

Instead, it was an eerie, rich cocktail of scents—formalin, high-purity rosin, and the heavy, woody perfume characteristic of damp cello strings.

As the flashlight beam swept across the cramped cabin, Old Zhou's pupils shrank, his breath catching violently in his throat.

In the exact center of the room stood the metal operating chair seen in the Polaroid!

The thick hemp ropes binding the backrest and armrests had been severed with a blade, leaving several spattered drops of darkened, congealed blood across the floorboards. On the iron console directly before the chair, a mechanical clockwork metronome clicked back and forth at an agonizingly measured pace.

*Tick... tick... tick...*

The monotonous, hollow cadence reverberated inside the sealed metal chamber, striking once every two seconds with suffocating precision.

"Little Pei, don't move!" Old Zhou clamped a hand onto Pei Yi'an's shoulder, his eyes fixed on the floor beneath the console. "Look at the deck!"

Crisscrossing the concrete floor were dozens of steel strings as fine as human hairs. One end of each string anchored to the metronome's mainspring on the console, while the other ends disappeared into the corrugated wall partitions, glinting with a cold, steely-blue sheen under the flashlight beam.

"This is no explosive trigger," Pei Yi'an halted, his demeanor unnervingly calm as he leaned forward to inspect the tension and gauge of the wire. "These are high-tension gut-core wound strings, typically reserved for the C-string of a concert-grade cello. The killer utilized the metronome's pendulum as a kinetic counterbalance. Sever any single string, and the upset equilibrium will trip the mechanism concealed within the walls."

Reaching into his breast pocket for his custom silver forceps, Pei Yi'an clamped the junction of a steel wire with unwavering fingers, tracing the connection under the flashlight's illumination.

Directly behind the console, engraved deep into the rusted iron sheeting with a hardened steel drill, was a massive Renaissance anatomical sketch—*The Vitruvian Man*.

Except the figure's head had been supplanted by a weeping cello, and embedded within its cardiac region was a miniature scrap of sheet music saturated in turpentine and rosin.

Printed across the header in crimson stamp-pad ink was a row of Chinese characters accompanying a prominent name:

**"Movement Two: Dedicated to the Deaf Juror—Principal Cellist of the Binjiang Philharmonic, Lu Siyuan."**

"Lu Siyuan?" Old Zhou ground out the name through clenched teeth. "Lu Chongshan's nephew—Binjiang's most celebrated young cellist!"

"More than that." Tucking his forceps away, the glint behind Pei Yi'an's glasses turned razor-sharp. "In the appendix to that thirty-year-old neuroblocking agent clinical trial, Subject 07 was a four-year-old child suffering from congenital auditory nerve hypoplasia. The legal guardian signed in the ledger was none other than Lu Siyuan's father."

"A calculated, thirty-year vendetta targeting the Lu family's bloodline!" Old Zhou spun toward the tempest outside the window.

At that precise moment, from the muddy clearing outside the dispatch cabin, a faint yet distinct footstep landed—

*Squelch.*

The unmistakable suction of heavy rubber boots pressing into wet mire.

Old Zhou and Pei Yi'an froze instantly, leaving only the mechanical metronome inside the cabin to measure the silence with its relentless, frozen *tick, tick*.

Out in the rain, a tall, slender silhouette beneath a massive black umbrella emerged through the torn chain-link fence, stepping across the industrial graveyard toward the dry dock.

Gripping his sidearm against the doorframe, Old Zhou's gaze hardened into cold steel; while Pei Yi'an adjusted his silver spectacles, his tongue pressing the final, biting chill of his peppermint lozenge deep into his throat.
