# Chapter 13: The Final Movement at the Grand Theatre

At 7:50 in the evening, barely ten minutes remained before the eight o'clock curtain chimes.

Above the rain-lashed expanse of the river, the Binjiang Grand Theatre resembled a colossal crystal seashell reclining across the stormy night, its soaring dome of tempered glass and titanium alloy trusses gleaming with an icy, blinding silver beneath flashes of lightning.

Outside the grand portico, luxury sedans converged in droves as the city's high-society elite, civic dignitaries, and corporate magnates filed across rain-drenched red carpets. Meanwhile, deep in the gloom of the subterranean stormwater retention vault beneath the theatre, a rusted iron maintenance hatch was kicked open with a violent crash.

Old Zhou emerged dripping with putrid, soot-black slurry, his trousers hitched above his knees, grimly shielding his pink thermos with its faded strawberry logo against his tactical vest while keeping his service pistol cocked in his right hand.

"Cough... hack! In thirty years of hunting killers and raiding smuggler dens, this is the very first time I've crawled through three kilometers of sewer pipe just to attend a high-brow symphony," Old Zhou grumbled, wiping rainwater from his eyes as he swept the service corridor leading backstage. "Little Pei, looking like this, do you reckon the security guards will pin me against the wall with riot forks?"

"From the perspectives of epidemiology and public sanitation, security would have ample justification to quarantine you and fumigate your entire vicinity," Pei Yi'an replied, dropping lithely from the ladder with his forensic kit, his trench coat mud-splattered yet his gaze behind gold-rimmed spectacles as dispassionate as a vernier caliper. "Nine minutes until the clocktower strikes eight. The guest dressing room is the third door on the left."

Evading patrol guards, the pair hurried along the carpeted, acoustically insulated backstage corridors.

The backstage area was a flurry of organized chaos: musicians clad in tailored tailcoats and sweeping evening gowns tuned their strings, applied rosin, and leafed through sheet music. The atmosphere hummed with the mingled aromas of turpentine, luxury perfume, and pre-performance adrenaline.

"Hold," Pei Yi'an halted suddenly, his eyes cutting like a scalpel through the ajar dressing room door.

Upon the lounge sofa inside, the evening's guest concertmaster lay slumped in deep slumber before the vanity mirror, breathing rhythmically. Pei Yi'an stepped inside, rolled back an eyelid, and pressed two fingers against his carotid artery: "Pupillary light reflex is sluggish, with faint traces of almond sweetness on the breath. An intravenous micro-dose of propofol—titrated with milligram precision to induce exactly three hours of sedation without risking respiratory depression."

On the brass valet stand beside the mirror, the tailor-made tailcoat was gone, along with the antique Guarneri violin from its velvet-lined case.

"He switched places! That brat put on the tailcoat and slipped into the orchestra pit!" Old Zhou's expression darkened as he lunged toward the stage wings.

"Captain Zhou, hold your ground," Pei Yi'an seized Old Zhou's wrist with unexpected, iron grip. "Eighteen hundred spectators occupy that auditorium, the front tiers lined with municipal leaders and chamber of commerce chairmen. If you charge in waving a sidearm and screaming about explosives, the resulting stampede will cause casualties far outstripping a targeted blast."

"Then what the hell do you propose I do? Stand by and watch him blow eighteen hundred innocent souls to kingdom come?!" Old Zhou ground his molars, his eyes rimmed with blood.

"The microwave detonation link was severed and failsafe-locked in the aqueduct; he holds no remote trigger," Pei Yi'an pointed to the sodden invitation. "The primary patron of tonight's gala is Chu Tiancheng, Chairman of Chu Bio-Pharmaceutical Corporation—the former vice-director of Municipal Hospital's pathology institute thirty years ago, and Dr. Lin Qingyuan's immediate supervisor."

Old Zhou shuddered violently, all disparate puzzle pieces snapping together like an electric arc. "Chu Tiancheng?! After Dr. Lin was murdered thirty years ago, he was the one who seized all of Lin's anti-cancer patents, founded Chu Bio-Pharmaceutical, and became the wealthiest tycoon in Binjiang!"

"Lin Qingyuan's cranium bears comminuted blunt-force trauma, and Chu Tiancheng built his financial empire upon that bloodstained intellectual property," Pei Yi'an said, swiftly slipping into an attendant's dark stagehand coat and clipping on an ID pass. "Qin Mo does not seek to slaughter the innocent. He intends, on the night of Chu Tiancheng's lifetime achievement commendation, to use Lin Qingyuan's bones to execute a death sentence thirty years overdue."

Out front, the chime signaling the opening of the performance rang out clear and sharp.

7:58 in the evening.

The glittering crystal chandeliers in the Grand Theatre's primary auditorium dimmed in slow unison as azure spotlights cascaded like liquid silver upon center stage. Thunderous applause erupted from the eighteen hundred patrons in attendance.

In the center box of the front row, sixty-something Chu Tiancheng, his silver hair groomed to absolute perfection, straightened the glistening "Civic Medal of Distinguished Contribution" pinned to his lapel, beaming as he nodded to surrounding dignitaries and socialites.

From the shadowed wings, the guest concertmaster, dressed in a sharply tailored black tailcoat, strode with poised grace into the focus of the spotlights.

His complexion was pale to the point of translucence, his nose aquiline, and deep within his hollow sockets burned two chilling pinpricks of cold flame. Clutched in his left hand was a violin whose scroll was capped with a gleaming brass ouroboros slide.

It was Qin Mo.

"Tonight, prior to commencing Brahms' Concerto," Qin Mo addressed the standing microphone, his voice cultured, resonant, and possessing an almost hypnotic cadence, "I take the liberty of dedicating this unscripted solo introduction to my mentor, who has slept for thirty years in the freezing silt of the Yangtze River—Dr. Lin Qingyuan."

Hearing the name "Lin Qingyuan," the triumphant grin on Chu Tiancheng's face froze in an instant into a ghastly, bloodless death mask.

While the audience exchanged puzzled whispers, the bow in Qin Mo's right hand descended through the air!

*Skrrrt—!*

It was no tone of gut or nylon, but a piercing, metallic shriek resembling a scalpel slicing through visceral pleura!

The thirteen filaments strung across the bow were braided from high-tensile nickel-titanium wire and human collagen fibrils. The moment hair bit string, the surround-sound matrix throughout the theatre wailed with a violent sub-bass howl!

"Ugh!"

In the front row, Chu Tiancheng clutched his chest in sudden agony, gasping for air as all color drained from his face.

At the edge of the proscenium, Pei Yi'an and Old Zhou closed in. Pei Yi'an's eyes narrowed on Qin Mo's fingerings: "Infrasonic resonance. The violin body houses a miniature piezoelectric transducer; the sub-harmonic frequency generated by the strings sits at precisely 7.83 Hertz—the intrinsic mechanical resonant frequency of the human myocardium! He is utilizing acoustic resonance to trigger ventricular fibrillation!"

"Chu Tiancheng has a clinical history of myocardial ischemia and coronary disease—this isn't mere assassination, it's an acoustic execution engineered to masquerade as natural cardiac arrest!" Old Zhou roared, tearing aside the stage curtains and lunging forward with the agility of a panther!

"Police! Freeze! Municipal Criminal Investigation Detachment!" Old Zhou's thunderous shout echoed through the cavernous hall.

The entire auditorium erupted into instant pandemonium!

A sneer of cold disdain flickered across Qin Mo's eyes; rather than halting, his wrist flicked into a breathtaking staccato flurry like a raging tempest!

Shockwaves of infrasound pulsated through the air. Chu Tiancheng's eyes rolled upward as he slipped from his seat, convulsing violently upon the carpeted floor!

Just as the bow hovered to strike the fatal crescendo—

*Whoosh!*

A streak of silver sliced the air, biting into the violin's spruce soundboard with uncanny precision!

It was a solid-silver No. 14 dissecting scalpel!

The blade embedded deep into the lip of the treble f-hole, severing the crystal oscillator circuit beneath; in a split second, the demonic hum collapsed into dead silence!

Qin Mo swayed slightly, raising his gaze toward Pei Yi'an standing at the apron of the stage.

Hands tucked into his coat pockets, Pei Yi'an stepped unhurriedly into the glare of the spotlights, his glasses catching the brilliant white glare: "Qin Mo, when you removed Lin Qingyuan's cranium thirty years ago, your purpose was not revenge, but preservation of evidence. If you slay Chu Tiancheng tonight, his death will be ruled acute myocardial infarction, and the atrocities of the Chu Corporation will be cremated alongside his corpse."

"Then tell me, did the law ever grant my master justice?!" Qin Mo rasped, three decades of suppressed fury and agony erupting from his throat like volcanic lava. "Thirty years! The killer basks in honors and medals, while my mentor's bones soaked in the sewer sludge for thirty years!"

"Your forensic post-mortem was never completed," Pei Yi'an said with tranquil calm, withdrawing a sealed, sterile evidence pouch from within his lapel.

Sealed inside was the micro-laser polarized imprint just harvested from the cranium's dentition in the aqueduct.

"Embedded within Lin Qingyuan's occipital fracture are 1.2-millimeter brass particulate residues, chemically identical to the custom brass mallet issued to Chu Tiancheng when he served as vice-director thirty years ago," Pei Yi'an's voice resonated clearly through the microphone across the hall. "The Criminalistics Division has simultaneously impounded Chu Tiancheng's vault ledgers and the original 1994 pathology manuscripts. The truth of thirty years ago shall be carried into a court of law by living forensic scientists—not buried in a grave by your vigilante executions."

Down in the stalls, Chu Tiancheng slumped upon the floor, ashen-faced and limp as wet clay upon hearing those words.

Qin Mo stared fixedly at the evidence pouch, his fingers trembling upon the bow until a solitary tear spilled silently down his hollow cheek.

*Clatter.*

The violin bearing three decades of torment slipped from his grasp, striking the polished teak boards with a hollow echo.

Old Zhou stepped forward, the handcuffs snapping crisply around Qin Mo's wrists. The veteran detective exhaled a heavy sigh, gently adjusting the rumpled collar of Qin Mo's tailcoat: "Kid... it's been thirty years. Come back to the station with me and drink a cup of hot water."

Tilting his head back toward the sea of glittering lights in the dome, Qin Mo's lips curved into an expression of relief—tinged with an eerie, knowing smile.

"Captain Zhou, Doctor Pei... do you truly believe that by apprehending me, this city's symphony has reached its finale?"

Qin Mo's whisper was barely audible, yet it sent a sharp jolt through Pei Yi'an's pupils:

"I was merely an apprentice bowing the strings. The true 'Ferryman'... at this very moment, sits within the morgue of your own Forensic Center, waiting for you to return with my bones."

In the distance above the stage, the deep, solemn chime of eight o'clock from the historic clocktower finally boomed through the driving rain.
