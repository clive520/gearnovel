# Chapter 1: The Dead Are More Polite Than the Living

In November, the river mist over Binjiang City was as thick as an overnight pot of rich soup that had boiled and then cooled.

At 5:40 a.m., a corpse floated up near the abandoned pontoon bridge of the old docks, wedged between two collision pilings overgrown with dark green moss, bobbing gently with the turbid river currents.

When Zhou Chenglin, Deputy Captain of the Municipal Criminal Investigation Detachment, arrived wrapped in his washed-out army-green jacket and clutching a giant stainless-steel thermos ten centimeters in diameter, three rings of morning joggers and early-market grocery shoppers had already swarmed outside the police cordon.

"All right, disperse, aunts and uncles! This isn't a movie set—no free eggs or boxed lunches here," Old Zhou slurped a mouthful of boiling goji and codonopsis tea, his voice rasping like two sheets of sandpaper rubbing together. "Step back beyond the yellow tape! Trample the footprints on this scene, and the bureau's fines will be mailed straight to your mailboxes."

The cordon was lifted a sliver, and a tall, slender figure slipped quietly inside.

The newcomer wore an immaculately pressed charcoal-grey wool trench coat over a crisp, white stand-collar shirt, with ultra-thin silver half-rim spectacles resting on the bridge of his nose. He carried a heavy black aluminum alloy forensic kit, his leather shoes treading across the wet gravel with a precise cadence, like a freshly calibrated quartz clock.

"Little Pei, you're here?" Old Zhou turned, his face wrinkling into a street-smart Maitreya smile. "Take a look—freshly fished out, still warm... er, well, never mind, the river's freezing; he's probably chilled to the bone."

Pei Yi'an stopped, his slender fingers lightly nudging his glasses frame, his gaze not lingering for even half a second on Old Zhou's wrinkled grin.

"Deputy Captain Zhou, according to the laws of thermodynamics and postmortem biophysical changes in a flowing aquatic environment of seven degrees Celsius, there is zero probability of anything being 'warm,'" Pei Yi'an's voice was crisp, calm, and utterly devoid of worldly warmth. "Furthermore, the odor of rehmannia root and angelica wafting from your thermos has severely contaminated the sampling of volatile organic compounds within a three-meter radius."

Old Zhou chuckled, entirely unoffended, and leisurely screwed the lid back onto his thermos. "Alright, alright. I was just worried you'd get hypoglycemia this early. The basement autopsy lab was lit all night again—are you planning to pickle yourself into a specimen too, kid?"

"The dead are more polite than the living," Pei Yi'an produced a flat tin box from his trench coat pocket. With a graceful flick of his slender fingers, the lid popped open, and he tossed a dark-green, extra-strong peppermint lozenge into his mouth. "At the very least, while lying there, they neither tell lies nor force others to ingest plant decoctions that reek of herbal decay."

Old Zhou gave a dry cough, completely tongue-tied, while Su Tang, the intern forensic assistant jogging over with an evidence crate, couldn't suppress a giggle.

"Morning, Teacher Pei! Morning, Captain Zhou!" Su Tang panted as she handed over sterile gloves and shoe covers. "Initial on-scene temperature measurements complete: water temperature 6.8°C, ambient air temperature 8.2°C."

Pei Yi'an pulled on the latex gloves. His hands were long-fingered and lean, steady without the slightest tremor. He knelt on the wet, cold waterproof tarp, his gaze falling upon the victim who had just been hauled ashore.

The deceased was male, visually estimated to be in his early thirties, dressed in an impeccably tailored bespoke designer suit. Now, however, the expensive cashmere fabric was waterlogged, heavy, and warped, the collar askew, and only a single hand-stitched Oxford shoe remained on his feet.

"No defensive wounds indicative of a violent struggle on the body; nail beds are clean, with no mud or sand trapped underneath," Pei Yi'an's fingertips brushed gently across the deceased's jawline and neck. "Facial cyanosis is indistinct, and no classic mechanical asphyxiation petechial hemorrhages are visible beneath the conjunctiva. Old Zhou, this was no accidental drowning."

The cynical playfulness on Old Zhou's face evaporated instantly, replaced by the sharp, hound-like glint characteristic of a seasoned detective. "Not drowned? But he was washed all the way from the mid-river vortex to the pontoon bridge here."

"A drowning victim's respiratory tract and lungs inhale massive quantities of river water and plankton, leading to acute pulmonary emphysema and forming fine, white mushroom-like foam around the mouth and nostrils," Pei Yi'an loosened the deceased's costly silk necktie, his motions tender, as if handling fragile porcelain. "His mouth and nose are as clean as if he had just slept on a five-star hotel's goose-down pillow. He was tossed into the river post-mortem."

"Murder and body dump," Old Zhou growled four heavy words, glancing up at the murky expanse of the river.

"To be precise, a murder disguised with extreme clumsiness, yet staged with immense arrogance regarding timing," Pei Yi'an stood, stripping off his outer layer of gloves, his expression stern to the point of ruthlessness. "There is pronounced, abnormal distension in the victim's epigastric region. On-scene conditions do not permit an invasive examination. Seal the remains immediately and transfer them to Autopsy Suite One at headquarters."

Forty minutes later, in Basement Level 1 of the Municipal Police Bureau.

This was the quietest sanctuary in the entire building. The air was permeated with a clinical chill—a distinctive scent mingling high-grade disinfectant, sub-zero air conditioning, and formalin.

The shadowless surgical lamps cast glaring white beams, illuminating the stainless-steel autopsy table like a sacred, frigid altar.

Pei Yi'an had changed into full sterile surgical scrubs; behind his pale blue protective visor, his clear eyes were focused without a speck of impurity. Old Zhou leaned against the observation window by the autopsy suite door, having swapped his thermos for an unlit cigarette, habitually sniffing it under his nose.

"Autopsy transcript, Case 1107, unidentified male floater," Su Tang sat before the recording terminal, her fingers tapping briskly across the keyboard.

Pei Yi'an gripped the No. 4 scalpel. The blade hovered momentarily above the deceased's sternum as he whispered, "Excuse me."

This was his sole ritual before making an incision—a murmur so soft it resembled an inadvertent sigh.

The cold, razor-sharp blade sliced smoothly down the anterior midline of the neck, dividing the sternum and abdominal wall. Under his masterful technique, the tissue parted cleanly to either side like opening flower petals.

"No pleural effusion in the thoracic cavity; bilateral lung surfaces are pale and collapsed, verifying that no violent, involuntary water inhalation occurred prior to death," Pei Yi'an moved with dazzling speed, the dissecting shears rotating nimbly between his fingers. "Opening the abdominal cavity. Stand by for olfactory sampling."

Su Tang immediately leaned forward with the sampling syringe. As a small three-centimeter incision was made in the gastric wall, a bizarre scent quietly drifted outward.

It was neither the usual pungent rot nor the acrid burn of gastric acid, but an eerie, sweet fragrance mingling bitter almonds, dried rose petals, and high-purity beeswax.

"What is that smell?" Old Zhou, outside the protective glass, straightened abruptly, pulling the cigarette away from his lips. "Did the guy drink perfume before he died?"

"Not perfume," Pei Yi'an's brow furrowed slightly for the very first time. He lifted a pair of solid-silver dissecting forceps and slowly probed toward the pyloric antrum of the stomach.

The tip of the forceps struck something solid, eliciting a faint yet crisp metallic ring—*clink*.

In that instant, everyone in the autopsy suite held their breath.

Pei Yi'an's wrist was rock-steady as he withdrew the forceps millimeter by millimeter. Once the mucus was rinsed away with sterile saline, a mysterious object encased in a layer of translucent, pale-yellow surgical bone wax was finally laid bare under the harsh surgical light.

It was neither a cyanide capsule nor a smuggled diamond.

It was an antique, ornate solid brass key, approximately six centimeters in length.

The bow of the key was meticulously sculpted into the totem of an ouroboros—a winged serpent swallowing its own tail. The serpent's eyes were set with two tiny, dark-red garnets that gleamed with an ominous luster in the frigid light. Along the lateral shank of the key, fine micro-engraving revealed a neat Latin abbreviation and a mysterious four-digit number:

**"M.S. 1934"**

"Good heavens..." Su Tang sucked in a breath of cold air, her stylus nearly slipping from her trembling fingers. "Who would swallow an antique key into their stomach?"

"It was not swallowed," Pei Yi'an clamped the hefty key with his forceps, his gaze more piercing than ever before. "There is retrograde contusion and hemorrhage along the esophageal mucosa, and the cardiac sphincter is torn. This key was forcibly shoved into the depths of his stomach using tube-feeding equipment while he was moribund and semiconscious."

Outside the observation glass, Old Zhou slammed his fist against the frame, sending ripples through the tea in his thermos.

"He wasn't protecting it, nor was the killer concealing stolen loot," Pei Yi'an murmured, the cold glint of the ouroboros mirrored in his lenses. "The killer treated this human being as a living safe full of riddles, and hid the key to the truth deep inside the victim's corpse."

At that very moment, the morgue clock chimed the top of the hour, its dull, lingering toll echoing through the subterranean depths.

Pei Yi'an turned, looking through the glass at Old Zhou's grim countenance, his thin lips parting slightly:

"Old Zhou, have the field squad ready the car. The dead has delivered his letter to us; now it's our turn to find the door that was locked thirty years ago."
