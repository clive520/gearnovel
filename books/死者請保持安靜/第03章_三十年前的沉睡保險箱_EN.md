# Chapter 3: The Sleeping Safe of Thirty Years

The violent roar of an engine tore a deafening gash through the narrow, rain-slicked old alleyway.

Completely matte-black and stripped of its license plates, the heavy-duty off-road vehicle charged forward like a berserk iron beast, hurling biting river winds and slurry straight toward the food stall's fragile plastic canopy!

"Get down!" Old Zhou bellowed, grabbing the nape of Pei Yi'an's trench coat with one hand and violently dragging him toward the blind corner of a greasy brick wall. At the same instant, his other wrist flicked, and the solid stainless-steel thermos—brimming with boiling tea and weighing over two pounds—flew whistling from his grip like a silver meteor.

*Crash—shatter!*

The weighty thermos struck the SUV's right headlight with pinpoint precision, spraying boiling goji and codonopsis tea mixed with shattered explosion-proof glass in every direction.

The driver had evidently never anticipated meeting such an unhinged "non-lethal civilian defensive ordinance." His vision impaired, he wrenched the steering wheel hard; the fender scraped violently past a concrete doorpost and slammed headlong into a massive corrugated sheet-metal dumpster by the roadside, the screech of crumpling metal drowning out the torrential downpour.

"Deputy Captain Zhou, according to formulas for projectile motion and rigid-body collisions, your launch angle was forty-two degrees with an initial velocity of approximately fourteen meters per second." Pinned against the brick corner with his spectacles knocked askew, Pei Yi'an remained as composed as if titrating reagents in a sterile laboratory. "However, while successfully disabling the enemy's optical illumination assembly, you squandered at least three hundred milliliters of premium antioxidant herbal hot beverage and irreversibly decommissioned a food-grade steel vessel with over five years of active service."

"Shut your trap, ancestor! You still have the leisure to calculate parabolas for me?!" Drawing his Type 64 service pistol from his waistband, Old Zhou leaned back against the wall, chest heaving, his greying temples drenched in icy rain. "Get in the car! Their target is that key; if two more of those show up tonight, we'll be skipping the morgue and moving straight into your refrigeration drawers!"

The SUV's reverse gear wailed shrilly, and a pitch-black muzzle emerged from the lowering bulletproof window.

Old Zhou squeezed the trigger without hesitation, planting two rounds cleanly into the sidewalls of the tires. Shoving Pei Yi'an into the battered Santana, Old Zhou stomped the accelerator to the floorboards. The decrepit engine gave a violent series of coughing retches, spewing black exhaust as it whipped into the labyrinth of old-town dead ends.

Twenty minutes later, in the Yunbin Hi-Tech Zone: the corporate headquarters of Hanhai Investment Group.

In stark contrast to the grime and mud of the historic quarters, this modern seventy-story skyscraper was forged of deep-blue double-glazed curtain walls and cold-rolled structural steel, rising beneath midnight flashes of lightning like an icy scalpel stabbing straight into the storm clouds.

Yet the tower's base preserved a jarring neoclassical façade of dark-red granite—the original site of the 1930s concession's "Mingsheng Trading House" clocktower. The modern financial empire had encased it within reinforced concrete like an antique specimen, flaunting an unspoken dominion over history.

"The building's security was upgraded to lockdown status right after Lin Huaiyuan vanished." Old Zhou cut the Santana's engine in the shadow of a rear-alley transformer room, shaking the rainwater from his jacket. "They can keep out modern hackers, but they can't block the ventilation shafts I memorized thirty years ago chasing burglars through the old trading company."

Straightening the damp lapels of his trench coat, Pei Yi'an pulled a fresh pair of sterile surgical gloves from his pocket and snapped them on. "When Maurice Stein engineered this subterranean bastion, he implemented a German purely mechanical hydraulic interlock. In that era, the ultimate security was not electrical circuitry, but physical geometry."

Slipping past infrared sensors, the two followed a cramped, damp service duct winding steadily downward.

The air grew increasingly subterranean and frigid. The biting exhaust fumes and synthetic rubber stench of the high-rise parking garage receded, replaced by the damp mossy tang of century-old masonry, oxidized iron, and the dull, cold perfume of petroleum lubricants settled over decades.

It was the scent of time itself, fermented in the dark depths of the earth.

Passing through a final cast-iron archway secured with a rusted padlock, the space opened abruptly before them.

It was an octagonal subterranean chamber spanning two hundred square meters. The perimeter stone walls were a meter thick, with massive load-bearing pillars carved with Renaissance-style acanthus leaf reliefs. And right in the center of the chamber loomed a massive circular vault door of pure dark iron, three meters high and weighing dozens of tons.

In place of a modern digital keypad or fingerprint scanner, the core of the door was dominated by a colossal cast-bronze relief disk depicting an ouroboros.

Where the serpent's head clasped its tail in the very center, there lay a deep, vertical keyhole.

"This is it." Old Zhou raised his tactical flashlight, the beam trembling across the mottled green patina as his breath hitched. "The old trading house's private vault... hasn't been opened in thirty years."

Stepping forward, Pei Yi'an used forceps to extract the brass key from its moisture-proof evidence pouch.

"The seventh-tier bitting counterbalances an internal gravity pendulum." Pei Yi'an's fingertips brushed gently along the freezing rim of the keyhole as he pressed his ear flat against the vault's dense metal skin. "Maurice Stein was a clinical obsessive. He mounted an inverted glass vial of concentrated sulfuric acid inside the tumbler; if the torque deviates by more than two degrees or the sequence falters, the acid will melt every interlocking gear within three seconds."

"Little Pei... how sure are you?" Cold sweat seeped into the palm gripping Old Zhou's sidearm. "Should we call the bureau's bomb squad?"

"Bomb disposal experts know how to defuse TNT, but they cannot hear the heartbeat of a classical master mechanic." Pei Yi'an's voice was placid as he bit down on his peppermint lozenge. "Remain silent, please. Listen to the bone conduction of the tumblers."

The entire subterranean vault sank into absolute, tomb-like stillness.

Only the muffled rushing of municipal drainage conduits far overhead and Pei Yi'an's steady, shallow breathing remained.

The brass key slid smoothly into the cylinder, eliciting a crisp, ancient metallic friction.

Pei Yi'an did not turn it immediately. Instead, guided by the Latin cipher "V.I.T.R.I.O.L." etched into the shank, he followed the harmonic frequencies of the seven letters: pushing the key three millimeters inward, rotating fifteen degrees counterclockwise, pausing for two seconds, then snapping it back half a notch to the right!

*Click.*

A crisp snap of spring steel, like dried bone resetting into its socket, detonated deep within the door!

Immediately following, hydraulic counterweights weighing several tons plummeted through internal shafts, and a gear train that had slumbered for nearly a century began meshing with ferocity, humming like the waking gears of an ancient cathedral clock!

Dust billowed from the seam like steam.

The colossal circular vault door eased outward by half a foot, unleashing a chilling draft heavy with pungent chemical preservatives and decayed paper.

Exchanging a glance, Old Zhou and Pei Yi'an hauled the iron door fully open.

The flashlight beam pierced the gloom, illuminating the center of the cavernous vault.

Contrary to expectation, there were no towering stacks of gold bullion or jewel-encrusted lockboxes in this legendary repository of colonial wealth.

In the center of the vast granite floor sat a lone, modest portable chest cast from black pig iron.

The chest was unlocked, its lid ajar.

Donning his sterile gloves, Pei Yi'an carefully hoisted the heavy iron cover. The flashlight pooled into the bottom of the container, revealing three artifacts resting in the cold air:

The first was a ten-centimeter reel-to-reel magnetic tape from the 1930s—a Telefunken model long out of production, its metallic rim gleaming darkly;

The second was a yellowed medical trial ledger bound with rough hemp twine, stamped with "TOP SECRET" seals, its title page reading: *Human Tolerance Trials of Novel Neuroblocking Agents (1994)*—and in the final column under guardian authorization, the signature read unmistakably: *Lu Chongshan*, the current chairman of Hanhai Group;

But the third item was modern, placed there only recently—

A brand-new Polaroid print.

The photograph depicted Lin Huaiyuan, who had died just yesterday. He was bound tightly with rough hemp rope to a metallic operating chair, his eyes wide and dilated with bottomless terror. And on the back of the print, written in deep crimson oil-based ink, was a line of Chinese calligraphy executed with perverse elegance:

**"The first confession has been delivered to the autopsy table. The second judge is tuning his strings. —Respectfully dedicated to Dr. Pei Yi'an"**

The temperature within the underground vault seemed to plunge straight to absolute zero in that instant.

Staring at the inscription on the back of the photograph, Old Zhou's face turned ash-grey, his lips trembling violently. "He... he's issuing an open challenge directly to you?!"

Pei Yi'an stared fixedly at the crimson brushstrokes, his slender fingers lightly brushing the edge of the Polaroid. Behind his lenses, deep within those eyes that were normally so detached and clinical, a sharp, dangerous flame ignited for the very first time.

"No, Deputy Captain Zhou." Pei Yi'an straightened, his voice whisper-soft like an unsheathed scalpel. "This is no challenge. This is an invitation to a serial autopsy."
