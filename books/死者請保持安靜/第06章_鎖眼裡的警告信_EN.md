# Chapter 6: The Warning Inside the Keyhole

No sooner had the chilling chuckle faded from the transceiver than the deep machinery inside the suspended dispatch cabin's iron walls erupted with an ear-splitting screech of grinding gears.

*Rumble—*

The entire suspended sheet-metal shack convulsed violently. The mechanical clockwork metronome—which had been ticking once every two seconds—suddenly surged into a frenzied blur, its needle sweeping across the calibrated dial and spitting out frantic, venomous *tick-tick-tick-ticks* like a striking serpent!

"Old Zhou! Beneath your feet!" Pei Yi'an barked sharply.

Inside the corrugated sheet-metal partitions surrounding the cabin, neither lethal gas nor fire erupted; instead, the savage roar of rushing water tearing through sluice valves reverberated from the deep. Gazing through the floorboards, the two massive floodgates at the basin of Dry Dock Number Two—rusted shut and abandoned for decades—had been pried open a three-foot breach by concealed hydraulic rams!

Swirling currents of the mighty Binjiang river—laden with biting river winds and yellowish-black silt—roared into the depths of the dry dock like a rampaging beast unchained from its cage. In the span of a single breath, the bottom of the ten-meter-deep dock was converted into a churning mud-swept sea, the waterline rocketing upward at an alarming half-meter per minute!

"Damn it! He's not trying to blow us up!" Old Zhou lunged toward the doorway, violently throwing his weight against the iron door handle, yet the heavy steel leaf was hopelessly jammed by external deadbolts dropped into place. "That bastard wants to drown the three of us alive in this dry-dock iron bucket—the exact same way he drowned Dr. Lin Qingyuan thirty years ago!"

"The exterior is locked by bidirectional gravity deadbolts triggered by water-level floats; pistol rounds cannot breach forty-five-millimeter quenched alloy lock cylinders." Pei Yi'an's voice remained calm to the point of brutality, but his hands swiftly buttoned his cuffs at his sides. "The dispatch cabin is only four meters above the dock floor; at the current flood rate, water will submerge this room completely within seven minutes."

"Stop calculating! Find a way to open the door!" Collapsed on the flooding floorboards, Lu Siyuan clutched his cello case, unleashing a shriek of raw despair. "I don't want to drown... I don't want to die in this river like my father!"

"Shut up! Can your sobbing pick the lock?!" Old Zhou snarled as he leveled his sidearm, firing two consecutive rounds into the explosion-proof laminated glass of the window. Amid the deafening muzzle blasts, only two spiderweb fractures blossomed across the dense glass, remaining utterly unyielding against the storm.

Pei Yi'an didn't glance at the window; his eyes settled upon the iron console with the precision of a surgical scalpel.

Directly beneath the wildly swinging metronome, a miniature emergency bypass cylinder forged of pure red copper was revealed. The rim of the keyhole was likewise engraved with an ouroboros, its mouth slightly parted, framing the deep vertical aperture in its center.

"An auxiliary bypass." Pei Yi'an swiftly produced the brass key recovered from Lin Huaiyuan's stomach, yet when he slid the key into the slot, it jammed rigidly at two-thirds depth. "Incorrect... the bitting depth matches, but an elastic counter-thrust damper resists from within."

"Little Pei! The water is already washing across the cabin deck!" Old Zhou watched as turbid, icy river water cascaded through the door seams like miniature waterfalls, submerging their ankles and rising toward the suspended cello strings.

Removing his spectacles, Pei Yi'an drew his solid-silver dissecting forceps from his breast pocket and gently guided the tips along the inner cylinder wall.

The cold beam of the flashlight pooled into the keyhole. Lining the interior cylinder was no ordinary pin-tumbler assembly; instead, four miniature acoustic tuning forks stood aligned with razor precision. Delicate as cicada wings, their tines hummed with a faint sympathetic vibration in response to the frantic metronome.

And on the brass bushing behind the tuning forks, an ultra-fine steel stylus had incised a clear German aphorism:

**"Only pure octaval resonance can soothe the shudder of an aggrieved ghost."**

"This is no mechanical lock; this is an acoustic resonance lock!" Pei Yi'an spun around, his piercing gaze spearing straight toward the kneeling Lu Siyuan. "This cylinder requires an exact acoustic frequency to disarm! Lu Siyuan, take out your cello!"

"Wh... what? Play my cello?!" Lu Siyuan shook violently like a sifter.

"The tuning forks are calibrated to octave harmonics of international pitch standard 440 Hz." Pei Yi'an ripped open the latches of the hardcase, thrusting the antique, dark-gold concert cello straight into Lu Siyuan's arms. "Use your bow. Sound the cello flageolet overtone on the C-string, holding it precisely at 65.4 Hertz! Tolerance cannot exceed 0.5 Hertz!"

"My... my hands are shaking... I can't play in tune..." Looking down at the freezing river water lapping past his shins, Lu Siyuan's teeth rattled uncontrollably.

"Lu Siyuan, listen to me!" Striding forward through the surging water, Old Zhou grabbed the young musician by the lapels, his bloodshot eyes blazing, his voice crashing like thunder: "Aren't you the concertmaster of the Binjiang Philharmonic?! Thirty years ago, your father and Lu Chongshan took another man's life for this godforsaken instrument! If you can't make this cello sing tonight, you not only fail the bow in your hands, you fail Lin Qingyuan who died in this shipyard thirty years ago! Now play!"

Old Zhou's roar—steeped in raw street grit and the fierce, unyielding courage of a veteran detective—struck like a sledgehammer into the depths of Lu Siyuan's soul.

The young cellist's pupils contracted violently as tears streamed down his cheeks. As the frigid river water lapped past his knees, those hands that had been trembling uncontrollably suddenly stilled into absolute, miraculous stillness the instant his fingers wrapped around the bow and fingerboard.

It was twelve thousand hours of relentless discipline etched into his bone marrow and muscle memory.

Taking a deep breath of air dense with rust and river silt, Lu Siyuan lowered his eyelids, his right arm sweeping through the air with resolute, tragic grace.

*Wooo—*

A deep, cavernous, weeping flageolet harmonic on the C-string—sounding like the mournful cry of a wounded leviathan—detonated inside the claustrophobic sheet-metal cabin!

The tone was pure beyond measure, vibrating through bone and soul with bottomless sorrow. The acoustic waves expanded through the damp air, striking with mathematical precision against the micro-tuning forks inside the copper keyhole!

*Hum—hum—hum—*

The four tuning forks shuddered violently in sympathetic resonance, triggering the internal hydraulic dampener to bleed off its pressure in an instant!

"Now!" Pei Yi'an's eyes flashed with electric intensity. Wrenching the brass key with his full grip, he turned it clockwise a full one hundred and eighty degrees!

*Clack—BOOM!*

The steel deadbolt jammed inside the doorframe disengaged, and the massive iron door blew outward under the colossal hydraulic pressure differential between inside and out!

"Hold on to me!" Old Zhou roared, scooping up Lu Siyuan and the cello case with one arm. Gracing the doorframe with one hand, Pei Yi'an rode the surging current with them as the buoyant force expelled all three through the threshold in the nick of time, clawing their way along the dock's retaining railings until they collapsed onto the unsubmerged high concrete platform!

When the three lay sprawled, soaked and gasping across the freezing concrete edge of the dock, Dry Dock Number Two beneath them had been thoroughly swallowed by the raging waters of the Binjiang, transformed into an abyss of ink-black fury.

At daybreak, the November river mist began tearing apart under the pale fish-belly grey horizon.

From the direction of the old municipal wharves, sirens wailed as they approached, their dazzling crimson and azure flashers cutting through the desolate wreckage of the shipyard.

Half-kneeling in the mud, Pei Yi'an caught his breath. He slowly uncurled his right palm—during the final instant when the lock popped open, his fingertips had deftly slipped out a miniature solid-silver capsule concealed deep within the cylinder core.

Twisting open the silver capsule, a rolled strip of ancient parchment sealed in red sealing wax—barely two fingers wide—tumbled out into his palm.

Dragging waterlogged trouser cuffs through the mire, Old Zhou shuffled over, still clutching his tin thermos stamped with the red double-happiness character, panting as he wiped muddy spray from his face: "Little Pei... what did that old fox... leave in the keyhole?"

Peeling away the wax seal with his torn latex glove, Pei Yi'an unfurled the parchment strip.

The faint, cold light of dawn glinted across the parchment, revealing handwritten lines executed in elegant Gothic French alongside neat rows of Chinese brushwork:

**"Volume One · The Overture Concludes. The brass key has unsealed thirty years of buried sin. Yet, all honors woven of falsehood shall inevitably be dismembered by the bone-deep truth. Dr. Pei Yi'an, when the second movement of the rainy night symphony sounds at the Binjiang Museum of Modern Art, the bronze ribs shall salute you. —Respectfully, The Ferryman"**

Staring fixedly at the parchment, Old Zhou exhaled a long plume of white vapor tinged with the scent of river mud, a cold, sardonic grin curling at the edges of the veteran detective's lips: "Damn it... Volume Two. That grandson really intends to put us through a meat grinder."

Expressionless, Pei Yi'an rose to his feet, facing the biting morning breeze coming off the river, carefully folding the parchment into a fresh evidence pouch.

Fishing a rain-softened tin box from his pocket, he slipped an unblemished peppermint lozenge into his mouth, his gaze piercing through the morning fog toward the skyscrapers across the river:

"Old Zhou, notify Su Tang to prepare the laboratory. We have finished reading the first corpse's letter."

Pei Yi'an nudged his raindrops-spattered glasses frame, his thin lips setting into a resolute line:

"Now, it's our turn to visit the art museum, and hear what the bronze bones have to say to us."
