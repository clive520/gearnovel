# Chapter 8: Occupational Scars Under the Lens

In the Subterranean Pathology Center on Basement Level 1 of the Municipal Police Bureau, sub-zero air conditioning locked the ambient room temperature at a precise sixteen degrees Celsius.

The three-and-a-half-meter colossal bronze statue, *The Flayed Prometheus*, had been hoisted by heavy-duty rigging pulleys and stabilized onto a custom reinforced autopsy platform. The concealed trapezoidal access hatch along the sculpture's back was fully unlatched; under the glaring brilliance of the shadowless surgical lamps, the human skeleton—painstakingly assembled with solid-silver wire—had finally been detached from its metallic matrix, laid out quietly upon a long stainless-steel dissecting table draped in absorbent white linen.

"Little Pei, do you think this killer started out as a tailor?" Wearing double-thick surgical gloves while still clutching his eye-searing pink smart thermos, Old Zhou leaned over the steel table, clicking his tongue in marvel. "Look at this silver wire—threaded all the way from the first cervical vertebra down to the coccyx, straighter than the hemp soles my old lady stitches."

"Master, ambient humidity is elevated. We recommend keeping your joints warm and hydrating on schedule!" The thermos's digital LCD screen blinked, chiming once more in a saccharine, perky electronic female voice.

Pei Yi'an was holding a pair of precision micro-dissecting forceps; his fingers paused for an imperceptible fraction of a second. Turning his head with complete impassivity, the gaze behind his spectacles was cold enough to match two unsheathed boning knives.

"Deputy Captain Zhou, if your electronic beverage container produces that substandard synthesized female timbre between 2,400 and 3,000 Hertz once more within the next five minutes, I will immediately employ an orthopedic oscillating bone saw to mechanically separate its motherboard from its lithium-ion battery permanently."

Ducking his neck, Old Zhou hastily jammed his calloused index finger over the mute sensor on the cap, chuckling dryly in sheepish surrender: "Easy now, easy, she's muzzled for good. Down to business: have you pinned the cause of death? Not a single hairline fracture on any of these bones—the guy couldn't have died of pure vanity, could he?"

"External bone fractures are crude signatures left only by pedestrian, bottom-tier criminals." Turning away, Pei Yi'an focused his scrutiny back onto the junction connecting the ivory-white cranium and cervical spine. "Su Tang, initialize the 3D digital depth-of-field microscope. Set polarized light to forty percent."

"Copy that! Main display connected!" Su Tang typed swiftly across her keyboard, gradually descending the microscope's optical sensor toward the posterior margin of the victim's foramen magnum.

Across the wall-mounted eighty-five-inch ultra-high-definition monitor, the cortical bone surface magnified instantaneously by two hundred times.

There, at the articulation between the atlas and occipital condyles—the fatal threshold guarding the medulla oblongata and high cervical spinal cord—a microscopic circular puncture hole measuring a mere 0.6 millimeters in diameter lay starkly exposed! The surrounding cortical bone was surgically smooth, without even microscopic fracture lines radiating outward.

"Do you see it?" Pei Yi'an lightly tapped the monitor screen with the tip of his forceps. "0.6 millimeters in diameter, approximately eight to ten centimeters in length—an ultra-fine, ultra-rigid triangular medical trocar needle. The killer thrust it squarely through the posterior midline of the neck at an infallible anatomical angle while the victim was completely off-guard, cleanly severing the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus nerve and the cardiorespiratory center."

Su Tang sucked in an icy breath: "No cries, no struggle... respiration and cardiac rhythm terminated in less than a second..."

"Precise, merciless, and elegant, exactly like pulling the mainspring from a pocket watch." Setting his forceps aside, Pei Yi'an stepped toward the victim's shoulder girdle and left clavicle. "Now, observe his occupational imprint."

The high-intensity spotlight cut obliquely across the victim's left clavicle and inferior mandibular margin.

"Along the midshaft of the left clavicle lies a spindle-shaped zone of osteosclerosis 3.2 centimeters long by 1.5 centimeters wide, with trabecular density thirty-five percent higher than the right; beneath the left mandibular angle sits a circular bone depression 1.2 centimeters across." Pei Yi'an measured the telemetry, rattling off data at breakneck speed: "This is localized pressure necrosis and compensatory bone hypertrophy induced by years of anchoring an instrument against a chin rest. Furthermore, the palmar aspects of the distal phalanges of the left second through fifth digits display delicate transverse linear furrows—permanent impressions worn by high-tension metallic musical strings."

"A violinist after all!" Old Zhou slapped his thigh hard. "Male, aged twenty-five to twenty-eight, approximately 175 centimeters tall, professional violinist, missing within the last five to seven days! Little Su, run those parameters through the bureau's missing persons database!"

Su Tang's fingers danced across the keyboard like lightning, cascading streams of data flooding across the screen.

In less than five seconds, a missing persons bulletin tagged with a crimson flashing alert popped onto the terminal!

"Found him!" Su Tang shouted in triumph. "Ye Chi! Twenty-seven years old, Associate Concertmaster of the Second Violins for the Binjiang Philharmonic! Five nights ago, after finishing a private rehearsal at the Municipal Cultural Center, he drove home alone. His vehicle was subsequently found abandoned beneath the riverside elevated expressway, his phone signal terminating completely!"

Old Zhou leaned in close to the screen, studying the dossier photo of the handsome youth with loose wavy hair holding his violin with fiery ambition, his brow knitting tightly: "The Binjiang Philharmonic... the Binjiang Philharmonic again! Cello principal Lu Siyuan, second violin associate concertmaster Ye Chi... is this Ferryman intending to dismantle the entire orchestra down to spare parts?!"

"This is no coincidence." Pei Yi'an's voice remained crystalline as he stepped back toward the fourth true rib bearing the micro-engraving. "Su Tang, confocal laser scan across the cortical surface of the fourth rib."

The azure laser beam swept meticulously across the bone, and the engraved staff on the main monitor magnified once more.

This time, directly beneath the musical staff bearing the four notes "D - E - A - D", an inscription etched in nanometer-scale laser calligraphy surfaced in German:

**Chaconne in D Minor — Johann Sebastian Bach.**

And inscribed at the very terminus of the composition's title was a performance timestamp calibrated down to the exact minute:

**"Curtain: Tonight at 8:00 p.m. sharp. Stage: Concert Hall One, Binjiang Grand Theatre."**

"Eight o'clock tonight?!" Old Zhou whipped his head toward the wall clock; the hands already pointed to 1:40 in the afternoon. "We have less than six and a half hours until eight o'clock!"

"This Chaconne is universally recognized as the most demanding solo violin composition ever penned, and history's most celebrated elegy." Removing his surgical mask, Pei Yi'an smoothed the hem of his white lab coat with his slender fingertips. "Legend holds that Bach composed this piece upon returning from travel to discover his wife had died suddenly. It is a monumental movement seeking resurrection from the abyss of mortality."

Right at that second, the double-leaf explosion-proof doors of the pathology suite were violently kicked open!

A duty detective dashed inside, breathless and drenched in sweat, completely neglecting his protective shoe covers as he waved an urgent fax dispatch, his face bleached white:

"Captain Zhou! Consultant Pei! An emergency at the Binjiang Grand Theatre!"

Old Zhou's gut lurched violently: "What happened at the theatre?! Speak!"

"Ten minutes ago, Wen Xueqing, the principal first violinist of the Binjiang Philharmonic, received an anonymous same-city courier delivery in her private dressing room!" The detective reported frantically. "Upon opening the package... inside was a violin bow strung entirely out of articulated human finger phalanges! The note enclosed warned—if the Bach Chaconne does not sound at the Grand Theatre at eight o'clock tonight as scheduled, scarlet blossoms of fire will detonate simultaneously beneath all 1,200 auditorium seats!"
