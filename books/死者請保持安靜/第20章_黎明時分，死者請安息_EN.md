# Chapter 20: At Dawn, May the Dead Rest in Peace

One month later, a crisp morning in late autumn.

The rolling Yangtze River cleaved through the heart of Binjiang, its morning mist shimmering with gentle ripples of gold and rose beneath the nascent sun.

Outside the grand bronze doors of Courtroom No. 1 at the Binjiang Intermediate People's Court, hundreds of journalists and citizens held their collective breath, watching the live sentencing verdict broadcast across outdoor monitors.

In the dock, clad in prison garb and hollow-cheeked, Song Huaide and Chu Tiancheng hung their heads as the sharp crack of the judicial gavel resounded like a thunderclap.

Spanning three decades of grand corruption, intentional homicide, and forged national pharmaceutical authorizations, the gavel fell on the conspiracy. Song Huaide was sentenced to capital punishment, Chu Tiancheng to life imprisonment, and not a single one of the twenty-three corrupt medical bureaucrats escaped the net of justice.

In the witness box, having delivered his final earthly testimony, Gu Zhiming rose slowly supported by two bailiffs. Gazing upward at the solemn national emblem, a serene smile graced the old pathologist's lips for the first time in thirty years. He passed away peacefully one week later, leaving his entire estate and patent royalties to establish the Binjiang Forensic Pathology Youth Research Endowment.

Qin Mo, recognized for substantial mitigating cooperation and long-term psychological duress, was spared capital punishment under statute and committed to a specialized forensic medical facility for inpatient rehabilitation.

At this moment, amidst the evergreen pines atop Binjiang's Nanshan Cemetery, a mild breeze brushed the white stone stairs.

Before a newly erected cenotaph of white marble lay armfuls of fragrant white chrysanthemums.

Engraved side by side upon the headstone were two names: **Lin Qingyuan** and **Chen Weiguo**.

Resting in the center of the granite plinth was an official dossier just validated by Bureau seals—**[Final Forensic Post-Mortem Certificate: The Homicide of July 17, 1994, at Dry Dock No. 2]**.

Under the finding of cause of death was inscribed in sharp, elegant ink four decisive words: **[Blunt-Force Traumatic Homicide]**.

And in the examiner's signature box stood three clean, incisive characters: **Pei Yi'an**.

Dressed in her navy police service uniform, Su Tang bowed three times before her father's monument, tears glistening at her lashes while a warm, radiant smile curled upon her lips.

"Dad, Uncle Chen," Su Tang whispered, her fingertips caressing the cool marble. "Your names have returned to the light—pure and unblemished."

Standing straight in his ceremonial tunic, a gleaming Second-Class Merit Star pinned to his lapel, Old Zhou snapped an immaculate, rigid salute toward the monument.

Uttering no words, Pei Yi'an placed a sprig of white lisianthus gently upon the dais, stepping back half a pace and offering a solemn bow.

The departed had been vindicated; clarity returned to the world of the living.

...

Late that night, at the familiar roadside food stall along Binjiang Road in the old quarter.

The clay pot atop the charcoal brazier bubbled merrily with steaming broth, filling the chilly night air with the rich aroma of braised tripe, aged ginger, and Shaoxing rice wine.

Having shed his stifling dress uniform for his faded, beloved casual jacket, Old Zhou slurped a hearty mouthful of hot vermicelli and ox offal with profound contentment.

At his elbow, the battered pink thermos stood proudly in the middle of the folding table.

Only now, affixed to the lid was a miniature silver-plated citation plaque custom-crafted by the Bureau's Logistics Department: **[Bureau First-Class Meritorious Thermos · Calibrated Mass: 450 Grams]**.

"Ah, pure bliss!" Old Zhou wiped his glistening lips, patting his stomach as he reclined in the plastic chair, pointing smugly at the flask. "Little Pei, look at that—Chief Lu actually did right by me for once! Even the Commissioner admitted that without the counterweight of my half-cup of Da Hong Pao, you'd be bunking as my neighbor up on the martyr's hill right now!"

Seated opposite him in an immaculate black trench coat, Pei Yi'an wielded a small pair of pocket silver forceps, systematically and surgically plucking every solitary coriander leaf from his soup bowl.

"Evaluating from mechanical and thermodynamic principles, your survival was 99.9% attributable to the reverse pressure-relief actuators of the bunker ventilation shaft," Pei Yi'an corrected dispassionately without lifting his eyes. "Your thermos merely served as an arbitrary 0.45-kilogram mass. Had we placed a lump of coal of identical heft upon the pan, the outcome would have been identical."

"You little brat, would it kill you to let me have my moment for once?" Old Zhou rolled his eyes, unscrewing his cap to take a satisfied gulp of warm tea. "I turn fifty-five next month. Chief Lu asked if I wanted to transition to the Police Academy as an honorary instructor. I figure it's time to take up fishing and tend some orchids, so I don't get dragged into the morgue at 3:00 AM to sniff formalin every night."

Pei Yi'an paused his forceps, his eyes behind gold rims lifting quietly to regard Old Zhou's weathered, yet thoroughly rejuvenated countenance.

"Pursuant to the Department's latest veteran retention directives, senior investigators commanding master technical credentials and thirty years of crime scene experience receive expedited approval for delayed retirement," Pei Yi'an pushed a plate of piping-hot pan-fried buns across the table. "Captain Zhou, I took the liberty of filing your workman's compensation report. You are granted three days of sick leave. Monday at 8:00 AM sharp—see you in the lab."

Old Zhou froze, looking down at the crispy golden dumplings, a grin tugging at the corners of his mouth despite his grumbling retort:

"Cheeky little imp... Are you dissecting my retirement plans now too?"

Just then, the radio on Old Zhou's duty belt—freshly fitted with new batteries—chirped with an urgent tactical alert:

"Dispatch to Captain Zhou Chenglin! Urgent dispatch to Pathologist Pei Yi'an!"

"River Patrol just salvaged an iron steamer trunk bound in heavy mooring chains off Pier No. 4! The casing bears extensive, antique wax seals!"

"On-scene officers request immediate technical forensic and Major Crimes backup!"

The night breeze of the riverside stall ruffled their coats.

Old Zhou and Pei Yi'an locked eyes across the table.

Exhaling a helpless, fond sigh, the veteran detective cranked the lid of his pink thermos tight, snapped his service pistol back into his holster, and flashed his signature, battle-hardened grin:

"Well, Little Pei, seems the dead in Binjiang aren't in the mood to let us get a full night's sleep after all."

Rising in measured grace, Pei Yi'an buttoned his trench coat, his slender fingers gripping the handle of the silver field kit gleaming under the starlight.

His gaze remained as cool as winter frost, yet in the shadows of the night, his lips curved into an imperceptible, confident smile:

"The dead are far more polite than the living."

"Let us move, Captain Zhou—and help them keep their peace."
