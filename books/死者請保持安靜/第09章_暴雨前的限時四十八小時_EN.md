# Chapter 9: 48 Hours Before the Downpour

At 3:15 in the afternoon, the torrential downpour drenched the Binjiang Grand Theatre's colossal silver-shell dome until steam drifted across the sky like mist.

Thirty police cruisers and heavy armored bomb disposal units with flashing crimson and azure lights cordoned off the theatre's four main perimeter checkpoints until not a drop of water could leak through. Explosive Ordnance Disposal officers in full blast-protection suits navigated the corridors clutching non-linear junction detectors, the atmosphere saturated with the acrid reek of ozone and storm dampness.

Inside the backstage second-floor dressing room of the concertmaster, the tension was frozen solid like brittle ice on the verge of fracturing.

"The Bureau Chief has issued an absolute directive: until explosive deflagration hazards are completely eliminated, under no circumstances will mass panic be tolerated." Executive Deputy Chief Lu Tianming stood braced in the doorway, hands on his hips, his face dark as iron as he stared at the trembling young woman seated before the vanity mirror. "Ms. Wen Xueqing, are you certain this parcel bypassed the backstage mailroom and manifested directly upon your vanity?"

The woman interrogated was Wen Xueqing, First Violin Concertmaster of the Binjiang Philharmonic Orchestra. Dressed in an opulent black evening gown, her exquisite stage makeup was completely washed away by tears, her hands clutching a sodden handkerchief as if even the physical strength to speak had been bled dry.

Directly before her on the velvet-draped vanity table lay that hair-raising, grotesque "violin bow."

The stick was forged from no rare Brazilian pernambuco, but assembled from fourteen polished, deathly-pale human proximal and middle phalanges, bound flush together by 0.1-millimeter solid-silver surgical wire. The knuckles were reinforced with clear epoxy resin, the frog anchored by a miniature brass slide sculpted into an ouroboros, and the white, premium Mongolian horsehair gleamed with an icy, tomb-like luster beneath the dressing room lights.

"Zhou Chenglin, Pei Yi'an, get in here and inspect this." Lu Tianming stepped aside, his brow tied into a knotted scowl. "Bomb squad ran it through portable X-ray; there are no explosive detonators inside the bow, but this contraption... whose bones was it cobbled together from?"

With one hand buried in his trench coat pocket and the other dangling his forcefully muted pink thermos, Old Zhou ambled casually into the dressing room. He cast a sidelong glance at the bone bow on the vanity, chewing half a tea leaf, his eyes as cold and calculating as ice.

Gloved in a fresh pair of sterile rubber gloves, Pei Yi'an stepped slowly forward. Rather than handling the bow immediately, he retrieved a miniature medical vernier caliper from his breast pocket and stooped down to measure the first phalanx.

"First phalanx length 4.1 centimeters, midshaft transverse diameter 1.2 centimeters, bone cortex density conspicuously elevated." Pei Yi'an's voice lacked even a tremor of emotion, as if he were cataloging mundane laboratory specimens. "The proximal articular margin exhibits microscopic lip-shaped osteophytes—pathognomonic wear from repetitive high-position left-hand string-stopping on a violin. The residual hydrogen peroxide scent along the osseous surface matches the skeleton inside the modern art museum's bronze statue with absolute fidelity."

"Which means..." Spitting out the dregs of the tea leaf, Old Zhou's expression darkened completely.

"These are the severed fingers of Ye Chi's left hand." Pei Yi'an straightened, stripping off his gloves and discarding them into a sterile evidence pouch. "After executing Ye Chi in Dry Dock Number Two, the killer not only subjected his axial skeleton to dermestid beetle defleshing, but deliberately amputated the left digits he took the greatest pride in, fashioned them into this violin bow, and presented it to the concertmaster seated right here."

Hearing the name "Ye Chi," Wen Xueqing recoiled as if struck by lightning, letting out a jagged, agonized sob that nearly caused her to collapse from her chair.

"Concertmaster Wen, you appear remarkably unsurprised that Ye Chi has been murdered?" Pei Yi'an turned, his gaze behind his lenses cutting through flesh and bone to scrutinize her autonomic responses. "When Deputy Chief Lu questioned the package's provenance moments ago, your pupils dilated by twenty percent, yet your eyes subconsciously darted toward the concealed door on the left of this vanity. That was where Ye Chi's personal instrument case originally rested, correct?"

"I... I have no idea what you're talking about!" Wen Xueqing clamped her hands over her ears, shrieking in hysteria, "Ye Chi and I were merely orchestra colleagues! His disappearance has absolutely nothing to do with me!"

"Colleagues?" Striding forward, Old Zhou braced both hands on the vanity table, his mouth curling into the dry, mocking grin of a seasoned detective. "Ms. Wen, the bureau's Financial Crimes Division audited your offshore accounts thirty minutes ago. On the twentieth of last month, the 'Contemporary Youth Arts Foundation' under Hanhai Group wired two million US dollars to your offshore trust as an anonymous sponsorship. And that seventeenth-century Amati antique violin in your hands valued at fifteen million—its guarantor was none other than Lu Chongshan himself."

The dressing room plunged into tomb-like silence, save for the furious roar of the storm scouring the glass outside.

Wen Xueqing's face drained from paper-white to ash-grey, her stubborn psychological defense completely detonated before the paper trail of colossal wealth.

"Ye Chi... Ye Chi discovered where that money originated..." Lowering her head in shattered despair, her tears spattered against her costly silk gown. "That foundation never nurtured young artists; it was an offshore laundering reservoir established thirty years ago when Lu Chongshan usurped the Municipal Hospital's pharmaceutical patents! Ye Chi uncovered Hanhai's original trial records; he intended to blow the whistle... he swore every string on my violin was saturated with the blood of the 1994 victims!"

"So you betrayed him?" Lu Tianming ground his teeth in rage.

"I didn't! I merely begged him to let it go!" Wen Xueqing screamed in defense, her pitch warping in terror. "I warned him how terrifying the Lu family's reach was, I told him we could never fight Hanhai! But he wouldn't listen... five nights ago after rehearsal, he climbed into a vehicle driven by Lu's bodyguards, and he never came back..."

At that very instant, the Bomb Squad Commander burst up the stairwell from the concert hall below, drenched in sweat, throwing the dressing room doors wide:

"Chief Lu! Captain Zhou! The situation beneath the auditorium has been confirmed!"

Lu Tianming whipped around: "Did you locate a timed explosive?!"

"Ten times worse than a timed explosive!" The commander wiped freezing sweat from his brow, his chest heaving violently. "The perpetrator modified the central plenum ventilation ducting beneath all 1,200 audience seats! The entire network is pressurized with cryogenic vaporized military-grade liquid explosive, wired to twelve banks of precision acoustic-resonance relays!"

"Acoustic relays?" Pei Yi'an's eyes narrowed.

"Yes! The receivers are frequency-locked exclusively to the melody of Bach's *Chaconne in D Minor*!" The commander's voice trembled. "The telemetry links directly into the theatre's master acoustic mixing console. Tonight at eight o'clock sharp, if the Bach Chaconne does not begin on the second, or if the pitch drifts by more than half a semitone, or if the performance halts for more than three seconds... the entire subterranean plenum will be detonated by high-voltage electrical arcs, triggering a catastrophic continuous fuel-air explosion!"

Old Zhou's pupils constricted violently, his grip on the pink thermos trembling in his palm: "That lunatic is taking 1,200 spectators as hostages for an elegy... and forcing Wen Xueqing to play this funeral hymn upon the stage using the dead man's finger bones!"

Pei Yi'an glanced at the pale bone bow upon the vanity, and then at Wen Xueqing, who had collapsed into a catatonic heap on the carpet.

"Wen Xueqing's forearm flexor tendons are already exhibiting functional tremors; currently, she cannot even sustain an open-string stroke." Pei Yi'an's tone was terrifying in its lucidity. Turning his head, he met the leaden gazes of Lu Tianming and Old Zhou:

"Old Zhou, instruct EOD to seal the intake dampers of the central plenum immediately, and pump dry ice into the ducts to suppress ambient temperature and retard vaporization."

Stepping forward on long legs toward the corridor, Pei Yi'an unbuttoned the top collar of his trench coat:

"It is currently 3:30 p.m. Before the curtain rises at eight o'clock tonight, we must locate a second living soul in Binjiang capable of performing the Bach Chaconne—or unmask the Ferryman holding the detonator in the dark."
