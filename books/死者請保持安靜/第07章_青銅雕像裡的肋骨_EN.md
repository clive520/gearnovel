# Chapter 7: Ribs Inside the Bronze Statue

Shrouded in sheets of rain, the Binjiang Museum of Modern Art resembled a colossal ghost flagship assembled of pure white marble and geometric glass, anchored silently in the center of the riverside plaza of the Yunbin Hi-Tech Zone.

At 10:00 a.m. sharp, the central rotunda of the "Grand Exhibition of Contemporary Deconstructive Somatic Art"—which should have been teeming with dignitaries and socialites—was cordoned off behind dense rows of glaring black-and-yellow police tape. Dozens of riot police armed with live ammunition held all invited art critics, collectors, and press correspondents at bay a hundred meters away, as a bizarre aroma mingling high-end perfume and pungent chemical disinfectant hung heavily in the air.

"Out of the way, make way! Bureau Chief special clearance on scene—don't block the corridor for our chief forensic pathologist!"

Old Zhou's boisterous, gravelly bellow shattered the oppressive silence inside the exhibition rotunda. Striding briskly through the revolving glass doors, he gripped in his right hand a bright pink smart voice-enabled thermos fitted with a glowing digital display—a "compassionate workplace wellness gift" Su Tang had purchased out-of-pocket this morning, unable to stomach the sight of his red double-happiness tin can any longer.

"Master, current water temperature is fifty-five degrees Celsius. Warmth maintained for two hours. Please remember to hydrate in a timely manner!" The thermos abruptly chimed in a perky, saccharine electronic female voice.

Old Zhou's weathered face twitched violently as he twisted the lid shut with lethal force, coughing dryly to mask his mortification before turning to Pei Yi'an at his flank: "Little Pei, can we disable the voice on this damned contraption? I've collared hundreds of murderers in my lifetime, and today a pink cup nearly gave me a nervous breakdown."

Pei Yi'an ignored Old Zhou's self-deprecating banter entirely. Clad in an immaculately spotless long black trench coat and wearing fresh sky-blue sterile nitrile gloves, his clinical gaze vaulted across the cordoned marble floor, fixing directly onto the three-and-a-half-meter-tall colossal bronze sculpture erected at the dead center of the rotunda.

The piece was titled *The Flayed Prometheus*, a three-year magnum opus by Yan Keji, Binjiang's premier avant-garde sculptor who had trained in Europe, slated for its global unveiling as the centerpiece of this morning's grand opening.

It was a monumental bronze male torso exuding raw power and cruel aesthetics, the metal across the thorax deliberately stretched and torn into openwork ribbons resembling peeled muscular striations. Yet merely twenty minutes earlier, while museum conservators were dusting the fixtures, the intense heat of the theatrical spotlights had coaxed a dark, viscous droplet from deep within the bronze ribcage, releasing a suffocating stench of formalin and bone glue.

When the conservators aimed a high-intensity inspection torch into the hollow cavity, the sight that met their eyes elicited an instantaneous chorus of bloodcurdling screams from everyone present—

Beneath the labyrinthine, masterful striations of cast bronze lay an authentic, intact, and starkly white human skeleton!

"Where is Master Yan?" Old Zhou shed his jovial mask in an instant, his gaze sweeping the perimeter like a predatory falcon.

"In the adjacent VIP lounge, suffering from arrhythmia and having just swallowed two nitro-glycerin pills." Su Tang hurried over carrying a forensic spectroscopy case, her face pale as she caught her breath. "Yan Keji insists the piece was cast in his suburban studio, but the clay modeling and bronze core casting were outsourced to an industrial foundry. He claims he had zero idea human bones had been sealed inside!"

"A clay mold cannot accommodate the thermal expansion coefficient of human bone in liquid bronze." Stepping onto the plinth in specialized shoe covers, Pei Yi'an's voice resonated with crystalline clarity. "The melting point of bronze is 1,083 degrees Celsius. At that temperature, hydroxyapatite crystals within human osseous tissue disintegrate, while organic collagen carbonizes in milliseconds. This skeleton was meticulously inserted into the bronze shell piece by piece using forensic anatomical techniques through an access hatch concealed on the sculpture's back, long after casting and cooling were complete."

Pei Yi'an switched on his high-intensity inspection lamp, beaming light through the slits between the bronze ribs into the inner sanctum.

Within the depths of the bronze Prometheus's chest, twelve pairs of human ribs were arranged with flawless precision. Every sternocostal articulation was sutured with 0.2-millimeter solid-silver surgical wire, while the costal cartilage had been replaced with translucent medical-grade silicone fixative, crystal-clear, like an ivory stalactite forest suspended in the belly of an iron furnace.

"Deceased is male, chronologically between twenty-five and twenty-eight years of age." Drawing a pair of slender silver forceps, Pei Yi'an reached through the bronze aperture, delicately scraping a microscopic trace of residue from the surface of the manubrium. "The bone is pristine with zero flesh, devoid of soft-tissue decomposition odors. This is no natural skeletonization; it was achieved via laboratory-grade dermestid beetle dermestophagy, augmented by low-temperature three-percent hydrogen peroxide bleaching."

"You mean... the killer is a professional dissector?" Old Zhou's tone hardened.

"Not merely a dissector, but an obsessive fetishist harboring a pathological reverence for the human skeletal architecture." Pei Yi'an's forceps came to rest over the midpoint of the victim's left fourth true rib. "Old Zhou, switch your torch to macro-polarized focus and observe here."

Leaning in through the polarizing filter of the tactical torch, Old Zhou sucked in a harsh, jagged breath.

Along the pale exterior shaft of the fourth rib, micro-laser engraving had inscribed a miniature musical staff with microscopic precision!

The staff opened with a bass clef, followed immediately by four deliberate notes: **D - E - A - D**.

And at the far right of the staff was etched a microscopic clockwork gear emblem, accompanied by an incised dedication:

**"Op. 02: Dedicated to the Second Violinist of the Bone Symphony."**

"DEAD... the deceased." Old Zhou murmured, his jaw clenching rock-hard. "Movement Two is here... The Ferryman wasn't joking. Inside a bronze statue at the modern art museum, he left us an entire human skeleton converted into sheet music!"

Just then, rapid, weighty footsteps echoed from the rotunda's entrance.

A broad-shouldered, stern-faced middle-aged officer strode in flanked by a cohort of plainclothes detectives. Wearing an impeccably tailored Senior Commissioner uniform, the insignia on his epaulets glinted cold and hard beneath the overhead lights—Lu Tianming, the newly appointed Executive Deputy Bureau Chief of Binjiang.

"Zhou Chenglin! Pei Yi'an!" Lu Tianming's expression was as dark as a thunderhead, his commanding voice booming beneath the museum dome before he had even arrived: "The Municipal Publicity Department and the Provincial Ministry have blown my phone lines off the hook! There are one hundred and twenty domestic and international journalists in this building! Forty-eight hours... Provincial headquarters gave us exactly forty-eight hours to identify the remains and close this case!"

Old Zhou arched an eyebrow, leisurely taking a sip of warm water from his pink thermos: "Deputy Chief Lu, save the fireworks. Our Little Pei has already deduced the victim's osteological age and postmortem preservation methods at a single glance. With this forty-eight-hour window, I wager we have time to treat the culprit to two rounds of bureau pork-and-cabbage dumplings."

Glaring daggers at Old Zhou, Lu Tianming drew a deep breath and turned his gaze toward Pei Yi'an, who remained transfixed by the bronze skeleton: "Consultant Pei, who was the deceased in life? Do we have any identifying physical markers?"

Retracting his forceps, Pei Yi'an peeled off his gloves, his clear eyes calmly meeting Lu Tianming's heavy, scrutinizing gaze.

"The xiphoid process bears a distinct inward depression, and the cortex at the lateral third of both clavicles exhibits marked bilateral thickening accompanied by chronic stress-induced osteophytes." Slipping a fresh peppermint lozenge into his cheek, Pei Yi'an announced with absolute detachment:

"This was no ordinary citizen. The deceased spent years clamping a hardwood instrument firmly between his left clavicle and mandible, repeating bowing and finger-pressure biomechanics for over eight hours a day."

Turning slightly, Pei Yi'an pointed toward the fourth rib bearing the musical staff within the hollow bronze chest:

"The deceased was a professional concert violinist. And his disappearance occurred no more than seven days ago."
