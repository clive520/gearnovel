# Chapter 15: Cryogenic Vault and Confined Respiration

The rubber seal of the gas mask pressed hard against facial nerves, echoing with heavy, disciplined respiration.

Through the polycarbonate lenses of the respirators, the mortuary vault was bathed in an oppressive crimson glow cast by emergency floor lamps. High-concentration sevoflurane and inert refrigerant aerosols hovered like a chilling veil, swirling slowly before settling across the room.

"Little Pei... these filter canisters... how long can they last?" Old Zhou leaned his back against the sealed hermetic lead door, his right hand gripping his service pistol, his left instinctively shielding the pink thermos tucked against his chest. His voice muffled through the one-way exhalation valve, sounding as if stifled behind thick wadding.

"Under an aerosol environment of this industrial concentration, standard activated carbon canisters provide at most twelve minutes of effective filtration," Pei Yi'an glanced at the luminous dial of his mechanical wristwatch, his voice smooth and devoid of emotional flutter. "After twelve minutes, the carbon beds will saturate and rupture; within two minutes thereafter, we will succumb to profound surgical anesthesia and hypoxic shock."

"Damn it all! I've cracked homicides my whole life—if I end up suffocating in my own department's morgue, the neighboring precincts will laugh at my ghost for thirty years!" Old Zhou gritted his teeth, glaring at the melted keypad panel by the portal. "Our radios went completely dead; the subterranean band is jammed solid. Even if Chief Lu upstairs suspects foul play, rallying the breaching team to cut through thirty centimeters of armored lead will take at least half an hour!"

"The Ferryman will not grant us half an hour," Pei Yi'an pivoted, his gaze settling anew on the skeletal remains resting upon the tray of the thirteenth freezer.

At that precise instant, the grille of the vintage micro-cassette recorder cradled across the headless skeleton's chest crackled with a burst of static.

Immediately thereafter, an electronically modulated voice—its pitch synthesized to erase age and gender—resonated with chilling serenity through the dead vault:

"Good evening, Pathologist Pei Yi'an, and Detective Zhou Chenglin."

The timbre was unnervingly measured, carrying the polite cadence of an academic discourse:

"Thirty years ago, Dr. Lin Qingyuan sought to seal the greatest pharmaceutical conspiracy in Binjiang within this underground bomb shelter. Yet he placed too much faith in institutions, too much faith in the morality of the living, and ultimately paid with his cranium shattered under the weight of metal and power."

A muscle near Old Zhou's eyelid twitched violently, his knuckles whitening around his sidearm.

"Tonight, you two demonstrated sharp intellect with scalpels and resonant frequencies. But justice is no cheap applause; it demands transaction through the logic of the departed." The voice paused, tinged with a razor-thin chuckle. "The hermetic seals of this chamber utilize Cold War mechanical pneumatic linkages. Any forced breaching will merely trip the auxiliary nitrogen flush, purging all remaining oxygen within ten seconds. The sole bypass cipher is etched upon the skeleton before you."

"You have ten minutes remaining to prove yourselves worthy of inheriting Lin Qingyuan's dissecting blade."

Click.

The recorder's capstans abruptly locked into stillness, plunging the crimson-lit room back into suffocating silence.

"Etched onto the bones?!" Old Zhou bolted forward, brandishing his tactical torch to inspect the ribcage on the tray. "Little Pei, look! Which bone has the inscription?!"

"Do not touch it with bare hands," Pei Yi'an barked coldly, sweeping an arm outward to bar Old Zhou's advance. "All two hundred and six bones in this frame are suspended by micro-metric wires calibrated to precise counterweights, connected directly to micro-switches beneath the tray. Any non-anatomical traction will be treated as tampering."

Pei Yi'an drew a measured breath, forcing his lungs into steady cadences to husband the dwindling oxygen inside his canister.

He unlatched his silver field kit, withdrawing a high-precision titanium orthopedic probe and a multi-wavelength forensic light source.

Cold violet-blue beams washed across the pristine skeleton. Under the 450-nanometer excitation wavelength, subtle micro-striations invisible to the naked eye fluoresced with an eerie, emerald radiance.

"This skeleton... is not Lin Qingyuan," Pei Yi'an bent down, his visor hovering barely five centimeters from the fourth thoracic vertebra as his probe tapped the distal radio-ulnar junction.

"Not Dr. Lin?!" Old Zhou froze. "Then who is this?"

"Examine the trabecular remodeling on the distal radius," Pei Yi'an tapped the osseous surface, eliciting a faint, distinct metallic chime. "An internal fixation screw—1.5-centimeter medical-grade titanium, utilizing Swiss first-generation non-self-tapping threads. Furthermore, the left clavicle shows malunion of an old fracture with prominent osteophyte proliferation along the subclavian groove—a textbook occupational deformity resulting from carrying heavy geodetic theodolites on a single shoulder."

A thunderclap detonated in Old Zhou's memory: "Thirty years ago... the young sapper who handled subterranean mapping alongside Dr. Lin... Chen Mo's biological father?!"

"Chen Weiguo, chief surveyor of Binjiang's municipal underground conduits in 1994," Pei Yi'an said flatly, tracing the UV beam down to the junction of the seventh thoracic vertebra and the sternum. "Three days prior to Lin Qingyuan's murder, Chen Weiguo supposedly perished in a 'gas explosion' during a tunnel inspection. Official records stated his remains were reduced to ash. In reality... his skeleton was preserved as a permanent biological coordinate within this shelter."

"The Ferryman kept him hidden here for thirty entire years..." Old Zhou's eyes reddened, his lips quivering behind the visor. "What exactly does he want us to find?!"

"The bypass mechanism is not on the door; it is in the drainage sump," Pei Yi'an traced the silver filament dangling beneath the seventh rib downward, terminating at the stainless discharge funnel under the rolling tray.

Set squarely in the center of that cast-steel drainage funnel was a mechanical balance pan!

Engraved along the beveled brass rim was an etched German inscription: **【 Ausgleichsgewicht : 450 Gramm 】**.

"Four hundred and fifty grams..." Old Zhou squinted at the counterpan. "What does that mean? Are we supposed to scavenge a 450-gram balance weight in a morgue?!"

"Four hundred and fifty grams represents the physiological standard weight of an adult male heart infused with saline," Pei Yi'an's eyes glinted with razor-sharp insight. "Thirty years ago, the architect designed the escape conduit of this vault as an 'offering of the heart.' The moment the pan registers downward force calibrated to the exact gram, the pneumatic valve vents in reverse, unlatching the deadbolts."

"But in this godforsaken place, all we have are bones and formalin! Where the devil do we find something weighing exactly 450 grams?!" Old Zhou stomped in frantic desperation, the pressure needle on his canister gauge twitching toward the red zone.

Pei Yi'an turned his head in measured stillness, his spectacles fixing upon Old Zhou's chest.

More specifically, upon the pink thermos clasped protectively in Old Zhou's arms.

Tracking his gaze downward, Old Zhou shrank back half a step as if stung: "Little Pei! You... you aren't eyeing my thermos, are you?! I've carried this cup for eight years! It's brewing top-grade Da Hong Pao tea from the bureau canteen!"

"Dual-layer vacuum stainless construction—tare weight 210 grams," Pei Yi'an rattled off figures with computational speed. "Prior to departing at 8:40, you filled it halfway from the canteen dispenser; judging by the thermal fill line, the volume sits at roughly 240 milliliters, or 240 grams. Summed together, total mass is precisely 450 grams, with a margin of error under 1.5 grams."

"Are... are you pulling my leg?" Old Zhou's eyes widened behind his visor.

"Captain Zhou, we have two minutes left," Pei Yi'an extended an open palm, his tone leaving zero room for debate. "Hand over the thermos, or in five minutes our literal hearts will be excised to serve as the 450-gram balance."

Clenching his jaw as his facial muscles twitched, Old Zhou relinquished his beloved pink flask with immense heartache: "Gentle! Don't you dare scratch the paint!"

Pei Yi'an took the mug, his movements as steady as a cardiovascular surgeon executing a coronary bypass.

Holding his breath, he cupped the flask with both hands, hovering its base one millimeter above the center of the balance pan.

Three. Two. One.

Fingers released.

Clack.

The pink thermos settled dead-center onto the stainless steel plate!

The pointer depressed by precisely three millimeters; an instant later, lubricated gear trains hummed beneath the chassis!

SHHHHHH—!!

A ferocious exhaust surge roared through the drainage bypass, vacuuming the swirling mist and anesthetic aerosol down into the deep earth!

Next came a resounding thud of disengaging cams from the massive lead door!

CLANG!

Driven by pneumatic pistons, the hundred-kilogram locking pins snapped free, allowing crisp, cool corridor air to rush into the chamber!

Old Zhou tore off his mask, gulping down greedy lungs of fresh air as he slumped against the doorframe. Wiping cold sweat from his brow, his very first impulse was to lunge back and scoop his thermos into his embrace, sighing with profound relief after verifying it was unblemished.

"Damn it... this mug of mine deserves a First-Class Citation of Merit today!" Old Zhou wheezed, breaking into a haggard grin.

Pei Yi'an, however, showed no amusement.

Standing beside the seventh thoracic vertebra, his silver forceps deftly extracted a rice-grain-sized microchip sheathed in sealed silicone from between the ribs.

From the tape player's speaker, past the spliced leader tape, drifted the Ferryman's final whisper:

"Excellently done, Doctor Pei. You are far more worthy of hearing the dead whisper than the quacks who falsified autopsies thirty years ago."

"Take the coordinates cached in Chen Weiguo's ribs and proceed to the rooftop of the Archives Building. The true arsonist who lit the blaze thirty years ago awaits your indictment there."

Retracting his tweezers, Pei Yi'an slipped the microchip into a sterile cryovial.

He turned, his gaze behind his gold frames as sharp as early winter ice as he stared down the subterranean corridor toward the archives tower:

"Old Zhou, drink your warm tea. Our final autopsy table awaits on the roof."
