# Chapter 2: Old Zhou's Thermos & Midnight Food Stalls

The November night rain arrived on schedule, like countless fine silver needles, soaking the mottled red-brick walls of Binjiang's old concession to a chilling core.

At 9:15 p.m., the municipal bureau's aged Santana cruiser—its paint peeling and chassis groaning in protest with every rotation of its tires—crept sluggishly over the flooded cobblestones before sputtering to a halt in front of the greasy, yellowed plastic awning of "Sanwan Food Stalls."

"Let me reiterate, Deputy Captain Zhou." In the passenger seat, Pei Yi'an made no immediate move to disembark. He had buttoned the collar of his trench coat up to the chin, his brow furrowed into an icy ridge. "While a humid environment of eight degrees Celsius does trigger a relative increase in gastric acid secretion, that hardly justifies replenishing caloric deficits with high-fat, high-purine animal viscera of dubious provenance."

"Cut it out, Little Pei. Stop talking all day like you're writing an autopsy report." Old Zhou pushed open the squeaking car door, one hand swinging his omnipresent jumbo thermos, the other waving vigorously in the air. "We've been cutting bodies all day; my stomach is rumbling loudly enough to echo. This 'Old Chen's Mutton House' has been here thirty years—that master broth is older than you!"

"In food microbiology, a broth 'older than myself' typically signifies an extreme concentration of heat-resistant endospore bacilli and nitrites." Despite his merciless critique, Pei Yi'an stepped out on his long legs, pacing off a stride of precisely eighty centimeters to clear a puddle swirling with iridescent gutter grease.

Steam billowed beneath the awning, dense with the savory richness of boiling mutton, the sharp pungency of crushed garlic, and the boisterous clamor of patrons drinking and playing finger-guessing games around a dozen folding tables.

With practiced ease, Old Zhou claimed an empty table by the wall. Giving a plastic stool a cursory swipe with a grease-stained rag, he slumped down and hollered, "Old Chen! Two bowls of mutton offal noodles, heavy on the scallions, light on the chili, a plate of stir-fried kidneys, and half a pound of sliced cold mutton!"

Pei Yi'an remained standing beside the table, paralyzed in place for seven solid seconds.

Pulling a pack of medical-grade alcohol wipes from his pocket with the clinical precision of a surgeon performing debridement, he scrubbed the soy-stained plastic stool three full times. Only after judging the surface bacterial colony count had reached the absolute boundary of his psychological endurance did he gingerly rest half of his buttocks on the seat.

"Little Su is back at the bureau running mass-spec on that bone wax; we probably won't have results till midnight." Old Zhou unscrewed his thermos, took a swig of piping-hot tea, and let his street-smart swagger settle into quiet gravity. "That ouroboros brass key—what do you make of it?"

Pei Yi'an tipped a peppermint lozenge from his tin box. Instead of chewing, he tucked it beneath his tongue, letting the piercing chill dispel the stale frying grease of the night market.

"Brass alloy, approximately sixty-five percent copper with trace amounts of tin and lead—hallmarks of 1930s metallurgical casting." Pei Yi'an tapped the tabletop lightly with his slender fingertips. "The bitting is stepped across seven distinct levels, a proprietary design of early 'Maurice Stein' mechanical tumbler locks. The tolerance between each tooth is less than 0.05 millimeters; no modern street locksmith could replicate it."

"Maurice Stein?" Old Zhou narrowed his bloodshot eyes. "The German-Jewish mechanic who ran a horology shop in the old concession during the thirties, and later supposedly designed the underground vaults for the Nationalist Central Bank?"

"Precisely. The 'M.S. 1934' stamped on the shank is his maker's mark and production year." Pei Yi'an analyzed coolly. "On the reverse, the Latin acronym 'V.I.T.R.I.O.L.' is the famous cipher of medieval alchemists: *Visita Interiora Terrae Rectificando Invenies Occultum Lapidem*—visit the interior of the earth, and by purifying you will find the hidden stone. In layman's terms: this key opens a Pandora's box buried deep underground."

Just then, the proprietor, wiping his brow with a greasy apron, bustled over with two steaming bowls of mutton offal noodles and a sizzling, glossy plate of stir-fried kidneys.

"Enjoy, officers! Kidneys fresh off the wok—tender as can be!" The owner chuckled, wiped his sweat, and turned back to his kitchen.

Old Zhou snapped apart a pair of disposable bamboo chopsticks, snatched a thick piece of kidney, shoved it into his mouth, and exhaled in bliss. "Pure earthly heaven! Little Pei, pick up your sticks. Staring at it won't fill your belly!"

Pei Yi'an gazed impassively at the deep-crimson kidneys on the platter, his eyes as detached as if examining histological slides.

"When fresh renal tissue is heated to one hundred degrees Celsius, the proteins coagulate rapidly; however, if stir-fried for under eighty seconds, un-denatured hemoglobin remains in the central medulla." Pei Yi'an adjusted his glasses and declared calmly, "More crucially, this porcine donor evidently suffered from chronic pyelonephritis prior to slaughter, exhibiting scattered white fibrotic nodules across the cortex. Were this on my dissecting table, I would recommend a resection for biopsy."

At the adjacent table, several middle-aged men with beer glasses and mouthfuls of kidney froze instantly. They could neither chew nor swallow, casting terrified glances at Pei Yi'an before quietly setting their chopsticks down.

"Lord have mercy!" A bite of mutton nearly lodged in Old Zhou's windpipe as he coughed, hissing in exasperation, "You're running an autopsy on a pig at a midnight snack stall! Two more sentences out of you, and the health inspectors will shut Old Chen down by sunrise!"

"I am merely stating objective pathological facts." Expressionless, Pei Yi'an drew his chopsticks and began methodically arranging every scallion ring along the bowl's perimeter at equidistant intervals. "Returning to the case. Has the database matched the victim's identity?"

Wiping his mouth, all levity vanished from Old Zhou's face. He retrieved a crumpled printout from his jacket's inner breast pocket and slid it across the table.

"Matched. Lin Huaiyuan, thirty-two, senior risk actuary returning from Wall Street. Appointed Chief Financial Risk Officer at Hanhai Investment Group in the New District six months ago." Old Zhou lowered his voice. "Record as spotless as a blank sheet: no priors, no known feuds. His only living kin is an elderly mother suffering from dementia in a nursing home."

"A modern financial executive who spent his days in five-star office towers drinking pour-over coffee," Pei Yi'an scrutinized the photograph—the man wore the identical bespoke suit found on the floater, his gaze sharp and ambitious, "why would he wash ashore at the old docks at 3 a.m., with an eighty-year-old vault key shoved down his esophagus?"

"And Hanhai Group filed a missing person report just this morning, claiming Lin took off yesterday afternoon with the audit papers for a tens-of-billions private equity fund before vanishing off the grid." Old Zhou slammed his thermos against the table with a dull thud. "Even stranger: Hanhai Group's principal shareholder happens to be the Lu family—the very lineage that inherited all properties from the old concession's Mingsheng Trading Company."

Pei Yi'an's eyes narrowed, a slice of cold mutton suspended in midair between his chopsticks.

"Maurice Stein's 1930s underground vault is situated directly beneath the current Hanhai Group headquarters—in the subterranean level of the old trading house's historic clocktower." Pei Yi'an spoke with rapid fire, mental gears interlocking at breakneck speed. "Lin Huaiyuan was not dumped at random. The killer is staging a demonstration against Hanhai Group, or rather, against the heirs of that bygone era."

"The old clocktower..." Old Zhou murmured, his gaze drifting toward the pitch-black curtains of rain beyond the awning.

The downpour intensified, drumming against the dilapidated plastic canopy like an urgent military march.

At that moment, Pei Yi'an's phone on the edge of the table vibrated violently, Su Tang's name flashing across the screen.

Pei Yi'an answered and tapped speakerphone.

"Teacher Pei! Captain Zhou!" Su Tang's voice came through breathless and trembling, punctuated by the shrill warning beeps of a gas chromatograph in the background. "The bone wax analysis is in! Beyond high-purity paraffin and beeswax, we detected a lethal trace of a rare plant alkaloid—*aconitine ester*!"

"Cause of death was poisoning via gastric mucosa absorption?" Old Zhou's jaw tightened.

"No! What's far worse is..." Su Tang took a sharp, jagged breath, her voice tight with terror, "inside the hollow barrel of that brass key, I found an electronic squib coiled in micro-tungsten wire! It's a miniature barometric remote sensor!"

"The killer planted a transmitter inside the key?" Pei Yi'an's pupils contracted.

"Yes! And the signal was activated by an external client just ten seconds ago! The transmission source... the ping places it less than three hundred meters from your position!"

Before her words could settle, the screech of tires skidding across wet asphalt and a brutal metal crunch echoed from the depths of the rain-drenched alleyway!

A pair of blinding high beams tore through the deluge, pinning their glare straight onto the fragile plastic awning of the food stall, casting the stark silhouettes of Old Zhou and Pei Yi'an against the greasy, soot-stained wall.

Old Zhou's grip on his thermos tightened until his knuckles turned white, every trace of street-wise slackness vanishing in a heartbeat, replaced by the razor-edged fury of a seasoned manhunter.

"Little Pei," Old Zhou drew his heavy service revolver from his hip, a grim, humorless smirk cutting across his mouth, "looks like they never intended to let us finish our mutton noodles in peace."
