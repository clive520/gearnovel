# Chapter 11: Clocktower Gears & The Missing Register

At 6:10 in the evening, the sky had been thoroughly swallowed by a downpour as thick and viscous as ink.

Barely one hundred and ten minutes remained before the curtain rose upon the mortal concert at the Binjiang Grand Theatre at eight sharp.

The aged Santana cruiser ploughed through floodwaters submerging the cobblestones past the knees in the historic quarter, screeching to an emergency halt at the terminus of "Clocktower Alley" in the deepest heart of the old concession. Beneath the furious scour of the tempest, a Gothic red-brick clocktower erected in 1922 towered imposingly amidst the ruins of colonial mansions, its four colossal Roman numeral dials—each three meters across—gleaming beneath flashes of lightning like four giant, unblinking white eyes peering cold and indifferent down upon the world.

"This godforsaken swamp—back when I was a beat cop thirty years ago, they swore it was slated for demolition. Thirty years later, this dilapidated clocktower has outlived my entire youth." Old Zhou shoved open the door, carefully shielding his pink smart thermos with one arm while hoisting a tactical searchlight high with the other. "Little Su, are you certain that miniature ouroboros brass slide on the bone bow traced back to this alley?"

In the rear seat, Su Tang balanced a ruggedized military laptop upon her knees, her fingers typing at frantic speed: "A hundred percent certain! I ran cross-checks against every five-axis CNC laser micro-engraving workshop in the municipality. In the whole of Binjiang, only this 'Stein Antique Horology Restoration Studio' commands the precision to execute micro-metric cold-laser incisions upon 0.1-millimeter phalanges!"

Sloshing through slurry, the three crossed the mottled cast-iron gates and hauled open the massive oak portal at the base of the clocktower.

*Cling-clang—*

The brass bell affixed to the lintel chimed with crisp, lingering resonance. The instant the heavy leaf swung shut behind them, the deafening tempest raging outside was miraculous cut off in an eyeblink.

Unfolding before their eyes was an awe-inspiring "labyrinth of time."

Across a cavernous three-story open atrium, hundreds of antique clocks and mechanical timepieces gathered from around the globe hung from the masonry and lined the display cases—Renaissance pendulum regulators, eighteenth-century gilded enamel chimes, monstrous industrial mercury escapements, and tens of thousands of raw brass gear trains interlocking in furious, frantic precision within the gloom.

*Tick-tock... tick-tock... clack-clack... clack-clack...*

Hundreds of mechanical impacts differing in frequency and cadence intertwined throughout the hall, humming like an entire synthetic jungle inhaling and exhaling in disciplined synchronization.

"Walking into this tomb, why do I feel like my own ticker is getting dragged out of rhythm by these damned clocks?" Old Zhou rubbed his chest, suppressing an involuntary shiver. "Little Pei, does this count as cardiac arrhythmia?"

"Objectively speaking, this constitutes acoustic frequency-following response—a common passive neurological adaptation to high-density, repetitive external sonic stimuli." Folding his black umbrella, Pei Yi'an paced through the shadows of countless whirling gears. "Time is fundamentally merely the physical measurement of thermodynamic entropy increasing; yet the perpetrator attempts through precision machinery to reverse entropy across a corpse—an act of supreme, pathological arrogance."

"Young man, you speak with profound insight."

A gravelly, ancient voice—resembling the rasp of oxidized gear teeth—drifted without warning from behind the workbench deep in the hall.

Beneath the dim yellow pool of a kerosene lamp, a hunchbacked elder in a canvas apron and binocular loupes slowly straightened his spine. His left eye was entirely blind, the socket sunken, while his withered right hand clutched an ultra-fine watchmaker's tweezers, resembling an animated skeleton draped in rough cloth.

"Municipal Criminal Investigation Detachment, Captain Zhou Chenglin. Old gentleman, are you the sole occupant of this workshop?" Flashing his police credentials, Old Zhou's eyes turned razor-sharp in an instant.

"Master Stein perished in the camps sixty years ago; within this tower, there have only ever been myself and these hands that never speak." Removing his loupes, the elder scrutinized the three with his clouded right eye, his gaze arresting rigidly upon the solid-silver dissecting forceps protruding from Pei Yi'an's trench coat breast pocket. "You carry the scent of formalin... and the dust of human bone. You are one who cuts away the mortal skin."

Stepping forward, Pei Yi'an retrieved from his evidence pouch the polarized macro-photographs of the "D-E-A-D" musical staff from the bronze statue's fourth rib, along with extreme close-ups of the ouroboros brass slide.

"A 1934 Maurice Stein dual-axis hairspring micro-regulation gear train, combined with 0.02-millimeter cold-laser micro-engraving." Pei Yi'an fixed his gaze upon the old watchmaker with glacial intensity. "Across this entire metropolis, only this room possesses the capability to forge such mechanisms. I want to inspect all apprentice rosters and client ledgers belonging to this studio from thirty years ago."

The elder's withered fingertips traced the ouroboros totem upon the glossy paper, his frail frame shuddering with sudden violence.

"This was no commission... this was that boy who fled thirty years ago..." A rattling wheeze like a rusted bellows scraped from the elder's throat, cloudy tears welling in the corners of his eyes. "He returned... he truly came back carrying scalpels and clockwork springs..."

"Which boy?! What was his name?!" Old Zhou clamped a hand onto the watchmaker's shoulder.

Trembling, the elder turned and hauled an iron-bound camphor chest from beneath the lowest tier of the workbench, lifting out a heavy, moth-eaten cowhide ledger. The parchment leaves were brittle and yellowed, the title page stamped with gilded lettering from 1994.

Old Zhou threw the volume open. Tucked between the brittle pages was an official hospital internship credential and an adoption agreement from thirty years ago!

Glaring out from the faded photograph was a fourteen-year-old youth with a melancholy gaze and refined, delicate features. And upon the legal guardian and apprentice signature lines were inscribed two names that struck Old Zhou's chest like leaden cannonballs:

**Sponsored Apprentice: Qin Mo.**

**Original Sponsoring Guardian: Chief of Pathology, Binjiang Municipal Hospital—Dr. Lin Qingyuan!**

"Qin Mo?!" Old Zhou gasped, shattered fragments of thirty-year-old memory colliding like a hurricane within his mind. "The medical prodigy and mechanical genius Dr. Lin Qingyuan took in right before he was murdered in Dry Dock Number Two?! When Dr. Lin was killed, that fourteen-year-old boy vanished into thin air... everyone assumed he had jumped into the river to follow his master!"

"He never died." Pei Yi'an's slender fingers glided across the margin of the ledger's final page, where a line of dark-blue fountain pen ink remained:

*"Bones for strings, blood for ink; thirty years hence, when the chimes toll again, judgment shall fall upon Binjiang."*

At that exact second, from within the colossal gear room atop the clocktower's third story, an ear-splitting, low-frequency electromagnetic whine tore through the rafters!

"Captain Zhou! Teacher Pei!" Su Tang—stationed beside her laptop near the entry—let out a terrified shriek. "A signal! The transmission antenna above our heads just emitted a high-frequency microwave pulse! The receiver... the receiver is locked onto the 1,200 chemical relay detonators under the seats at the Grand Theatre!"

Unholstering their weapons, Old Zhou and Pei Yi'an sprinted up the spiral cast-iron staircase, charging headlong into the clockwork machinery chamber at the summit of the tower!

As they kicked open the dust-encrusted iron bulkhead, the spectacle awaiting them arrested the battle-hardened detective dead in his tracks—

In the center of the four-meter-tall monumental bronze clock escapement, suspended from the cable originally carrying the counterweight, hung a digital countdown timer!

Across the timer's glowing LCD panel, crimson digits were bleeding away at millisecond velocity:

**"01 : 15 : 22" — Exactly one hour and fifteen minutes remained until eight o'clock sharp!**

Far more fatal, bolted onto the timer's casing was a massive mechanical pallet fork assembled from thirteen human radius and ulna bones, wedged immovably into the drive gears of the hundred-ton bronze municipal bell that tolled the top of every hour!

Looking up into the colossal skeletal clockwork mechanism, the crimson glare of the countdown mirrored cold and blinding in Pei Yi'an's glasses:

"This municipal bell was never engineered to tell time."

Pei Yi'an's voice was whisper-soft, yet it drove into the air like a poisoned needle:

"The moment eight o'clock strikes, the bell will toll, and the acoustic shockwave paired with the microwave pulse will detonate the Grand Theatre simultaneously. The Ferryman never planned to sit in the audience watching the show... he stands atop this thirty-year-old clocktower, preparing to toll the death knell for the entire city with his own hands!"
