# Chapter 5: The Unspoken Last Words

The torrential downpour hammered relentlessly against the dilapidated corrugated iron roof of the dry dock, producing a muffled roar like the thundering stampede of ten thousand steeds.

Outside the dispatch cabin door, the muddy rubber boots halted three paces short of the threshold.

Gripping his sidearm in both hands, Old Zhou pressed his back flush against the frigid iron doorframe, every sinew in his body coiled like a drawn recurve bow. Just as the brim of the massive black umbrella tilted slightly and a pale, gaunt hand reached out to push the door, Old Zhou lunged laterally, leveling the dark muzzle square between the stranger's eyebrows!

"Don't move! Municipal Criminal Investigation Detachment!" Old Zhou barked, his voice detonating like a thunderclap.

The black umbrella jerked violently backward, its ribs thrashing wildly in the tempest. Illuminated by the glaring white cone of the tactical flashlight, the figure caught in the crosshairs was no hardened, desperate thug, but a young man clad in a tailored black overcoat, his complexion translucent with terror.

He appeared roughly twenty-seven or twenty-eight years old, his arms clutching a heavy, shockproof aeronautical hardcase for a cello, his refined features contorted in the pelting rain, his lips bruised blue, shuddering like a solitary withered leaf on the verge of falling in late autumn's gale.

"Don't... don't shoot! I'm Lu Siyuan!" the youth cried hoarsely, a heart-wrenching sob cracking in his throat. "That person sent me here... he said if I didn't reach this dry dock before 4:00 a.m., I would never touch a cello again in my life!"

Pei Yi'an stepped slowly out from the suspended dispatch cabin, his long-handled black umbrella held rock-steady before him, his clinical gaze skimming past the cello case in Lu Siyuan's arms before fixing upon the youth's violently trembling hands.

"Severe spasticity in the flexor carpi ulnaris bilaterally, accompanied by classic neurogenic tremors in the distal digits." Pei Yi'an stated impassively, not sparing so much as a glance at the leveled firearm. "Mr. Lu, for an internationally renowned young concert cellist, exposing your forearms to a damp, frigid environment of six degrees Celsius for an extended period constitutes an act of professional suicide scarcely different from ingesting potassium cyanide."

Lu Siyuan stared blankly at this forensic pathologist who possessed the cold demeanor of an ice sculpture, as if the primal dread of death had momentarily deserted him in sheer bafflement: "You... how do you know..."

"Get inside, fold your umbrella, and whatever you do, don't trip the steel cello strings on the deck." Holstering his revolver, Old Zhou swept a watchful gaze across the desolate, pitch-black shipyard before yanking Lu Siyuan across the threshold into the suspended dispatch cabin.

When the beam of the flashlight illuminated the *Vitruvian Man* carved into the iron wall with a weeping cello, alongside the scrap of sheet music bearing his name directly beneath it, Lu Siyuan's knees liquefied, collapsing heavily to the deck as his cello case struck the floor with a dull, hollow boom.

"He really found me... he came back... the retribution from thirty years ago has finally fallen upon our house..." Lu Siyuan buried his face against the freezing metal lip of the surgical chair, tears mingling freely with icy rainwater and streaming down his cheeks.

"What in the hell happened thirty years ago?" Old Zhou seized Lu Siyuan by the collar, his voice laced with suffocating, smoldering fury. "Who is this 'Ferryman' pulling all the strings from the shadows?! Why did he tie that thirty-year-old tape to Lin Huaiyuan's corpse?!"

"I don't know his name... I swear I don't!" Lu Siyuan clutched his head in agony. "All I know is that my father didn't die of a sudden myocardial infarction thirty years ago—he committed suicide! On the night before he took his life, he smashed every stereo and music score in our house in a manic fit, repeating the exact same words over and over: *'We treated living people like lab rats, and we treated the dead like voiceless garbage; sooner or later, this river will wash all the blood and sin back to our doorstep!'*"

Pei Yi'an did not interrupt Lu Siyuan's broken lamentation. Slipping on fresh sterile gloves, he retrieved a portable precision audio restoration deck from his forensic kit—equipment dispatched via emergency courier from the municipal bureau's evidence lab.

Beside the mechanical clockwork metronome on the console that still swung with tireless rhythm, Pei Yi'an gingerly mounted the 1930s Telefunken reel-to-reel magnetic tape—recovered from the Hanhai Group subterranean vault—onto the drive spindles.

"A layer of calcium carbonate crystals and ferric oxide dust seven micrometers thick has settled across the tape surface, yet the polyester base remains structurally uncompromised." Pei Yi'an adjusted the calibrated dials with micro-metric precision, tuning the scanning frequency of the laser playback head. "The dead cannot produce sound with their vocal cords, but magnetic powder faithfully preserves the final vibrations of the air from thirty years ago."

*Tick... tick... tick...*

Amid the metronome's sterile cadence, the restoration deck's signal indicator shifted slowly from a cautionary amber-red to a serene emerald green.

A blast of electrical static hissed through the sealed sheet-metal chamber like a sandstorm. Then, a cold, long-buried human voice—preserved from the subterranean depths thirty years prior—pierced through the foggy corridors of time, echoing with haunting clarity:

*"Chongshan, have you lost your mind?! The clinical neurotoxicity of this blocking agent hasn't cleared baseline metabolism! You tripled the dosage unauthorized, and Subject 07's auditory cortex has suffered irreversible neurological damage! This is murder!"*

It was the voice of a young, articulate man, vibrating with indignation and insurmountable despair.

Immediately following, another deep, chilled, yet unmistakably familiar adult voice rolled through the speaker—the voice of Lu Chongshan, now chairman of Hanhai Group, recorded thirty years in his youth:

*"Dr. Lin Qingyuan, don't forget whose capital financed your laboratory. The privatization restructuring of Mingsheng Trading House takes place next month, and these novel drug patents are our sole collateral for foreign credit lines! As for that child... he was born deaf anyway. Contributing to medical science is the only worth his existence will ever offer!"*

*"You monster! I will expose you to the Methodist College of Medicine! I will drag every single trial protocol and audit draft into the broad light of day!"*

*"Expose me? Once you walk out of Dry Dock Number Two in this shipyard, do you truly believe anyone alive will ever hear your voice again?"*

The audio dissolved into violent physical scuffling, the clang of metallic instruments crashing across concrete, and the muffled, choked gasps of a human body absorbing blunt trauma.

Finally, there came the monstrous, cavernous impact of dead weight cleaving the river's surface—*Splash!*

The recording terminated abruptly, leaving only the dry hiss of blank magnetic tape.

Absolute, subterranean chill settled over the dispatch cabin.

Lu Siyuan slumped limp against the floor, his face drained of all color; Old Zhou's fists were clenched so violently that his knuckles gleamed a terrifying, bloodless white.

"Lin Qingyuan..." Old Zhou ground the words past his teeth. "The Chief of Pathology at the old Concession Municipal Hospital who vanished without a trace thirty years ago... Lin Huaiyuan's biological father!"

"This closes the entire causal loop." Pei Yi'an powered down the unit and removed the spool, the razor-sharp gaze behind his lenses piercing straight to the philosophical core. "Thirty years ago, Lu Chongshan murdered Dr. Lin Qingyuan in Dry Dock Number Two, staged it as a suicidal leap into the river, and built his Hanhai empire upon stolen pharmaceuticals and usurped real estate. Thirty years later, Lin's son Lin Huaiyuan unearthed the truth—yet on the very eve of exposing the conspiracy, he was murdered and dumped into the identical river by the exact same methodology."

"Wait a second..." Old Zhou's veteran instincts caught a fatal dissonance. "If Lin Huaiyuan was the avenger, then who is this 'Ferryman' hunting in the shadows? If he's executing vengeance on behalf of a ghost from thirty years ago, why would he slaughter Dr. Lin's only son as well?!"

"Because Lin Huaiyuan compromised."

An aged, impeccably cultured voice floated softly from a silent handheld transceiver resting in the corner of the cabin.

The transceiver's red LED flashed furiously, the speaker carrying the wail of the tempest outside along with the deep, sonorous plucking of cello pizzicato—a melody that sounded like the grim hymn of the reaper himself:

"Lin Huaiyuan leveraged the audit records to extort thirty million in hush money from Lu Chongshan. He betrayed the noble soul of his father, reducing justice to a commodity for barter. Dr. Pei, as I once stated, the dead are far more polite than the living—for the dead never kneel before their father's murderer for soiled scraps of paper currency."

Snatching the transceiver, Old Zhou roared into the grille: "Who are you?! Where the hell are you hiding?!"

A soft, chilling chuckle slipped from the speaker, sounding like the scrape of a scalpel along polished glass:

"Deputy Captain Zhou, why so impatient? Look beneath your feet—the timer for the Third Requiem commenced the exact second Mr. Lu Siyuan stepped across your threshold."
