# Adventure Gear: The Files of the Impossible

## Chapter 1: Midnight Phantom Concert in the Music Room

At midnight, the century-old campus of Luyang Elementary School sank into dead silence, with only the draft whistling through the creaky window frames.

Suddenly, an eerie, faint blue luminescence flickered to life inside the third-floor music room, which should have been pitch black.

Immediately following the glow, an elegant yet spine-chilling piano melody cut through the night air—it was Mozart's Serenade.

Security guard Old Zhang, patrolling the hallway, rubbed his eyes in terror, nearly dropping his flashlight to the ground. Gathering his courage, he crept toward the glass window of the music room and froze instantly.

The piano bench was completely empty, yet the black and white keys of the fifty-year-old grand piano were depressing and rebounding rhythmically, as if played by an invisible hand!

Even more terrifying, glowing blue specks of dust hovered above the piano lid, dancing lightly over the keystrokes like ethereal phantoms.

"Ghost... a ghost is playing the piano!" Old Zhang screamed in a broken voice and bolted down the hall at full speed.

By the next morning, rumors of the "Midnight Phantom Music Concert" had spread like wildfire throughout Luyang Elementary School.

"I heard that old piano is cursed! At midnight, a departed music teacher returns to practice!" Whispered several younger students in the corridor, their faces pale.

"Nonsense," remarked a lean boy calmly by the window of Class 6-2.

Ian Lu adjusted his thin metallic frames, a uniquely crafted brass monocle resting over his right eye. In his utility vest pockets were neatly arranged fountain pens, vernier calipers, and a thick leather notebook.

"Every seemingly supernatural phenomenon in this world, once you disassemble its motion-transmission chain, is merely the meshing of gears and the release of energy," Ian said evenly while sketching a floor plan of the music room.

"Wow! Director Ian, your poker face hasn't changed a bit in centuries!" A crisp, cheerful voice rang from the doorway.

Aoi Shen, a newly transferred student, entered carrying an art bag. Wearing a vibrant green headband with eyes shining with curiosity, she wore a pair of exquisite metal tuning forks hanging from her wrist.

"Still, Ian, Guard Zhang swore that the keys actually moved by themselves, and there truly was glowing phantom fire in the air!" Aoi teased with a wink.

"Seeing with one's own eyes does not guarantee truth. The human retina is easily deceived by optical disguises," Ian closed his notebook and stood up. "Let us go; physical evidence never lies."

Arriving at the third-floor music room, they ran directly into fifth-grade invention fanatic Sparky Fang squatting by the threshold, wielding an 8mm wrench against the keyhole.

"Sparky, what are you doing here?" Aoi asked with amusement.

"Hehe! Sister Aoi, Boss Ian!" Sparky lifted his round face smudged with two streaks of black grease and grinned. "I heard the old piano gained a soul, so I'm here with my latest 'Ultrasonic Magnetic Field Detector' to bust the ghost!"

"Do not disturb the scene," Ian cautioned as he walked into the silent classroom, where golden sunlight illuminated the dusty black grand piano.

Ian pulled down his brass monocle, adjusting its lateral micro-gear to switch to "Ultraviolet Multi-Spectral Filter Mode."

"Look here," Ian pointed to the ceiling ventilation duct beside the piano. "There are microscopic friction traces from nylon fibers along the grill, along with faint yellow liquid stains."

Aoi stepped forward, drew her 'Resonance Tuning Fork' from her pocket, and struck it gently against the iron harp frame of the piano.

"Hummm—" A clear, resonant chime vibrated through the quiet hall.

Aoi closed her eyes, sensing the subtle tremors transmitted back to her fingertips: "Strange... the middle-range hammers suffered uneven mechanical strikes, not the soft touch of human fingers, but stiff, cold mechanical levers flicking repetitively!"

"Sparky, deploy Spiky," Ian immediately ordered, gesturing toward the air duct.

"On it! Spiky, move out!" Sparky pulled out a fist-sized clockwork metal hedgehog from his backpack, wound its key, and dropped it into the ventilation tube.

The mechanical hedgehog 'Spiky' scampered deftly along the sheet-metal pipe, extending a miniature periscope from its spine that beamed real-time footage to Sparky's handheld monitor.

"Found it!" cried Sparky. "Inside the pipe bend, there's a modified clockwork timer connected to a revolving camshaft!"

"Whenever the spring-wound shaft rotates, the cams pull dozens of ultra-thin transparent fishing lines dropped down behind the hammer action, forcing the keys down," Ian jotted down mechanical equations in his notebook. "This is the physical truth behind the 'ghost pianist.'"

"Then... what about the floating luminescence seen from outside?" Aoi inquired.

Ian approached the vent, used tweezers to sample a drop of condensed liquid on the iron bar, and sniffed it: "An ultrasonic cold-mist humidifier, loaded with a diluted mixture of vitamin B2 and fluorescein solution. Under UV illumination, the microscopic mist droplets glow brightly in the air."

"Good grief! Who went through all this trouble to stage a phantom spectacle at school?" Sparky scratched his head in bewilderment.

At that precise moment, hurried footsteps sounded from the shadows of the back door—a teenage boy in a blue work apron was desperately attempting to slip away!

"Stop right there!" Ian barked sharply.

Sparky reacted with lightning reflexes, slapping a launcher button on his toolbox. A magnetic suction net whistled through the air, wrapping neatly around the runner's ankles and tripping him to the floor.

The trio approached to find Ah-De, an apprentice from the town's antique clock and instrument shop.

Blushing deeply, Ah-De hung his head in dejection: "Don't turn me in... I'm not a criminal..."

"Why install clockwork contraptions in the music room to pretend to be a ghost?" Ian asked coldly.

With red-rimmed eyes, Ah-De sniffled and pointed at the piano: "The school is renovating next week, and the principal decided to discard this old broken piano as scrap iron... But this was the very last instrument my late grandfather tuned! I wanted to create a ghost rumor to scare people away so no one would dare touch it..."

Hearing Ah-De's tearful explanation, Aoi sighed softly and offered him a clean handkerchief: "So it was out of love and remembrance."

"However, Ah-De, lies and panic can never truly protect memories," Ian looked at the piano wooden rim. "Sparky and I can help you repair the aged escapement mechanisms and tune the strings, so it can play true music once more at the school anniversary."

"Really?" Ah-De looked up in astonishment.

As dusk descended, the three youths dismantled the contraptions in the air pipe, and golden sunset bathed the restored piano keys.

The first campus mystery was solved, and the legend of the Clockwork Detective Agency had officially begun.
