# Adventure Gear: The Files of the Impossible

## Chapter 1: Midnight Phantom Concert in the Music Room

At twenty minutes to midnight amidst the biting autumn gale, the century-old campus of Luyang Elementary School sank entirely into a pitch-black, ink-like stillness.

Nestled between misty mountains and the churning bay, this historic academy was founded in the early twentieth century. Deep within the grounds loomed weathered red-brick halls, their Gothic pointed arch windows suffocated under tangled tendrils of creeping ivy that rustled like whispered conspiracies in the bleak night wind. Towering Formosan cypresses and ancient banyan trees blotted out the stars, slicing the walkways into bottomless ravines of shadow.

Night guard Old Zhang gripped a heavy six-cell steel flashlight, a burdensome ring of brass keys clattering against his hip. With every step, the metallic clatter echoed through the cavernous, hollow colonnades, ringing with an unsettling desolation.

"Rotten weather..." Zhang muttered, cinching his wool collar tight against his nape. The seaward draft bore a raw chill laden with brine and decomposing leaf litter that seeped beneath his threadbare jacket, inducing violent shivers.

Having completed his rounds through the first-floor administrative suites and the second-floor natural science laboratory, Zhang turned back toward the gatehouse to brew a piping-hot kettle of roasted oolong. Yet just as his boot touched the flagstones of the eastern courtyard, a faint, preternatural luminescence flickered to life in the shadowed recesses of the third floor above.

Zhang froze mid-stride, blinking against the sting of the wind. That radiance was not the warm halogen beam of an electric torch, nor the emerald glow of an exit luminaire, but a pale blue phantasmagoria radiating arctic chill, resembling phosphorescent fire dredged from an ocean trench!

That eerie glow emanated from the innermost recess of the third-floor west wing—the historic music conservatory, decommissioned and padlocked for three months.

Then, a crisp, crystalline, yet chillingly mechanical piano melody filtered through the cracked wooden louvres, cascading into the dead quiet of the midnight campus—it was Mozart's *Serenade in G Major (Eine kleine Nachtmusik)*!

The rendition was astonishingly brisk and flawless, its pitch immaculate and every staccato ornament measured with vernier precision. Yet it was precisely this breathless perfection, entirely devoid of human cadence or emotional warmth, that transformed the cheerful classical masterpiece into a macabre nocturnal visitation.

A cold chill surged down Zhang's spine, the flashlight beam quivering violently against the flagstones. He knew with absolute certainty that the old music room had been shuttered since summer due to ceiling leaks, its heavy oaken portal barred by an eight-centimeter hardened iron padlock!

"Who... who goes there? Sixth-grade pranksters? Come out at once!" Zhang bellowed, his voice echoing across the courtyard but trembling with hollow dread, magnifying the terror.

No answer returned. Only the piano continued its frenzied sprint through the dark, accelerating with mechanical ruthlessness as the notes hailed down like shards of ice.

Inhaling raggedly, Zhang raised his oak security baton and ascended the spiraling wooden stairs toward the third floor, step by squeaking step. Each tread groaned beneath his boots, and the stagnant air grew thick with mildew, mothball naphthalene, and the sour scent of ancient paper.

He reached the third-floor landing. Corridor sashes stood agape, the draft whipping cobwebs above the transom into a wild frenzy. The massive timber door remained shut fast, heavy brass chains looped through the hasp, with undisturbed dust choking the keyway.

With trembling palms, Zhang pinned the flashlight to his chest and pressed his temple against the small diamond-cut frosted pane set into the door's center.

Peering through the grime, the vista greeting his eyes froze the marrow in his bones.

In the room's epicenter, a shaft of cold blue luminescence descended upon a half-century-old black 'Apollo 1932' nine-foot concert grand piano, its lid propped open like the wing of a nocturnal swan.

The mahogany bench was totally deserted; not even a phantom silhouette occupied the seat!

Yet, the eighty-eight ivory and ebony keys were depressing and rebounding with violent, virtuoso rhythm—bass fundamentals plunging, trebles skipping, sharps undulating as if pounded by invisible phantom hands!

More terrifying still, in the empty air above the soundboard hovered a lazily swirling mist of pale blue luminescence, within which thousands of frozen, glimmering dust specks swirled like celestial fireflies, waltzing in sync with the tempo!

"Ghosts... it's a ghost! A ghost is playing the piano—!"

Unable to bear the terror a second longer, Old Zhang unleashed a curdled, piercing shriek. His flashlight clattered onto the hardwood, rolling down the dark hallway, as he stumbled down the spiral stairs, leaving one boot behind as he fled the brick building in utter hysteria.

By seven-thirty the following morning, as golden dawn burned away the coastal mist over the athletic grounds, rumors of the "Midnight Music Room Phantom Concert" had swept every classroom like an inferno.

In corridors, around drinking fountains, and beside morning sweep-up leaf piles, students gathered in hushed clusters, wide-eyed and ashen, breathlessly exchanging ever-wilder embellishments of the urban legend.

"Did you hear? Guard Zhang saw the old music teacher who passed away decades ago floating over the keys playing Mozart!" A fifth-grade girl with twin pigtails whispered, her eyes wide with terror.

"It wasn't a teacher! My older brother heard it's the spirit of a sailor from an antique shipwreck, bound to the piano's iron harp, emerging on full-moon nights to drag someone down to the deep!" Another boy chimed in with solemn dread.

"Silence! What is the meaning of this nonsense!"

A thunderous roar boomed from the hall junction. Disciplinary Director Zhou Yan marched into view brandishing his ever-present black infraction ledger, his visage stern as an iron magistrate as his hawk-like gaze pinned the hallway:

"Gathering to peddle superstitious drivel before morning homeroom! Whosoever utters another syllable regarding 'phantom pianos' will find their name inscribed in this ledger with two hours of restorative detention!"

The crowd scattered instantly, darting into their respective homerooms with lowered heads.

Yet, in the rearmost window seat of Class 6-2, a lean, upright youth remained entirely detached from the commotion.

Ian Lu sat bathed in morning light, thin titanium spectacles resting on his bridge, with a uniquely crafted brass monocle affixed over his right eye. He wore a hard-wearing navy canvas utility vest, its exterior loops meticulously housing three drafting fountain pens, a stainless vernier caliper, and an optical pocket magnifier.

At that moment, Ian's fountain pen moved across a leather-bound gridded notebook, sketching an architectural floor plan of the third-floor west wing, annotating coordinate axes, fenestration vectors, hinge moment arms, and prevailing draft trajectories.

"Every seemingly supernatural phenomenon that defies common sense is, at its root, merely the interlocking of mechanical transmission, optical refraction, and chemical kinetics," Ian remarked without looking up, his tone even and dispassionate. "Ghosts are simply the self-deceptions of those deficient in physical science, magnified through the lens of terror."

"Wow! Director Ian, your universe-weary poker face is as infuriatingly pompous as ever!"

A silvery, melodious laugh drifted from the doorway.

Aoi Shen, who had transferred to Luyang less than a month prior, strolled in with light steps. She wore an ivory hoodie, a clover-green silk band tied around her shoulder-length bob, her bright eyes dancing with vitality. Hanging from the strap of her canvas art tote was a pair of elegant sterling silver tuning forks, chiming faintly as she walked.

Setting her tote onto a desk, Aoi rested her chin on her hands, peering down at Ian's schematic: "Still, Director Ian, I just paid Guard Zhang a visit at the gatehouse! His blood pressure crested one-sixty, and he swore upon his honor that the keys moved autonomously amidst floating blue mist! If it were human pranksters, how could anyone infiltrate a padlocked room suspended ten meters in mid-air and vanish without a trace?"

Ian adjusted his brass monocle, a razor-sharp glint illuminating his dark pupil:

"Eyewitness testimony is often the greatest optical trap. The human retina possesses merely three types of cone cells, and the visual cortex is notoriously susceptible to misdirection via polarized illumination and stroboscopic persistence," Ian snapped his notebook shut. "As for padlocks and sheer walls... before the levers and pulleys of classical mechanics, a truly sealed chamber has never existed."

"Sounds like you've already caught the scent of turning gears?" Aoi's eyes danced with excitement as she rubbed her hands together. "What are we waiting for? Clockwork Detectives, move out!"

"Before deployment, we require a field technician capable of physical breaching within tight tolerances," Ian rose, smoothing his vest pockets.

"No need to search! The Grand Master has been standing by on-site!"

The back door was shoved open, revealing a round, mop-headed boy grinning impishly.

Fifth-grade mechanical prodigy Sparky Fang stood panting, his chubby cheeks streaked with black molybdenum disulfide grease. Slung across his small frame was a heavy, three-tier expanding brass mechanic's chest, forcing him to tilt slightly as he walked, wielding an oily 8mm ratchet wrench.

"Boss Ian! Sister Aoi!" Sparky scrambled forward, his round face glowing with manic glee. "I heard the Apollo grand grew a soul! I've already assembled my 'Infrasonic Geomagnetic Resonance Ghost-Catcher' and I'm ready to blast that phantom with high-voltage EMP!"

"Keep your dangerous toys holstered, Sparky," Ian placed a firm hand on the boy's shoulder. "The cardinal rule of forensics is non-contamination of physical evidence. Let us move before Director Zhou returns from his faculty assembly."

Slipping past the custodial staff, the trio climbed to the third-floor landing.

A yellow quarantine tape barred the entrance to the music room. Morning sunlight filtered through the lancet stained-glass window, dappling the floorboards yet failing to dispel the oppressive gloom of the aged corridor.

Ian halted two paces before the threshold and crouched. Drawing his brass monocle from his vest, his fingers adjusted the miniature lateral knurled thumbwheel.

With two sharp metallic clicks, the internal optical lenses shifted into "Grazing-Angle Polarized Grazing-Light Mode."

Ian leaned down until his cheek nearly brushed the floorboards, sweeping his flashlight at a flat fifteen-degree grazing angle:

"Examine the particulate sedimentation," Ian pointed toward the floor beneath the doorframe. "Last night's airborne dust settled uniformly to a depth of 0.05 millimeters. Within a 1.5-meter radius of the door, aside from Guard Zhang's frenzied rubber-soled escape tracks, there are no secondary footprints, no fabric wipes, and no drag scuffs."

"Meaning no human entered or exited through the main portal last night," Aoi nodded, extracting a charcoal pencil to plot Zhang's stride lengths onto her art pad.

Sparky crouched before the brass padlock, calipers measuring the shackle clearances: "Ian, this three-inch antique padlock remains securely latched with an eight-millimeter shackle engagement, devoid of hydraulic shear or lever distortion marks. Bypassing it would require the original double-bitted pin-tumbler key, and the sole spare sits inside the administration's fireproof safe!"

"Then the vector of breach originates from within," Ian stood, glancing at Sparky. "Sparky, pick the lock. Maintain zero structural impact."

"Consider it done! Behold my custom eight-gauge spring-steel rake!" Sparky grinned, sliding a tension tool and pick into the keyway. With several minute metallic clicks as the pins set, the heavy padlock popped open with a dull clack.

Sparky nudged the heavy timber door open. Moving like ghosts, the trio slipped into the darkened music conservatory, drawing the portal shut behind them.

The interior was pitch-black, blinds pulled tight, the air thick with the pungent aroma of rosin, damp wool felt, and oxidized timber. Sunlight penetrated only through louvre slats, slicing the gloom into parallel bands of illumination.

Upon the semicircular stage stood the Apollo 1932 concert grand, blanketed in dust, looming like an ancient ebony sarcophagus in the dimness.

Donning white cotton gloves, Ian approached the instrument. Rather than lifting the fallboard, he circled the chassis with measured steps, his monocle constantly adjusting focal depth, his gaze dissecting the floor like a surgeon's scalpel.

"The dust beneath the piano bench is completely undisturbed," Ian pointed downward. "When the piano played last night, no biological mass occupied that stool."

Aoi stepped to the piano's flank, lifting the heavy rim with delicate fingers. The cast-iron harp plate gleamed dull bronze in the gloom, with over two hundred high-carbon steel strings stretching taut in parallel arrays.

Unclasping her sterling silver tuning forks, Aoi struck the middle-C tuning fork gently against the central iron strut.

"Ding————"

A pure, shimmering overtone resonated through the quiet hall. Aoi shut her eyes, pressing the rounded stem of the vibrating fork against the spruce soundboard, holding her breath to absorb the microscopic tremors traveling through the wood grain.

After a full minute, Aoi opened her eyes, her gaze clear and certain:

"The strings are telling their tale!" Aoi pointed toward the whippen and hammer rail. "The residual metallic fatigue profile across the strings is completely unnatural! When human fingers strike keys, the soft finger pads yield subtle variations in force, velocity, and damping, producing rich acoustic harmonics across the soundboard."

"Yet right now, across the twenty-four strings from Middle C to Treble G, the internal stress distribution is absolutely uniform! The impact was stiff, cold, and relentlessly abrupt, as though... twenty-four identical miniature mechanical pushrods struck the action with uniform torque!"

"Mechanical hammers!" Sparky gasped. "Is there an automaton hidden inside the piano's belly?!"

"There is no automaton inside; the cavity volume is insufficient," Ian moved to the front of the keyboard, bending low with his monocle switched to 10x microscopic magnification, examining the ivory keyfronts.

"Look here," Ian pointed flat tweezers at the minuscule gap between Middle C and D. "Along the underside lip of the ivory veneer lies a microscopic circular furrow under 0.1 millimeters wide, bearing traces of clear polymer resin."

"Ultra-High-Molecular-Weight Polyethylene fiber—Dyneema braid!" Sparky recognized it instantly. "Thinner than human hair yet stronger than aircraft cable, practically invisible in dim lighting!"

Ian straightened up abruptly, his sharp gaze fixing on the ceiling two and a half meters directly above the piano.

The ceiling was fashioned of ornate molded plaster panels, and set into the molding directly over the piano was a rectangular antique brass ventilation grill!

Ian engaged the monocle's ultraviolet fluorescence filter. Under UV emission, faint streaks of luminous yellow-green residue flared along the grill's lower rim!

"The gears have meshed," Ian's lip curved faintly. "The drive engine is not within the piano, but inside the ventilation shaft!"

"Leave it to me! Spiky, time to crack the case!"

Sparky snapped open his toolbox lid, hoisting out 'Spiky' the clockwork hedgehog.

Forged of brass and lightweight titanium, the fist-sized automaton featured retractable quills along its carapace, four rubberized suction tracks underneath, and a fiber-optic periscope lens mounted upon its snout.

Sparky slotted the key, winding twelve deliberate turns, and tossed Spiky toward the three-meter ceiling. With soft pneumatic clicks, the vacuum tracks grabbed the brickwork, scurrying nimbly up the sheer wall into the ventilation duct!

Sparky cradled his monochrome monitor, watching real-time footage beamed through the fiber-optic link.

The duct interior was choked with black soot, Spiky's treads pressing clear ruts through the grime. As the robot rounded the first ninety-degree sheet-metal elbow, the screen shuddered, and Sparky gasped:

"Heavens above! Look at that! There's an entire miniature mechanical workshop inside the air pipe!"

Ian and Aoi leaned in immediately.

Bolted securely to the galvanized ductwork via neodymium magnetic clamps sat an exquisite clockwork apparatus!

It was a heavy-duty escapement powertrain repurposed from an antique pendulum grandfather clock. A massive brass mainspring barrel turned a forty-centimeter stainless steel camshaft fitted with twenty-four hand-carved brass cams cut to precise harmonic curves!

Beneath the cams stood twenty-four spring-loaded rocker levers, each trailing an ultra-thin line of Dyneema braid that dropped straight down through the grill slats toward the piano keys below!

"Ingenious... truly magnificent!" Even Ian breathed an appreciative murmur. "A purely mechanical 'Cam-Programmed Clockwork Sequencer'!"

Ian took the controller, steering Spiky closer to inspect the cams: "As the mainspring barrel rotates the camshaft at constant angular velocity, the profiled cams trip their respective levers, pulling the transparent lines to depress the piano keys! By calculating the cam curvature and rotational speed, Mozart's entire serenade was transcribed into physical mechanical motion!"

"That solves how the phantom piano played!" Sparky pumped his wrench triumphant. "No ghosts, no curses—it's an authentic classical mechanical player piano!"

"Still, Ian," Aoi pointed to a miniature cylinder beside the cams, "what about the floating blue mist and luminescent dust Guard Zhang saw?"

Ian rotated Spiky's camera toward the right. Flanking the camshaft was a pressurized aluminum aerosol can wired to a high-frequency ultrasonic piezoelectric atomizer nozzle, its tip flanked by a row of miniature UV LED emitters!

"The optical misdirection revealed," Ian explained to Aoi. "The canister holds a concentrated solution of food-grade riboflavin (Vitamin B2) and sodium fluorescein. When the clockwork trips, the damper valve actuates the ultrasonic atomizer, misting sub-micron cold droplets through the grill above the piano."

"In the dark, invisible UV rays strike the descending droplets, exciting electron transitions in the fluorescein molecules to emit cold-blue luminescence between 480 and 500 nanometers! Because the aerosol descends slowly upon thermal drafts, to Guard Zhang's terrified eyes, it resembled dancing ghostly dust hovering over the keys!"

"Fluorescent photoluminescence combined with ultrasonic aerosolization!" Aoi exhaled softly, before furrowing her brow. "Yet... who would design such an exquisite mechanism, and why orchestrate a ghost scare at school?"

Ian did not answer immediately. Walking to the rear utility rack, his gaze settled upon a water-damaged wooden plaque.

Affixed to the plaque was a thirty-year-old photograph showing a silver-haired master technician tuning this very Apollo grand with a tuning fork, wearing a brass lapel badge: *Chen's Antique Horology & Instruments · Master Tuner Chen Henian*.

Ian brushed his fingertip across the lower shelf, inspecting a streak of golden grease:

"Swiss molybdenum-based clockwork mainspring grease with a faint bitter almond scent. Across all of Luyang, only Chen's Antique Horology uses this expensive lubricant."

Ian looked toward the winding arbor in the duct: "Sparky, calculate the torque storage of that mainspring barrel."

Sparky calculated rapidly: "A 1.5-meter clockwork spring driving twenty-four cams under high load can only sustain fifteen minutes of continuous output at full wind!"

"Precisely," Ian looked out the louvres toward the schoolyard. "Meaning the perpetrator could not have set this up months ago. He must slip in every night or two to rewind the mainspring and refill the fluorescein canister!"

"And after school today, the custodial crew begins their comprehensive cleanup, boarding up all doors and windows in the old wing," Aoi's sharp intellect grasped the implications. "If he wants to maintain the illusion, he must return this afternoon!"

"We wait for our quarry," Ian said calmly.

The trio restored every clue to pristine order, withdrew into the corridor, relatched the padlock, and concealed themselves inside an abandoned choir riser storage room diagonally opposite the conservatory.

Hours ticked past. The afternoon sun drifted westward, breeze rustling through the empty hallway as the brick schoolhouse sank back into silence.

At four-thirty, the dismissal bell rang across campus. Student crowds dispersed, and twilight enveloped the grounds.

At five-fifteen, feather-light footsteps ascended the wooden stairs.

A lanky teenager wearing a denim vest and a flat cap checked his surroundings before darting toward the music room.

Slender and around sixteen years of age, a tool roll bulged at his hip. Slipping a tension shim into the padlock hasp, he popped the lock with practiced ease, slipped inside, and latched the door behind him.

"Move!" Ian commanded over their radio link.

Sparky sprinted across the hall, slamming a heavy steel bar through the rear fire exit handles to cut off retreat, while Ian and Aoi shoved the main double doors open wide!

Inside, the youth was balancing on the piano bench with an extended winding key, opening the ceiling vent. Whirling in shock at the thunderous entrance, his boot slipped, and he crashed onto the floorboards!

"Don't move!" Sparky shouted from the rear, aiming his suction net launcher.

Ashen-faced and chest heaving, the youth dropped his brass winding key, which rolled across the floor with a hollow clatter.

"Apprentice Ah-De," Ian stated calmly, identifying the youth. "The sole apprentice of Chen's Horology & Instruments, and grandson of Master Tuner Chen Henian. Am I correct?"

Ah-De shuddered violently, closing his eyes in defeat as his arms fell limp: "How... how did you find me?"

Aoi stepped forward, offering him a clean handkerchief without an ounce of reproach, her voice gentle: "Ah-De, why build such a brilliant clockwork apparatus, risking police arrest, just to haunt an old piano?"

Tears welled in Ah-De's eyes before cascading down his cheeks. Turning toward the battered Apollo grand, his voice broke:

"Because... because next week, the school is hauling it away for scrap metal!"

Ah-De dropped to his knees beside the instrument, his calloused hands caressing the worn lacquer:

"This Apollo grand was shipped from Europe when the school was built. Fifty years ago, my grandfather spent three whole months rebuilding every pin, string, and hammer! His name and guild hallmark are still engraved deep inside the soundboard pinblock!"

"Last month, a teacher visiting our shop mentioned the room was being gutted for a digital lab, and this piano was marked for demolition..."

Wiping his tears on his sleeve, Ah-De sobbed: "This is the last piece of craftsmanship my late grandfather left in this world! I wanted to buy it, but our shop is failing, and I couldn't raise thirty thousand dollars for transport and overhaul... So I used the clockwork skills my grandfather taught me to stage a haunting! I wanted the movers to be terrified, to delay the demolition by a few months, anything to buy time..."

Hearing the boy's grief, the music room fell silent. Amber sunset flooded through the blinds, warming the worn piano and the boy's tear-streaked face.

Sparky lowered his net launcher, rubbing the back of his head with reddened eyes. Aoi knelt beside Ah-De, pressing the handkerchief into his palms with tender sympathy.

Ian approached, picking up the brass winding key:

"Ah-De, fog woven of panic and deception can never protect precious memories. When the demolition sledgehammers strike, the illusion shatters instantly."

Ah-De hung his head in despair: "I know... but what else could I do? The pinblock is loose, three treble strings are snapped, the action is seized with moisture... To the administration, it's just firewood..."

"To those ignorant of mechanics, it is scrap; but in the hands of true craftsmen, it is an irreplaceable masterpiece," Ian's eyes hardened with resolve as he turned to Sparky and Aoi. "The soundboard is solid Bohemian spruce, and the iron harp is flawless. The pitch drifted because of oxidized tuning pins, and the action bound because the buckskin pads on the jacks dried out."

"Sparky has ultrasonic descalers and precision lathes. Aoi's tuning forks can resolve pitch deviations down to 0.1 Hertz. And I... know how to recalibrate the escapement geometry," Ian pressed the winding key back into Ah-De's palm. "We have four days until the centennial jubilee. The four of us will overhaul this Apollo together, and let its true voice convince the school on stage!"

Ah-De's head snapped up, his tears freezing in utter astonishment: "Really... truly? You would help me?"

"Never underestimate the Clockwork Detective Agency!" Sparky grinned proudly, thumping his chest. "My pack holds micrometers and genuine Swiss Moebius oil!"

"This is where physical evidence and memories truly belong," Aoi smiled radiantly.

Over the next three twilights, the dusty conservatory transformed into their secret workshop. Sparky dismantled every damp whippen, polishing pivot pins with jewelers' rouge; Ian recalculated string tension gradients, restringing snapped wires; while Aoi struck her silver forks again and again, tuning all eighty-eight keys until they rang with crystalline brilliance.

On Wednesday morning, the Centennial Jubilee ceremony commenced in the grand auditorium. As the principal took the lectern to bid farewell to the retiring instrument, Ah-De, dressed in a crisp white shirt, walked calmly to the piano.

Inhaling deeply, his fingers descended like swallows.

Instantly, an immense, warm, and soul-stirring rendition of Mozart's *Rondo alla Turca* swept the hall! No longer the rigid staccato of midnight clockwork, but a living symphony infused with two generations of craftsmanship and four youths' devotion!

The entire assembly was captivated, erupting into thunderous, standing applause. With tears in his eyes, the principal declared on the spot: the Apollo grand would be designated a permanent school treasure, preserved within the new archives and maintained by Chen's Horology forever!

At dusk following the ceremony, purple sunset set the western sky ablaze.

Ian, Aoi, and Sparky stood on the balustrade of the abandoned rooftop cupola, evening breeze in their hair, chilled sodas in hand, radiant smiles upon their faces.

"Case Number One, completely cracked!" Sparky gulped down his soda, wiping his mouth triumphantly.

"Ah-De was weeping as he bowed to us," Aoi watched the town below, her eyes reflecting the twilight. "Using the truth to protect someone's deepest wish... there is no better feeling in the world."

Leaning against the parapet, Ian looked out over the salt-scented bay, his monocle reflecting the twilight: "Solving puzzles is merely the prologue. Understanding the gears turning inside the human heart... that is the true calling of a detective."

At that moment, an uncanny gust whipped across the terrace, blowing the heavy cupola door ajar with a creak.

And upon the dust-covered telescope plinth in the terrace center lay a mysterious envelope, black as midnight.

Sealed not with wax, but with an exquisite sterling-silver clockwork gear, spinning autonomously without any mainspring at precisely one turn per second!

"A... an envelope?!" Sparky nearly dropped his soda can.

Ian's pupils constricted as his hand tightened instinctively around his field notebook—the wheels of destiny had officially begun to turn!
