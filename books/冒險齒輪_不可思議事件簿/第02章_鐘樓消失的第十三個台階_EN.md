# *Adventure Gear: The Files of the Impossible*

## Chapter 2: The Vanishing Thirteenth Step of the Clock Tower

At three in the afternoon, a sudden, violent rainstorm lashed against the century-old red-brick spires of Luyang Elementary, submerging the entire campus in a murky, deep-sea gloom.

Rainwater roared through the antique copper downspouts of the old schoolhouse, churning up thick, blinding curtains of white mist in the stone courtyards. In the very heart of the campus, the four-story Gothic clock tower—erected in 1912—stood like an ancient ironclad titan beneath the bruising storm clouds, stoic amidst flashes of lightning and rolling thunder. At the tower's summit, the heavy minute hand of the giant skeletonized brass dial ticked with solemn, monotonous clacks, its acoustic vibrations slicing through the rain to rattle against every listener's eardrums.

The back door of Class 6-1 was violently flung open. Accompanied by a tearful, terrified shriek, a soaked-to-the-bone third-grade boy stumbled across the threshold, his knees buckling as he collapsed onto the floor.

"H-Help! The clock tower ate him! Lin Hao fell right through the thirteenth step!" The little boy scrubbed frantically at the mixture of rain and tears on his face, his lips pale blue and his hands quivering like autumn leaves in a gale.

The classroom, previously hushed in quiet study, erupted into immediate pandemonium. Outside, a jagged blade of lightning tore across the sky, blanching the corridor in blinding white light before a deafening thunderclap rattled the windowpanes in their wooden frames.

"The urban legend of the clock tower's thirteenth step... it's real!" several timid girls gasped, clutching their textbooks to their chests as they recoiled half a step.

By the window, Lu Yan laid down his steel fountain pen. Looking up, the brass micro-monocle perched over his right eye caught the lightning, gleaming with a cold, analytical luminescence. He rose to his feet, casually straightening the lapels of his canvas utility vest, and strode directly over to the shivering boy.

"Take a deep breath, little guy." Shen Xingkui had already hurried over, pulling a clean cotton towel from her canvas tote to drape gently over the boy's trembling shoulders. Her voice was soothing, possessing a melodic calm that seemed to quiet even the fiercest tempest. "Don't panic. Tell us slowly: Who is Lin Hao? And what were you two doing inside the locked clock tower during such a torrential downpour?"

Sniffling into the warm towel, the boy pieced his story together between ragged breaths. His name was Zhou Xiaoyu from Class 3-2, and the trapped boy was his best friend and desk mate, Lin Hao. When their afternoon gym class was rained out, the two had snuck into the vintage clock tower through a ground-floor maintenance grate during the caretaker's shift change, desperate to retrieve an antique brass astronomy medallion they had accidentally kicked through a low ventilator slit the previous week.

"We were counting every single step along the spiral stairway... Normally, there are exactly twelve stone steps between the first and second floors, and twelve between the second and third," Xiaoyu whimpered, his face bleached with terror. "But right when that thunder crashed, an extra, glistening dark stone step suddenly appeared out of nowhere at the second-floor landing! Lin Hao stepped right onto it—and it tilted downward like a trapdoor over a swamp! He let out one scream and vanished down the black hole! And then... then the step snapped shut all by itself!"

"The staircase swallowed a living human whole? Haha! That sounds straight out of a vampire coffin trap in a medieval dungeon!"

A clamorous rattle of clanking metal components echoed down the hallway as Fang Xiaoke barged through the door. His mop of unruly curls was plastered with soggy leaf fragments, heavy-duty leather gloves wrapped his hands, and his three-tier brass craftsman's chest bounced against his hip. Perched on his shoulder was the mechanical hedgehog, "Piqiu," balled up into a compact, whirring sphere.

"Yan-ge! I dug up the complete hydraulic drainage blueprints for the school's bedrock foundations!" Xiaoke shouted enthusiastically, whipping a roll of aged cyan blueprints from a side pouch. "According to the Harbor Engineering Bureau archives from a century ago, the clock tower's foundation is reinforced entirely with deep-sea granite and red-copper trusses. Aside from a rainwater cistern and overflow flumes, there's no record of any secret human chamber down there!"

Lu Yan stepped to the blackboard, using a piece of chalk to sketch a precise, twelve-division vertical elevation of a staircase:

"In the realm of Euclidean geometry and rigid-body mechanics, physical mass cannot spontaneously generate from nothing, nor can it evaporate into the void. This so-called 'extra step,' unless a product of hysterical illusion, must obey the laws of mechanical displacement and linkage transmission." Lu Yan drew a bold chalk slash between the twelfth step and the landing platform. "Heavy downpour, lightning, an active tread that emerges before snapping back... This is an automated mechanical interlock that triggers only when an exact environmental threshold is breached."

"And Lin Hao has been trapped down there for over ten minutes already," Shen Xingkui said, her eyes turning serious as she unfastened her sterling silver tuning forks from her bag. "The clock tower has been boarded up for years. If the space beneath is an unventilated cistern cavity, carbon dioxide levels could rise rapidly. We need to breach that mechanism and pull him out immediately!"

"Piqiu! Special Operative Mode—engage!" Fang Xiaoke gave the central brass winding crown on the hedgehog's back three brisk clockwise turns. With a sharp *click*, the creature's thirty-six alloy sensory spines flared open, and twin micro-aperture cyan optic lenses flared to life amidst the gloom.

Shielded beneath two broad black umbrellas, the trio battled the driving tempest across the flooded courtyard, arriving at the massive Gothic arched entryway of the ancient clock tower.

The iron exterior gates were bound by a rusted chain, but exactly as Zhou Xiaoyu had described, two bars of the ground-level cast-iron ventilation grille had been sawed away, leaving a narrow aperture just large enough for a slender student to squeeze through.

Agile and composed, Lu Yan was the first to slide through the opening, with Xingkui and Xiaoke following right on his heels. As they shook the sheets of rain from their utility coats, the tower's frigid, damp interior air washed over them like a crypt breeze.

The tower's interior resembled a towering, hollow well constructed of fired brick and ashlar stone. A spiral granite staircase hugged the curving inner perimeter, coiling upward into the vaulted shadows dozens of meters above. The atmosphere was thick with the sour musk of damp limestone, blended with the nostalgic sting of antique clock oil and oxidized verdigris.

Dozens of meters overhead, the master transmission arbor and gravity counterweight pulleys of the clockwork mechanism groaned with subterranean clatters. The roar of rain pummeling the louvered belfry windows echoed through the cavernous vertical chamber in rolling, thunderous waves.

"Eyes on the stone steps," Lu Yan instructed as he halted before the lowest riser. He clicked the micro-adjustment ratchet of his brass monocle, switching the optical element into high-contrast polarized reflection mode.

He angled his high-intensity halogen flashlight upward, casting a razor-sharp beam of parallel white light flush against the ascending stairs:

"The flight from the ground floor to the first landing comprises exactly twelve steps. Each riser measures eighteen centimeters in height, with a tread depth of twenty-eight centimeters. Observe the wear curvature—the center of each slab has worn down nearly two centimeters, with a dried crust of lichens along the margins measuring 0.3 millimeters thick. This proves that these twelve granite slabs have remained undisturbed since their installation in 1912."

"Then where in the world is this mystical thirteenth step Xiaoyu saw?" Fang Xiaoke crouched at the edge of the landing platform, tapping his brass calipers against the seam between the twelfth step and the landing slab. "Yan-ge, this seam is barely five millimeters wide, packed tight with ancient lime mortar. It's solid as bedrock—you couldn't slide a sewing needle through it!"

Shen Xingkui stepped slowly onto the twelfth step. Without speaking, she lifted her wrist in a fluid motion, striking her twin sterling silver tuning forks together. A crystalline tone at standard pitch—440 Hertz (A4)—rang pure and piercing through the vault.

Kneeling swiftly, she pressed the spherical resonance terminals of both tuning forks firmly against the micaceous veins along the junction of the twelfth tread and the landing platform. Her eyes closed softly, her dark eyelashes fluttering in the dim beam of light.

"Xingkui-jie, do you hear anything?" Fang Xiaoke whispered, holding his breath.

"Shh... listen." Xingkui opened her eyes, an extraordinary brightness gleaming within them. "The acoustic propagation medium suffers a radical phase discontinuity right here!"

She tapped the eleventh step with her knuckle: "Below step eleven, the tuning fork's vibrational envelope exhibits high-frequency standing-wave damping characteristic of solid granite bedrock—the echo is deep, dense, and subterranean."

Then, she pressed her fingertip hard against the rear half of the twelfth tread where it met the landing:

"Yet right here, the harmonic decay stretches three times longer! Beneath this landing platform, barely ten centimeters below the veneer, lies a massive resonant cavity displacing over fifteen cubic meters! And what's more... I can hear water dripping inside!"

"A resonant cavity? Water flow?" Lu Yan's eyebrows arched slightly as his mind seized upon the pivotal physical variable in the deduction chain. "Xiaoke, bring out the clock tower's exterior facade diagram!"

Xiaoke unrolled the blueprint immediately. Lu Yan's finger traced rapidly along the drawing, pausing at the heavy gargoyle water spout carved into the tower's second-story exterior wall:

"The northwest exterior corner of the second floor features a hand-carved granite gargoyle drainage spout. During ordinary showers, runoff from the roof valley gutters discharges cleanly out of the beast's jaws into the courtyard. But in a cloudburst like today's—with precipitation exceeding fifty millimeters per hour—the century-old copper downspout cross-section is mathematically guaranteed to suffer catastrophic overflow!"

"And where does that overflow go?" Xingkui pressed.

"Directly into a gravity counterweight cistern concealed within the hollow wall!" Lu Yan's eyes flared with razor-sharp deduction, his knuckle knocking firmly against the double-wall partition on the plan. "Archimedes' buoyancy principle paired with Pascal's hydraulic multiplication! When this tower was erected in 1912, the master engineer deliberately diverted a fraction of storm overflow into a concealed copper vessel inside the masonry!"

"When the captured rainwater reaches a critical threshold weight—say, sixty kilograms—the gravitational pull of the vessel overcomes the lead counterpoise, hauling on a dual-linkage parallelogram pantograph tucked beneath the landing!" Fang Xiaoke gasped, practically bouncing on his soles as the mechanical architecture materialized in his mind. "Once the linkage is loaded, a recessed stone slab tucked beneath the landing is jacked upward by exactly eighteen centimeters! The precise height of a standard stair riser!"

"Precisely," Lu Yan affirmed. "That is why routine inspections will only ever count twelve steps. Without the water ballast, the thirteenth step rests flush beneath the landing pavement, its seams disguised by surface stone grain. But the instant a fierce squall fills that hidden ballast cistern, the thirteenth step rises!"

"B-But why did Lin Hao drop through when he stepped on it?" Zhou Xiaoyu asked, trembling at the thought.

"Because it was never engineered as an ordinary weight-bearing stair—it is a unidirectional gravity-tripped trapdoor!" Lu Yan's gaze hardened. "The buoyant water ballast can only sustain equilibrium when the step is unloaded. The second an additional human mass exceeding thirty kilograms steps upon it, the torque equilibrium shatters. The bottom sear releases, tilting the slab downward at a forty-five-degree angle like an escape chute, dumping the intruder into the subterranean vault before high-tension return springs snap the tread back into locked position!"

"Piqiu! Find the trip sear latch, quick!" Fang Xiaoke commanded, dropping onto his knees.

The rotund clockwork hedgehog emitted a chirpy electronic beep. Four articulated miniature alloy paws slid from its undercarriage, scuttling like a living ferret along the hairline seam between the twelfth step and the platform. A solitary sensor spine on its back flared with cold light as a fiber-optic endoscope slithered through the minuscule gap.

The liquid-crystal monitor strapped to Xiaoke's wrist flickered to life, streaming razor-sharp, monochromatic micro-footage from the depths of the masonry:

Suspended beneath the massive stone slabs lay an intricate mechanical assembly forged from cast bronze gears, heavy wrought-iron linkages, and balance beams! Though coated in blackened grease from decades past, the critical load-bearing trunnions and ratchet teeth gleamed with the dark-blue luster of molybdenum alloy—indisputable proof that a master craftsman had recently cleaned, lubricated, and calibrated this antique mechanism!

Even more crucially, deep within the chamber below, Piqiu's passive infrared sensor detected a faint thermal signature registering at thirty-six and a half degrees Celsius—a living human body!

"We found Lin Hao! He's trapped in a vaulted room barely four meters below us! Strong vital signs, but he seems to have sprained his ankle—he's propped against the corner!" Fang Xiaoke yelled in triumph.

"Lin Hao! Can you hear us?" Xingkui pressed her lips to the stone fissure, shouting with all her might.

A few agonizing seconds later, a faint, trembling voice filtered up through the floorboards: "X-Xingkui-xuejie? Lu Yan-xuezhang? Is that really you? I'm so cold... It's pitch-black down here, there are huge wooden clockwork crates everywhere, and the heavy doors are all chained from the outside!"

"Hold tight, Lin Hao! Stay exactly where you are and don't exert yourself. We are triggering the master release to retrieve you right now!" Lu Yan's calm, steady timbre cut through the stone, pouring unshakeable reassurance into the frightened boy below.

Lu Yan turned briskly to Fang Xiaoke. "Xiaoke, since this assembly relies on water ballast and a spring-loaded sear, there is zero need for destructive breaching. All we require is to impart a counter-rotational torque directly upon the trip pawl to lock the tread into an open slide position."

"Understood! Leave it to my high-tensile carbon extension probe!" Xiaoke snapped a two-meter sectional extension rod together from his chest, securing a micro-ratchet socket head at its tip.

Guided by the live feed from Piqiu's optic fibers, the probe threaded cleanly through the five-millimeter seam, seating its socket with absolute precision over the release eyelet of the bronze sear.

"Xingkui, heave with me on three!" Lu Yan gripped the center of the shaft, and Xingkui grasped the upper handle.

"Three, two, one—lever!"

Combining their strength, they threw their full body weight into the lever! Deep within the stone stairs came a resonant, industrial *CLACK* of hardened steel snapping back. Immediately, accompanied by the rumble of counterweighted chains, the 1.2-meter-wide black granite tread of the thirteenth step swung cleanly inward, locking into a smooth, forty-five-degree stone slide leading straight into the darkness!

"We got it! The descent chute is locked!" Xiaoke cheered, pumping his fist.

"I'll scout the descent first. Xiaoke, remain up top to monitor the structural torque on the linkages. Xingkui, follow right behind me." Decisive and swift, Lu Yan clipped his carabiner, tucked his legs, and slid smoothly down the polished stone slide into the shadowy chamber below.

His rubber boots landed softly upon thick, dry teak floorboards. The powerful beam of Lu Yan's torch cut through the century-old darkness of the sealed crypt.

Before them lay an ancient atelier spanning forty square meters, concealed directly beneath the bell tower's load-bearing stone barrel vault. The towering brick perimeter was lined with pegboards holding hundreds of antique clockwork gears, oversized brass vernier calipers, and dozens of yellowed vellum schematics detailing celestial orbital paths.

Curled beside a mound of cedar shavings in the corner sat third-grader Lin Hao. Though his left ankle was swollen, he was otherwise uninjured, his small hands clutching the reclaimed brass astronomy medallion tightly against his chest.

"Lu Yan-xuezhang!" Catching sight of Lu Yan and Xingkui sliding into view, Lin Hao burst into tears of pure relief.

Xingkui immediately knelt beside him. Drawing cold-pack gel and elastic wraps from Xiaoke's field first-aid kit, she deftly stabilized the boy's sprained ankle with gentle, reassuring efficiency. "Dry your tears, little hero—you were exceedingly brave. Keep your weight off that foot for now, and we'll have you back in the sunlight in no time."

Yet in the center of the workshop, Lu Yan's attention was thoroughly captivated by a massive walnut workbench dominating the chamber.

The tabletop was immaculate, swept clean of dust. Resting near the center was a jeweler's precision screwdriver, and beside it stood a vintage brass hurricane lantern whose glass chimney still radiated a faint, lingering warmth!

What caused Lu Yan's pupils to contract sharply, however, were two distinct objects resting atop a midnight-blue velvet pad in the middle of the desk:

The first was a miniature mechanical dodecahedron, ten centimeters across, forged from sterling silver intertwined with an unfamiliar violet alloy. Bereft of any visible battery contacts or mainspring barrels, dozens of microscopic gears within the dodecahedron's skeleton were ticking silently, rotating in perpetual, flawless unison at exactly one beat per second!

In metallurgical composition and micron-level craftsmanship, it hailed unmistakably from the exact same source as the mysterious self-spinning gear recovered from the Apollo grand piano the night before!

And resting beside the dodecahedron was an envelope crafted from heavy black cotton rag paper. Stamped in deep crimson wax was an ancient heraldic sigil: an Ouroboros serpent swallowing a clockwork winding key.

Wearing clean white cotton gloves, Lu Yan lifted the envelope with surgical care. Unfolding the sheet within, his eyes scanned elegant lines of calligraphic fountain pen script, composed in an archaic and delightfully challenging prose:

"To the youthful and keen riddle-solvers of the Gear:
Hearty congratulations on seeing through the hydrostatic cistern and the acoustic cavity to rescue the straying lamb.
Yet the ancient mists over this campus are far from spent—
When the high noon sun crowns the meridian,
The eight-hundred-kilogram bronze statue of the Founding Principal upon the quad
Shall take its first waking stride in a century before the eyes of all.
Can your proud laws of physics stay the midnight promenade of the Bronze Colossus?
—Oliver, The Clockwork Magician"

Xingkui stepped forward, peering over his shoulder at the elegant calligraphy, her delicate brow furrowing. "The Clockwork Magician, Oliver... He isn't some petty prankster. He is utilizing the century-old engineering marvels of the entire campus to throw down a formal gauntlet to our agency!"

"The bronze principal statue on the quad..." Xiaoke's startled voice came crackling down through the probe's acoustic transmitter from the landing above. "Yan-ge! That bronze statue weighs eight hundred solid kilograms! Its pedestal is bolted into a concrete caisson two meters deep—how could it possibly take a stroll on its own?!"

Lu Yan secured the black envelope and the perpetual mechanical dodecahedron into a specialized lead-lined pocket within his utility vest. Looking up toward the vaulted opening, the monocle over his right eye burned with fierce, intellectual exhilaration:

"Given a firm fulcrum and a sufficiently long lever, Archimedes could move the entire Earth. Given air cushions and rolling friction coefficients, even an eight-hundred-kilogram bronze colossus can glide across stone without leaving a whisper." A cool, confident smile curved the corners of Lu Yan's lips. "Oliver... No matter how miraculous your clockwork illusions appear, the Gear Detective Agency accepts your challenge!"

The storm began to abate. Through jagged rifts in the retreating bruised clouds, brilliant beams of golden late-afternoon sunlight burst forth, slicing through the tower's louvered belfry windows to drape the antique red bricks in molten, comforting gold.

Using a mechanical pulley harness, Fang Xiaoke and Lu Yan carefully hoisted the injured Lin Hao back onto the second-floor landing. When the homeroom teacher and school nurse waiting anxiously in the hallway beheld the boy brought safely back to light, tears of relief welled in their eyes.

The mystery of the vanishing thirteenth step was thoroughly vanquished. Following the detailed engineering diagrams provided by Lu Yan, the campus facilities crew promptly drained the overflow reservoir hidden in the second-floor wall partition. The dreaded extra stone tread retreated flush beneath the landing pavement, returning the ancient spiral stairway to its tranquil, century-old twelve-step count.

Back in Class 6-2 by the sunlit window, the evening glow cast the long, resolute silhouettes of Lu Yan, Shen Xingkui, and Fang Xiaoke across the floor toward the blackboard.

A fresh page was turned in the grid-ruled Kraft notebook, upon which the coordinates of their next investigation were penned with razor precision:

【Case File 003: The Midnight Promenade of the Bronze Principal Statue】—The second duel between the laws of classical physics and the sorcery of clockwork had officially commenced!
