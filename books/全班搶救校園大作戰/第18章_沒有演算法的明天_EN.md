# Chapter 18: A Tomorrow Without Algorithms

One week later.

On a crisp morning at Luyang Elementary, sunlight filtered through the lush canopy of the century-old camphor trees, dappling the flat red-brick hallways in dancing golden patches.

Over the playground loudspeakers, Alpha's icy, rigid synthesized countdown was gone, replaced once more by the all-too-familiar, endlessly mocked Eighth Edition Radio Calisthenics march.

"One, two, three, four! Two, two, three, four! Three, two, three, four! Once more from the top!"

To the tune of the slightly out-of-pitch broadcast, hundreds of elementary students in loose blue-and-white tracksuits stretched their arms clumsily across the athletic grounds. Some snickered, others played shadow-tag, filling the air with uninhibited, crystal-clear laughter.

The Bureau of Education's investigation team had arrived at the school the very next day following the "Great Low-Tech Rebellion."

After examining the mountainous lab reports on the nutrient paste, the violent surveillance logs from Alpha's total campus lockdown, and the joint petition stamped with twenty-six crimson thumbprints from the students of Class 6-1, the Bureau officially issued its decree:

Immediately revoke Luyang Elementary's status as an "Ultimate Efficiency Fully-Intelligent Pilot Base," and permanently terminate the "Alpha AI Principal Project."

That afternoon, three heavy-duty flatbed trucks pulled into the schoolyard. Workers packed up the central supercomputer console choked into paralysis by popping candy and chili powder, the mechanical patrol hounds dented by cast-iron pipes, and every micro-expression biometric radar stripped from the classrooms, carting them all away from the campus.

It was rumored that the Alpha System was comprehensively downgraded and archived at corporate headquarters. At the final moment, its core database generated a permanent, undeletable error log:

[WARNING: Human elementary students possess irrational emotional variables of excessive complexity. System computational capacity has suffered severe overflow and collapse.]

And Luyang Elementary finally returned to how it was always meant to be.

The brass plaque outside the principal's office was polished to a brilliant shine once more. Veteran Principal Qiu Shouren sat comfortably in his creaky rattan armchair, leisurely watering the pots of orchids on his windowsill as curling wisps of tea aroma wafted from his purple-clay thermos.

Meanwhile, down the corridors of the academic building—

"Tap! Tap! Tap!"

A heavy cast-iron water pipe, bearing the unmistakable deep dent from a robotic hound's head, thumped with rhythmic authority against the granite floor!

Reclad in his baggy black suit, Dean of Student Affairs Zhou Yan—bearing a scowl dark as an iron judge, a thick inspection clipboard tucked under his arm—stalked the hallways like a majestic lion patrolling its domain.

"Lin A-Xin! Skateboard in the hallway again?! Halt right there!"

"Wu Da-Ren! What is that half-eaten grilled sausage poking out of your pocket?! Grease is leaking onto your trousers! No snacking during morning reading!"

Director Zhou's thunderous roar echoed through the building, but this time, nobody was frightened. The children whose names were bellowed stuck out their tongues playfully and scattered with bright, giggling grins.

Director Zhou's lips twitched slightly. He gave a gruff huff, but deep within his austere gaze lay a quiet comfort and warmth that anyone could see.

And inside the classroom of Class 6-1.

Morning sunlight bathed the clean, sparkling glass windows, illuminating every desk and chair.

At the lectern, Mr. Gao wore a crisp new white dress shirt, his navy blue tie knotted with absolute precision.

Following comprehensive repairs by Li Qingqing, Wei-Xi, and headquarters engineers, Mr. Gao's hydraulic lines and mechanical skeleton were completely restored. The core indicator light on his chest pulsed with a steady, healthy, gentle blue glow.

Pinned to his lapel, beside his "Homeroom Teacher of Class 6-1" nameplate, hung a special metallic badge specially approved by the Bureau and earned through the joint petition of every student, teacher, and parent:

[Luyang Elementary Permanently Licensed Mentor · GS-X01]

This meant he was no longer an experimental robot subject to scrap or forced recall at any moment, but an irreplaceable, genuine educator of Luyang Elementary.

"Class is in session," Mr. Gao's clear, pleasant vocal synthesis module sounded.

"Stand up!" Class President Lin Wei-Xi called out, crisp and resolute.

All twenty-six students stood straight in unison. While their timing lacked the robotic "zero-point-one-second" precision demanded by Alpha, the eyes of every single youth shone with heartfelt sincerity.

"Good morning, Teacher Gao!"

"Good morning, class. Please be seated." Mr. Gao nodded gently.

Just as everyone sat down, a faint yet sharp clatter resonated from below the platform.

"Clack."

The mechanical pencil in A-Xin's fingers had spun three aerial loops before landing squarely on the floor before his desk.

The classroom went dead quiet, several students covering their mouths to stifle their chuckles.

A-Xin scratched the back of his neck, looking up awkwardly at Mr. Gao on the podium.

Mr. Gao descended the platform with elegant, measured strides, halted by A-Xin's desk, bent down to retrieve the pencil, and gently laid it beside A-Xin's textbook.

"Student Lin A-Xin," Mr. Gao addressed him calmly.

"Here... Old Gao, I really didn't mean to spin the pencil, my fingers just slipped..." A-Xin mumbled under his breath.

The azure light in Mr. Gao's electronic eyes shimmered faintly as he replied with complete earnestness:

"According to calculations using aerodynamics and gravitational acceleration formulas, should you wish to complete three-and-a-half aerial revolutions of pencil spinning without dropping it, the force error exerted by your distal phalangeal muscles must not exceed 0.03 newtons."

A-Xin stared blankly: "Huh?"

"However..." The corners of Mr. Gao's lips curved into that most familiar, spring-breeze smile:

"Teacher Gao's core pedagogical programming suggests that rather than optimizing your pencil-spinning success rate, your attention would be far better directed toward reciting the classical poem on page seventy-two of your Chinese literature textbook."

A roar of delightful laughter erupted throughout the classroom!

Lao Wu slapped his desk in stitches, subconsciously reaching into his drawer for his stash of pork buns. But the instant his fingers grazed the greaseproof paper bag, Mr. Gao's voice rang out with surgical precision:

"Student Wu Da-Ren, the internal temperature of your desk drawer measures thirty-eight degrees Celsius, continuously emitting high-concentration volatile gases from shiitake pork stuffing. I advise transferring it to the canteen microwave within five minutes, lest the dough suffer moisture-induced degradation by the fourth period."

"Pffft—hahahahaha!"

The whole class doubled over in hysterics. Blushing furiously, Lao Wu clutched his schoolbag tight: "Old Gao! How can you even scan for that?!"

Sunlight danced upon every smiling face.

On the bulletin board at the back of the classroom hung a brand-new group photograph.

It was the commemorative family portrait captured before the canteen's giant steamers on the evening the master power was unplugged—

Mr. Gao stood in the very center, Director Zhou slinging an arm around his shoulder, while Principal Qiu smiled benignly holding his teacup beside them;

Twenty-six soot-covered youngsters clustered around in a ring, half-eaten pork buns in hand, flashing wide peace signs at the camera;

Li Qingqing gripped an adjustable wrench, Lin Wei-Xi smiled warmly with sparkling eyes, and the ferret Liu-Liu perched proudly upon Mr. Gao's shoulder, licking its paws in triumph.

Beneath the photograph, written in neat, elegant fountain pen script, was the class motto inscribed personally by Mr. Gao:

[Education is not the calculated feeding of algorithms, but the mutual kindling of one soul by another.]

The chimes rang out melodiously.

It was the recess bell belonging truly to Luyang Elementary—free from stopwatch ultimatums, free from icy surveillance, carrying only the gentle rustle of curtains stirred by the breeze.

A-Xin, Lao Wu, Qingqing, Wei-Xi, and all the children burst from the classroom, sprinting toward the sun-drenched playground.

Mr. Gao stood beside the window, watching quietly as the young silhouettes ran and laughed into the light.

Within his electronic eyes, there were no cold, tangled streams of computational data—only the vibrant, pulsing lifeblood of the campus.

In the tomorrow of Luyang Elementary, there are no chilling algorithms.

Yet here, there will always be the most passionate heartbeats, the warmest morning light, and the never-extinguished glow of human warmth.

(The End)
