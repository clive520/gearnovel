# Chapter 09 — Memories That Don't Match

September 23rd, Sunday. 6:30 in the morning.

Lin Che was woken by a rapid burst of message notifications.

In the group chat, A-Da had sent five messages in a row: "The mirror's open!!" "It opened at seven!" "And it's super bright!!" "Hurry up!!" "I'm already at school!!"

Lin Che sat up in bed. Today was... the seventh day.

He got dressed, grabbed the blue pencil and stuffed it into his pocket, and hopped on his bike.

Haisheng Elementary in the early morning was quiet, like another world itself.

The 7-Eleven sign by the school gate had now been holding its "Haisheng Bookstore" blue for several minutes straight. It was like a broken TV, stuck between two channels.

Lin Che paused under the sign for a few seconds before pedaling on.

In front of the old auditorium, Ching-Ching and A-Da had already arrived.

"You finally made it!" A-Da waved. "Look, quick!"

The mirror was wide open. The silvery-white light was brighter than any day before, as if someone had poured an entire sheet of moonlight into the mirror.

The cracks on the wall had multiplied too. Thin glowing lines stretched out from the mirror frame like branches, crawling across half the wall.

"The last day," Ching-Ching said. "Every mystery has to be solved today."

Just as they were about to enter the auditorium, Lin Che spotted someone.

Standing at the school gate was a girl. Backpack on, completely still, as if waiting for someone.

It was Hsiao-Yun. Kuo Hsiao-Yun. The quietest and smartest girl in Class 6-1. She usually kept to herself, always ate lunch alone, and sat in the corner of the last row.

"Hsiao-Yun?" A-Da shouted. "What are you doing at school?"

Hsiao-Yun turned. Her eyes were red, as if she hadn't slept all night.

"My memory..." her voice was soft and trembling, "has gained a piece that doesn't belong to me."

The three of them froze.

Hsiao-Yun pulled a sheet of paper from her backpack. It was densely covered in her ballpoint handwriting.

"Last night, I suddenly 'remembered' something," she said. "I remember eating lunch with a group of people. Someone stole a piece of my chicken leg, someone poured juice for me, everyone laughed together."

"But," her voice began to shake, "I have never eaten lunch with anyone. Not once."

She looked at the three of them, tears welling in her eyes: "That memory was so warm. So warm... it felt like it was stolen."

Lin Che's heart clenched.

He understood it all too well. That feeling of someone else's happiness barging into your own mind.

"It wasn't stolen," Ching-Ching said, her tone uncharacteristically gentle. "That's the memory of the you in World B."

Hsiao-Yun's eyes went wide.

"World B...? There really is another me?"

"There is." Lin Che nodded. "And," he paused, "in that world, you eat lunch with a group of people every day at noon."

Hsiao-Yun opened her mouth, but nothing came out.

Ching-Ching glanced at her: "Do you want to come in with us? To see the other you with your own eyes."

Hsiao-Yun lowered her head. After a long while, she nodded.

The four of them walked into the auditorium.

In the mirror, the old auditorium of World B was wide open too. The other side was already crowded with people.

"You're late!" Xiao-Yang stood at the front, hands on his hips, his voice alarmingly loud. "We've been up since six!"

Da-ge laughed at him: "You're the one who woke all of us up at five."

The four from World A passed through the mirror.

Hsiao-Yun was the last one.

The moment her foot stepped into World B, her whole body shivered slightly.

"Welcome." B Ching-Ching smiled at her. Then she turned and called behind: "Hsiao-Yun! Come here, quick!"

A girl squeezed out of the crowd.

Ear-length hair, round glasses, a thick novel tucked into the side pocket of her backpack.

It was the Hsiao-Yun of World B.

Two Hsiao-Yuns, face to face.

Same eyes, same nose, same nervous expression.

But behind B Hsiao-Yun stood a whole group of people.

"Who's that?" B Hsiao-Yun's friends crowded over, sizing her up curiously. "Whoa, there are two Hsiao-Yuns now!" "So cool!" "Which one is the real one?"

"They're both real!" B Hsiao-Yun nudged her friends and laughed.

She laughed so naturally, as if having friends by her side was the most obvious thing in the world.

A Hsiao-Yun looked at her and suddenly asked: "Do you... always have people eating lunch with you, every day at noon?"

B Hsiao-Yun blinked: "Of course. Why?"

"Nothing." A Hsiao-Yun lowered her head, her voice tiny. "I just... think that's really nice."

The atmosphere went quiet for a second.

One of B Hsiao-Yun's friends, a girl with a ponytail, suddenly walked over and took A Hsiao-Yun's hand: "Come on, eat lunch with us later! You guys will be here until noon anyway, right?"

A Hsiao-Yun froze completely.

"...Okay," she said. Her voice was as small as a mosquito's.

But Lin Che saw the corner of her mouth curve upward, just a tiny, tiny bit.

The meeting began.

The two classes gathered on the World B lawn, sitting in one big circle.

Ching-Ching and B Ching-Ching each took out their notebooks. The two notebooks lay open together, like two pieces of a puzzle.

"First, let's compare the differences between the two sides," Ching-Ching said.

She listed them one by one:

"One, the bookstore. World A has a 7-Eleven; World B has Haisheng Bookstore."

"Two, the slide. World A doesn't have one; World B does."

"Three, the farm. World A has a concrete lot; World B has a class farm."

"Four, the class monitor. In World A it's Yu-Ching; in World B it's Xiao-Yang."

"Five, the bulletin board. World A has a notice board; World B has An-An's drawings all over it."

"Six, A-Da's nickname. World A has none; in World B he's called Da-ge."

"Seven..." B Ching-Ching picked it up. "Hsiao-Yun's friends. Over here, our Hsiao-Yun has a group of best friends."

Everyone turned to look at A Hsiao-Yun.

She kept her head down, ears reddening.

Ching-Ching closed her notebook and looked around at everyone: "Have you noticed? None of these differences are random."

"Not random?" A-Da asked.

"Mm." Hsiao-Yun suddenly spoke. Her voice was still small, but very clear. "The bookstore, the slide, the farm, the bulletin board... those are 'things.' But the monitor, the nickname, the friends, the drawings on display... those all connect to one thing."

"What thing?" Xiao-Yang leaned in.

"How the class treats each and every person in it," Hsiao-Yun said. "In World B, people are encouraged, people are seen, people have friends. In World A..."

She didn't finish the sentence.

But everyone understood.

Ching-Ching's eyes lit up: "So starting from some moment, the two worlds' 'way of treating people' completely split apart."

"If we can find that moment..." B Ching-Ching continued.

"...then we'll know where all of this began," Lin Che finished.

The wind swept across the lawn, tousling everyone's hair.

"Alright!" Xiao-Yang stood up and raised a fist to the sky. "The Two-Class Joint Investigation officially begins!"

"Yay—!" all the children shouted together.

Suddenly, from the World B side, someone screamed.

"The slide!"

Everyone turned.

The slide by the lawn flickered. Like a TV changing channels, the blue slide became a translucent shadow for an instant, then snapped back to normal.

The World B children all went pale.

"The overwriting... is speeding up," Ching-Ching murmured.

Just as Lin Che was about to speak, he saw something.

Da-ge stood beside the slide, comforting everyone. The sun was bright, and everyone's shadow lay clear on the ground.

Only at Da-ge's feet, there was no shadow.

For an instant. Then the shadow came back.

Lin Che rubbed his eyes.

He didn't tell anyone.

But his heart was pounding louder than his morning alarm.

10:30. The four of them got ready to return to World A.

"We have to be back before eleven," Ching-Ching checked her watch. "The three-hour limit."

Hsiao-Yun stood before the mirror and glanced back.

B Hsiao-Yun and her friends were standing on the lawn, waving at her.

"See you in a bit!" the ponytail girl shouted. "Don't forget to eat lunch with us!"

Hsiao-Yun didn't answer. She just nodded firmly once, then stepped into the mirror.

Back in World A, the four of them walked out of the auditorium.

Lin Che looked back. The cracks on the wall had gained one more line.

A new, glowing crack was crawling toward the playground.
