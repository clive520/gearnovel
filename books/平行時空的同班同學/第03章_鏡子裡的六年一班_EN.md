# Chapter 03 — Class 6-1 in the Mirror

That voice landed like a small pebble dropped into the hearts of all three of them. Plop.

Inside the auditorium, it was so quiet that only their heartbeats could be heard. The silvery-white light flowed slowly across the mirror's surface.

"Is... is she talking to us?" A-Da's voice trembled a little.

Ching-Ching took a deep breath and stepped forward. She was the class monitor — when something happened, she was always the first to step up.

She raised her voice toward the mirror: "We can see you! We can see you guys!"

The image in the mirror shuddered.

The "other Ching-Ching" widened her eyes and turned to shout into the classroom: "Come quick! They can hear us!"

Inside the classroom, several students immediately came running.

The image grew clearer and clearer — it wasn't a reflection in a mirror, but rather like a "window."

On the other side of the window was a classroom. It looked a lot like their Class 6-1 at Haisheng Elementary: same blackboard, same desks and chairs, same windows.

But everywhere, things were different.

The bulletin board at the back of the classroom was covered in drawings, colorful and lively, like a little garden. A string of shell wind chimes hung by the window, turning gently. On the teacher's desk sat a thriving pothos plant.

And their uniforms—

Ching-Ching's gaze swept over sharply.

"Their pins are on the right," she said quietly.

Only then did Lin Che notice: everyone on the other side of the window wore their uniform pin on the right side of their chest. Their own were on the left.

The children on the other side of the mirror were also looking at them through the "window." Both sides stared at each other, and nobody said a word.

It was a strange feeling. Like walking into a room with mirrors on all four walls — everyone in the mirror looked vaguely familiar.

A-Da suddenly pointed across: "Wait! That... is that me?"

On the other side of the window, a boy stood in the middle of a crowd. His hair also had a tuft sticking up, his uniform was also half-tucked, but—

He stood tall, with three or four classmates gathered around him, as if listening to him talk. He gestured as he spoke, and halfway through, he laughed at his own joke first.

A classmate beside him patted his shoulder and called out: "Da-ge!"

"Da-ge?" A-Da's eyes went wide. "He— he has a nickname?"

Ching-Ching ignored him. Her gaze swept the classroom across the way and finally stopped at the very center.

A boy sat there. He sat up straight, chin slightly raised, talking with the classmate next to him. The classmate laughed as he listened, and someone even turned around specifically to look at him.

That boy was Lin Che.

And he wasn't Lin Che.

He had the same short hair, the same brows and eyes. But the expression on his face was one that Lin Che had never seen on his own face—

Confident, bright, like the sun. Glowing.

The boy in the mirror seemed to sense something. He looked up, straight toward the "window."

The moment their eyes met, Lin Che's heart clenched tight.

That face — he'd seen it before. Just last night, in the ripples of the mirror. The face that had waved at him.

The boy in the mirror paused for a moment, then— smiled.

He raised his hand toward Lin Che and waved.

Exactly the same gesture.

"You know him?" A-Da leaned in and whispered.

Lin Che didn't answer. He felt like something was stuck in his throat.

Ching-Ching stepped forward and shouted through the "window" at the other Ching-Ching: "Are you... also Class 6-1 of Haisheng Elementary?"

The other Ching-Ching nodded. Her voice came through the mirror's surface sounding a bit like it was passing through water: "Yes. You too?"

"Yes."

The two identical-looking girls looked at each other across the mirror.

"What's today's date?" Ching-Ching asked.

"September 18th," the other Ching-Ching answered. "You?"

"...September 18th."

Both sides fell silent at the same time.

The same day. The same school. The same class.

Ching-Ching frowned. Her brain was spinning fast — if both sides were the same day, the same school, the same class, then why did the two classes look so completely different?

"On your side..." the other Ching-Ching suddenly spoke, her tone a little hesitant, "has anything strange happened recently?"

"Yes." Ching-Ching answered without hesitation. "The store by the school gate — some people remember it as a bookstore, some remember it as a convenience store."

The other Ching-Ching's eyes went wide: "Us too! And — here, that store has always been Haisheng Bookstore, but someone in our class remembers it being a 7-Eleven."

"A 7-Eleven?" Ching-Ching immediately pressed. "Who remembers that?"

"A-Bao." The other Ching-Ching said. "He swears he buys Yakult at that 7-Eleven every day. But here, it's always been a bookstore."

Ching-Ching lowered her head, pulled the small notebook from her pocket, and wrote several lines quickly. Her hand was shaking, but her writing stayed neat.

"So the memories of both worlds are starting to 'not match up,'" she murmured.

Just then, footsteps came from outside the auditorium.

All three of them froze.

It was the school's janitor. He pushed his cleaning cart past the auditorium entrance.

He glanced inside.

The three of them held their breath — they were standing right there, right in front of the glowing mirror. There was no way he could miss them.

But the janitor only yawned and muttered to himself: "This old auditorium... when are they ever going to tear it down..."

Then he pushed his cart and walked away.

From beginning to end, he never saw them. And he never saw the silvery-white light.

"He... didn't see us?" A-Da's voice trembled.

Ching-Ching stood where she was, face pale but calm: "It's not that he didn't see us. It's that he didn't see what's happening here."

Lin Che suddenly thought of himself yesterday — if Ching-Ching hadn't walked in yesterday, would he have been just like the janitor, seeing nothing at all?

On the other side of the mirror, the other Ching-Ching called out anxiously: "Hey! Are you still there?"

"Yes!" Ching-Ching snapped back to attention and answered loudly.

But the ripples on the mirror's surface were speeding up.

The silvery-white light flickered, and the image swayed like a reflection in water. The sounds from the classroom across the way grew distant, then near, then distant again.

"It's... it's closing!" Lin Che said.

"Tomorrow!" The other Ching-Ching pressed both hands against the mirror and shouted, "Come back tomorrow, same time!"

"Okay!" Ching-Ching nodded hard.

In the mirror, the "other Lin Che" also stepped forward.

Through the blurring image, he mouthed something at Lin Che.

Lin Che read it.

It was: "See you tomorrow."

The next second, the silvery-white light contracted and vanished.

The mirror returned to being an old, dust-covered mirror.

In the auditorium, only the breathing of three children remained.

"...Did we just talk to 'another us'?" A-Da finally spoke, his voice still shaking.

"Mm." Ching-Ching put her notebook away and stood up. "And I think I know what we're doing tomorrow."

"What?" Lin Che asked.

"Going over." Ching-Ching looked at the mirror, her eyes bright. "If we want to figure all this out, we have to go over and take a look."

A-Da jumped to his feet at once: "I'm going! I'm definitely going!"

Lin Che looked at the mirror. His reflection there was pale, quiet, shoulders hunched.

He thought of the smile on that "other him."

He thought of that sentence he still couldn't remember: "Don't you want to give it a try?"

"I'm going too," he heard himself say.

By the time the three of them walked out of the old auditorium, the sun was about to sink into the sea.

No one noticed that the silvery-white crack in the lower-right corner of the mirror had grown—

a little longer than yesterday.
