# Chapter 02 — The Strange Happenings at Haisheng Elementary

The next morning, Lin Che walked through the school gate with two dark circles under his eyes.

He'd barely slept all night. Every time he closed his eyes, the mirror, that smile, and the words "Don't you want to give it a try?" took turns spinning through his head.

But strangely enough, when he walked through Haisheng Elementary's gate, he realized he wasn't the only one acting "off."

In front of the convenience store by the school gate, a crowd of Class 6-1 students had gathered, arguing fiercely.

"I swear! I swear, when school let out yesterday, this place was still 'Haisheng Bookstore'!" A-Bao waved his arms, his face flushed red.

"As if!" Xiao-Mei shot back. "This has always been a 7-Eleven! I buy my Yakult here every single day!"

"Yeah, yeah, A-Bao, did you stay up all night gaming again and fry your brain?"

"I didn't! I really remember! The bookstore lady even gave out stickers!"

The classmates burst out laughing, patted A-Bao on the shoulder, and filed through the gate one by one. Nobody took him seriously.

Only Lin Che stood frozen in place.

Because he remembered too.

He remembered that on the first day of school, the sign on that store by the gate was blue and read "Haisheng Bookstore," with a yellowed book-fair poster taped to the glass door. He remembered buying a pencil there.

But now, what stood before his eyes was a green 7-Eleven sign, with a buy-one-get-one coffee poster on the glass door.

It was as if that bookstore had never existed.

"Lin Che, what are you looking at?"

A voice came from behind him. It was the class monitor, Ching-Ching. She was carrying a stack of homework notebooks and followed his gaze.

"Nothing." Lin Che shook his head.

Ching-Ching glanced at him, said nothing, and walked through the gate on her own.

During morning self-study, Lin Che kept thinking about it. He secretly glanced toward Ching-Ching's seat—

Ching-Ching had her head down, writing in a small notebook. She wrote quickly and intently, occasionally pausing to tap her forehead with the tip of her pen.

Lin Che quietly shifted his chair a little closer to her.

In the notebook, neat handwriting filled line after line:

"9/17 — Crack appears in the old auditorium mirror. Silvery-white. Glows."
"9/17 — Lin Che says he didn't see it (lying)."
"9/18 — Store by the school gate: most people remember 7-Eleven, A-Bao remembers Haisheng Bookstore."

Lin Che's heart skipped a beat.

Ching-Ching suddenly looked up, meeting his eyes exactly.

"...You're peeking," she said.

"I— I wasn't—"

"You were." Ching-Ching closed the notebook and stared at him. "And I need to correct yesterday's record. I didn't 'maybe see wrong.' I definitely saw that crack, and it was glowing."

Lin Che was stunned: "Then why did you say yesterday..."

"Because I wasn't sure if I should trust my own eyes." Ching-Ching said it plainly. "So I went home and looked things up."

She pulled a printout from her desk and lowered her voice: "Haisheng Elementary's website says the old auditorium was 'closed for certain reasons' thirty years ago. But nowhere in the entire school history does it say what those 'certain reasons' were."

"Thirty years?" Lin Che's eyes went wide.

"Mm." Ching-Ching nodded, a certain light in her eyes — one Lin Che knew well. It was the look she got whenever she was about to crack a tough math problem. "So there are two suspicious things. One, that mirror. Two, everyone's memories."

She paused.

"Lin Che, you remember that bookstore too, don't you?"

Lin Che opened his mouth, then finally nodded.

Ching-Ching looked at him — didn't laugh at him, didn't say "how could you possibly remember that." She just opened her notebook and wrote on the last line:

"Lin Che: remembers Haisheng Bookstore."

Then she tucked the notebook into her backpack and said quietly, "After school, let's go to the old auditorium again."

"We?"

"Yes." Ching-Ching glanced at him. "Two people. Less scary."

The words had barely left her mouth when a head suddenly popped up between the two of them.

"What are you two whispering about? I want in!"

It was A-Da.

Wu Da, Class 6-1's idea machine. He always had a tuft of hair sticking up, his uniform always half-tucked, and his desk drawer always stuffed with strange things — rubber bands, marbles, springs picked up from who-knows-where. He had the wildest ideas, the biggest guts, and the loudest voice.

"Nothing." Ching-Ching frowned.

"There was! I heard you! The old auditorium, right?" A-Da's eyes lit up. "Then let me tell you something even crazier — I dreamed about Lin Che last night!"

"You dreamed about me?" Lin Che tensed.

"Yeah! I dreamed you were in the old auditorium, talking to a... a guy who looked just like you." A-Da gestured wildly. "I was about to call out to you, but then you both turned around and looked at me at the same time, and I woke up scared."

A few classmates overheard and crowded over, laughing: "There goes A-Da again." "Did you watch a horror movie last night?" "A-Da's dreams could be a movie, seriously."

"Ugh, you guys are the worst, I'm telling the truth!" A-Da protested.

Amid the laughter, A-Da suddenly stared at Lin Che, tilting his head as if thinking about something.

"Also," he said, "I think I've seen you in a mirror before."

The air went quiet for a second.

Then A-Da burst out laughing at his own joke: "Haha, just kidding! What are you, a mirror monster?"

Everyone laughed again as the class bell rang. The students scattered.

Only Lin Che stood where he was, his palms slick with cold sweat.

After school, four o'clock sharp.

The sun hung low at the edge of the playground, stretching the three of them into long shadows. Lin Che, Ching-Ching, and A-Da — who had insisted on tagging along — stood before the old auditorium's iron gate.

"It's locked." A-Da reached out and pushed.

"It was open yesterday," Lin Che said.

Ching-Ching stepped forward, crouched down, and examined the gate carefully.

"The lock hasn't been tampered with," she said. "But — there's light in the gap."

All three of them held their breath at the same time.

From the gap in the iron gate seeped a faint thread of silvery-white light. Exactly like yesterday.

"Push it open," Ching-Ching said.

The three of them pushed together. With a long creak, the iron gate slowly swung open.

Inside the auditorium, everything was as usual. Dust, curtains, sheet-covered chairs.

Then they saw it.

Behind the stage, that old mirror was glowing.

Not the faint, barely-there glow from yesterday. Today, the mirror's surface rippled in rings, like water after a stone had been dropped in. Silvery-white waves spread out from the center of the mirror, growing wider, faster and faster.

"Whoa..." A-Da's mouth fell open. "What is this cool thing?"

But Lin Che had already taken two steps forward.

In the rippling waves, he vaguely saw — not his own reflection.

It was "somewhere else."

That place looked a lot like a classroom. A blackboard, desks, chairs, windows. Chalk writing on the board, potted plants on the windowsill.

But it was also different.

The bulletin board at the back of that classroom was covered in drawings, so many they were about to fall off. A string of shell wind chimes hung by the window, turning gently.

"Is that... our classroom?" A-Da whispered.

"No." Ching-Ching shook her head. "Our board is a notice board. There are no drawings."

Just then, the image in the mirror moved.

The back of a girl walked past the back of the classroom. She wore a Haisheng Elementary uniform, her ponytail swinging, and stopped in front of the bulletin board.

Then, as if she'd heard something, she turned around—

Lin Che and the others gasped at the same time.

That girl was Ching-Ching.

And she wasn't Ching-Ching.

She looked exactly like Ching-Ching, but the pin on her uniform was on the right side of her chest. Ching-Ching's pin was on the left.

And she was smiling. A relaxed, happy smile — the kind of smile that no one in Class 6-1 had ever seen on Ching-Ching's face.

The "Ching-Ching" in the mirror looked at the three of them.

Her mouth slowly opened, as if she wanted to say something.

The ripples suddenly intensified. The silvery-white light flared up, so bright the three of them had to squeeze their eyes shut.

Then, from the deepest part of the light, they heard a voice.

A girl's voice, from very, very far away, on the other side of the mirror, carrying surprise—

"Can you... can you see us?"
