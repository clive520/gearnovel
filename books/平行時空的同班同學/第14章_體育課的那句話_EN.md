# Chapter 14 — The Words from PE Class

9:10 at night. On the playground, the fifty-two children had not dispersed.

The clock's image had vanished. But that image would stay in everyone's hearts.

"Three hours left," Xiao-Yang said. He looked at everyone. "But we don't just have three hours. We have fifty-two people."

The children glanced at one another. No one knew who started it, but slowly, everyone gave a firm nod.

B Lin Che walked up to Lin Che.

"Ready?"

Lin Che looked at him. His throat was dry. He thought of the fog from earlier, and the words he couldn't hear.

"Ready," he said.

The two of them walked to the bend in the track.

"Here," B Lin Che said. "That day, Coach Liu stood right here. And you stood here."

He stepped back two paces and pointed at the ground beneath his feet.

Lin Che looked down. The white starting line on the track glowed faintly in the moonlight.

The two of them stood facing each other.

"It might hurt," B Lin Che said. "Remembering forgotten things is sometimes like tearing the scar off a wound."

"It's fine," Lin Che said. "I've... been hurting for a long time already."

B Lin Che looked at him and held out his hand.

Lin Che held out his hand.

The two hands clasped together under the moonlight.

Silvery-white light spilled from between their fingers.

Memories came surging in, like a dam bursting open.

Lin Che saw it.

September. Sunshine. The playground.

Coach Liu's whistle sliced sharply through the sky.

"Today, we're picking our relay team." Coach Liu's voice was loud and warm. "The anchor leg — who wants to try?"

All twenty-six students had their heads down. You looked at me, I looked at you — not one hand went up.

Lin Che stood at the very back of the line. His hand slowly, slowly rose just a little.

Just a little. Like a baby bird learning to fly, hesitating over whether to leave the branch.

Coach Liu's eyes swept across the class.

He saw Lin Che.

He walked over. One step, two steps, three steps.

Lin Che's heart pounded. His half-raised hand was still in the air.

"You're more on the quiet side." Coach Liu smiled, his tone gentle. "For the anchor leg, let's leave it to the kids who run fast."

The half-raised hand dropped.

Like a bird folding its wings and returning to the branch. Never to fly again.

"..." Lin Che watched that version of himself.

He watched himself lower his head, retreat to the back of the line, retreat to the window, retreat to the corner.

He watched himself never raise his hand again. Never raising his hand in class, never speaking at recess — even when someone called his name, it took him an extra beat to turn around.

Then the images became a fast-forwarding film.

He saw A-Da telling jokes on the podium. The class laughed and scattered, and no one listened to the activity he was about to propose.

He saw Hsiao-Yun sitting alone in the last row, eating her lunch box with her head down.

He saw An-An shoving her sketchbook into the deepest part of her drawer.

He saw the empty space behind the playground — the place where a slide should have stood.

These images flashed by, one after another.

Then, the fog lifted.

"Ahh—"

Lin Che collapsed onto his knees on the track, gasping for air.

Tears fell onto the red track, one by one.

"I remember now..." his voice was hoarse. "I remember everything."

"He said I was quiet."

"He said, leave it to the kids who run fast."

The playground went so silent that only his breathing remained.

The fifty-two children quietly gathered around.

"It was that sentence." Ching-Ching's voice was soft. "The branching point was that sentence."

"On our side, Coach Liu said, 'I think you can do it,'" B Lin Che said. "On your side, Coach Liu said, 'Leave it to the kids who run fast.'"

"The same day, the same place, the same person," Xiao-Yang picked up. "But the words that came out were different."

"So the two worlds split apart from that one sentence," Hsiao-Yun said.

The children looked at one another. The answer was so simple, and so heavy.

"Coach Liu..." Lin Che said quietly. "Did he do it on purpose? Did he think I couldn't do it?"

"He didn't do it on purpose." B Lin Che walked over and crouched beside him. "In that world, he just saw that version of you."

"He was afraid you'd get hurt. He was actually protecting you."

Lin Che looked up.

"On our side, Coach Liu saw a 'me who wanted to try.' So he gave me a push," B Lin Che said. "On your side, he probably saw a 'very nervous me.' So he pulled me back."

"Both sentences were meant to be kind. It's just that the results were completely different."

Lin Che lowered his head, and the tears fell again.

But this time, he felt something inside him loosen.

"I understand now," he said. "That sentence wasn't my fault. And it wasn't Coach Liu's fault."

"It was just... one of the possibilities."

Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote the final line:

"Branching point confirmed: PE class, the third day of school. The sentence Coach Liu said to Lin Che."

"Now the question is," Hsiao-Yun pushed up her glasses, "now that we know the branching point, what next?"

"How do we 'fix' the two worlds back?"

No one knew the answer.

The sea breeze blew over. Under the street lamps, the children's shadows swayed.

"Maybe..." Lin Che suddenly spoke. His voice was still hoarse, but firm.

"Maybe we should go back to that day."

Everyone looked at him.

"Back to the moment before Coach Liu opened his mouth." Lin Che stood up and looked toward the mirror. "Not to change it. But to... face it."

Ching-Ching's eyes lit up.

"A re-enactment," she said. "If we re-stage that day at the branching point — will the mirror take us back to that moment?"

"It's possible," Hsiao-Yun said. "The mirror responds to us. It's always been responding to us."

"Then what are we waiting for?" Xiao-Yang rolled up his sleeves. "Let's race!"

Suddenly, all the children were filled with energy.

The fifty-two of them gathered toward the center of the playground.

Just then, A-Da suddenly pointed behind the playground: "Look!"

On the empty lot, the shadow of the slide had almost become a real slide in the moonlight.

A-Da picked up a pebble and threw it.

"Clang."

The stone struck the shadow and made a sound.

As if it had hit real sheet metal.

All the children froze.

"It's almost here..." A-Da's voice trembled.

"We have to hurry too," Ching-Ching said.

She looked down at her watch.

"9:50."

The fifty-two children stood on the moonlit playground.

The cracks on the wall, like silver vines, were crawling from the auditorium toward the playground.

And above their heads, the giant clock was silently marching toward midnight.
