# Chapter 21 — The Swap

11:58 p.m. Da-ge's words still echoed through the auditorium.

"Swap?" A-Da's eyes went wide, looking through the silver mist at the face identical to his own.

"Swap." Da-ge smiled. "You and I have the same brain. In my world, you'll do even better than me. And in your world, I'll be needed too."

"But..." A-Da's voice trembled. "In your world, everyone likes you. Why would you..."

"Because," Da-ge said, "I want to know if I can still make people laugh once I've left the applause behind."

He paused: "And I want to help your world. I want the people there to know that Wu Da's ideas are worth listening to."

A-Da's tears fell.

"You idiot..." he laughed, tears streaming down. "You really are... the most handsome me in the whole world."

Da-ge laughed loudly: "Of course! We have the same face!"

All the children laughed. And as they laughed, their eyes reddened.

"Okay." A-Da wiped his tears. "I'll swap with you."

He turned to face his twenty-six World A classmates.

"Everyone," he said. "I'm leaving."

No one spoke.

A-Da looked at them one by one. Hsiao-Mei, Iron Ox, Tzu-Han, A-Bao...

"A-Bao." He suddenly said. "From now on, my seat is yours to look after. The marbles in my desk drawer are all yours."

A-Bao's eyes instantly reddened.

"Wh-who wants your marbles!" he shouted, but his voice shook uncontrollably.

"And you, Hsiao-Mei." A-Da said. "Next time you cry, remember — the number one joke master A-Da may be gone, but the jokes will stay."

Hsiao-Mei burst into tears with a loud "waah."

"A-Da..." Tzu-Han said quietly. "I'm sorry. I said your proposal was boring last time..."

"Why are you apologizing!" A-Da laughed. "You guys! Only now you realize you'll miss me!"

The children gathered around.

Ching-Ching walked up to him.

The two of them — one the most serious person in class, the other the loudest.

"Wu Da," Ching-Ching said.

"What?" A-Da puffed out his chest. "Going to scold me? It's your last chance!"

Ching-Ching looked at him. Her eyes were red.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I always told you to be quiet."

A-Da froze.

"Your ideas were actually really good," Ching-Ching said. "I was in too much of a hurry to keep order, and forgot to listen to you."

Tears slid down A-Da's face.

"You..." his voice choked. "I've been waiting six years for you to say that."

Ching-Ching's tears fell too.

In the dim auditorium, the two of them hugged tightly.

Finally, Lin Che walked over.

A-Da looked at him and suddenly smiled.

"Quiet ghost," he said. "I'm leaving it to you."

"Leaving what to me?" Lin Che asked.

"Everything," A-Da said. "From now on, you take care of the watching. I'll take care of... being watched, in the other world."

He paused: "But don't worry, I'll miss you a lot. When I miss you, I'll go pick strawberries at the farm."

"Then what about me?" Lin Che said. "What do I do when I miss you?"

"You watch the birds." A-Da grinned. "The way the white-eyes flies past — that's me saying hi."

Lin Che's eyes grew hot.

He held out his hand.

A-Da grasped it.

"11:59," Ching-Ching announced. Her voice trembled.

"Go, quick!" the children shouted.

A-Da turned to face the mirror.

On the other side of the mirror, Da-ge turned too.

The two of them reached out their hands at the same time.

Stepped forward at the same time.

In the silver mist, two identical figures walked toward each other.

One step. Two steps.

The instant they brushed past each other—

Two hands smacked together in midair.

Smack!

Silvery-white light burst from their palms, illuminating the whole auditorium.

So bright that everyone shut their eyes.

Then, the light slowly dimmed.

The children opened their eyes.

In front of the mirror stood a person.

The same hair, the same uniform, the same expression — a little nervous, and very brave.

It was Da-ge.

The Da-ge of World B.

"Hi," he said. "I'm here."

The auditorium went quiet for a second.

Then A-Bao charged over and hugged him.

"Welcome! Welcome to our class!" he bawled. "You're our Da-ge now!"

The children swarmed over.

And on the other side of the mirror—

In the World B auditorium, A-Da stood before the silver mist.

The World B children watched him quietly.

A-Da was a little nervous. He scratched his head, about to say something—

"Da-ge!" B A-Bao was the first to shout.

Then, twenty-six voices shouted together:

"Welcome home, Da-ge!"

A-Da froze where he stood.

Tears poured down like a faucet that couldn't be turned off.

"I..." he choked. "I'm home."

In both auditoriums, cries and laughter rang out.

Lin Che stood before the World A mirror, looking across through the thin silver mist.

Across the way, B Lin Che stood there too.

The two Lin Ches, face to face, separated by a mirror growing dimmer and dimmer.

"One minute left," B Lin Che said. His voice came softly through the mist.

"Mm." Lin Che nodded.

"Are you scared?" B Lin Che asked.

"I was at first," Lin Che said. "But not anymore."

"Why not?"

"Because I know that whether the mirror closes or not," Lin Che smiled, "you'll still be here. In here."

He pointed at his chest.

B Lin Che smiled too. He also pointed at his chest.

"Same here."

"Hey." Lin Che suddenly said. "One last thing."

"Say it."

"Next PE class," Lin Che said, "remember — you already run really well."

B Lin Che's eyes reddened.

"You too," he said. "Next time a bird flies past your window, remember to wave at it."

The two boys, separated by a silver slit growing narrower and narrower, both smiled.

"One last minute," B Lin Che said.

"Then let's take our time saying it," Lin Che said.

The mirror's light was now only a narrow slit.

11:59 p.m.

And on both sides of the slit, all fifty-two children crowded close.

The final farewell had begun.
