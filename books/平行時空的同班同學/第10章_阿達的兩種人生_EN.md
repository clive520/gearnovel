# Chapter 10 — A-Da's Two Lives

Twelve noon. The four from World A passed through the mirror once more.

The World B campus was filled with the smell of lunch.

"We've been waiting for you!" Xiao-Yang waved at the school gate. "It's curry today!"

On the lawn, the World B children had spread out a huge picnic mat, with lunch boxes lined up in a row.

Hsiao-Yun stood outside the crowd, clutching her backpack straps, hesitating to go over.

"Hsiao-Yun! Over here!" the ponytail girl waved.

A Hsiao-Yun walked over step by step and sat down beside B Hsiao-Yun.

Lunch boxes opened one by one. B Hsiao-Yun's friends rushed to put food in her box and pour her juice.

"Try this!" "My mom made this!" "Hey, that chicken leg is mine!"

A Hsiao-Yun held her own lunch box, picked up a small bite of rice, and slowly put it in her mouth.

She suddenly lowered her head. Her shoulders trembled slightly.

"What's wrong?" B Hsiao-Yun was startled.

"Nothing." A Hsiao-Yun's voice was muffled. "It's just... so this is what it feels like to eat together."

She looked up. Her eyes were wet, but she was smiling.

"It's so good," she said.

Everyone laughed.

Not far away, A-Da sat alone, chewing on a piece of bread, lost in thought.

Lin Che walked over and sat down next to him.

"No lunch box?" Lin Che asked.

"Mom didn't have time this morning. She just gave me a hundred bucks." A-Da shrugged. "I'm not that hungry anyway."

Looking at the crowd on the lawn, he suddenly said: "Hey, don't you think Da-ge is really impressive?"

"Impressive?"

"Look." A-Da jutted his chin. "Just now Xiao-Yang said he wanted to hold some 'last day assembly,' and Da-ge immediately figured out the groups, the activities, the whole schedule."

On the lawn, Da-ge stood at the center of the crowd, waving a sheet of paper and loudly handing out tasks. The kids around him kept nodding, and some were even taking notes.

"In our class, I'd do the same thing." A-Da's voice dropped. "I'd make the groups too. I'd think up activities too. But—"

"But everyone just laughs." He smiled, an ugly smile. "And says: there goes A-Da clowning around again."

Lin Che opened his mouth, but didn't know what to say.

Because he knew what A-Da said was true.

"You know what?" A-Da suddenly said. "I actually wasn't joking."

"What?"

"About the mirror." A-Da looked into the distance. "I'd seen you before we ever found the mirror."

Lin Che froze.

"One day last week, after school, I went behind the auditorium to pick up a marble, and I saw you in the old auditorium, talking to a person who looked exactly like you."

"I thought it was a ghost and ran off scared. Thinking back on it, that was probably..."

"The world inside the mirror." Lin Che finished, his voice soft.

"Yeah." A-Da scratched his head. "So I always knew you weren't just some quiet ghost. You're just... someone who hasn't been seen yet."

Something gently bumped into Lin Che's heart.

"You're not just some clown either," Lin Che said.

A-Da turned to look at him.

"I mean," Lin Che stammered, "your ideas are actually really good. It's just... the kids in our class are too used to treating you as the funny A-Da."

A-Da stared at him blankly, then burst out laughing with a "pfft."

"Thanks." He thumped Lin Che on the shoulder. "Man, you barely talk, but when you do, it hits the spot."

Just then, Da-ge walked over toward them.

"Hey, what are you two doing hiding over here?" Da-ge plopped down. "Come on, I need hands."

He spread out the paper in his hand, covered in messy flowcharts.

"For the last day, we want to hold a 'Two-Worlds Class Reunion.'" Da-ge's eyes sparkled. "Both classes play games together, sing together, exchange gifts. You guys have to join in too!"

"That sounds awesome!" A-Da blurted out, then shrank back a little. "But... why would you listen to my opinions?"

"Why wouldn't we?" Da-ge raised an eyebrow. "You and I have the same brain. Your ideas are my ideas."

A-Da was stunned.

"I just got lucky," Da-ge shrugged. "I found a group of people willing to listen to me talk. That's all."

"That's all" — those two words dropped into A-Da's heart like a little pebble, sending out ring after ring of ripples.

"Then..." A-Da took a deep breath and suddenly stood up. "I think the reunion should have a 'Two-Worlds Card Exchange' activity!"

Da-ge's eyes lit up: "Card exchange? Go on!"

A-Da started talking. He stuttered a bit at first, but talked faster and faster, his hands beginning to gesture.

Da-ge listened seriously, nodding at the key points and taking notes.

Lin Che sat nearby, watching.

He suddenly felt that A-Da's shoulders seemed to have straightened a little.

One o'clock in the afternoon, by the World B farm.

Da-ge picked a few strawberries from the field and pressed them into the three visitors' hands. The one he gave A-Da was especially big.

"Farm manager's privilege." Da-ge winked. "Our strawberries are extra sweet."

A-Da put the strawberry in his pocket. He couldn't bear to eat it.

"I'll eat it after I get back," he said.

Suddenly, a commotion came from the direction of the farm.

"The strawberries! The strawberries are gone!"

Everyone ran over. On the field ridges, several strawberry plants... had vanished.

Not pulled out. The whole plants, soil and all, were gone. In their place remained only a small patch of clean, bare cement.

"The overwriting..." B Ching-Ching's voice trembled. "It's starting here too."

The children gathered around that patch of cement and fell quiet.

"Over in World A, it's all cement there," Lin Che said softly.

Everyone looked up at him.

"So this place is turning into our side," he said.

"And our side is turning into this place," Ching-Ching picked up.

"The two worlds are eating each other."

No one spoke. The sea breeze swept across the farm, swaying the few remaining strawberry plants.

Two o'clock. Time to head back.

The four of them walked to the mirror.

A-Da was second-to-last to go in. He glanced back at World B.

On the lawn, Da-ge was still discussing the reunion with his classmates. They surrounded him, listening, eyes bright.

A-Da reached into his pocket and gripped the strawberry.

The strawberry was cool. But his palm was warm.

"If only..." he whispered.

"A-Da!" Ching-Ching called from the other side of the mirror.

"Coming!"

He took a deep breath and stepped into the mirror.

That night, A-Da sat at his desk and placed the strawberry under his desk lamp.

The strawberry was very red. Red like a tiny sun.

He didn't eat it. He just looked at it.

On the desk lay a note from his mom: "Dad and Mom will be late tonight. There's a lunch box in the fridge."

A-Da folded the note into a tiny paper airplane and placed it beside the strawberry.

Then, on the last page of his homework notebook, he wrote three words.

"I want to stay."

When he finished, he stared at those three words for a long, long time.

Finally, he gently tore that page out.

Crumpled it into a ball, and tossed it into the trash can.

But those three words had already taken root in his heart, like seeds.
