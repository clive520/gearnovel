# Chapter 05 — The Two Lin Ches

September 20th, Thursday.

During morning self-study, Lin Che kept squeezing the slip of paper in his pocket. His fingers had already rubbed it soft.

Math class. The teacher was explaining dividing fractions. Lin Che's eyes looked at the blackboard, but his mind was counting the clock's second hand.

School dismissed. Four o'clock. One more afternoon.

He had never felt that a day of classes was so long.

During the noon cleaning period, Ching-Ching walked over carrying a mop.

"You're going in alone today?" she asked, eyes on the floor, her tone as casual as if she were discussing what they had for lunch.

"...Yes." Lin Che hesitated. "How did you know?"

"The Lin Che from World B slipped you a note yesterday." Ching-Ching looked up. "I saw it. And you've been out of it all day — too easy to guess."

Lin Che had nothing to say to that.

Ching-Ching pressed a page torn from her notebook into his hand. It was covered in the rules she had compiled.

"Be back before 4:20," she said. "And—"

"Don't touch 'the other me,'" Lin Che finished.

"As long as you know." Ching-Ching nodded and walked off with her mop.

A-Da came running from the other end of the hallway and shouted right into Lin Che's ear: "If anything dangerous happens, yell! We'll be waiting outside for you!"

"Keep it down," Lin Che jumped, but couldn't help smiling.

4:05 p.m. In the old auditorium, the mirror lit up on time.

The silvery-white light rippled outward in rings, as if waiting for him.

Lin Che stood before the mirror alone. He took a deep breath, closed his eyes, and stepped forward.

Coolness. Ringing in his ears. Then all sound vanished.

When he opened his eyes again, he was in the old auditorium of World B.

And B Lin Che was standing right in front of the mirror, smiling at him.

"You really came."

"You asked me to, didn't you?" Lin Che said quietly.

B Lin Che laughed and led him out of the auditorium.

Haisheng Elementary in this world seemed to be draped in a warm filter. The sunset light was softer than theirs, the salt in the wind fainter, and even the flowers by the wall bloomed larger.

When they passed the slide behind the playground, B Lin Che suddenly hopped on and patted the spot next to him: "Sit."

Lin Che hesitated, then climbed up and settled in.

Two identical boys sat side by side on the blue slide, feet dangling in the air, swinging.

"You all looked shocked when you saw the slide yesterday," B Lin Che said. "So... over where you're from, there's no slide here, right?"

"Mm." Lin Che nodded. "Over there, it's just an empty lot."

"Over here, everything is still here," B Lin Che said.

Lin Che said nothing. He thought of Ching-Ching's notes: both worlds' memories were starting to "not match up."

"So what is it you wanted to tell me?" Lin Che asked.

B Lin Che looked toward the distant sea and was quiet for a while.

"The third day of school. PE class." He began. "Our class was picking the anchor leg for the relay."

"The whole class looked down. Nobody dared take it. Me neither. I was always... very quiet."

Lin Che froze: "You? Quiet?"

B Lin Che laughed: "Sounds unbelievable, right? But before that day, I was just like you. Sat by the window, never talked, never raised my hand — like a shadow in the classroom."

"Then what?" Lin Che's voice tightened.

"Then Coach Liu walked up to me." B Lin Che said. "He asked: 'Don't you want to give it a try? I think you can do it.'"

Lin Che's heart thumped hard.

That sentence... he'd heard it before. In dreams. In front of the old auditorium's mirror. On that day he couldn't remember.

"I took the anchor leg," B Lin Che went on. "I won. The whole class lifted me up. After that day, I seemed to become a different person."

"Not become," B Lin Che turned to look at him, eyes bright. "It was finally daring to be myself."

Lin Che lowered his head and looked at his hands.

"Did your class pick an anchor leg that day too?" B Lin Che asked.

"I think so." Lin Che said. "But... I can't remember."

"Can't remember?"

"Mm." Lin Che's voice grew softer. "I only remember the sunshine was nice, and Coach Liu blowing his whistle. Everything else... is fog."

B Lin Che looked at him, then suddenly reached out and took his hand.

"Maybe you just forgot. Not that it never happened."

The moment their two hands touched—

The world lit up.

Silvery-white light burst from between their hands.

Then, memories crashed in like ocean waves.

Lin Che saw it.

He saw the other end of the red track under the sun, the relay baton rolling to his feet. He saw himself bend down to grab it, and then — run.

The wind roared in his ears. The whole class screamed from the sidelines: "Lin Che! Lin Che! Lin Che!"

He saw the finish line. He saw himself sprint across it first. He saw himself being tossed into the sky by his classmates. The sky was so blue it gleamed.

Those were B Lin Che's memories.

Warm, sparkling memories, belonging to another him.

The scene shifted.

The same playground, the same sunshine. But the colors seemed a little paler.

He saw himself standing by the track. Head down. Fist clenched, then released.

Coach Liu walked up to him. His mouth was moving, speaking.

But the sound was like it came through a thick layer of water. Not a single word was clear.

Lin Che strained desperately to hear, tried to move toward the teacher, but his feet were nailed to the ground — he couldn't budge.

Just before the image shattered, he thought he saw Coach Liu's mouth form three words.

Three words.

What three words?

The next second, he came to.

"Ah—"

Lin Che flung himself backward, pulling his hand free of B Lin Che's. Both of them collapsed to the ground, gasping for air.

"Did you... see it too?" B Lin Che's voice trembled.

Lin Che nodded. His back was soaked with sweat.

"I saw you win," Lin Che said. "The class tossed you up. The sky was so blue."

B Lin Che was silent for a long time.

"I saw you standing by the track," he said slowly. "Coach Liu was talking to you. But... I couldn't hear what he said either."

"In your memory, that day has a hole in it." B Lin Che looked at him, concern in his eyes.

Lin Che lowered his head and said very softly: "Yeah."

The two of them fell silent again for a while.

"It's okay." B Lin Che suddenly stood up, dusted off his pants, and reached out his hand — but halfway through, he pulled it back and gave a thumbs-up instead.

"We'll find the answer." He smiled. "The two of us, together."

Lin Che looked at him.

Suddenly, he felt like he wasn't quite so envious anymore. Or rather — it wasn't that he was "not envious." It was that something else had joined the envy.

He couldn't quite say what it was.

"It's almost 4:20," was all he managed to say in the end.

B Lin Che walked him back to the auditorium.

Before the mirror, the two of them looked at each other.

"Tomorrow, I'll introduce you to everyone in our class," B Lin Che said.

"Okay."

Lin Che turned and walked into the silvery-white light.

When he returned to his own world, Ching-Ching and A-Da were indeed waiting outside the iron gate.

"One minute late!" A-Da rushed over. "We thought the mirror ate you!"

"The important thing is you got back safe." Ching-Ching looked at his face and frowned. "You're really pale. What happened?"

Lin Che smiled a little: "I think I'm... starting to remember."

On the way home, the sunset dyed the sea gold.

Lin Che walked alone, the three words spinning in his head.

Coach Liu's mouth. Three words. What could they be?

When he reached the convenience store by the school gate, he instinctively looked up.

At that exact second, the green 7-ELEVEN sign flickered.

In that flicker, Lin Che saw it clearly — the sign had turned blue, and on it were four words:

"Haisheng Bookstore."

Just for an instant, and then it was back to normal.

Lin Che stood rooted to the spot, his heart pounding like a drum.

Today was the fourth day.

And the world was starting to go wrong.
