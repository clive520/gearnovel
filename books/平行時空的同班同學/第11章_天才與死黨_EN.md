# Chapter 11 — The Genius and Her Best Friends

2:10 in the afternoon. The investigation headquarters was set up in the World B classroom.

The two-class investigation team split into three groups: Ching-Ching and B Ching-Ching handled interviews; Xiao-Yang and A-Da hunted for clues around the campus; and Hsiao-Yun... all by herself, handled putting every clue together.

The blackboard was covered in sticky notes. Each one was a "difference between the two worlds."

Hsiao-Yun stood before the blackboard, her eyes behind her glasses frighteningly focused.

She did one thing: she rearranged the sticky notes by "time."

"Everyone, help me remember," she said. "When was the earliest time you felt 'this world is strange'?"

The interviews began, one by one.

B A-Bao: "I remember not long after school started, Lin Che suddenly got super popular. Everyone said he could run really fast."

A Hsiao-Mei: "Our Lin Che? He's always been really quiet."

B Student: "The PE class on the third day of school — the whole class went crazy! Lin Che ran the anchor leg and won!"

A Student: "PE class? The third day? Wasn't that just picking runners? Nothing special."

Hsiao-Yun paced back and forth before the blackboard, writing down every sentence she heard on sticky notes.

"The earliest 'differences' you all remember land on the exact same time." She pushed up her glasses.

"The first week of school. And specifically — PE class."

"Wait." Ching-Ching stood up. "You mean before that, the two worlds were completely identical?"

"Yes." Hsiao-Yun nodded. "If the two worlds branched apart at some moment, then everyone's memories before that moment should be exactly the same."

"We just have to find the 'first memory that differs,' and we'll find the branching point."

Xiao-Yang clapped: "A genius is a genius!"

B Hsiao-Yun's friends cheered from the side too: "Go Hsiao-Yun!" "Hsiao-Yun is our brain!"

A ponytail girl ran out of the classroom and came back carrying two bottles of juice.

"Here!" She stuffed one into B Hsiao-Yun's hands and the other into A Hsiao-Yun's. "You're Hsiao-Yun too, so you get one as well!"

A Hsiao-Yun took the juice and froze for a few seconds.

"...Thank you," she said quietly, her ears turning red.

Leaning against the door, Lin Che watched this scene, his heart feeling warm.

Suddenly, he heard Hsiao-Yun call his name.

"Lin Che." Hsiao-Yun walked over and stared at him. "That PE class day — do you remember what you did?"

Lin Che's heart skipped a beat.

"I... don't remember."

"Not at all?" Hsiao-Yun's eyes were like two small mirrors.

Lin Che shook his head.

"But the you in World B," Hsiao-Yun flipped through her interview notes, "remembers it perfectly clearly. He remembers taking the anchor leg, winning, being tossed into the air."

"All twenty-six people's memories line up. Only you—" She looked at him. "Only your memory is broken."

The classroom went quiet.

Lin Che suddenly felt everyone's gaze land on him.

"So," Hsiao-Yun's voice was soft, but it hammered into his ears, "the branching point isn't just on that day — it's also... connected to you."

Lin Che opened his mouth, but nothing came out.

Instinctively, he touched his head. The fog seemed to have thinned a little more.

The playground. The whistle. The warmth of the sun.

Then, blank.

"It's okay." B Lin Che suddenly walked over and patted his shoulder — but stopped halfway, changing it to a "you can do it" fist in the air.

"We'll remember it together," he said. "Two of me added together have to be stronger than one, right?"

Lin Che looked at him and slowly nodded.

The investigation target was set.

Ching-Ching wrote on the blackboard: "Target: PE class, the third day of school."

Hsiao-Yun added below it: "The first memory that differs."

Everyone looked at the blackboard, and suddenly felt one step closer to the answer.

Just then, someone at the back of the classroom gasped.

"The bulletin board!"

Everyone turned.

On the bulletin board, An-An's drawings were "changing channels" one by one. One second it was a watercolor landscape; the next, it became... an ordinary notice.

Like someone flipping channels with a remote control, frame by frame.

A few seconds later, the bulletin board returned to normal. All the drawings were there, not one missing.

"The overwriting... has even entered the classroom," B Ching-Ching's face went pale.

The children gathered around the bulletin board and stayed quiet for a long time.

Finally, Xiao-Yang spoke. His voice wasn't as loud as usual.

"We need to move faster."

Four o'clock in the afternoon. In front of the mirror.

The investigation team prepared to return to World A. Today's progress: the branching point was located.

Before leaving, B Hsiao-Yun slipped a small note to A Hsiao-Yun.

The note read: "Tomorrow is the last day. Let's solve the mystery together."

Below it were two little stick figures holding hands — one with a ponytail, one with round glasses.

A Hsiao-Yun folded the note and tucked it into the deepest part of her pocket.

She glanced back at World B. The group of best friends were waving at her.

She didn't wave back. She just said to herself, very quietly:

"I'm... someone with friends now."

Then she stepped into the mirror.

Back in World A. The four of them walked out of the auditorium.

Lin Che suddenly stopped.

"You guys go ahead. I want to be alone for a bit."

Ching-Ching glanced at him and nodded. A-Da patted his back. Hsiao-Yun pushed up her glasses.

The three of them left. Lin Che was the only one left on campus.

He walked to the edge of the playground, crouched down, and touched the red running track.

The sunset dyed the playground orange. The sea breeze blew in waves.

"The third day of school..." he murmured.

He tried hard to remember. The images of that day flickered like shadows in fog.

The whistle.

The sun.

A crowd of people with their heads down.

Then, an adult walked toward him. Mouth opening and closing.

Three words.

"..." Lin Che closed his eyes.

The three words were still inaudible.

But this time, his head didn't hurt.

He stood up and brushed the dirt off his knees.

"Tomorrow," he said to the empty playground, "tomorrow, I will definitely remember."

The sea breeze carried those words toward the old auditorium.

The cracks on the wall flickered, as if answering him.
