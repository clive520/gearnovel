# Chapter 13 — The Overwriting Strikes

Seven o'clock in the evening. The children of Haisheng Elementary had all gone back to their homes.

But on this night, not one of them could sleep.

Lin Che sat at his desk, staring at the little mirror he'd drawn in the corner of his textbook.

Suddenly, that "drawn mirror" flickered.

Silvery-white light, flashing once and gone. Like a real mirror.

Lin Che rubbed his eyes. The textbook was still just a textbook. But his heart was pounding frighteningly fast.

At the same time, in Ching-Ching's room.

Ching-Ching was flipping open her notebook, reviewing today's records.

Suddenly, she froze.

The writing in her notebook had changed.

"Day six, the mirror opened early and closed late" — the handwriting of that line slowly, slowly turned into another handwriting.

It was B Ching-Ching's handwriting.

A few seconds later, the writing changed back.

Ching-Ching's hand trembled slightly. She closed the notebook, then opened it again. The writing had already returned to normal.

In A-Da's room.

A-Da lay in bed, looking at the strawberry on his desk. Under the desk lamp, it glowed red like a tiny sun.

Suddenly, on the wall across from him, a row of shadows appeared.

Many, many shadows — like cards, like letters, like classmates' raised hands.

One flash, and they were gone.

A-Da sat up in bed, staring at the blank wall.

In Hsiao-Yun's room.

Hsiao-Yun was looking at the note B Hsiao-Yun had given her. Two little stick figures holding hands — one with a ponytail, one with round glasses.

Suddenly, beside her desk, the shadow of a chair appeared.

As if someone had just sat down to keep her company while she read.

Then, the shadow vanished.

Hsiao-Yun reached out and gently touched that spot. Nothing.

The group chat buzzed.

Ching-Ching: "Everyone... did anything strange happen at your homes?"

Lin Che: "Yes."

A-Da: "Yes!!"

Hsiao-Yun: "Yes."

An-An: "I... the drawings in my sketchbook just moved on their own."

Ching-Ching: "The overwriting isn't just at school anymore. It's in every corner."

A-Da: "Then what do we do?"

Ching-Ching: "Come back to school. We need everyone."

Eight o'clock at night. On the Haisheng Elementary playground, flashlight after flashlight flicked on.

All twenty-six children of Class 6-1 had come. Some by bike, some running, some dashing out in slippers.

The mirror in the old auditorium was wide open. Its silvery-white light made the auditorium bright as day.

On the other side of the mirror, the twenty-six children of World B had all arrived too.

Fifty-two people, gathered on the final night, across a single mirror.

"We've all seen it," B Ching-Ching said. "Our houses and streets here are changing too."

"So," Xiao-Yang's voice had lost its usual booming laugh, but it was steady, "tonight, all fifty-two of us will solve this mystery together. No one is allowed to give up."

"No one gives up!" the children shouted together.

A-Bao suddenly walked up to B A-Bao.

"I'm sorry," he said. "On Saturday, we were the ones who called you fakes first."

B A-Bao blinked and scratched his head: "I was wrong too. I'm the one who laughed at you for not having a bookstore."

The two of them glanced at each other, then suddenly laughed at the same time.

"Even."

On the playground, the children split into three groups.

Group One, led by Ching-Ching and B Ching-Ching: map out every "overwritten" place in both worlds.

Group Two, led by Hsiao-Yun and B Hsiao-Yun: line up all the interview records by time.

Group Three, led by Xiao-Yang and A-Da: check every corner of the campus to see where else things were changing.

As for Lin Che, B Lin Che pulled him over to the red running track at the edge of the playground.

"Come," B Lin Che said. "Sit here."

Lin Che sat at the edge of the track. At night, the playground was half-lit by the street lamps.

B Lin Che stood before him and took a deep breath.

"I'll tell you that day, from beginning to end."

"The third day of school, PE class. Coach Liu blew his whistle and called everyone together to pick the relay team."

Lin Che's heartbeat began to speed up.

"The whole class looked down. Nobody dared take the anchor leg. Including me. I stood at the very back of the line, head bowed."

"Then, Coach Liu saw me."

Lin Che's hand unconsciously gripped the track.

"He walked up to me and said—"

B Lin Che's voice came as if from far away:

"Don't you want to give it a try? I think you can do it."

Lin Che's head suddenly began to ache fiercely.

Fog. Such thick fog. He tried desperately to push it aside, but inside the fog, nothing was visible.

"Then what..." his voice trembled.

"Then I took the anchor leg," B Lin Che said. "I ran. The wind was so strong. The whole class was shouting my name."

Lin Che closed his eyes. Those images seemed to flicker, rising up behind the fog.

The red track. The white finish line. A sea of waving hands.

But those were B Lin Che's images. Not his.

He opened his eyes and shook his head.

"Still no good..." his voice was full of frustration. "I still can't remember my version of that day."

B Lin Che looked at him, then suddenly crouched down to meet his eyes.

"You know what?" B Lin Che said. "Our two memories are separated by a mirror."

"Your memory has a missing piece, and mine has an extra piece. If we put them together—"

He raised his hand and opened his palm.

"Tonight, at the place where the branching happened, let's hold hands once more."

Lin Che looked at that hand.

The last time they held hands, he'd seen B Lin Che's victory.

If tonight, in this place, at the edge of the track—

Maybe the fog would clear.

"Okay," Lin Che said.

He reached out his hand—

Just as the two hands were about to touch, a blinding light erupted from the direction of the auditorium.

"The mirror!" someone screamed.

Everyone looked toward the old auditorium.

The mirror's light was brighter than daylight. The silvery-white glow shot into the sky, and above the playground, it projected an image.

In the image was a giant clock.

The clock's hands were pointing at... eleven.

Then the clock began to move. Tick. Tock.

The hands swept past 11:59.

The next second, the world in the image — the entire world — crumbled away like sand, slowly, slowly.

The fifty-two children tilted their heads up, staring at that image. None of them could say a word.

The clock vanished. The mirror's light returned to its original silvery-white.

"What..." A-Da's voice shook uncontrollably. "What does that mean?"

"The end of the seventh day," Ching-Ching said, her voice more serious than it had ever been. "If we haven't solved the mystery before the seventh day ends—"

"One of the worlds will vanish, just like that."

The playground fell terrifyingly silent.

The sea breeze blew over. Under the street lamps, the children's shadows stretched long, long.

Xiao-Yang looked down at his watch.

"Right now, it's nine o'clock at night."

"Three hours until midnight."

The fifty-two people turned toward the mirror in the old auditorium at the same time.

The final night had begun.
