# Chapter 24 — Classmates from a Parallel World (The Grand Finale)

Time blew forward gently, like a sea breeze.

A few weeks passed. Class 6-1 was no longer quite the same.

The Friday party happened. Da-ge's proposal, with the whole class taking part.

The venue had balloons, games, and a booth Da-ge designed called "Guess Which World This Is."

Ching-Ching was in charge of collecting money, saying with a smile: "This is for building the slide!"

They were going to turn the empty lot behind the playground into a slide.

The blueprint was drawn by An-An, the budget calculated by Hsiao-Yun, and the promotion dreamed up by Da-ge.

Lin Che was in charge of the posters. His handwriting was neat and quiet, and everyone who passed by would stop to look.

On the day of the party, they raised a lot of money. One step closer to the slide.

In class, Lin Che still raised his hand. Sometimes he got it right, sometimes wrong.

When he got it wrong, he wasn't afraid anymore. Because he knew it was okay.

Hsiao-Yun's friends went from three, to five, to eight.

She was still the smartest person in class. But now, her smarts went toward solving puzzles, calculating budgets, and writing invitation cards.

An-An's drawings covered half a wall. In art class, the teacher said one thing to her.

"An-An, your drawings seem to have light in them."

An-An smiled. She wrote that sentence on the cover of her sketchbook.

Ching-Ching was still the class monitor. She still kept order.

But now, after keeping order, she would add: "Alright. Does anyone have something to say?"

And Da-ge would always be the first to raise his hand.

Everything was getting better.

Time passed quickly. In the blink of an eye, it was June.

On graduation day, the sea breeze was especially gentle.

The students of Class 6-1, in their uniforms, sat on the playground.

Behind the playground stood a brand-new blue slide, sparkling.

The younger students were already lining up for it.

"We did it," Ching-Ching said softly.

"We did it," Da-ge said.

The class representative went up to give the graduation speech. That person was Lin Che.

He stood on the stage, looking at the sea of people below.

He thought of that PE class day. He thought of the hand raised halfway.

He took a deep breath.

"Dear teachers and fellow students, hello." He began.

His voice wasn't loud. But it was very clear.

The audience was silent. Everyone was listening.

"In these six years, I've learned many things," he said.

"I learned math, writing essays, and running the relay."

"But most importantly—"

He paused, and smiled.

"I learned to be seen. And I learned to see others."

The applause rolled in like ocean waves.

Lin Che saw Ching-Ching crying, Hsiao-Yun laughing, An-An holding up her camera, and Da-ge clapping hard.

He suddenly thought of another person. The him in the other world.

"Did you see it?" he said in his heart. "I'm shining now too."

After the graduation ceremony, Lin Che walked alone into the old auditorium.

The iron gate wasn't locked. He pushed it open gently.

The mirror still stood on the stage, covered in thick dust.

Lin Che walked up to it and wiped a small patch of dust off with his sleeve.

In the mirror was reflected a boy. He'd grown taller. His shoulders were straight. There was light in his eyes.

"Hey," Lin Che said. "I'm graduating."

The mirror watched him silently.

He took the whale sticker out of his pocket.

The sticker had gone completely transparent, leaving only a very, very faint outline.

He gently stuck it onto the mirror's frame.

"Returning the whale to the sea," he said with a smile.

Then he straightened his collar, ready to leave.

The instant he turned around—

The mirror's surface suddenly rippled.

Very faint. So faint, like a breeze passing over water.

Lin Che whipped his head around.

The mirror still showed his reflection.

But inside that ripple, another image surfaced—

A classroom. A blackboard. A group of children.

Then, in that classroom, a hand went up.

It was B Lin Che.

He stood up and answered a question. The teacher nodded, and his classmates clapped.

Then, he turned his head and smiled toward the mirror.

In that moment, two boys, separated by two worlds, saw each other.

"See you tomorrow, other me," Lin Che whispered.

The ripple faded. The mirror returned to being an ordinary piece of glass.

Lin Che stood there for a while, then turned and walked out of the auditorium.

Outside, the sunlight was blindingly bright. The sea breeze carried the smell of the sea.

On the playground, his classmates waved at him: "Lin Che! Photo time!"

"Coming!" He smiled and ran over.

After the graduation photos, Da-ge returned to the classroom alone.

He walked to his seat and opened the drawer — the drawer that had once belonged to A-Da.

Inside lay a strawberry, quiet and still.

Gray. Like a faded star.

Da-ge gently took it out.

He thought of the boy who'd high-fived him inside the mirror.

"You..." he laughed. "You wouldn't even eat a strawberry."

He walked to the back of the playground, and beside the brand-new slide, he dug a small hole.

He placed the strawberry inside, gently, and covered it with soil.

"Next year, maybe strawberries will grow here," he murmured to himself. "When they bear fruit, I'll come pick one."

He stood up and dusted off his hands.

The sea breeze swept past the slide, making a soft sound, as if saying "okay."

And in that parallel world—

The same June. The same graduation ceremony.

In front of the World B bulletin board stood a boy.

He looked at the card on the board, densely packed with handwriting.

The last line of the card read: "Thank you for letting me be seen."

The boy smiled. He reached out and gently touched the card.

"You're welcome," he said. "You let me be seen too."

On the way from school, he passed the school gate.

The store there was now a 7-ELEVEN. Its green sign glowed in the sunset.

He walked in and bought a bottle of Yakult.

"To A-Bao," he said, raising the bottle gently toward the sunset.

Somewhere out there, the A-Baos of both worlds were sipping their Yakult.

Outside the window, a white-eyes bird flew past.

Two worlds. Two Class 6-1s.

The same blue sky. The same sea breeze.

The same children, growing up.

They might never meet again.

But they will always be—

Classmates from a parallel world.

And the story will always remember them.

Just like the mirror remembers everyone who ever looked into it.

And it remembers that dusk—

When a quiet boy pushed open the door of the old auditorium, and saw another version of himself.

(The End)
