# Chapter 20 — The Farewell Begins

11:55 p.m. The mirror's light had dimmed to the haze of dusk.

"World B, hurry!" Ching-Ching stood by the mirror, shouting. "One at a time, no pushing!"

The children immediately lined up.

Each one, as they passed the mirror, paused and glanced back.

B Ching-Ching walked up to Ching-Ching. The two monitors stood face to face.

"Serious monitor," B Ching-Ching smiled, "remember to smile."

"Carefree monitor," Ching-Ching smiled too, though her eyes were red, "remember to be serious."

The two of them hugged tightly.

Then B Ching-Ching walked into the mirror.

B Hsiao-Yun walked up to Hsiao-Yun and pressed a note into her hand.

Hsiao-Yun looked down — a new note. It had two little stick figures. This time, they were waving.

"Goodbye, my partner," B Hsiao-Yun said.

"Goodbye," Hsiao-Yun said. She didn't cry. But her glasses fogged up.

B An-An and An-An hugged, and held on for a long time.

"Keep drawing," B An-An said.

"Mm." An-An nodded. "I'll draw for the whole world to see."

B A-Bao rushed over and shoved a bottle of Yakult into A-Bao's hands.

"The last one! From our world!" he shouted.

A-Bao caught it, froze for a second, then yelled: "I-I'll drink it all! And keep the empty bottle!"

Everyone laughed. And as they laughed, the tears fell harder.

One by one, the children of World B walked into the mirror.

Xiao-Yang was among the last few. He walked up to Ching-Ching.

"Hey," Xiao-Yang said, "your card — I'm putting it on my headboard."

"Your laugh," Ching-Ching said, "I'll remember it for a long time."

Xiao-Yang smiled. That huge laugh of his — the one that carried through the whole hallway — rang out once more.

Then he, too, walked into the mirror.

Now only B Lin Che and Da-ge remained.

Da-ge walked up to A-Da.

"The card," Da-ge said, his eyes bright. "I got it. I put it up on our class bulletin board."

A-Da opened his mouth.

"Da-ge, I..." His voice caught.

The words he wanted to say spun a hundred circles in his throat, but couldn't come out.

Da-ge looked at him and suddenly opened his arms.

"Let's hug," he said. "Think of it as... a reservation for our next meeting."

A-Da threw himself forward and hugged him tight.

No light. No memory exchange. Only two heartbeats.

Then Da-ge let go, stepped back, and walked into the mirror.

A-Da stood where he was, not moving at all.

B Lin Che was the last one.

He walked up to Lin Che.

Two identical boys, face to face.

"Thank you," B Lin Che said. "Thank you for showing me the power of quiet."

"Thank you," Lin Che said. "Thank you for showing me the courage to shine."

"Neither of us is the 'better one,'" B Lin Che said.

"We are each other's other half," Lin Che said.

The two Lin Ches extended their fists and bumped them lightly.

"Goodbye," B Lin Che said.

"No," Lin Che said. "It's 'see you next time.'"

B Lin Che smiled. He turned and walked into the mirror.

The silver light rippled once. Then it settled.

In the old auditorium, only the twenty-six children of World A remained.

And a mirror growing dimmer and dimmer.

The silence lasted a long time.

"Is it over..." Hsiao-Mei asked quietly.

"Not yet," Ching-Ching said. "The mirror hasn't fully closed. Until it does, we all stay here."

The children sat down in the auditorium. No one wanted to go home.

Lin Che walked over to A-Da. A-Da still stood before the mirror, motionless.

"A-Da?" Lin Che called softly.

A-Da didn't turn around. His eyes were fixed on the blurry silver light in the mirror.

"Lin Che." A-Da's voice was hoarse. "What if... what if I want to go over?"

Lin Che froze.

"Go over where?"

"World B," A-Da said.

The auditorium suddenly fell silent.

Everyone had heard.

"Are you crazy?" A-Bao jumped up. "The mirror's about to close! Once you go over, you can't come back!"

"I know," A-Da said.

He turned around. His eyes were red, but bright.

"I keep thinking," he said, "on our side, I'm the A-Da whose jokes get him told to 'be quiet.'"

"But over there, I'm Da-ge. The Da-ge everyone listens to, everyone trusts, everyone calls 'Da-ge.'"

"I wrote a note once," his voice trembled, "with the words 'I want to stay.' Then I threw it into the trash can."

"Because I thought that was too selfish."

The children listened quietly.

"But just now, when Da-ge hugged me," A-Da said, "I suddenly thought—"

"Maybe staying isn't selfish. Maybe it's brave."

No one spoke.

Ching-Ching stood up and walked over to him.

"A-Da," she said. "Have you really thought this through?"

A-Da looked at her and nodded firmly.

"I've thought it through," he said. "I want to go over."

"It's not because our world is bad." His voice steadied. "It's because there's an empty spot over there."

"A spot named 'Wu Da.' And that spot is me."

A collective gasp rose in the auditorium.

Lin Che looked at A-Da. He remembered the A-Da who'd chased him around the playground, shouting "Lin Che! Lin Che!"

He remembered A-Da saying: "You're just someone who hasn't been seen yet."

He understood now. When A-Da said that, he hadn't only meant Lin Che.

Ching-Ching looked down at her watch.

"11:57."

A-Da walked up to the mirror. The silvery-white light had dimmed to a thin mist.

He slowly raised his hand.

The instant his fingertip touched the mirror's surface—

Inside the mirror, a point of light suddenly lit up.

Like a star flashing once in the fog.

"Da-ge?" A-Da's eyes went wide.

On the other side of the mirror, Da-ge's face appeared in the mist.

He was smiling.

His hand pressed against the mirror too, separated from A-Da's by a thin layer of silver light.

"I heard you," Da-ge said. "The mirror carried your words over."

"So," he smiled, tears glistening in his eyes, "I have a proposal."

Everyone in the auditorium held their breath.

Da-ge's voice came through the silver mist, clear and distinct:

"Let's swap."

"You come to my world, and be seen."

"I go to your world, and help others be seen."

11:58 p.m.

And the mirror's light was still slowly, slowly dimming.
