# Chapter 17 — Back to That Day

On the frozen playground, the sunlight didn't move at all.

The fifty-two children stood there, as if they'd stumbled into a painting.

"What now?" A-Da asked. His voice echoed across the empty playground.

"He can't see us," Ching-Ching pointed at little Lin Che in the scene.

Little Lin Che stood at the back of the line, hand half-raised, eyes on the ground.

"Can't he?" Lin Che suddenly said.

He crouched down, facing little Lin Che directly.

"Hey," he called softly.

Little Lin Che's shoulders twitched.

He slowly, slowly lifted his head.

His eyes looked straight at Lin Che.

He could see.

All the children gasped.

"He... he can see you!" B Ching-Ching whispered.

"Only Lin Che can see Lin Che," Hsiao-Yun said. "Because this is 'Lin Che's day.'"

Little Lin Che's eyes went wide, his mouth a perfect circle.

He looked from A Lin Che to B Lin Che.

"You..." his voice was so small it was barely audible. "Who are you?"

He could hear them!

"We are..." A Lin Che opened his mouth, not knowing how to say it.

"We are you," B Lin Che said.

Little Lin Che froze.

"I'm you, if that day you raised your hand, and the teacher said 'you can do it,'" B Lin Che said.

"I'm you too," A Lin Che said. "I raised my hand. But the teacher had me step back."

Little Lin Che lowered his head and looked at his half-raised hand.

"So..." he said quietly. "This hand ended up going down after all, didn't it?"

A Lin Che didn't answer.

"It did go down," B Lin Che said. "But that doesn't mean you weren't brave."

Little Lin Che looked up, his eyes misty.

Just then, the scene moved.

Coach Liu walked over. One step, two steps.

"Time is moving again!" A-Da shouted.

Coach Liu opened his mouth—

Pop.

The world reset.

The sunshine was the same. The line was the same. Little Lin Che's hand had risen back to halfway.

Coach Liu stood at the front of the line again, as if none of what just happened had ever happened.

"This is..." Ching-Ching's eyes went wide. "A loop?"

"This day keeps repeating," Hsiao-Yun murmured. "Because that day has two possibilities. The world can't choose, so it keeps replaying."

"Then what do we do?" A-Da scratched his head.

The children began brainstorming solutions.

Xiao-Yang dashed up to Coach Liu and yelled: "Teacher! Pick 'you can do it'!"

Coach Liu didn't react at all.

A-Da picked up a stone and threw it at the baton in the scene — the stone passed through and rolled away.

Ching-Ching flipped open her notebook and read the rules out loud at Coach Liu — of course, that didn't work either.

"It's no use." B Lin Che shook his head. "Everything here is memory. We can't change it."

"Then what did we even come here for?" Xiao-Yang sat down in defeat.

The children fell quiet.

Lin Che looked at the little version of himself.

Little him raised his hand halfway once again, hesitating once again.

"Maybe we didn't come here to change anything," Lin Che said softly.

"We came here to 'see.'" He turned around. "And to let him see too."

"See what?" A-Da asked.

"To see that both possibilities are real," Lin Che said. "To see that whichever path he takes, he'll grow up, he'll be loved, he'll be... okay."

The wind seemed to stir.

Even though this wind was a memory's wind, all the children felt it.

"How do we let him see?" B Ching-Ching asked.

Hsiao-Yun thought for a moment: "Two Lin Ches, each tell half. Tell him about your lives. One tells 'after the hand went down,' the other tells 'after running out.'"

"Let him see that both paths go somewhere," she said.

The two Lin Ches exchanged a glance.

Then, together, they walked up to little Lin Che.

Little Lin Che looked up at them. His eyes were like two tiny wells.

B Lin Che spoke first.

"I ran the anchor leg," he said. "The whole class was shouting my name. The wind was strong, and I sprinted across the finish line. From that day on, I became the brightest person in my class."

"But," he paused, "I get tired too. Everyone depends on me. I can't stop. I'm afraid that one day, I won't be able to run anymore."

Little Lin Che listened quietly.

A Lin Che spoke next.

"I didn't run," he said. "I retreated to the very back. I became very quiet, quiet as a shadow."

"But," he took a deep breath, "I also learned how to watch. I saw small things others couldn't see. I found the mirror in the old auditorium. I met Ching-Ching, A-Da, Hsiao-Yun, and An-An."

"Then, I walked all the way here, to you."

Little Lin Che's eyes brightened a little.

"So," the two Lin Ches said at the same time—

"Both paths are good."

The playground went so silent that only the memory's cicadas could be heard.

Little Lin Che looked at his own hand.

The half-raised hand still hung in the air.

"But..." his voice was tiny. "I don't know whether to raise it, or let it go."

"You don't have to know right now," A Lin Che said.

"That day's you only had to be the you of that moment. That's enough," B Lin Che said.

Little Lin Che slowly, slowly lifted his head.

For the first time, his eyes looked directly at the two Lin Ches.

"Then... can I hug you?"

The two Lin Ches froze.

Then they crouched down at the same time and opened their arms.

Little Lin Che threw himself forward.

Three Lin Ches, holding one another.

In that instant—

Silvery-white light bloomed from among the three of them.

The light spread like ripples, sweeping across the entire playground.

The world of the memory began to brighten. So bright it hurt.

The children couldn't help but close their eyes.

When they opened their eyes again—

They were back in the old auditorium.

The mirror was still there. But the cracks on its surface had shrunk.

Only a little. But they had truly shrunk.

"Look!" A-Da rushed to the mirror.

The cracks were slowly, slowly closing toward the center.

Like a wound healing.

"We did it!" Xiao-Yang jumped up.

"Not yet," Hsiao-Yun said.

She pointed at the mirror. The cracks had closed halfway — and stopped.

Inside the mirror, the frozen playground was still there. Little Lin Che's hand was still raised halfway.

"There's one last step," Hsiao-Yun said. "And that step isn't inside the mirror."

"Where is it?" Ching-Ching asked.

Hsiao-Yun turned and looked at the two Lin Ches.

"It's inside yourselves."

The auditorium fell silent.

Ching-Ching looked down at her watch.

"11:20."

The two Lin Ches looked at each other from one step apart.

They both knew Hsiao-Yun was right.

The words not yet spoken, the thing not yet let go—

It was in both of their hearts.

"Next," B Lin Che said, his voice soft, "it's our turn."

Lin Che nodded.

The two identical boys placed their hands over their hearts at the same time.

"There's still a mirror between us," B Lin Che said.

"Tonight, we shatter it," Lin Che said.

The sea breeze slipped through the gap under the auditorium door, making the mirror's surface gleam faintly.

11:20.

The real battle was only now beginning.
