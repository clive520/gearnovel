# Chapter 22 — The Final Minute

11:59 p.m. The slit in the mirror was only as wide as a finger.

The fifty-two children crowded on both sides of the slit.

"Quick, quick, final goodbyes!" Xiao-Yang shouted from the World B side.

Ching-Ching put her ear close to the slit.

B Ching-Ching's voice floated out from the slit: "Hey! Serious monitor!"

"Here!" Ching-Ching called.

"Our class has a mission for you!" B Ching-Ching said.

"Say it!"

"Tomorrow morning, when you walk into the classroom," B Ching-Ching's voice carried a smile, "remember to smile!"

"Mission accepted!" Ching-Ching's tears fell again. But this time, she really smiled.

Hsiao-Yun squeezed up to the slit.

"B Hsiao-Yun!" she called.

"I'm here!" The voice from the other side was small, but very clear.

"I'll keep deducing things," Hsiao-Yun said. "You keep having friends."

"You too," B Hsiao-Yun said. "You have twenty-six friends now."

Hsiao-Yun's glasses fogged up again.

An-An pressed her face to the slit: "B An-An! I've put my sketchbook on top of the drawer now!"

"Me too!" B An-An shouted. "We made a promise — drawing is a gift!"

"Drawing is a gift!" An-An shouted back.

A-Bao held up his Yakult bottle: "B A-Bao! I'll keep the empty bottle!"

"Idiot! If you keep one, keep a full one!" B A-Bao laughed. "Next time, bring a full one!"

"Deal!" A-Bao wiped his tears.

Da-ge — the new Da-ge — stood on the World A side and pressed his face near the mirror.

"Hey," he said.

"Hey." From the other side of the slit came A-Da's voice.

The two Wu Das, who had swapped worlds, smiled through a slit of light.

"We did it," Da-ge said.

"We did it," A-Da said.

"Take good care of my world," Da-ge said.

"You too. Take good care of my world," A-Da said.

The two of them pressed their faces against the mirror's surface at the same time. It was cool.

"See you next time," they said at the same time.

Xiao-Yang's voice suddenly exploded from the slit:

"Everyone—!"

Everyone gathered close.

"For the last time, let's all shout one thing together!" Xiao-Yang shouted. "I'll count down!"

"Three!"

"Two!"

"One!"

Fifty-two voices rang out at the same time:

"We are — classmates from parallel worlds!"

The sound pierced through the slit in the mirror, through two worlds, echoing in two auditoriums.

When the shouting ended, everyone was crying. And everyone was smiling too.

Lin Che and B Lin Che walked up to the mirror last.

They raised their hands at the same time and pressed their palms against the mirror's surface.

Separated by a thin, nearly-vanished layer of silver light, the two hands overlapped.

"See you tomorrow, other me," Lin Che said.

"See you tomorrow," B Lin Che said.

The slit began to close.

The silvery-white light pulsed, pulsed, like a heartbeat.

Slower and slower.

Slower and slower.

Then, it stopped.

Darkness.

Twelve o'clock sharp.

In the old auditorium, it was so quiet you could hear the dust settling.

The children stood where they were. None of them moved.

The mirror was just a mirror now. Covered in dust, no cracks, no light.

"The mirror..." Hsiao-Mei's voice was tiny. "It's closed."

No one answered.

After a long while, A-Bao suddenly burst into tears.

"I-I never got to say goodbye..."

"You did," Ching-Ching said. Her voice was hoarse, but steady. "We all did."

"Will they remember?" Hsiao-Mei asked.

"Yes," Ching-Ching nodded. "Because we won't forget either."

Da-ge walked up in front of everyone. He clicked on his flashlight, shone it on his face, and made a funny face.

"Hey, hey, hey," he grinned, "you've got me now. New jokes, new ideas, new proposals—"

The children looked at him, and slowly, one by one, they smiled.

"That's right." A-Bao wiped his tears. "We still have Da-ge."

"Da-ge is still here!" the children echoed.

Ching-Ching walked to the mirror and touched the old glass.

"Thirty years ago, someone stood here too," she said softly. "Just like us."

"They went home. It's time for us to go home too."

The children stood up one by one and brushed the dust off their clothes.

Some were still crying. Some had already started laughing.

Ching-Ching pushed open the old auditorium's door.

Outside was a September night. The sea breeze blew over — salty, cool.

The empty lot behind the playground was quiet.

The shadow of the slide was gone.

The children walked out of the auditorium and into the night.

Lin Che walked at the very back.

He glanced behind him.

Inside the old auditorium, the mirror stood silently in the darkness.

Like a secret that had been kept for thirty years, finally allowed to rest.

"Thank you," Lin Che whispered.

He turned and walked into the moonlight.

At the school gate, the Haisheng Bookstore sign glowed with a small light in the night.

The blue sign, like a sea.

The children passed it, and everyone looked up at it.

"The bookstore's still here," Hsiao-Mei said.

"Mm." Ching-Ching said. "It won't disappear again."

The sea breeze swept across the empty playground.

12:05 a.m.

The day had ended.

And both worlds were still here, safe and sound.
