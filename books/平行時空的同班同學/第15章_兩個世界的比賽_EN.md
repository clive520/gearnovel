# Chapter 15 — The Race Between Two Worlds

Ten o'clock at night. On the playground, the fifty-two children began to "re-stage that day."

A-Da volunteered to be the host. He climbed onto the podium and used a flashlight as a microphone.

"Ladies and gentlemen of the audience! Welcome to — the Class 6-1 of Two Worlds, Century Grand Re-enactment!"

The children dissolved into laughter. The tense atmosphere loosened a little.

"Order! Order!" A-Da cleared his throat. "Now, please welcome Coach Liu!"

Xiao-Yang jumped out of the crowd. With the whistle Da-ge had contributed hanging around his neck, he imitated Coach Liu's manner and put the whistle in his mouth.

"Tweet—"

The whistle echoed under the night sky. The children laughed even harder.

"No, no, Coach Liu isn't that handsome," B Ching-Ching called out, laughing.

"Handsome or not doesn't matter! What matters is the aura!" Xiao-Yang put his hands on his hips.

"Line up! Class 6-1, line up!" he shouted.

Everyone from both classes ran onto the track.

Fifty-two people formed a crooked, zigzagging line in the moonlight.

"Today, we're picking our relay team." Xiao-Yang imitated Coach Liu's tone — about seventy percent accurate. "The anchor leg — who wants to try?"

The children looked at one another. Then, playing along perfectly, they all lowered their heads.

This scene was exactly like that day.

Lin Che stood at the back of the line. His hand, without him noticing, rose just a little.

Just a little.

"You!" Xiao-Yang's hand pointed at him.

Lin Che froze.

Xiao-Yang strode over, stood in front of him, and imitated Coach Liu's voice:

"Don't you want to give it a try? I think you can do it."

The playground went quiet for a second.

Then, no one knew who shouted first.

"Lin Che! Run!"

"Run! Run!" Fifty-two voices rang out together.

Lin Che looked at them. Under the moonlight, every face sparkled.

He took the baton A-Da handed him — it was a flashlight.

He stepped up to the starting line.

"On your marks—" A-Da yelled. "Go!"

Lin Che ran.

He hadn't run this hard in a long time. The wind rushed past his ears. The nighttime playground flew backward beneath his feet.

Street lamp after street lamp receded. His breathing grew heavier. His heartbeat grew louder.

Along the track, fifty-two children chased after him, shouting as they ran: "Lin Che! Lin Che! Lin Che!"

Lin Che's eyes went wet.

The finish line was a white handkerchief held taut by two kids.

He charged through it.

"He made it!" A-Da jumped up. "Lin Che is first across the line!"

The children swarmed around him. Some patted his back, some grabbed his hands, some lifted him up—

"Wait! Don't touch him!" Ching-Ching shouted from the side, but it was already too late.

Lin Che was tossed into the sky.

He looked at the night sky. So many stars, like little light bulbs.

In that instant, he felt the bird inside him — the one that had folded its wings — stir just a little.

He landed in the children's hands. Laughing, they set him down.

Lin Che stood there, catching his breath.

He smiled. And as he smiled, tears fell again.

"Hey, why are you crying again!" A-Da was startled.

"I don't know." Lin Che wiped his tears, still smiling. "I just felt... so this is what it's like, running with everyone watching you."

The children went quiet for a second, then all laughed.

B Lin Che walked over and stood beside him.

"Pretty cool, right?" B Lin Che said. "The feeling of the whole class shouting your name."

"Pretty cool." Lin Che nodded. "So this is your world."

B Lin Che shook his head: "This isn't just my world."

He pointed at everyone on the playground: "This is your world too. Starting tonight."

Lin Che froze.

Just then, a silvery-white light erupted from the direction of the old auditorium.

"The mirror!" The children turned.

The mirror's light shot into the sky, brighter than the time before.

The silvery-white glow swirled above the playground, like a glowing river.

Then, the light slowly, slowly dimmed.

It returned to its original brightness.

"It... responded," Hsiao-Yun said, both excitement and confusion in her voice. "But it didn't take us anywhere."

"Is something missing?" A-Da asked.

Hsiao-Yun stared at the mirror, her glasses reflecting the moonlight.

"It was brightest at the moment Lin Che crossed the line," she said. "But the light still wasn't enough."

"It wants more." Hsiao-Yun turned around. "It wants 'that day' itself."

"What we re-staged was only 'like that day,'" Ching-Ching continued. "Not 'that day.'"

"Then what do we do?" Xiao-Yang frowned. "We can't really go back to the past."

No one spoke.

Lin Che looked down at the flashlight baton in his hand.

"Maybe..." he said slowly, "we have to bring this re-enactment to the mirror."

Everyone looked at him.

"What happened that day took place on the playground. But the road to that day is inside the mirror," Lin Che said.

"If we act out that day once more, in front of the mirror..."

"The mirror will open the door," Hsiao-Yun finished, her eyes lighting up.

The children looked at one another.

"Then what are we waiting for?" Xiao-Yang yelled. "Everyone, move out!"

The fifty-two children marched into the old auditorium in a grand procession.

In front of the mirror, the children formed a semicircle.

The silvery-white light reflected on every face.

In the mirror were reflected two Class 6-1s. Identical people, identical nervousness, identical hope.

"Come on," B Lin Che said.

He held out his hand.

Lin Che held out his hand.

The two hands clasped together once more.

No pain. No fog.

Only very, very warm light.

The mirror's surface began to ripple.

Just as the ripples spread, the cracks on the wall let out a sudden "crack"—

And crawled another long stretch toward the playground.

"Look!" A-Da shouted.

Where the cracks passed, the ground began showing overlapping images of the two worlds: grass one moment, cement the next; the shadow of a slide one moment, empty land the next.

"The worlds... are overlapping faster now," Ching-Ching's voice trembled.

She looked down at her watch.

"10:30."

"An hour and a half until midnight."

In the mirror, the ripples spread wider and wider.

And at the center of the ripples, an image faintly emerged—

Sunshine. A playground. A crowd of children with their heads down.

That day.

The fifty-two children held their breath.

The mirror had begun to "remember."
