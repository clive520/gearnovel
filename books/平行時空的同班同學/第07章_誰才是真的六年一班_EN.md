# Chapter 07 — Which Is the Real Class 6-1?

September 22nd, Saturday.

At nine in the morning, Lin Che was woken by his phone vibrating.

It was a group chat message. A-Da had sent a sticker, followed by Ching-Ching: "Meet at the school gate at 2 p.m. I found something new."

"Something new?"

Ching-Ching: "I'll tell you when we meet."

At two o'clock, the three of them met on time at the school gate. The school wind ensemble had practice on Saturdays, so the gate was open.

On the way, Ching-Ching pulled a clear plastic folder from her backpack. Inside was a sheet of yellowed paper.

"I had my cousin print this from the county library's old newspaper database," she said.

"A school newsletter column from thirty years ago, titled 'The Seven-Day Mystery of the Auditorium.'"

Lin Che leaned over. Faintly printed on the yellowed paper were a few lines of text.

"That autumn, students said the mirror in the old auditorium glowed. And it did so for seven days in a row. On the eighth day, the auditorium was closed for certain reasons. And after that, it never opened again."

"Seven days..." Lin Che murmured.

"If it's the same this time," Ching-Ching said, "then tomorrow is the last day."

A-Da was unusually quiet, kicking a small stone along behind them.

As they walked through the gate, Lin Che looked up at the 7-Eleven sign. It was still flickering, and even more violently today — the green sign was now turning entirely blue for half a minute at a time.

"Time is running out," Ching-Ching said.

The old auditorium. The three of them pushed open the iron gate.

The mirror was already open.

Silvery-white light flowed across its surface. But it was 2:30 in the afternoon — normally it didn't open until 4:05.

"It's early," Ching-Ching frowned, jotting down the time.

"And the crack has gotten longer," Lin Che pointed at the lower-right corner of the mirror. The silvery-white crack had split into two branches, like a tiny lightning bolt.

The three of them passed through the mirror.

World B. Today, the entire World B class had come.

On the lawn beside the slide, a crowd of children sat or stood around. When they saw the three, everyone waved.

"They're here!" Xiao-Yang shouted.

At first, the mood was great. Everyone chatted, swapped snacks, and compared the differences between the two worlds.

"Is your Yakult the same as ours?" B A-Bao asked.

"It's the same. What else would it be?" A-Da said.

"Oh—" B A-Bao drawled. "But you guys don't even have a bookstore. So sad. Over here, we've got a bookstore, a farm, a slide — everything."

The atmosphere instantly cooled.

"We had a bookstore," Ching-Ching said, her tone hard.

"Had?" B A-Bao shrugged. "If it's not there now, it's not there. Your world is so boring."

A-Da jumped up: "What did you say?!"

"I said the truth." B A-Bao didn't back down. "The back of your school is all bare — you don't even have a slide. What makes you think you're the real Class 6-1?"

"We ARE the real one!" A-Da roared. "You guys are just fakes in a mirror!"

The air froze.

The World B children went quiet, one by one, looking over.

"Who are you calling a fake?" Da-ge stepped forward, his face darkening.

"Y-you guys!" A-Da shouted back. "This whole world is fake!"

Lin Che saw B Lin Che's eyes dim for a moment.

"Enough!" Xiao-Yang strode over and stood in front of A-Da. "Your world is the weird one! So serious and boring — you barely even laugh! There's no way WE branched off from a world like that!"

"Rules are rules!" Ching-Ching stepped forward too, standing face to face with Xiao-Yang. "WE are the real Class 6-1!"

The two class monitors stood thirty centimeters apart, glaring at each other.

The children on both sides split into two camps, tensions at a boiling point.

Before anyone knew it, the sun had begun to tilt.

No one noticed the time.

The argument grew more and more heated. The World B children said World A was boring; the three from World A said World B was fake.

Lin Che tried to break it up, but his voice was too small and was drowned out instantly.

Only B Lin Che never joined the quarrel from beginning to end. He kept looking at Lin Che.

Suddenly, B Ching-Ching shrieked: "The mirror! The mirror is shrinking!"

Everyone turned.

The light leaking from the old auditorium flickered on and off, like a lamp about to go out. The silvery-white ripples were contracting toward the center, ring by ring.

"Go back, quick!" Xiao-Yang yelled.

The three of them turned and dashed toward the auditorium.

Halfway there, A-Da suddenly stopped.

"A-Da!" Ching-Ching shouted back.

A-Da stood where he was, head down.

"Go back for what?" His voice was muffled. "Our side has nothing anyway. Not even a bookstore."

Lin Che and Ching-Ching froze at the same time.

The mirror's light grew dimmer and dimmer. The ripples had nearly shrunk to a single point.

"What nonsense are you saying!" Ching-Ching ran back to pull him.

"I'm not going back!" A-Da shook off her hand, his eyes reddening. "You two go back yourselves! I'm staying! There's a slide here! A bookstore! And... and someone everyone calls Da-ge..."

His voice caught in his throat.

Lin Che looked at him and suddenly understood.

A-Da didn't really want to stay. He just... felt his own world was too lousy.

"A-Da." Lin Che walked over and stood in front of him. "Do you remember?"

"Remember what?"

"The 7-Eleven sign by the gate keeps flashing into a bookstore," Lin Che said. "You say our side has nothing. But if the sign is flashing, it means the bookstore hasn't disappeared."

"It just... hasn't come back yet."

A-Da stared at him blankly.

"And besides," Lin Che's voice was soft but steady, "all three of us are here. If you don't go back, we'll be one person short."

The mirror's light had dimmed almost to nothing.

"Now!" Ching-Ching grabbed A-Da's left hand.

Lin Che grabbed A-Da's right hand.

"Run!" The three of them bolted.

Just one second before the ripples vanished—

The three of them plunged headfirst into the mirror.

Plop.

The old auditorium. World A.

The three of them collapsed to the floor, too breathless to speak.

It was a long time before A-Da spoke. His voice was so small it was barely audible.

"Sorry."

"There's nothing to be sorry for," Ching-Ching said. "If you really wanted to stay, we couldn't have stopped you. But you came back."

She looked at him: "So there must be something in your world that you want to protect."

A-Da kept his head down and said nothing. But the corner of his mouth twitched.

Lin Che turned to look at the mirror.

The mirror's surface had returned to its old, worn look. The crack in the lower-right corner had become three branches, like lightning — or like a thin little tree.

Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote:

"Day six. The mirror opened early and closed late. The crack has split into three."

She closed the notebook, and her voice was soft, yet sounded like an announcement:

"Tomorrow is the seventh day."

The three of them walked out of the auditorium in silence.

Under the sunset, on the empty lot behind the playground, the shadow of the slide was a little darker than yesterday.

Its outline was almost visible now.

"Hey." A-Da suddenly spoke. "Tomorrow, let's take a photo of the 7-Eleven sign."

"Why?"

"Because if the bookstore really comes back tomorrow," A-Da scratched his head, "at least someone should remember that it was once a 7-Eleven."

Ching-Ching looked at him and suddenly smiled.

Lin Che smiled too.

The sea breeze swept across the empty playground, ruffling the shadow of the slide ever so gently.
