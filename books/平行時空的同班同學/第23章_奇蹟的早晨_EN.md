# Chapter 23 — The Morning of Miracles

September 24th, Monday.

The sun rose as always. The bell at Haisheng Elementary rang as always.

But when the children of Class 6-1 walked into the classroom, they all felt that something was different.

The blackboard had been wiped spotless. The windows were open. The sea breeze blew in, puffing the curtains out.

"Good morning." Ching-Ching walked into the classroom. She stood on the podium and took a deep breath.

Then, she smiled.

"Good morning!" The whole class jumped, and then smiled too.

Morning self-study. The teacher hadn't arrived yet.

Da-ge sat in A-Da's seat. He felt around the desk drawer — there was a marble inside.

"A-Bao," Da-ge said quietly.

"Mm?" A-Bao turned around.

Da-ge took out the marble: "This one's for you. A-Da left it for you."

A-Bao took the marble, his nose reddening.

"Thanks," he said. "I'll keep it in my backpack."

Da-ge grinned, stood up, and said loudly: "Oh, right! This Friday, let's hold a class party! I've got some awesome ideas!"

The classroom went quiet for a second.

Da-ge's heart tightened. He braced himself to be told to "be quiet."

But Ching-Ching spoke. She said: "Okay. Tell us about them."

Da-ge froze.

Then, he smiled. So wide his eyes curved into crescents.

"Alright!" He rolled up his sleeves. "First, we're going to..."

The whole class turned around and listened to him.

Class 6-1, for the first time, quietly listened to Wu Da explain an entire proposal.

The class bell rang. First period was math.

The teacher wrote a problem on the blackboard: "Who can solve this?"

In the classroom, a hand went up.

Not Da-ge. Not Hsiao-Yun.

It was Lin Che.

The teacher paused for a second, then smiled and said: "Lin Che, tell us."

Lin Che stood up. His voice wasn't loud, but it was very clear.

He got it right.

"Very good!" the teacher said.

Lin Che sat down. His heart was pounding.

But he smiled.

At recess, Hsiao-Yun walked over to him.

"You raised your hand," she said.

"Mm." Lin Che nodded.

Hsiao-Yun looked at him, then suddenly smiled too: "Good."

Noon, lunchtime.

Hsiao-Yun held her lunch box and stood by her seat.

She glanced at the corner of the last row — her seat.

Then, carrying her lunch box, she walked to the middle of the classroom.

"Can I... sit here?" she asked.

Hsiao-Mei and Tzu-Han looked up, froze for a moment, then nodded frantically: "Of course! Sit, sit, sit!"

Hsiao-Yun sat down and opened her lunch box.

Today, she didn't polish the lunch box until it gleamed.

Because she was too busy talking.

In the afternoon, during classroom decoration time.

An-An stood in front of the bulletin board, holding a drawing in her hands.

"An-An, put it up!" A-Bao shouted.

"Yeah!" Da-ge shouted. "It's your gallery!"

An-An turned and looked at everyone.

Then, gently, she taped the drawing up.

It was the sea. The quiet sea, like a sigh.

"It's so beautiful," Hsiao-Mei said.

"An-An, draw me a cat!" "Draw the school for me!"

An-An smiled: "One at a time."

After school, Lin Che walked alone to the old auditorium.

The iron gate was locked. He looked in through the window.

The mirror stood silently on the stage.

Covered in dust. No light. No cracks.

Lin Che stood outside the window and looked for a long time.

"Hey," he said softly. "I did it. I raised my hand today."

The mirror didn't respond.

Lin Che smiled a little and turned to leave.

Just as he reached the edge of the playground, the whale sticker in his pocket fell out.

He bent down and picked it up.

The sticker had turned gray.

The little blue whale was gone. Only a faint gray outline remained.

Lin Che stared at it blankly.

"Because it doesn't belong to this world," a voice said.

It was Ching-Ching. She'd somehow appeared behind him.

"Things from World B fade along with the mirror," she said. "The color, the warmth... in the end, they'll become transparent."

"Will it disappear?" Lin Che asked.

"Maybe someday," Ching-Ching said. "But not today."

Lin Che looked down at the gray whale.

"It's okay." He put it back in his pocket. "Before it disappears, I'll remember."

"I'll remember it was blue. I'll remember it swam over from another world."

Ching-Ching looked at him without speaking. She took a card out of her backpack.

It was B Ching-Ching's card: "Even a serious monitor should remember to smile."

A corner of the card had already turned gray too.

"I'll remember too," Ching-Ching said.

The two children stood at the edge of the playground. The sea breeze blew over.

"Hey," Ching-Ching suddenly said, "let's go buy a pencil."

"A pencil?"

"The bookstore by the gate," Ching-Ching said. "It's open."

Lin Che paused for a moment, then smiled.

"Okay."

Inside Haisheng Bookstore, the boss lady beamed.

"What would you like to buy today?"

"A pencil," Lin Che said.

He picked a blue one. Exactly the same as the one in his pocket.

"That makes two," he murmured to himself.

Outside the bookstore, the sunset dyed the sea gold.

"See you tomorrow," Ching-Ching said.

"See you tomorrow," Lin Che said.

On the way home, Lin Che suddenly stopped.

He looked up at the sky.

A white-eyes bird flew over from the direction of the banyan tree, circled once, and flew off.

Lin Che watched it.

Then, slowly, he raised his hand and waved at it.

"Hi," he said. "A-Da."

The sea breeze carried that word far, far away.

And in a certain parallel world—

A boy called Da-ge, standing in a strawberry field, let out a huge sneeze.

"Achoo!"

"What's wrong?" the classmate beside him asked.

"I don't know." He scratched his head and smiled. "I just feel... like someone's calling my name."

He picked the reddest strawberry and put it in his pocket.

In the sunset, that strawberry was red, like a tiny sun.

And both worlds were living their own days, well and truly.
