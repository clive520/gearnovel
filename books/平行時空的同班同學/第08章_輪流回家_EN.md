# Chapter 08 — Taking Turns to Go Home

At 8:30 in the evening, A-Da pedaled his bike and raced up to the school gate.

He'd gotten an earful from his mom — when he came home that afternoon, he'd left his jacket on the banyan tree outside the old auditorium. His mom was halfway through the laundry when she noticed a jacket was missing, and chased him out the door to go fetch it.

Just as he squeezed the brakes to stop, he froze completely.

From the direction of the old auditorium, there was light.

Not streetlight. It was silvery-white light, flickering on and off, leaking through the cracks of the auditorium windows.

A-Da's heart instantly began to race. He grabbed his phone and messaged the group chat: "You guys, come to school now!! Something's wrong with the mirror!!"

He even attached a blurry photo.

Twenty minutes later, all three of them had arrived. More than three, actually — A-Bao of World A saw the message and came racing over on his bike too.

Ching-Ching carried a small backpack containing a flashlight and her notebook. Lin Che was still panting — clearly he'd run the whole way.

The four of them pushed open the iron gate and walked into the auditorium.

The mirror wasn't fully open. Only a slit of silvery-white light remained, like a door that hadn't been shut properly.

Through the slit, World B was faintly visible. The faces of B Ching-Ching, Da-ge, and Xiao-Yang crowded into that thin line of light, full of worry.

"You finally came!" The voice was tiny, as if traveling from far away. "Someone from our class is missing!"

"Who's missing?" Ching-Ching leaned closer.

"A-Bao!" Da-ge said. "The A-Bao who argued with you this afternoon! He hasn't come back since then!"

All four hearts sank at once.

"He's... on our side," Lin Che suddenly said.

He pointed at the corner of the auditorium. In the darkness, a small figure huddled behind a stack of chairs, shivering slightly.

It was the A-Bao of World B.

"A-Bao!" A-Da rushed over.

B A-Bao looked up. His face was smudged, his eyes red — completely different from the provoking look he'd worn that afternoon.

"I... I just wanted to see if your world was really that boring..." he said in a small voice. "But I got lost. By the time I came back, the mirror had already closed..."

"When did you come over?" Ching-Ching asked.

"This afternoon... around three."

Ching-Ching looked down at her watch. 9:02 p.m.

"Six hours," she said, her voice soft but her face changing color.

"What happens at six hours?" A-Da asked.

Before Ching-Ching could answer, A A-Bao had already walked up to B A-Bao.

Two identical people stood in the dark auditorium, looking at each other.

"Y-you... who are you?" B A-Bao's voice trembled.

"I'm you." A A-Bao's eyes went wide. "You really... really look exactly like me..."

Suddenly, B A-Bao murmured three words.

"Yakult."

At the exact same second, A A-Bao opened his mouth too: "Yakult."

Both of them froze at the same time.

"How did you know I was going to say that?" B A-Bao stepped back.

"I don't know!" A A-Bao was frightened too. "My mouth moved on its own!"

Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote quickly: "9 p.m. Two A-Baos, six hours on the same side. Sync has begun — they spoke the same words at the same time."

"Sync?" Lin Che asked.

"Yes." Ching-Ching looked up, her eyes bright in the darkness. "When two selves stay in the same world for too long, they start to sync. Habits, movements, words — they all start converging."

"And if it goes on even longer?" A-Da's voice shook a little.

Ching-Ching was silent for a moment.

"I don't know yet. But—" She looked toward the empty lot outside the auditorium. "Do you remember the shadow of the slide?"

"I suspect that if the sync goes on too long, one of them will grow faint. Like a shadow, overwritten by the other world."

The auditorium fell terribly silent.

"So A-Bao has to go back tonight," Ching-Ching said.

"But the mirror is already closed!" A A-Bao panicked. "We'd have to wait until tomorrow..."

"He can't wait until tomorrow." Ching-Ching said. "If we're right, in another six hours — three a.m. — he might start to fade."

"Then what do we do?" A-Da scratched his head.

Lin Che looked at the mirror. The silvery-white slit was still faintly pulsing.

"The mirror didn't close all the way," he said.

Everyone turned to look at him.

"Today the mirror stayed open especially long," Lin Che said slowly. "Maybe it only 'shrank,' and didn't 'close.' What if..."

"If there are children who can see it around, it will open." Ching-Ching finished his thought. "Like it does every afternoon."

The two of them exchanged a glance.

"Let's try it." Ching-Ching stood up and walked over to the mirror.

The five children gathered in front of the mirror.

At first, nothing happened. The slit stayed a slit, and the light stayed faint.

A minute passed.

"Please..." B A-Bao whispered. His eyes reddened again. "Please, let me go home."

Suddenly, the slit brightened.

Then the dust on the mirror's surface parted in rings. The slit slowly, slowly widened a little.

Just enough for one person to squeeze through sideways.

On the other side of the slit, all the children of World B crowded in.

"A-Bao! A-Bao!" B Ching-Ching's voice came through the slit, choked with tears.

"Hurry!" Ching-Ching shouted.

B A-Bao took a step forward, then stopped.

He turned and looked at A A-Bao.

"I'm sorry," he said. "This afternoon... I shouldn't have said those things. Your world isn't boring at all."

A A-Bao looked at him and suddenly smiled.

"I forgive you," he said. "You're another me, after all. And—"

He paused: "Honestly, I think the back of our school is really empty too."

The two A-Baos laughed at the same time.

Then B A-Bao turned sideways and squeezed into the slit.

The instant he passed through, the light in the slit flared bright, and then—

It closed.

The mirror's surface returned to darkness. Only the crack in the lower-right corner still pulsed faintly.

"Did he make it?" A-Da whispered.

No one answered. Everyone crowded up to the mirror, straining to look inside.

Suddenly, the mirror's surface glowed faintly. B Ching-Ching's voice came through, small and blurry:

"He made it! He's here!"

Everyone let out a huge sigh of relief.

The four children sat on the floor, none of them speaking. After a while, A-Da laughed first.

"We were like a movie just now."

Everyone laughed. And as they laughed, their eyes grew a little wet.

Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote the rules down one by one:

"Rule One: Only children can see the mirror."

"Rule Two: Two selves, together on the same side for over six hours, will sync."

"Rule Three: Suspected — over twelve hours, one will be overwritten."

"Rule Four: The two classes must take turns. No pair of selves may stay in the same world for more than three hours."

She tore out the page of rules and taped it beside the mirror.

"This is the 'rotation schedule,'" she said. "Tomorrow, the moment the mirror opens, the people on the other side will see it first thing."

"Tomorrow is the last day," Ching-Ching said, her voice soft but steady. "The seventh day."

The four children walked out of the auditorium, ready to head home.

At the doorway, Lin Che suddenly stopped.

"Look."

The other three followed his gaze.

The silvery-white crack wasn't only on the mirror anymore.

It had climbed from the mirror's wooden frame onto the wall. A thin line of faintly glowing light, like a tiny bolt of lightning, spreading upward along the wall.

The four of them tilted their heads up and stared at the crack. None of them could say a word.

"The world..." A-Da's voice trembled. "The world is cracking."

The sea breeze swept through the night campus, rattling the old auditorium's windows.

The crack glowed quietly in the moonlight.

The seventh day was about to arrive.
