# Chapter 12 — An-An's Drawings

4:20 in the afternoon. The Sunday campus was as quiet as if someone had pressed pause.

The investigation team was about to leave the school. Passing the Class 6-1 classroom, Lin Che suddenly stopped.

Inside the classroom, there was a sound.

A very, very soft sound — the sound of a pencil sliding across paper.

The four of them moved to the window and peered inside.

In the last row of the classroom, a girl sat with her head down, drawing something intently.

It was An-An.

Hsu An-An, Class 6-1's "art officer" — though really, she'd been voted into the role to handle classroom decorations. She rarely spoke and barely stood out.

"An-An?" A-Da shouted.

An-An jumped, frantically shoving her sketchbook into her desk drawer.

"Wh-why are you guys still at school?" Her face turned red.

"We should be asking you!" A-Da walked into the classroom. "Coming to school on a Sunday to draw? You're way too serious!"

An-An lowered her head and said nothing.

Ching-Ching walked over to her desk. The drawer hadn't closed all the way, and a corner of the sketchbook peeked out. On the cover was a sea.

"You draw really well," Ching-Ching said.

An-An froze. She looked at Ching-Ching as if she couldn't believe her ears.

"Really," Lin Che added. "The waves look like they're moving."

An-An's face turned even redder. She said quietly: "It's... the only thing I like doing."

"The only thing?" Hsiao-Yun suddenly asked.

"Mm." An-An nodded. "Mom says drawing is useless. The teacher says schoolwork is more important."

She paused: "So I only draw when no one is around."

The classroom went quiet for a second.

"An-An." Ching-Ching suddenly said. "Do you want to see a place where your drawings are hanging on the wall?"

An-An's eyes went wide.

4:50 in the evening. The five of them passed through the mirror.

An-An was the last one. She clutched her sketchbook to her chest, holding it tight.

The World B Class 6-1 classroom. An-An walked up to the bulletin board.

She looked up, for a long, long time.

The bulletin board was covered in drawings. The banyan tree, the ocean waves, the orange cat, the school gate.

Each one looked like something she would draw. And each one was... louder than anything she'd ever drawn.

"Is this... drawn by me?" Her voice trembled.

"By 'you,' yes."

An-An turned around.

At the classroom door stood a girl who looked exactly like her. Shoulder-length hair, watercolor stains on her cuffs.

It was the An-An of World B.

The two An-Ans stood face to face. Neither said a word.

Then B An-An suddenly smiled. She raised her own sketchbook: "Want to compare?"

The two girls opened their sketchbooks at the same time.

The first page of both was a sea. A An-An's sea was quiet, like a sigh. B An-An's sea was surging, like laughter.

"Your sea is so quiet," B An-An said.

"Your sea is so loud," A An-An said.

The two of them burst out laughing at the same time.

"Our class bulletin board is my gallery," B An-An said, her voice full of pride. "Teacher Chen says drawing is a gift. Every time a classmate has a birthday, I draw a picture for them."

"Everyone here loves my drawings."

A An-An listened quietly. Her eyes slowly reddened.

"Mom says drawing is useless. The teacher says schoolwork matters more." She said softly. "So I keep my sketchbook in the deepest part of my drawer."

B An-An looked at her and suddenly said one thing.

"But you're still drawing."

A An-An froze.

"In the deepest part of your drawer," B An-An said, "you still draw every single day. You haven't given up, have you?"

A tear slid down A An-An's cheek.

But she was smiling.

"Mm." She nodded. "I haven't given up."

B An-An tore a drawing from her sketchbook and pressed it into her hands.

It was a picture of two girls watching the sea side by side. On the back was written:

"To the other me: keep drawing."

Suddenly, a commotion came from the back of the classroom.

"The bulletin board changed again!"

Everyone turned.

On the bulletin board, three drawings... were gone. In their place were three sheets printed with "School Office Notice."

No flickering. No channel-changing. It had simply, silently, changed.

"It won't change back." B Ching-Ching's voice was soft. "This time, it's real overwriting."

The classroom fell so quiet you could hear the sea breeze.

Six o'clock in the evening. The investigation team got ready to go home.

As they reached the school gate, Da-ge suddenly yelled: "The bookstore! Where's the bookstore?!"

Everyone looked toward the gate.

The blue sign that read "Haisheng Bookstore" was gone.

In its place hung a green 7-ELEVEN sign. The store was lit, and behind the counter stood a stranger in a store uniform.

The World B children all stood frozen.

"The boss lady..." B Ching-Ching murmured.

"This place has become the 7-Eleven from our side," A-Da said quietly.

No one spoke. They just stood there, staring at the green sign.

6:20 p.m. The five passed through the mirror and returned to World A.

As they walked out of the school gate, Lin Che looked up.

The green 7-ELEVEN sign was gone.

In its place hung a blue "Haisheng Bookstore" sign. A wooden door frame, with a handwritten recommendation card taped to the glass door.

A-Da walked over slowly and pushed the glass door open.

An auntie with a kerchief on her head looked up, beaming: "Oh my, why haven't you kids come by in so long?"

A-Da froze.

"Auntie... you know us?"

"How could I not know you?" the boss lady laughed. "Aren't you the ones who always come in for stickers? Here, there are new ones today too."

She pulled a stack of stickers from her apron pocket, one for each of them.

Lin Che took his sticker. A small blue whale.

Exactly the same as the one in his pocket — the one from World B.

He pulled the blue pencil out of his pocket.

On the shelf sat a whole row of identical pencils. Blue bodies, with the words "Haisheng Bookstore" printed on them.

"Auntie," Lin Che asked, "how long has this store been here?"

"How long?" The boss lady thought for a moment. "Over twenty years, I'd say. Why?"

Lin Che shook his head: "No reason. It just... feels nice."

Outside the bookstore, the five of them stood in the sea breeze.

Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote:

"Day seven, evening. The overwriting has begun to set. Irreversible."

She looked up toward the empty lot behind the playground.

In the sunset, the shadow of the slide had grown so dense it no longer looked like a shadow.

Its outline could almost be touched.

"Before tomorrow..." A-Da's voice was dry. "How many more places will permanently turn into the other world's version?"

No one answered.

Lin Che looked down at the whale stickers in his hand.

Two stickers, two worlds. Identical.

He suddenly couldn't tell which one had come from where.

The sea breeze blew over. The bookstore's sign swayed gently in the wind.

The worlds were weaving together.

And the seventh day had only one last night left.
