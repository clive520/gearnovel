# Chapter 16 — The Mirror Cracks

The image in the mirror grew clearer and clearer.

Daytime. September. The playground on the third day of school.

The sunlight was blindingly bright. The children could even "smell" the scene — the smell of grass, the smell of the sea breeze.

Fifty-two people crowded in front of the mirror, not one of them speaking.

In the image, Coach Liu blew his whistle.

Then, everyone saw it.

The mirror's surface split apart.

The image in the mirror divided into two halves.

Left: Coach Liu walked up to little Lin Che, his mouth opening—

"You're more on the quiet side. For the anchor leg, leave it to the kids who run fast."

In the left image, little Lin Che lowered his head and stepped back.

Right: Coach Liu walked up to little Lin Che, his mouth opening—

"Don't you want to give it a try? I think you can do it."

In the right image, little Lin Che's eyes lit up and he nodded hard.

The same second. Two possibilities. One mirror, two worlds.

The fifty-two children watched this scene, none of them moving.

"That's it." Hsiao-Yun's voice was soft. "The mirror has torn 'that day' in half."

"It wasn't the mirror that tore it," Ching-Ching said. "That day always had two possibilities."

Just then, the crack at the center of the mirror began to widen.

"Crack—"

A fracture spread from the center of the mirror to both sides, like lightning, like a growing tree.

The crack climbed out of the mirror frame, into the wall, up to the ceiling.

"Get back!" Xiao-Yang yelled.

The children scrambled backward.

Wherever the crack passed, the world "changed channels": the auditorium floor was wood one moment, cement the next; the audience seats had chairs one moment, and were empty the next.

"The worlds are overlapping... no, they're squeezing each other!" A-Da clutched his head.

The images of the two worlds, like two film reels stacked together, switched rapidly.

"It can't hold on." B Lin Che stared at the mirror. "The mirror is going to crack open!"

"It won't," Lin Che suddenly said.

He took a step forward.

"Lin Che!" Ching-Ching grabbed his sleeve.

"It won't crack." Lin Che looked at the mirror, his voice steady. "It just wants us to see clearly."

He kept walking forward.

At his feet, the cracks suddenly stopped.

The "channel changing" in the auditorium slowly stopped too.

Like a storm that had been paused out of nowhere.

"Look," Lin Che said.

The two images in the mirror were still there. The Lin Che on the left hung his head; the Lin Che on the right was smiling.

But the cracks were no longer spreading.

"The mirror is waiting." Hsiao-Yun walked over, her glasses reflecting the light. "It's waiting for Lin Che."

"For me?"

"It's waiting for you to finish watching these two possibilities," Hsiao-Yun said. "And then make a choice."

"What choice?" A-Da asked.

"It's not about choosing which one." Hsiao-Yun shook her head. "It's... whether you can accept both at the same time."

The children fell quiet.

Lin Che stood before the mirror, one step from its surface.

The him on the left hung his head. The him on the right was smiling.

Both were him. And neither was the whole of him.

"I understand now," Lin Che said softly.

He reached out his hand and slowly touched the mirror's surface.

The instant his fingertip met the glass—

The image rippled like water.

Lin Che's finger sank into the mirror.

No pain. Cool. Like reaching into a pool of warm water.

"Shall we go in?" B Lin Che asked.

Lin Che turned and looked at the fifty-two children.

"It's not just me," he said. "It's us."

Xiao-Yang was the first to react. He shouted: "Everyone, hold hands!"

The fifty-two children, in front of the mirror, took one another's hands.

Normally, the mirror only let eight people through at a time. But tonight, the mirror refused no one.

At the very front stood the two Lin Ches. A Lin Che on the left, B Lin Che on the right.

They stretched out their other hands at the same time and pressed them against the mirror's surface.

Silvery-white light surged from the mirror, swallowing the fifty-two children one by one.

The world turned a single silvery-white.

Then—

Sunshine.

Blinding sunshine.

Lin Che opened his eyes.

He was standing on a playground. The playground of a September, the start of a school year.

The sky was brilliantly blue. In the sea breeze was the smell of grass.

The fifty-two children stood behind him, all frozen in shock.

"This place is..." A-Da's voice trembled. "That day?"

"That day," Ching-Ching nodded.

On the playground, the Class 6-1 line was formed. Children with their heads down, glancing at one another.

That Coach Liu stood at the front of the line.

He wore his PE uniform, a whistle around his neck. He looked just like the present day — maybe a little younger.

"Teacher!" A-Da tried to run over.

"Wait!" B Lin Che stopped him.

A-Da's hand passed right through Coach Liu's body.

Like passing through a mass of light.

The children all went quiet.

"We... can't touch them?" Hsiao-Yun's voice tightened.

"We can't." B Lin Che said. "This is just 'that day' inside the mirror. A memory, not reality."

Just then, in the scene, little Lin Che slowly raised his hand halfway.

Coach Liu saw him.

He walked over. One step, two steps, three steps.

All fifty-two children held their breath.

Coach Liu opened his mouth—

And the next second, the entire playground trembled.

The sky flickered with static, like a TV screen.

"What's happening?!" the children cried out.

The world in the scene began switching rapidly between the "two possibilities": Coach Liu's mouth now about to say one thing, now about to say the other.

"Time is running out." B Lin Che said quietly. "The world outside is waiting for us."

He looked down at his watch — but the hands of his watch stayed frozen in place.

"Here, time is stopped," Hsiao-Yun said. "Stopped right before that sentence was spoken."

"How much time do we have?" Ching-Ching asked.

Hsiao-Yun shook her head: "I don't know. Maybe an instant. Maybe forever."

"But we know one thing—" She looked at everyone. "Before midnight, we have to make the 'Lin Che' here see both possibilities at the same time."

The children looked at the little Lin Che in the scene.

He held his hand halfway up, waiting for Coach Liu to speak.

As if... waiting for them.

Who knew what time it was now, in the outside world.

The time inside the mirror stood frozen before that sentence.

And the real battle was only just beginning.
