# Chapter 01 — The Mirror in the Old Auditorium

September sea breezes still carried the tail of summer. When they swept across Haisheng Elementary School's playground, they whipped the national flag on the flagpole until it snapped and flapped.

The school dismissal bell had rung a long time ago. In Classroom 6-1, only one boy was still sitting in his seat — head down, turning his backpack over and over, his face growing paler by the second.

"No, no, no... where's my homework?"

His name was Lin Che, the quietest student in Class 6-1. Average grades, a soft voice, sitting by the window in the second-to-last row. He never raised his hand, and teachers rarely called on him. If you asked anyone in class, "What kind of person is Lin Che?", eight out of ten would pause, then say: "Uh... he's, well, very quiet."

Only Lin Che himself knew the truth. He wasn't spacing out. He was "watching."

He loved to watch. He watched the birds in the banyan tree outside the window, watched his classmates laugh and play at recess, watched the sunset paint the whole classroom orange. He felt the world was full of small details that other people never noticed.

For example — he'd just noticed that the floor tile behind the classroom's back door was a slightly different color from the others.

"Found it!" Lin Che pulled his flattened home-communication notebook out from the very bottom of his desk drawer and let out a sigh of relief.

He stuffed the notebook into his backpack and was about to leave when he froze.

Outside the window, a bird in the banyan tree suddenly burst into the air with a sharp cry. At the same moment, Lin Che noticed — the sunset light seemed to "flicker."

How could he describe it? It was as if someone had swept an invisible flashlight across the sky behind everything. Just for a split second, so fast that Lin Che thought his eyes were playing tricks on him.

He shook his head, slung his backpack over his shoulder, and walked out.

The corridors of Haisheng Elementary after school were so quiet that his own footsteps echoed extra loudly. When he passed the end of the first-floor hallway, his steps slowed.

In front of him was the old auditorium.

The old auditorium had been out of use for years. A wooden sign on the door read, "Under Renovation — Do Not Enter," and the iron gate was padlocked. But right now... that iron gate was open a crack.

Lin Che thought about pretending not to see it. After all, he was a "quiet kid who never caused trouble."

But just as he passed the gate, a "light" seeped through the crack.

It wasn't sunset light. Sunset was orange and warm. This light was silvery-white, very faint, as if it were seeping in from somewhere very, very far away — and it made Lin Che's heart thump hard.

As if something were tugging at him, he pushed the iron gate open and stepped inside.

The old auditorium was dark and musty, smelling of old wood. The stage curtains hung still, and the audience seats were thick with dust. Holding his breath, Lin Che walked step by step toward the back of the stage.

Behind the stage, there was a mirror.

It was a huge, ancient mirror set in a wooden frame, its surface so dusty it barely reflected a person. Lin Che remembered hearing from older students when he was little that the old auditorium had a "mirror that couldn't show your reflection." Everyone treated it as a ghost story, and nobody had ever actually come to check.

Right now, that mirror was glowing silvery-white.

Lin Che took two steps forward.

On the mirror's surface, the dust parted in rings, as if an invisible hand were wiping it away. Then, inside the silvery-white glow, a face appeared.

A boy's face.

The boy looked about the same age as Lin Che. Same short black hair, same eyebrows, same nose — it was almost like the "Lin Che" in the mirror.

But it was also different.

The boy in the mirror was smiling. Not a faint, polite smile, but a smile with bright, sparkling eyes, like someone always ready to rush out on an adventure. The boy in the mirror looked... happy.

Lin Che froze.

At that very moment, the boy in the mirror spotted Lin Che too. He widened his eyes with a "Oh wow, I can see you!" expression, then raised his hand and waved at Lin Che—

"!" Lin Che staggered back a full step, his heel banging into a chair with a loud CLANG.

By the time he came to his senses and looked again, the mirror had already returned to normal. Just dust, and an old mirror reflecting his own pale face.

The silvery-white light had vanished. As if it had never existed.

"Huff... huff..." Lin Che panted, his heart pounding as if it would leap out of his chest.

"Who are you? What are you doing in here?!"

A voice suddenly rang out from the auditorium entrance. Lin Che jumped clean off the floor in fright.

Standing at the door was Class 6-1's class monitor, Chou Yu-Ching.

Ching-Ching had her backpack on and homework notebooks in her arms. She frowned at him: "Lin Che? School's over, why are you still in the old auditorium?" She stepped inside and glanced around suspiciously. "What are you doing in here all alone?"

"I... I..." Lin Che opened his mouth, not knowing what to say. Tell her "I saw a boy who looks exactly like me waving at me in the mirror"? Even he thought that sounded ridiculous out loud.

"I just... came in to take a look." He lowered his head and said quietly.

"Take a look?" Ching-Ching raised an eyebrow. "What's there to look at in an old auditorium under renovation?"

Lin Che didn't answer.

Ching-Ching stared at him for a few long seconds. She was the kind of person who had to find a reason for everything — and the class's most universally acknowledged hardest person to fool. She always believed there were no mysterious strange things in the world, only causes that hadn't been discovered yet.

"Whatever." Ching-Ching finally said. "It's getting dark anyway, hurry home. It's dangerous to be here alone at night."

She turned around, took two steps, then stopped.

"Huh?"

"What's wrong?" Lin Che asked.

Ching-Ching didn't answer. She just stared at the mirror behind the stage.

"That mirror... is a little strange," she said. "Look, in the lower-right corner of the mirror, there's a thin crack. But when I was at the door just now, I definitely didn't see any crack."

Lin Che followed her gaze.

In the lower-right corner of the mirror, there was indeed an extremely thin crack — so fine it was like a single strand of hair, almost invisible.

But — that crack was silvery-white. And it was faintly pulsing with light, as if... it were breathing.

"There's no crack." Lin Che heard himself say. "You must have seen wrong."

Ching-Ching stared a few more seconds, then let out an "Mm...": "...Maybe. It's getting dark, that's all."

She didn't look again. Slinging her backpack on, she walked out of the auditorium: "Come on, let's lock up."

Lin Che followed her out. The moment the iron gate closed, he glanced back.

Inside the old auditorium, in a place he couldn't see, the mirror's silvery-white crack was still faintly glowing, pulsing on and off.

On the way home, the sun had already sunk completely below the sea's horizon. A little orange-red glow still lingered at the edge of the sky.

Walking along, Lin Che suddenly remembered something.

The third day of the new school year. It was PE class, he thought.

That day... what happened that day?

He clearly remembered the sunlight was wonderful that afternoon. He remembered the sound of Coach Liu blowing his whistle. He remembered his classmates running around the field. But when he tried to remember further, his memory was like it was blocked by fog — no matter how hard he tried, he couldn't recall.

Every time he got to this part, his head gave a faint, dull throb.

"Whatever." Lin Che muttered to himself. "It probably wasn't anything important anyway."

But that night, lying in bed with his eyes closed —

In the darkness, that face — "looking just like him, yet so happy" — floated back up.

And that sentence. He felt like he'd heard it somewhere before, but he couldn't remember who said it, or when —

"Don't you want to give it a try?"

Outside the window, in the direction of Haisheng Elementary School, on the roof of the old auditorium, a thin sliver of silvery-white light flickered once... and was gone.
