# Chapter 19 — The Final Class Reunion

11:40 p.m. After the applause died down, the old auditorium fell silent.

"Did we... do it?" A-Da asked quietly.

"We did it," Ching-Ching said. Her voice trembled a little. "Both worlds are still here."

Suddenly, A-Bao pointed at B A-Bao: "So now... can I touch you?"

Everyone froze.

B A-Bao walked over and held out one finger.

The two fingertips touched, ever so lightly.

Nothing happened.

No memory exchange. No syncing. No fading.

"Yay—!" A-Bao jumped up. "We can touch each other now!"

The children cheered. People from the two worlds began hugging, high-fiving, and thumping shoulders.

The fifty-two of them dissolved into laughter.

Lin Che and B Lin Che, still holding hands, laughed too.

Only Hsiao-Yun didn't laugh.

She stood in front of the mirror, staring at it.

"Have you noticed," she suddenly said, "the mirror's light is changing?"

The children fell quiet.

The silvery-white glow on the mirror was still bright. But it seemed... a little fainter than before.

"It's dimming," Ching-Ching walked over.

"Why? Didn't we succeed?" A-Da grew anxious.

"Seven days," Hsiao-Yun said, her voice soft. "The mirror only opens for seven days."

"It was the same thirty years ago. Once seven days passed, it stopped."

The children looked at one another.

"So..." Xiao-Yang's voice was a little dry. "The moment midnight comes, the mirror will close?"

"Mm." Hsiao-Yun nodded. "And after it closes this time—"

She didn't finish the sentence.

But everyone understood. Thirty years ago, the auditorium had been sealed for thirty years.

Tonight was the last time.

The joyful mood ebbed away, like a tide going out.

"Don't be so gloomy!" Da-ge suddenly shouted, jumping onto a chair. "Didn't we know from the start that today was the last day?"

"That's right!" Xiao-Yang shouted too. "We were always going to say goodbye!"

"Yeah!" the children chimed in.

"So," Da-ge raised the paper in his hand — the one covered in flowcharts, "don't we still have the 'Two-Worlds Class Reunion'?"

"The last twenty minutes, we're spending them laughing!" Xiao-Yang shouted.

The children's eyes lit up again.

"Reunion! Reunion!" Fifty-two voices shouted together.

"But no running wild in front of the mirror." Ching-Ching instantly went into class-monitor mode. "Group One handles decorations, Group Two handles snacks, Group Three—"

"Card exchange!" A-Da shouted. "Don't forget my idea!"

"Right! Card exchange!" Da-ge clapped. "Everyone write down what you want to say to the people in the other world!"

The children immediately got busy.

An-An and B An-An were in charge of making the cards. The two girls put their heads together and produced little cards all over the floor in the blink of an eye.

Hsiao-Yun and B Hsiao-Yun were in charge of writing. One wrote the words, the other drew illustrations — they worked together flawlessly.

A-Da and Da-ge were in charge of handing out the cards. The two of them weaved through the crowd like a pair of fish.

Ching-Ching and B Ching-Ching were in charge of arranging the tables and chairs. The two monitors didn't need to speak — their coordination was frighteningly smooth.

Xiao-Yang was in charge of the mood. He stood on a table, directing everyone in song.

Lin Che and B Lin Che stood before the mirror, watching it all.

"Beautiful, isn't it?" B Lin Che said.

"Mm." Lin Che nodded.

He suddenly pulled the blue pencil out of his pocket.

"This pencil," he said, "I bought it at Haisheng Bookstore."

"OUR Haisheng Bookstore." He paused. "It disappeared once. And now, it's come back."

B Lin Che looked at the pencil and smiled.

"Your world will get better and better," he said.

"Yours too," Lin Che said.

The two boys stood side by side, watching the children of both classes bustle about.

A-Da slipped off to a corner by himself and took the strawberry out of his pocket.

The strawberry was still very red. In the dim auditorium, it glowed like a tiny lamp.

"Hey." Da-ge suddenly appeared and crouched down beside him.

"You can't bear to eat it?" Da-ge grinned.

"Mm." A-Da nodded.

"Silly." Da-ge said. "Strawberries go bad if you don't eat them. Our strawberries are going to bear fruit again tomorrow."

He paused: "And I'll go pick new ones tomorrow."

A-Da opened his mouth.

"Da-ge," he said quietly, "after the mirror closes... will we never see each other again?"

Da-ge didn't answer.

After a while, Da-ge patted his back and stood up.

"Who knows." He smiled. "Maybe the kids from thirty years ago said the same thing."

"But look," Da-ge pointed at the mirror, "thirty years later, it opened again."

A-Da followed his hand and looked at the mirror.

The silvery-white light flowed gently. Inside it were two worlds.

"So," Da-ge's voice was soft, "maybe one day, we ourselves — who knows — might meet again."

A-Da put the strawberry back in his pocket, very carefully.

"Then I'll save it," he said. "For next time."

"Okay." Da-ge held out his hand.

The two hands shook firmly.

No light. No memory exchange.

Only the warmth of the palm.

11:50 p.m.

The reunion began.

In the auditorium, the light was dim. The fifty-two children formed a circle.

Xiao-Yang stood in the middle and raised a bottle of juice.

"To the two worlds!" he shouted.

"To the two worlds!" fifty-two voices answered.

"To today!"

"To today!"

"To us!"

"To us—!"

The children clinked their bottles. Juice spilled everywhere, and no one cared.

The card exchange began.

A-Bao pressed his card into B A-Bao's hands. It read: "Sorry. And, thank you."

B A-Bao pressed his card back: "Next time, come eat Yakult in our world."

An-An received B An-An's card, on which two girls were drawn holding hands.

Hsiao-Yun received B Hsiao-Yun's card. It read: "You'll always be my best partner."

Ching-Ching received B Ching-Ching's card: "Even a serious monitor should remember to smile."

Xiao-Yang received Ching-Ching's card: "Your laugh is very loud. It sounds very nice."

Everyone was smiling. Everyone was crying.

A-Da pressed his card into Da-ge's hands. The card was packed with writing. The last line was:

"Thank you for letting me be seen."

Da-ge looked at the card and smiled. He opened his mouth, about to say something—

Just then, the mirror's light suddenly flickered.

All the children stopped what they were doing.

The silvery-white light slowly, slowly dimmed.

Like a lamp being gently blown out.

"The mirror..." Ching-Ching's voice trembled. "It's closing."

11:55 p.m.

There wasn't much time left.
