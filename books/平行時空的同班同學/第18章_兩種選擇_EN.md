# Chapter 18 — Two Choices

11:20 p.m. In the old auditorium, it was so quiet that only breathing could be heard.

The fifty-two children retreated to the walls, forming one great circle.

In the center of the circle stood the two Lin Ches.

"Let's begin," B Lin Che said.

Lin Che nodded.

They held out their hands at the same time and clasped them together.

Silvery-white light seeped from between their fingers.

But this time, they did not let go.

Memories, like two rivers, converged between their two hands.

Lin Che saw all of B Lin Che.

He saw a boy standing in the middle of the playground, smiling like the sun. Everyone circled around him.

He saw that boy, alone in the middle of the night, hiding under his blanket, practicing a speech for the next day.

He saw that boy fall, and no one in the class saw it. He picked himself up, dusted off his pants, and kept smiling.

He saw that boy, looking at his own reflection in the mirror, and the first thing he said wasn't "hello," but—

"Aren't you tired?"

Lin Che's heart clenched.

B Lin Che saw all of Lin Che too.

He saw a boy sitting by the window, watching the white-eyes bird in the banyan tree for an entire recess.

He saw that boy quietly remembering every classmate's small habits: A-Da loves spinning his pen, Ching-Ching wrinkles her left eyebrow when writing, Hsiao-Yun polishes her lunch box until it gleams after every meal.

He saw that boy standing before the old auditorium's mirror, face white with fear — and still walking in.

He saw that boy writing "it's okay if I never remember" late at night, and then erasing the words.

B Lin Che's nose stung.

The clasped hands began to tremble.

"I..." A Lin Che spoke first. His voice was hoarse.

"I've always been envious of you," he said.

The auditorium was so quiet you could hear tears hitting the floor.

"I think about it every day," Lin Che's voice shook. "If I'd been the one who ran that day, if the whole class had shouted MY name — would I... be living a better life than now?"

"Would I be... more worth liking?"

The children's eyes reddened.

B Lin Che didn't speak right away. He just gripped Lin Che's hand harder.

"Me too," B Lin Che finally said. "I envy you too."

Everyone froze.

"Everyone looks at me," B Lin Che said. "So I can't stop. I can't stop running, can't stop smiling. Even my sadness has to hide."

"I'm so tired. But I never dared say it, because the whole class depends on me."

"Every time I see you," B Lin Che looked at him, "I think — what if it were me sitting by the window, watching the birds? How nice that would be."

Lin Che's tears fell.

"We..." he choked out. "We've been envying each other all along."

"Because we both only saw the half we didn't have," B Lin Che said.

The two Lin Ches lifted their heads at the same time.

"Your world needs your light," A Lin Che said.

"Your world needs your quiet," B Lin Che said.

"You run, and I'll watch the road for you," A Lin Che said.

"You watch, and I'll run for you," B Lin Che said.

Then, they smiled at the same time.

And as they smiled, they cried at the same time.

The tears fell onto their clasped hands, warm.

"So," Lin Che said, "it's not that one way is better. It's that I kept forgetting—"

"Forgetting what?" B Lin Che asked.

"Forgetting that I'm already me." Lin Che said. "The quiet me is a complete me too."

B Lin Che nodded hard: "I forgot too. The shining me also has the right to say I'm tired."

At that very instant—

Silvery-white light erupted from their clasped hands.

The light shot up to the auditorium roof, illuminating every face.

The cracks on the mirror's surface vanished one by one, as if stitched shut by the light.

"The cracks are healing!" A-Da shouted.

The light lasted for several seconds. Then, it slowly dimmed.

On the mirror's surface, only one thin line remained. Fine as a strand of hair.

"Only a tiny bit left!" B Ching-Ching said.

Hsiao-Yun stared at the mirror. Inside it, the frozen playground was still there.

Little Lin Che still held his hand halfway up.

But this time, it was different.

His eyes no longer looked at the ground.

He was looking at them. And he was smiling.

"The last step," Hsiao-Yun's voice was soft, "is to let 'that day' complete itself."

"What do you mean?" A-Da asked.

"We've been trying to make the decision for him," Hsiao-Yun said. "To raise the hand or not. To run or not."

"But now, we all understand," Ching-Ching picked up. "Whichever one he chooses, it's fine."

"So," Hsiao-Yun looked at everyone, "what we have to do is let go. Let that sentence speak itself."

The children looked at one another.

"Let that day choose for itself," Lin Che said. His voice had never been so calm.

"Whichever sentence it is," B Lin Che said, "we're ready now."

The two Lin Ches walked up to the mirror.

In the mirror, little Lin Che slowly, slowly lowered his half-raised hand.

No. He didn't lower it.

He raised it to its very highest.

Then—

The scene began to move.

Coach Liu walked over. One step, two steps, three steps.

He opened his mouth.

A voice came from the mirror.

Not one sentence. Two.

Two sentences, overlapping, sounding at the same time:

"You're more on the quiet side. For the anchor leg, leave it to the kids who run fast."

"Don't you want to give it a try? I think you can do it."

The two sentences echoed through the auditorium.

Then, the image in the mirror split into two.

On the left, little Lin Che stepped back. Quietly, he became the shadow by the window.

On the right, little Lin Che sprinted out. He became the hero the whole class cheered for.

Two images, side by side, steady, playing at the same time.

No flickering. No fighting. No overwriting.

"Both worlds are here now," Ching-Ching's voice trembled.

"No one has to disappear," Hsiao-Yun said, her eyes gleaming fiercely behind her glasses.

The last thin line on the mirror's surface slowly vanished.

The mirror was whole again.

But it was no longer an ordinary mirror—

It reflected both worlds at once, like a window that would never close.

The children looked at the mirror, no one speaking.

Then, no one knew who started clapping first.

The applause echoed through the old auditorium.

The fifty-two children clapped, and cried, and laughed, all at once.

Lin Che turned and looked at everyone.

He suddenly remembered the dusk when the mirror first glowed.

That dusk, he thought he'd seen "someone better than him."

Now he knew.

That person was himself.

11:40 p.m.

And inside the mirror, both worlds were glowing.
