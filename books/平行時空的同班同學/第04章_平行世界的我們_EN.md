# Chapter 04 — Us in the Parallel World

September 19th, Wednesday.

That morning, three people in Class 6-1 were especially off.

Ching-Ching forgot to get her communication notebook signed by her mom. A-Da snickered to himself three times during morning self-study and got punished with standing. And Lin Che kept staring out the window — not even the white-eyes bird he loved, perched in the banyan tree, could bring him back.

Because their hearts had all been left behind in that mirror in the old auditorium.

During morning self-study, the classroom was noisy. Yu-Ching stood up and slapped her desk: "Quiet! Morning self-study is supposed to be quiet!"

The whole class went silent for exactly three seconds, then started buzzing again.

A-Da told a joke. A few classmates laughed twice, then got glared down by Yu-Ching: "Wu Da, quiet."

A-Da rubbed his nose and sat down.

Lin Che watched all of it, and suddenly thought of the "other A-Da" in the mirror yesterday — the A-Da surrounded by everyone, called "Da-ge."

"If only..."

He quickly shook his head and tossed the thought away.

After school, 3:50 p.m. The three of them gathered at the back of the classroom.

Ching-Ching spread open her notebook, like she was reading out a battle plan: "First, let's confirm the rules. One, the mirror opens at 4:05 and closes at 4:20. Two, we absolutely have to be back before it closes. Three—"

"Three, don't touch 'the other you.'" A-Da cut in.

"How do you know that?" Ching-Ching glared at him.

"I guessed." A-Da said it with total confidence. "That's how it works in movies."

Lin Che took a pencil from his pocket. A blue body, with the words "Haisheng Bookstore" printed on it.

"Why did you bring a pencil?" A-Da asked.

"...No reason." Lin Che gripped it a little tighter. "I just felt like I should."

At four o'clock sharp, the three of them stood before the old auditorium's iron gate. This time, the gate was open.

Inside, the mirror had already begun to glow. Silvery-white ripples spread in rings, as if someone had lit a lamp beneath the water's surface.

And on the other side of the mirror, a group of children was already standing there.

The people of World B had been waiting for them all along.

"They're here!" the other Ching-Ching shouted. Her hair was in a ponytail today, her smile bright, her uniform pin on the right side of her chest.

"Let's go." Ching-Ching took a deep breath and walked toward the mirror first.

"Wait, wait, wait—" A-Da suddenly grabbed her. "If anyone should go first, it should be me, right? I'm braver."

"You're just louder," Ching-Ching said.

"That's a kind of bravery too!"

In the end, A-Da went first. He stood in front of the mirror, squeezed his eyes shut, and yelled: "Charge—!"

Then his whole body "sank" into the mirror's surface, like walking through a door with no door.

Lin Che watched A-Da's back disappear into the silvery-white light. He froze for a second, then hurried to follow.

The feeling of crossing over was hard to describe.

It was like stepping into a pool of cool water. First came a buzzing in his ears, then all sound vanished — he couldn't hear the wind, the cicadas, or even his own heartbeat.

Then, plop.

He opened his eyes.

He was standing in the old auditorium.

The same dust, the same curtains, the same old mirror. But — the smell of the air was different. The old auditorium back home had a stuffy, moldy smell. This one smelled faintly of sunshine.

"Whoa—" A-Da's voice came from up ahead. "You guys, come out and look!"

The three of them walked out of the auditorium.

And then they all froze.

This was Haisheng Elementary.

The same red-brick walls, the same breezeway, the same playground. But—

"A slide!" A-Da pointed behind the playground and yelled.

Behind the playground stood a slide. A blue slide, looking a little old, yet shiny and bright, with a few younger kids lined up beside it, waiting their turn.

Lin Che froze. Behind their own school playground, there was only an empty lot.

But here, the slide stood right there.

"So this place really does have everything," Ching-Ching murmured, pulling out her notebook and writing quickly.

"And, did you guys notice?" She looked up. "The sky here... it seems bluer."

Lin Che looked up. It was true. The sky was so clean and blue it looked like it had been washed.

The three of them headed toward the Class 6-1 classroom.

The classroom door was open. In the doorway stood a crowd of children.

The two classes stood face to face for the first time.

At first, nobody spoke. Everyone looked at one another, as if gazing into a mirror.

Then, the "Da-ge" from the other side spoke first. He strode over and patted A-Da on the shoulder: "So you're me?"

A-Da froze completely. He was usually the biggest talker, but in this moment, he couldn't get out a single word.

Da-ge laughed: "Why are you so nervous? We look exactly the same — you should think that's cool!"

A few World B classmates crowded over too, looking at A-Da curiously: "Wow, they really do look alike." "But he seems so small." "Da-ge, is this what you used to look like?"

"What do you mean 'used to'? We're the same age, okay!" Da-ge laughed and scolded back, and the whole group dissolved into laughter.

Standing in the middle of the laughter, A-Da suddenly felt like he'd become a "supporting character."

In his own class, he was the A-Da whose jokes got him told to "be quiet." But here, everyone listened to Da-ge.

On the other side, Ching-Ching had met her match too.

"You're me?" The other Ching-Ching tilted her head and looked at her, smiling happily. "Why do you look so serious?"

"I'm the class monitor," Ching-Ching answered, her tone a little stiff.

"Class monitor?" The other Ching-Ching blinked. "I'm not the class monitor. Our class monitor is Xiao-Yang."

She pointed to the side. A tall, slim boy stood at the classroom door, arms slung over classmates' shoulders as he chatted. Halfway through, he suddenly burst out laughing, so loud the sound carried all the way into the hallway.

"That's Zhao Xiao-Yang," the other Ching-Ching said. "He's the one who leads everything in our class. He's great at setting the mood."

Ching-Ching looked at the loud-laughing boy, then at herself.

The same person, living in a completely different position.

Quietly, a new line appeared in her notebook: "World B: monitor = Xiao-Yang. Yu-Ching (me) = free."

And Lin Che stood in place, unmoving the whole time.

Because he knew that person hadn't shown up yet.

"Lin Che!"

A voice came from inside the classroom.

Lin Che looked up.

In the center of the classroom, the "other Lin Che" was walking toward him.

The way he walked was completely different from Lin Che. Chest out, strides long — classmates automatically parted to make way as he passed.

And he was smiling.

"I've been waiting for you," the Lin Che of World B said.

"For me?" Lin Che's voice came out hoarse.

"Of course." B Lin Che said it like it was obvious. "Because I always knew that one day I'd see you."

He pointed at his own chest: "Right here, I've always had a feeling — like there's another me in this world, living a different life."

Lin Che stared at him blankly.

He suddenly noticed something. When B Lin Che spoke, the classmates around him would stop to listen. The way they looked at him was the way people looked at someone important.

That kind of look — Lin Che had never seen it before. At least, no one had ever looked at him that way.

A strange feeling slowly rose from his chest. Sour, and warm.

It wasn't dislike, and it wasn't anger.

It was envy.

He was so envious he could barely speak.

"What's wrong?" B Lin Che asked with concern.

"Nothing." Lin Che lowered his head. "I just... think it seems really nice here."

B Lin Che tilted his head and studied him for two seconds, then suddenly smiled.

"Then how about—" He held out his hand. "Come alone tomorrow? I want to talk to you."

Lin Che looked at that hand.

He remembered A-Da's words: "Don't touch 'the other you.'"

Just as he was about to answer—

"Time!" Ching-Ching suddenly shouted. "It's almost 4:20!"

The whole group instantly fell into a panic.

The three of them hurriedly said goodbye to the people of World B and dashed back to the auditorium.

Lin Che ran at the very back. Just before he stepped into the mirror, B Lin Che caught up and stuffed a folded slip of paper into his hand.

"See you tomorrow." B Lin Che winked at him.

The next second, Lin Che plunged headfirst into the silvery-white light.

When he opened his eyes again, he was back in the old auditorium. Ching-Ching and A-Da stood beside him, gasping for breath.

"Huff... huff... just in time." Ching-Ching checked her watch. "4:19."

The three of them leaned against the wall. None of them spoke. They just laughed.

On the way home, Lin Che walked alone at the very back. He unfolded the slip of paper in his palm.

On the paper was handwriting identical to his own, two lines:

"Tomorrow, I want to tell you something."

"It's about 'that sentence.'"

Lin Che stopped walking.

The sea breeze blew in from the seawall, rustling the paper in his hand.

He suddenly remembered — today was the third day the mirror had opened.

And tomorrow would be the fourth.
