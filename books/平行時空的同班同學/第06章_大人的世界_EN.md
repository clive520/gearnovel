# Chapter 06 — The World of Adults

September 21st, Friday.

At 7:40 in the morning, Lin Che stopped in front of the school gate.

The 7-Eleven sign was flickering.

Not swaying in the wind. It was flickering like a TV with bad reception — one moment it was the green 7-ELEVEN, the next it was the blue bookstore, flashing back and forth.

But the parents dropping off their kids, the uncle riding past on his scooter, the teacher walking in to buy a newspaper — not one of them looked up.

"They can't see it." A voice came from beside him.

It was Ching-Ching. She stood behind Lin Che, backpack on, staring at the sign.

"I've been standing here counting," she said. "From 7:30 until now, the sign has flickered twelve times. Not a single adult noticed."

"Why?" Lin Che asked.

Ching-Ching shook her head: "I don't know yet. But maybe that's a good thing."

"A good thing?"

"At least no one will come and take the mirror away," she said.

A-Da charged over from behind, his hair sticking up even higher than usual: "Morning! You guys saw that too, right?"

The three of them walked through the gate side by side.

None of them absorbed a single word of the morning's classes.

In math class, Lin Che drew a tiny mirror in the corner of his textbook.

In Chinese class, Ching-Ching kept writing in her strange-happenings log under her desk pad.

In social studies class, A-Da folded a paper airplane and wrote on it: "Mission after school."

4:05 p.m. The old auditorium. The mirror lit up right on time.

The three of them passed through the mirror together.

The third time, Lin Che thought. He was already getting used to that cool feeling.

World B. Today, four people had come to meet them.

B Lin Che, B Ching-Ching, Da-ge, and someone with a very loud laugh — Xiao-Yang.

"Welcome!" Xiao-Yang spread his arms wide, smiling like the sun. "Welcome to the other Haisheng Elementary!"

"Keep it down, the whole school's going to hear you," B Ching-Ching laughed and shoved him.

Ching-Ching looked at Xiao-Yang, then at herself. In this world, even the class monitor was always smiling.

"Come on! Let's show you around the school!" Xiao-Yang swung his arm in a grand gesture.

The seven of them set off in a bustling group.

First stop: the Class 6-1 classroom.

The bulletin board at the back of the classroom was covered in drawings. Landscapes, animals, comics — a riot of colors, so full they were about to spill off.

"All of these were drawn by Hsu An-An!" Da-ge introduced her. "She's amazing at drawing — the pride of our class!"

Lin Che thought of the An-An in World A — the girl who always hid her sketchbook in the deepest part of her desk drawer.

The same An-An. A different world.

Second stop: the little farm behind the classroom.

A few small plots growing scallions, water spinach, and strawberries. A lush patch of green, with an orange cat sunbathing on the field ridge.

"Our class adopted it!" B Ching-Ching said. "Xiao-Yang is the farm manager."

"Feel free to steal strawberries anytime!" Xiao-Yang laughed loudly. "I mean — come pick them!"

A-Da's eyes shone. Over in World A, this was a big stretch of concrete.

Third stop: the slide.

A-Da was the first to charge up. He slid down, ran back up, slid down again.

"We have to move this thing over to our side!"

Da-ge laughed at him: "Hey, you're acting just like me when I was little."

"We're the same age!" A-Da yelled, and the two of them chased each other around the slide.

Lin Che watched them, and the corners of his mouth lifted without him noticing.

Halfway through, Lin Che suddenly asked B Lin Che: "Can everyone in your class see the mirror?"

"Of course." B Lin Che nodded. "All twenty-six of us can see it."

"What about adults?"

B Lin Che shook his head: "We tried. We brought Teacher Chen to take a look—"

"And?" Ching-Ching leaned in immediately.

"The teacher said it was just a very dirty mirror," Da-ge cut in. "And told us to go get a rag and wipe it clean."

"My brother came to look too," B Lin Che said. "He's in eighth grade now. He couldn't see anything at all, and said we were acting in a movie."

Ching-Ching lowered her head and wrote in her notebook: "Adults can't see it. Eighth graders can't see it either."

"Why can only we see it?" A-Da asked.

Everyone looked at each other.

"Because there's no more magic in grown-ups' eyes!" Xiao-Yang put his hands on his hips and announced loudly.

Everyone burst out laughing. Though they all knew that wasn't the real answer.

Fourth stop: the bookstore by the school gate.

Haisheng Bookstore. A blue sign, a wooden door frame, and a little blackboard out front that read: "Today's Recommendation: The Library by the Sea."

The glass door swung open, and an auntie with a kerchief on her head stepped out.

"Oh my, it's you little rascals." She beamed. "School's out early today?"

"Boss lady!" B Ching-Ching greeted her.

The boss lady pulled a stack of stickers from her apron pocket, one for each of them: "Here, here, there's enough for everyone."

Lin Che took his sticker. It was a small blue whale.

He suddenly remembered the pencil in his pocket. A blue body, with the words "Haisheng Bookstore" printed on it.

Exactly the same four words.

"Auntie," he blurted out, "do you... do you sell pencils here?"

"Pencils?" The boss lady thought for a moment. "We do — we've always sold them. Do you want one? I'll give you one for free!"

Lin Che shook his head and gripped the pencil in his pocket: "No need. I already have one."

Just as they were about to leave—

The sign flickered.

The blue "Haisheng Bookstore" turned into a green 7-ELEVEN for a single instant.

It lasted only a second, but all seven children saw it.

"Wh... what was that just now?" Even Da-ge's voice changed.

The World B children stood frozen, faces pale.

"It's starting on your side too." Ching-Ching opened her notebook, her voice heavy. "Over on our side, the 7-Eleven flashes into a bookstore. On your side, the bookstore flashes into a 7-Eleven."

"What in the world is going on..." B Ching-Ching murmured.

"Overwriting," Ching-Ching said. "The two worlds are overwriting each other."

Standing at the bookstore entrance, the seven children suddenly found themselves speechless.

The sunset fell on the blue sign. The sea breeze blew over, ruffling the little whale stickers in their hands.

After a long moment, Xiao-Yang spoke first.

"Then what are we waiting for?" he said loudly. "Let's hurry up and find the cause!"

"But where do we even start..." A-Da scratched his head.

"With the places that don't match," Ching-Ching said. "Every place where the two worlds don't line up is a clue."

B Ching-Ching nodded: "We'll write down all the strange things on our side too. Let's investigate together!"

"Both classes, together." Xiao-Yang held out his hand.

Da-ge stacked his hand on top. Then B Ching-Ching. Then B Lin Che.

The three of them glanced at each other.

Ching-Ching was the first to reach out and stack her hand on top. Then A-Da. And finally, Lin Che.

Seven hands stacked together, raised high—

"Yay—!"

Time was almost up. The three of them dashed back to the auditorium and jumped into the mirror.

Back in the old auditorium of World A, the three of them were still catching their breath.

"Day five." Ching-Ching opened her notebook and wrote: "The overwriting goes both ways."

As they walked out of the auditorium, A-Da suddenly stopped and pointed at the empty lot behind the playground.

"You guys, look!"

All three turned at the same time.

In the sunset, on that empty lot, a faint shadow had appeared—

The shadow of a slide.

The shadow was very faint, like a pencil sketch washed over by water. But it was unmistakably the shape of an entire slide.

"It's... coming over," Lin Che said softly.

No one spoke.

The sea breeze swept across the empty playground.

The shadow slowly faded away.

But all three of them understood — the boundary between the two worlds was growing thinner.

And time was running out.
