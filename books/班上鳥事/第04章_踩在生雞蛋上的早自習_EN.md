# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 4: Walking on Raw Eggs During Morning Study

If Class 6-1 a few minutes earlier had resembled an active volcano on the verge of catastrophic eruption, then now, the entire room felt as though it had been flash-frozen and plunged eight thousand meters into the Mariana Trench.

*“Thump, thump, thump!”*

The sound of Zhou Ziqiang rapping his knuckles against the floor by the back door rang out as distinctly as a funeral toll in the deadened air.

Down the hallway, the crisp click of high heels against terrazzo floor tiles was drawing steadily nearer at a measured cadence of two paces per second—*“clack, clack, clack, clack.”* Light, rhythmic, and steeped in the elegance of one freshly graduated from the halls of teacher training, the sound nonetheless ground terrifying fractures across the eardrums of these wild children of Class 6-1.

A-Tang didn't even have time to wipe away the stinging sweat that trickled from his brow straight into his eyes.

Dropping to one knee, his arms wrapped securely around the shoebox housing "Little Ma," he scrambled on hands and knees like a startled civet back to his desk. With excruciating caution, he wedged the shoebox into the deepest recess of his desk compartment, then hastily hauled over his rumpled navy-blue heavy jacket—reeking of baked sunshine—and draped it seamlessly over the box, leaving only the pre-punched ventilation holes clear on either flank.

Immediately afterward, A-Tang leaned forward, pressing his chest flat against the wooden edge of the desk, channeling his body heat through the timber in an unbroken current.

"To your stations."

Without turning her head, her lips scarcely parting, Lu Shuqing issued her martial decree in an ultra-low frequency that was barely more than friction in the air:

"Level One Silence Alert. If anyone makes a sound, I'll stuff them into the mop basin and wring them dry."

Those words were akin to pouring subzero water into boiling fat; all twenty-six students instantly seized up in a state bordering on physiological rigor mortis.

Sneakers previously discarded across the hallway and aisles were stripped off in a blur of frantic, trembling hands. In silent unison, every boy slipped into hole-ridden gray cotton socks, their heels hovering completely aloft, ten toes clutching the freezing floor tiles like talons as they drifted toward their respective desks in an eerie, gliding "phantom float."

The wooden chairs, which on any normal day would screech with ear-splitting ferocity at the slightest nudge, were now gripped firmly by the backrests and seats with white-knuckled intensity. With veins bulging along their arms, the children hoisted the heavy chairs vertically two centimeters off the ground, then lowered them back down—slowly, evenly, with the micro-millimeter precision of bomb disposal technicians placing dynamite upon a cliff's edge.

The entire sequence unfolded with breathtaking fluidity; not a single wooden slat surrendered so much as a whisper of a creak.

Yet the soul enduring the most agonizing torment was none other than physical education monitor Chen Dashan.

This "Big Megaphone," who usually ruled the athletic grounds with his 120-decibel boom, had been drafted by Class President Lu Shuqing to serve as "Human Silencer and Martial Law Commander." His massive bulk was wedged into the second row near the door, his neck as thick as an ancient stump, his face flushed the color of pig liver from oxygen deprivation and sheer forced restraint.

Right at this second, Old Wu, sitting diagonally behind Big Megaphone, underwent a terrifying facial transformation!

A stray shred of cabbage leaf lingering in Old Wu's mouth had accidentally slipped down his trachea. His Adam's apple twitched violently, his nostrils flared in spasms, and physiological tears flooded his eyes—the ultimate physiological catastrophe that no mortal could resist: an imminent, earth-shattering sneeze!

Old Wu reeled backward, his chest expanding with terrifying rapidity, his mouth already gaping open into a wide circle, poised to discharge a sneeze potent enough to rattle the entire wing!

Big Megaphone's eyeballs nearly shot from their sockets!

Without an instant's hesitation, Big Megaphone's calloused right hand—the size of an iron skillet—flashed through the air like a bolt of lightning, his five vise-like fingers snapping shut over Old Wu's mouth and nose with a muffled *smack*! Simultaneously, his left hand balled into an iron fist, vibrating violently less than a centimeter from Old Wu's nose, his usually good-natured visage contorting into the terrifying scowl of a temple guardian with severed facial nerves!

Big Megaphone uttered not a single word, yet his brows, his nostrils, and every ripple of muscle on his cheeks screamed a desperate, silent code:

*【If you dare sneeze, I'll twist your head off and kick it into a desk drawer like a football!】*

Choked within an inch of his life, Old Wu's eyes rolled white, his throat issuing the muffled *glug-glug* of a drowning victim as he forced that titanic sneeze all the way back down into his duodenum.

In that hair-raising nick of time, a vicious silent turf war flared up in the back row.

Two boys who habitually detested each other, fighting over the chalk-drawn "demilitarized line" bisecting their desk, lunged simultaneously for a rolling mechanical pencil.

On any other day, both would have brandished chairs to curse each other's ancestors. But now, four hands locked in midair in a white-knuckled grapple, veins coiling like serpents. The flushed faces of the two youths drew impossibly close, their noses practically colliding, yet not a wisp of breath escaped their lips; only their masseter muscles bulged in manic exertion, their faces as hideous as giant squids tearing into each other in the silent depths of the ocean.

With a trembling index finger, the boy on the right scratched three venomous characters into the wood: 【LET. GO. DOG.】

The boy on the left countered with an insulting twitch of his facial muscles, silently mouthing four emphatic syllables through gritted teeth: 【GET. THE. FUCK. OUT.】

Across the entire classroom, twenty-six souls fought the fundamental human instinct to produce sound in a manner bordering on self-harm.

Even an act as mundane as unzipping a pencil case had been deconstructed into seventeen microscopic procedures: thumb pinching the metal pull-tab, forefinger bracing the fabric edge, inching forward at the glacial pace of one millimeter per second, each interlocking metal tooth engaging like delicate vascular surgery where the slightest misstep would detonate the patient's heart.

Meanwhile, in the front row before the podium, Lu Shuqing sat in tranquil repose.

Her right hand rested upon her weighty copy of *Full-Color Comparative Anatomy*, seemingly engrossed in a diagram of avian pectoral muscle skeletal attachments. Yet beneath the desk, her left hand was clenched into a fist so tight her fingernails dug deep into her palm.

Because deep within her cerebral cortex, that self-proclaimed "Imperial Lord" was unleashing a devastating psychic barrage:

*“Hot! Scorching! Murder!”*

Little Ma's shrill, arrogant voice was shrieking in exasperation within Shuqing's mind:

*“Is that filthy male ape crushing me with his belly?! What did he consume this morning—garlic or fermented sweet potatoes?! This blistering heat is like the sulfur pits of the underworld! Tell him to peel that foul rag off me! My royal contour feathers are being incinerated!”*

A violent twitch seized the corner of Shuqing's eyebrow.

Marshaling a merciless psychic hammer in the depths of her consciousness, she slammed it down without pity:

*【Complain one more time, and I'll have Lin Jingtang flatten you into a sparrow-shaped pancake.】*

*“Insolence! Ill-mannered female ape!”* Little Ma fluttered its uninjured left wing within the box in feeble protest. *“This is a legitimate petition for fundamental survival rights! Furthermore, the ambient humidity here violates all acceptable parameters, and the particulate concentration exceeds seventy percent! Is this hovel plastered together with lime dust and monkey dander?! Cough... cough! If my royal respiratory tract suffers permanent impairment, your entire tribe could never compensate me!”*

*【Quiet. The enemy is outside. Make another peep, and I will pour povidone-iodine down your gullet,】* Shuqing threatened chillingly in her mind.

Sensing the unadulterated murderous intent radiating from Shuqing's consciousness, Little Ma finally grumbled in defeat, tucking its head deeper into the soft paper nest, though still muttering a parting grievance under its breath:

*“...Savages. An utterly barbaric tribe. Once my wings have fully regenerated, I shall summon every raptor in the mountains to level this concrete grotto to the ground...”*

*Click.*

The final crisp tap of a high heel came to a dead halt outside the open front doorway of Class 6-1.

In that single instant, even the faintest heartbeats in the classroom seemed to freeze solid. Twenty-six pairs of eyes locked in unblinking, synchronized focus onto the sunlit patch of hallway floor just beyond the threshold.

A slender, graceful shadow fell first across the terrazzo floor before the teacher's podium.

Next, a foot clad in a gentle cream-white low-heeled pump crossed the classroom threshold.

Substitute Homeroom Teacher Wen Yating finally stepped into the dead center of Class 6-1's collective field of view.

Today, Wen Yating wore a soft, pale-yellow knit cardigan paired with a gracefully pleated light-gray skirt, her thick black hair swept into an elegant chignon with a tortoiseshell clip, exposing a neck as slender and fair as a swan's. She clutched a six-hundred-page volume of *Contemporary School Counseling Practices* to her breast, while her other hand carried a tasteful canvas tote containing a concealed canister of high-potency pepper spray, two boxes of gastrointestinal pills, and the staff first-aid manual specially signed off by the principal.

During the three minutes leading up to her entrance, Wen Yating had stood around the corner of the corridor taking ten deep, deliberate breaths against the wall.

She was an educational master's graduate steeped in rigorous academic discipline. In her philosophical worldview, there were no inherently bad students, only unhealed psychological wounds. Before setting foot in this building, she had constructed the most thorough, heroic psychological defenses imaginable:

She had anticipated a bucket of viscous black ink tumbling from the transom window overhead;
She had anticipated a colossal toad leaping straight onto her chest the moment she opened the podium drawer;
She had even prepared herself for a transparent tripwire strung across the podium to send her sprawling flat on her face.

She had gone so far as to script her opening remark in her notebook: *“Class, I know you wish to test my boundaries, but love has no boundaries.”*

Yet—

The instant Wen Yating crossed the threshold with a saintly, martyred smile, she felt as though she had slammed headlong into an invisible wall of solid rubber, freezing rigid right in the center of the podium.

The anticipated hurricane had not arrived.

The anticipated jeers, shrieks, paper airplanes, and airborne chalk stubs were nowhere to be found.

What met the young teacher's gaze was a surreal tableau she had never encountered in her twenty-four years of life, not even in her most feverish nightmares.

The entire classroom was clean, bright, and as reverently somber as a sixteenth-century monastery perched atop an Alpine summit.

Morning light bathed twenty-six student desks in serene tranquility.

Every single student—all twenty-six hellions whom three prior educators had annotated in files as possessing "manic antisocial tendencies"—sat bolt upright without exception, their hands folded in immaculate symmetry across the center of their desks like finely chiseled marble statues.

Nobody spoke.

Nobody fidgeted.

There was not even the customary rustle of turning a textbook page.

In the front row by the window, the class president—as delicate as a classical ink-wash painting—held a brick-thick medical tome, her gaze devout and merciful, radiating the profound wisdom of an ascetic who had transcended the cycle of life and death.

And in the dead center of the room, Lin Jingtang—the "Demon King" said to be drenched in the professional blood of countless instructors—lay draped across his desk in an attitude of profound, touching reverence. His cheek was pressed flat against the wood, curling like an infant asleep in the womb, his eyes gazing toward the podium with glassy serenity, his lips even graced with a saintly, peaceful smile.

Wen Yating's fingers clamped tight around her counseling text, her knuckles blanching with strain.

Her throat went terribly dry, a sudden, bone-chilling draft shooting straight up her spine to the base of her skull.

Th-This... this wasn't right.

This was horrifyingly wrong!

As a devoted disciple of Adlerian psychology, Wen Yating understood the compensatory mechanisms governing human behavior. The performance of these children had entirely transcended the physical boundaries of "being quiet"—this was an extreme, biologically unnatural, surreal stupor!

Could this be... the legendary "collective dissociative catatonia"?

Or had the departures of three consecutive teachers inflicted such irreversible collective trauma that, when confronted with their fourth instructor, these fragile children had activated the highest-order defense mechanism: "Extreme Cold-Violence Emotional Isolation"?!

Standing before the podium, the piece of chalk in Wen Yating's palm grew damp and crumbly with cold sweat.

Parting her lips, she attempted to pierce this maddening, eerie silence:

"G-Good morning, class..."

Wen Yating's soft, melodious voice rippled through the air with a distinct tremble.

Under normal circumstances, such a greeting would be answered by a chorus of rowdy barks: *"Morning, Teacher—drop dead soon—!"*

Yet today, not a single soul below opened their mouth.

Confronted with their new teacher's greeting, all twenty-six students merely lifted their eyelids in synchronized, slow-motion unison, offering Wen Yating a stiff, benevolent, yet hollow smile that made her scalp tingle with dread.

Then, with an oscillation more delicate than a breeze stirring a spider's silk, the entire class nodded in unison.

The scene resembled twenty-six venerable monks offering a silent Buddhist benediction to a wayward pilgrim.

*Plip.*

A single bead of cold sweat rolled noiselessly down Wen Yating's sculpted chin, landing with a heavy splash upon the sign-in register.

Three minutes into her tenure in Class 6-1, the star graduate of the Normal University Psychological Institute finally felt her knees knocking together in helpless terror.

For the first time in her life, she discovered that the most terrifying weapons in existence were neither spring-loaded traps nor rubber rattlesnakes.

It was silence.

A holy, immaculate silence potent enough to drive a human soul to madness.
