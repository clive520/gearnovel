# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 6: Millet and Cotton Swabs on the Tip of the Tongue

At nine-ten in the morning, the preparatory bell for the second period—mathematics—chimed along the corridor in a sequence of metallic, reverberating thuds.

Standing around the bend in the hallway, her spine braced against the cold, rough pebble-dashed wall, Wen Yating downed three gastrointestinal pills followed by half a sachet of liquid antacid.

Minutes earlier in the ground-floor Student Affairs Office, Director Zhou Yan had brandished his three-section telescopic baton, coming within an inch of leading four groundskeepers on a raid up to the third floor to search Class 6-1. It was Wen Yating who had wrapped both arms desperately around Director Zhou's elbow at the final second. As an intellectual baptized in the waters of higher teacher education, her professional dignity refused to let her bow to corporal punishment and authoritarian force within her very first hour on the job.

"Love and empathy are the sole remedies," Wen Yating muttered to the wall, patting her flushed cheeks vigorously with both hands. "You can do this, Wen Yating. They are merely exhibiting collective selective withdrawal born of post-traumatic stress. If you embrace them with unconditional love, the thickest ice will eventually melt."

Having reconstructed her psychological fortifications, Wen Yating drew a deep breath and, treading as softly as possible in her flat leather shoes, stepped once more across the front threshold of Class 6-1.

Yet the young teacher, brimming with lofty educational zeal, could never have conceived that down below the podium, not a single one of the twenty-six students sitting as upright as pine trees held the slightest thought of Adler or Bai Juyi.

Every single nerve ending in the entire room was held hostage by the stomach of the miniature tyrant resting in the deepest recess of a desk drawer.

*“CHEEP——!!”*

A protest—infinitesimally faint, yet piercing enough to drill clean through a mortal skull—was hammering frantically like a war drum within the marrow of Lu Shuqing's brain via telepathic link.

*“Starving! My imperial stomach is being eaten through by its own gastric juices!”*

Within the shoebox, Little Ma was clawing frantically at the paper shavings with its right talon, its yellow-rimmed beak snapping open and shut with manic desperation in the dark:

*“Do these low-caste apes comprehend nothing of avian metabolic rates?! My body temperature registers at forty-two degrees Celsius! Forty-two degrees! Two hours without nourishment, and my blood sugar has plummeted into the Mariana Trench! Serve the royal banquet at once! Fresh grass seeds! Newly hulled millet! Delay any longer, and I shall commit regicide by chewing my own contour feathers to death!”*

In her seat by the window in the front row, Lu Shuqing's posture remained impeccably upright, yet the corner of her lips was twitching at a microscopic amplitude.

Tilting her head ever so slightly, she feigned brushing aside a stray wisp of hair from her brow, her fingernail tapping out three long beats and two short beats against her spectacle frames with blazing speed.

In the row behind, A-Tang instantly received the encrypted transmission: 【The Supreme Commander has entered hypoglycemic berserk mode; initiate the underground supply line immediately.】

Cold sweat beaded instantly across A-Tang's brow.

Feeding a bird might appear idyllic and heartwarming in a zoo nature documentary; yet within a classroom presided over by a paranoid master's graduate in psychology, the operational difficulty rivaled assembling a nuclear warhead beneath the sofa of the Oval Office.

First came the rationing of provisions.

As the daughter of an experienced country veterinarian, Shuqing had penned a ruthlessly strict, borderline obsessive "Fledgling Emergency Nutritional Formula" during morning homeroom: 40% hard-boiled egg yolk paste (supplying lecithin and premium lipids), 30% sugar-free, warm-water-softened pure oat mash (supplying carbohydrates), 20% ultra-fine ground calcium powder, and 10% trace glucose electrolyte solution.

If any solid particulate exceeded a single millimeter, it carried the lethal risk of choking off Little Ma's straw-thin esophagus.

And this ultra-precision, specialized military ration was currently held in the custody of Old Wu, seated directly behind physical education monitor Chen Dashan.

Old Wu sat dead center in the third row.

At this moment, the chubby lad—whose daily existence usually revolved entirely around gluttonous gorging—exhibited the discipline of a veteran special agent. He plastered his plump torso flush against the leading edge of his desk, his crossed legs forming an impenetrable visual blind spot below. Cradled within the palm of his left hand was a tiny sphere wrapped in sterile tin foil—a paste of hard-boiled egg yolk and softened oats that he had painstakingly kneaded two hundred times against his palm using the beveled edge of a plastic ruler during the ten-minute recess.

It was as silken and delicate as the soufflé of a world-class French patisserie.

"Now, class, please turn to page fourteen of your mathematics textbook; we shall examine our first sample problem."

Standing at the podium, Wen Yating spoke with gentle sweetness as she turned to face the blackboard.

Determined not to trigger that terrifying scene of "collective ear-covering" again, Wen Yating had deliberately selected the softest stick of colored chalk available, applying it as delicately as a swan's feather brushing across raw silk.

In the identical microsecond Wen Yating's back turned and the chalk grazed the slate—

"Move!"

Without turning her head, merely vibrating her throat muscles, Lu Shuqing released a breath-thin syllable.

In an instant, the underground logistics grid of Class 6-1 sprang into precision motion at ten meters per second!

With a subtle flick of Old Wu's wrist, that olive-shaped foil packet skimmed across the polished terrazzo floor like a miniature curling stone, tracing an arrow-straight, noiseless trajectory straight toward the space beneath A-Tang's desk!

Having already discarded his shoes, A-Tang's five sock-clad toes were as dextrous as a chimpanzee's fingers. The split second the foil packet slid beneath his desk, his toe muscles flexed with explosive precision, snatching the packet mid-glide and hoisting it into the lip of his desk drawer without a whisper of sound!

The entire handoff transpired in precisely 0.8 seconds, leaving not a single scuff mark upon the floor.

And in that very heartbeat, Wen Yating at the podium had just completed the words "Given triangle ABC."

A flutter of apprehension in her chest, Wen Yating instinctively glanced back over her shoulder to gauge her students' reactions—

*“SWOOSH!”*

The entire room completed a multi-dimensional metamorphosis in 0.01 seconds.

A-Tang sat as straight as an alpine pine, his right hand gripping a needle-sharp pencil, his gaze burning with an insatiable, fiery thirst for Euclidean geometry as he drilled into the triangle on the board without so much as flaring a nostril; every student around him sat in solemn rectitude, twenty-six pencils scratching across scrap paper in disciplined unison, their expressions as grave as finalists seated at the International Mathematical Olympiad.

Wen Yating blinked in astonishment, a wave of indescribable warmth and validation washing over her heart.

"Everyone... is so terribly earnest," Wen Yating murmured with a bashful purse of her lips. The heavy boulder upon her chest easing slightly, she turned back to continue writing out the equation.

The soft scraping of chalk resumed from the blackboard.

A-Tang's mission officially entered its most perilous, high-stakes phase.

Pinning his knees tight against the desk frame, his left hand reached deep into the compartment, delicately peeling back a corner of the heavy navy-blue jacket.

The shoebox lid was slid open a three-centimeter crack.

Faint morning light pooled into the box, illuminating Little Ma's dust-gray head bobbing in frantic agitation.

*“Hurry! You clumsy ape! I am about to faint from inanition! Present the royal tribute at once!”*

Little Ma's shrill, arrogant telepathic complaints continued battering Shuqing's mind, while Shuqing relayed clinical instructions through barely perceptible ventriloquism to the rear: "Moisten the swab. Scoop a mung-bean-sized portion. Insert at a forty-five-degree angle from the corner of the beak. Do not obstruct the nares."

A-Tang's fingers were trembling.

These hands could shatter a beer bottle from thirty paces with a slingshot, or flatten Black Charcoal with a solid brick, yet gripping this featherweight wooden cotton swab, they felt as though hoisting a ten-thousand-pound iron mallet.

Using the cotton tip, A-Tang delicately absorbed a solitary drop of lukewarm water, then rolled it gently against the foil's edge, lifting a tiny yellow paste pellet scarcely a third the size of a mung bean.

Holding his breath, bringing his face flush with the opening of the drawer, he locked down every involuntary twitch of his nostrils.

Trembling with care, that pellet infused with the aroma of egg yolk and oats hovered poised before Little Ma's beak.

Little Ma's yellow-rimmed mouth had been primed to unleash a barrage of royal curses, but the moment that rich, savory egg fragrance drifted into its sesame-sized nostrils, the fallen aristocratic avian froze solid.

*“Wh... What is this scent?”*

Little Ma's black-bead eyes dilated into wide saucers.

Its tiny beak reflexively parted a hairline crack.

Moving with lightning precision and angelic tenderness, A-Tang swiped the swab tip across the gap, delivering the soft, warm paste smoothly onto Little Ma's parched tongue.

A fledgeling's swallow was clumsy and comical. Little Ma's scrawny, bare neck stretched upward with a violent heave, the delicate pink throat muscles rippling as the nutrient paste slid down the esophagus into its hollow, deflated crop.

In that fleeting second, the interior of the shoebox plummeted into an eerie stillness.

Seated in the front row, Lu Shuqing could distinctly hear the arrogant creature sucking in a breath of transcendent shock within the psychic realm:

*“...Good heavens.”*

That formerly acerbic, piercing voice now carried a rare, disoriented ecstasy, as though its very soul had taken flight:

*“Th-This velvety richness... this profound, earthy sweetness... Could this be the fabled 'Phoenix Nectar' spoken of only in the Mythic Epoch?!”*

*“Slurp... To think these hairless giant apes have mastered alchemy of such sublime caliber?!”*

Within the shoebox, the fledgling kicked its claws in a frenzy of greed, those previously dull black eyes flaring with emerald famishment. Rearing its head, stretching its tender beak open to an impossible angle, its throat issued a series of eager, wheedling gasps:

*“More! Another spoonful for your Sovereign! Faster! Clumsy male ape, insert that white rod once more! I grant you royal pardon for suffocating my belly earlier! Be quick!”*

Watching the tiny creature craning its neck in ravenous, adorable desperation within the drawer, the tight, terrified grimace on A-Tang's face broke into an uncontrollable, tender upward curve.

Scooping another droplet of yolk mash with the swab—his movements far more fluid now—he delivered it into that tiny mouth with boundless patience and reverence.

One pellet, two pellets, three pellets...

Little Ma swallowed like an industrial paper shredder on overdrive. Its emaciated chest, where rib bones had previously been visible, swelled into a plump, rounded pouch before A-Tang's very eyes, as if a fat quail's egg had been tucked beneath its downy feathers.

Morning light slanted through the box's air holes, dappling across the scrapes on the back of A-Tang's hand.

The youth kept his head lowered, beads of sweat glistening across his brow, his gaze so intensely focused it seemed the entire cosmos had contracted into this single shoebox. Throughout the rough-and-tumble savagery of his childhood, he had never experienced a feeling like this—that somewhere in this wide world existed a life so impossibly fragile, sustained entirely by the gentleness of his fingertips, the warmth of his flesh, and the vigilance of his care.

If he grew careless for an instant, this spark of light would snuff out; yet as long as he remained tender, that spark would burn brighter, warmer, and stronger.

A strange, burning emotion sprouted and twined around the roots of A-Tang's rugged heart. It was not the thrill of conquest, but a heavy, sanctified joy known as "responsibility."

Yet this covert undercover feeding operation, viewed through the eyes of Wen Yating upon the podium, had transformed into an entirely different, earth-shattering psychological phenomenon.

Throughout the forty-minute mathematics period, Wen Yating whipped her head around every three minutes on the dot.

With each turn, she sought to catch the crack in her students' prankster facade.

Yet each scene that greeted her eyes proved more staggering, more heart-rending than the last!

For forty continuous minutes, not a single one of the twenty-six students whispered to a neighbor, not one glanced out the window, and not a single soul even requested a restroom pass!

Particularly Lin Jingtang, seated dead center in the room—the school's most notorious delinquent, troublemaker, and thug!

His gaze was so scorching, so profoundly anchored, as though he sought to fuse his very soul into the desk before him! Though both his hands remained hidden beneath the desk (Wen Yating assumed he was pinching his thighs in agony to maintain concentration), his face radiated the saintly luminescence of a holy martyr! Those beads of sweat rolling down his brow were undeniably the physical hallmarks of cerebral overexertion during high-intensity mental arithmetic!

And every other student in the class—each time Wen Yating pivoted, their collective gazes snapped in synchronized focus toward the center of the room, every eye weighted with a solemn, breathless gravity, as if the fate of all humanity hung in the balance!

"They..."

The fingers gripping the chalk trembled violently, warm tears welling unbidden within Wen Yating's eyes.

"They aren't possessed at all..."

Within the depths of her soul, Wen Yating wept in a tumult of grief and euphoric redemption:

"I was mistaken... Adler was right! Buried deep within the heart of every troubled youth lies a seed desperately yearning for recognition, desperately striving for excellence! Prior teachers saw only their rough edges, answering them with violence and cold apathy, driving them behind high, thorn-covered ramparts!"

"Yet today, met with my love and tolerance, they have finally lowered their shields! They are using this torturous, ascetic focus to prove to me that they are not wicked children! They are crying out from the depths of their souls: *'Teacher, look upon us! We, too, wish to master geometry!'*"

Sniffling her stinging nose, Wen Yating's heart hammered so hard with gratitude it threatened to burst through her ribs.

This was an educational miracle!

This was a historic triumph worthy of immortalization in the provincial teacher training white papers!

*“DING-DONG-DING-DONG—”*

The dismissal bell chimed right on schedule.

This time, Wen Yating did not flee.

Standing tall and dignified in the center of the podium, cradling her textbook to her bosom, she gazed out through tear-brimmed eyes at all twenty-six students.

"Cl... Class," Wen Yating choked back a sob, bathed in the sacred glow of maternal devotion, "Teacher is... profoundly moved today. Thank you for revealing to me the pure strength residing in the depths of your souls. This period has been the finest mathematics lesson of my entire career."

Below, the students exchanged bewildered glances, their eyes glazed over, scarcely daring to inhale.

Beneath his desk, A-Tang stealthily snapped the shoebox lid shut, stuffing the remaining half of the yolk-stained cotton swab down into his sock, gazing in utter mystification at Teacher Wen, who was weeping like a rain-soaked pear blossom on the podium.

Inside the shoebox, having gorged itself to satisfaction, the little sparrow rolled onto its back, its round, taut belly facing the ceiling, and emitted an audible, tiny burp laden with the scent of cooked egg yolk into the soft paper nest:

*“BURP~~~~.”*

In the front row, Lu Shuqing silently snapped her notebook shut, pushing up her glasses with a deadpan expression, releasing a long, imperceptible sigh in her heart.

The misunderstanding, it appeared, was galloping toward an irreversible abyss at a terrifying, hilarious velocity.
