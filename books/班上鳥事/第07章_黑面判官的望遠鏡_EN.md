# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 7: The Black-Faced Judge's Binoculars

In the campus folklore of Luyang Elementary School, the office drawers of Student Affairs Director Zhou Yan had long harbored three sacred relics revered as taboos by generations of graduates: a hefty sixty-centimeter brass disciplinary ruler, a box of navy-blue carbon paper reserved exclusively for recording demerits and formal warnings, and a pair of Russian-made 8x military-grade binoculars decommissioned from army special forces.

At this exact moment, these heavy rubber-armored binoculars—their lenses glinting with a baleful ultraviolet sheen under the midday sun—poked their twin barrels slowly from the dense blossoms of a neatly trimmed bougainvillea hedge along the second-floor library balcony of the Integrated Activity Building.

The reticle of the crosshairs was locked unblinkingly onto a classroom two hundred meters away, at the easternmost terminus of the third floor of the Zhongxiao Building.

Director Zhou Yan lay prone across the concrete windowsill.

Upon the bridge of his nose sat the same rimless gold-wire spectacles he had worn for decades, his gaunt, sharply chiseled face shrouded in shadow, his razor-thin lips pressed into an uncompromising, bloodless line.

To his left rested an open black leather-bound hardcover notebook. Upon its freshest page, penned in glaring scarlet ballpoint ink, was a colossal Arabic numeral framed by three bold exclamation marks—

**"0"**.

Zero.

One entire, unbroken week.

From Monday dawn to Friday afternoon: five full instructional days, forty class periods, two thousand four hundred minutes.

Upon the schoolwide weekly disciplinary infraction tally finalized by the Student Affairs Office every Friday, Class 6-1 had turned in a chilling, unprecedented, blood-freezing scorecard of immaculate blankness:

Running in corridors: Zero instances.
Classroom disruption or insolence: Zero instances.
Willful destruction of public property: Zero instances.
Missing homework or uniform violations: Zero instances.
Even the corridor mops—which were invariably hurled as javelins whenever rain fell—hung inverted in pristine, inspected ranks upon the drying racks, entirely free of dust.

When the student teacher had presented this tally onto the grand desk of the Student Affairs Office this morning, every veteran educator in the room had bolted to their feet simultaneously, their faces blanching as if handed an unconditional declaration of war signed by the instigators of World War III.

"When things are abnormal, demons lurk."

Lowering the binoculars, Zhou Yan pressed his index fingers firmly into his aching, parched eyeballs, a guttural rasp escaping his throat like a bloodhound catching the scent of fresh gore.

Thirty years in disciplinary administration had endowed Zhou Yan with the razor-sharp predatory instincts of a wild beast. He understood the rascals of Luyang Elementary far too well, especially the hellions of Class 6-1 whose veins flowed with diesel and gunpowder—a biological species that would suffer acute dermal necrosis if prevented from raising a racket for three consecutive hours!

An entire week without a peep? An entire week of law-abiding perfection?

This was by no means a moral epiphany, nor was it some miracle of Adlerian psychology wrought by that greenhorn chit Wen Yating!

In the physical laws of the natural world, the waters across an entire bay draw back hundreds of meters in eerie tranquility on the eve of a tsunami; half an hour before a category-five typhoon makes landfall, atmospheric pressure plummets to its absolute nadir, the sky bruised a spectral, suffocating violet!

"The barometric depression before the tempest..."

Grinding his teeth, Zhou Yan flipped open the notebook and drew a thick, vicious red underscore beneath that glaring "0":

"Those little beasts are lying in collective dormancy. They are secretly orchestrating a graduation riot of unprecedented proportions, potent enough to level the entire campus."

Drawing a long, steadying breath, Zhou Yan hoisted the heavy binoculars back to his eyes. The knurled focus ring yielded a soft metallic click as his vision bored through the sprawling canopy of the ancient banyan fronting the Zhongxiao Building, piercing cleanly through the windows of Class 6-1.

It was currently twelve-twenty in the afternoon: statutory schoolwide naptime.

On any prior Friday lunch break, the windowsills of Class 6-1 would have served as fortified tactical redoubts. Paper planes would have darkened the courtyard like locust swarms, hyperactive boys would have straddled the sills spitting watermelon seeds at pedestrians below, and chalk dust would have been channeled as smoke grenades down the vents into the Academic Affairs Office on the second floor.

Yet what Zhou Yan beheld through the high-definition coated glass was a surreal tableau that might have brought a Vatican cardinal to his knees in prayer.

Not a single light was illuminated within the classroom.

The curtains had been drawn two-thirds shut, leaving only a one-foot ribbon of aperture along the sill, casting a flat, razor-sharp golden stripe across the terrazzo floor.

Within the deep gloom bordering that golden ribbon, not a single one of the twenty-six students lay slumped across their desks asleep.

Every soul sat bolt upright in their chairs, their postures locked in a geometric symmetry bordering on the fanatical.

And right in the dead center of the room—

Zhou Yan dialed the focal ring with a violent snap, his pupils shrinking to needlepoints!

Surrounding Lin Jingtang's desk, arrayed in a tight concentric circle resembling some esoteric religious ward, clustered six or seven core conspirators.

Chen Dashan—Big Megaphone—knelt on one knee, his hulking frame serving as an impenetrable black screen obstructing all line-of-sight from the rear windows; Old Wu held the gleaming lid of a stainless steel lunchbox in both hands, his solemnity mirroring an altar boy cradling a paten before a tabernacle; and in the front row, Class President Lu Shuqing leaned slightly askew, the lenses of her dark-rimmed glasses catching the ambient glare as her left hand wielded medical tweezers and her right hovered with a micro-pipette, inching with glacial, microscopic focus into the depths of Lin Jingtang's desk!

And behind that fiercely barricaded desk, Lin Jingtang himself kept his eyes clamped shut.

His visage was bloodless, his lips trembling faintly, his burly arms encircling the desk compartment as if cradling the most sacred, fragile relic in creation, radiating the tragic, sublime resolve of a martyr poised to die for an unspeakable faith!

*Snap!*

The black ballpoint pen in Zhou Yan's grip snapped clean in two under the sheer violence of his grip, viscous black ink pooling across his fingertips in a glaring blotch.

"A séance..."

A film of bone-chilling sweat broke out across Zhou Yan's neck, his Adam's apple bobbing in terror, his skull reverberating with deafening alarm bells:

"What Wen Yating wept about in the office was real?! Those lawless brats are genuinely conducting feudal superstition in the classroom, summoning an eldritch entity from the void?!"

Snatching up the notebook, Zhou Yan used the jagged fracture of the plastic pen to gouge lines of murderous notes into the paper:

【12:23 PM. Target class entirely unslept. Concentric ritual circle established around Subject Lin Jingtang; Subject Lu Shuqing wielding metallic ceremonial instruments; Subject Chen Dashan commanding perimeter defenses. Demeanor fanatical, solemn. Suspected dangerous collective psychogenic mass ritual underway. Threat Assessment: MAXIMUM.】

Snapping the journal shut, Zhou Yan sprang forward like an enraged black leopard, snatching the iron disciplinary binder from the table, preparing to storm up to the third floor to catch them in the act!

Yet the split second his boots crossed the threshold of the library, another inconceivable vision pinned his legs to the tiles.

At the base of the stairwell leading to the third floor of the Zhongxiao Building, a massive, towering silhouette drifted silently from the shadows of the corridor with the eerie fluidity of liquid mercury.

It was physical education monitor Chen Dashan.

This human dreadnought—160 centimeters tall, sixty-five kilograms, whose casual stomping could rattle the ground-floor ceiling fans—currently padded across the tiles clad only in a pair of threadbare, thick-soled gym socks.

With every stride he took, his toes caressed the floor first, followed by a slight outward roll of the lateral foot, before the heel kissed the chilly stone with featherweight delicacy. Sixty-five kilograms of dead weight, mediated by this sequence of anti-biomechanical shock absorption, failed to produce even half a micro-decibel of acoustic reverberation across the marble expanse!

Right at that heartbeat, a boy from neighboring Class 6-2, having just finished his lunch and swinging two popsicles by their sticks, came sauntering up the stairs with a carefree swagger, whistling a tuneless ditty.

The whistle was crisp, piercing, and shockingly offensive within the sepulchral hush of the hall—

*“Wheep—whoo—wheep—”*

In that microsecond, Zhou Yan witnessed a horror that would haunt his dreams to his dying day.

Standing with his back to the landing, Big Megaphone Chen Dashan stiffened as though hit by ten thousand volts, every follicle on the nape of his neck standing on end in a thousandth of a second!

Big Megaphone snapped his head around.

His broad, usually good-natured face warped into a monstrous, predatory mask akin to a deep-sea abyssal carnivore. His eyeballs bulged, his nostrils flared in violent spasms, his mouth peeling back in a feral, skin-crawling sneer as his entire bulk launched forward like a silenced cannonball, surfing the wall in a terrifying, soundless glide across five meters of tile!

Before the boy from Class 6-2 could whistle the fourth note—

*“MMMPH—?!”*

A massive hand the size of a braided palm fan, encrusted with calluses and sweat, descended from behind like a set of tempered steel manacles, clamping over the lower half of the boy's face with surgical, brutal finality!

Simultaneously, Big Megaphone's left arm locked into a reverse sleeper hold, operating with the cold economy of a jungle commando snatching a sentry, hoisting the forty-kilogram boy clean off his feet and pinning him flush against his chest.

The entire sequence—choke, muzzle, and takedown—flowed with seamless artistry, taking no more than 0.5 seconds from initiation to completion.

Throughout the entire ambush, aside from the faint, brittle *click* of the dropped popsicles hitting the floor, not a single whimper escaped down the hallway!

Hauling the frantically kicking boy backwards in a spectral glide, Big Megaphone dragged him silently into the deepest stall of the boys' lavatory.

Through the narrow seam of the stall door, Zhou Yan faintly caught Big Megaphone's throat rasping out an ultimate, blood-curdling aspirate threat, like a rabid hound gnashing its teeth:

*【Whistle one more time... and I'll shove those popsicle sticks clean up your nostrils... Blink if you understand me!】*

Rooted at the corner of the stairwell, Student Affairs Director Zhou Yan stood entirely petrified.

Cold sweat drenched his shirt in successive waves, his knuckles gripping the iron binder trembling violently with pure, unadulterated shock.

Th-This was no childish mischief.

This was a premeditated, organized special operations strike unit equipped with dedicated silenced sentries!

Even the corridor defensive perimeter had been constructed with such flawless rigor that any uncontrolled acoustic variable would be subjected to physical "targeted neutralization" within half a second!

Zhou Yan swallowed with immense difficulty, his esophagus feeling so parched it threatened to strike sparks.

Meanwhile, in the dim sanctuary of Class 6-1 on the third floor, around the desk Zhou Yan had branded the "altar of the dark séance," the true covert operation had reached its absolute climax.

The shoebox lid had been nudged half open.

Morning light was entirely blocked by A-Tang's broad shoulders, leaving behind a warm, soothing veil of twilight.

Nourished by a solid week of rich egg-yolk mash, Little Ma's previously shriveled frame had visibly plumped into a rotund little sphere, like an inflated balloon. Across its pink, bare skin, neat rows of dark-brown tubular pinfeathers were thrusting forth like bamboo shoots after a spring deluge. The popsicle-stick splints secured by Shuqing remained rock-solid along the fractured wing, yet Little Ma's glossy, beady head could now be hoisted with regal, insufferable arrogance.

At this very moment, Lu Shuqing was using tweezers to grip a sterile cotton ball soaked in lukewarm tea, tenderly wiping away the minute grime adhering to Little Ma's right talons.

Within the psychic plane, the imperious Lord was issuing ceaseless royal decrees:

*“The left! The left talon! A grass seed is lodged there! Do you female apes comprehend nothing of the imperial grooming protocol?!”*

*“And that fat one! Keep that bento lid steady! If you dare drop a crumb of my royal yolk puree on the floor, I shall sentence you to three thousand leagues of exile to weed the back mountain!”*

The blue veins along Shuqing's temple twitched faintly. She applied a fraction more pressure with the tweezers without changing her expression; Little Ma gasped sharply in her mind, instantly tucking its claws back into the paper nest.

"Callus formation is progressing splendidly," Shuqing murmured, her voice dialed to near-inaudibility. "One more week, and we can remove the wooden splints to commence basic wing-expansion rehabilitation."

Sprawled across the desk, his nose scarcely three centimeters from the shoebox, A-Tang's dark eyes brimmed with unblemished joy, his nose gently nuzzling the soft down of Little Ma's breast: "Hear that, Little Ma? Qingzi says you'll be flying soon."

Little Ma puffed out its tiny chest proudly, snorting haughtily in Shuqing's mind: *“Hmph! Naturally! My skeletal framework was forged from the sacred Firmiana tree; what is a trifling fracture to my majesty?!”* Yet its little beak nuzzled affectionately against the bridge of A-Tang's scarred nose with unmistakable devotion.

However, just as the core conspirators basked in the miracle of life, Hygiene Monitor Xu Fangyu (Xiao Yu), standing on the perimeter, suddenly turned as white as fresh linen.

Xiao Yu was the school's undisputed germaphobe champion, her olfactory receptors as keen as a bloodhound's.

At this precise heartbeat, Xiao Yu's nostrils quivered violently.

Bending swiftly, she brought her face to the edge of the box, and in the next second, her eyes dilated in the blind panic of one who had stepped squarely upon an anti-tank landmine!

Clutching Shuqing's sleeve, Xiao Yu forced a maximum-level red alert through clenched teeth in a vibration fainter than a mosquito's wing:

"Lu Shuqing... we have a catastrophic emergency."

"It... it just dropped a massive load at the bottom of the box."

"And... the stench has already begun to breach containment."
