# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 12: Feathers the Paper Cannot Wrap

Outside the shattered window frames, the torrential rain roared like the charge of ten thousand galloping stallions.

Yet within this devastated classroom of Class 6-1, time seemed frozen by an agonizingly heavy, suffocating force.

Director Zhou Yan stood framed in the doorway, the long-stemmed black umbrella in his hand steadily dripping icy water onto the terrazzo floor. Upon that elongated visage—known by generations of students behind his back as the "Black-Faced Judge"—the facial muscles were twitching violently with terrifying frequency.

His gaze first swept across the sea of shattered window glass covering the floor, shifted toward the scorched, dislodged ceiling-fan switch that sparked faintly with singed plastic, and finally locked onto the human mountain of over a dozen tangled children who had crushed three wooden desks beneath their collective weight.

Splintered corners, twisted steel legs, hundreds of scattered ballpoint pens, and pools of red and blue ink seeped freely into the standing water on the floor, releasing a pungent, metallic tang.

And at the very summit of that tangled heap of bodies, fresh blood from Lin Jingtang's forehead dripped steadily onto the back of his own hand.

This scene was ten times more catastrophic than any "critical campus violence incident" Zhou Yan had ever processed in the disciplinary archives of the Student Affairs Office.

"Fine... just fine..."

Zhou Yan squeezed the words through the icy depths of his throat, his knuckles whitening as his grip crushed the umbrella's metal ribs.

"A whole month..." He took a heavy stride forward, his leather soles crunching sharply into the broken glass with a sickening crackle. "For an entire month, you acted like mutes in the hallway and monks on the playground! You even hovered your chairs two centimeters off the floor during morning self-study!"

Zhou Yan's chest heaved violently as thirty days of festering suspicion, anxiety, and authoritarian paranoia detonated like a volcanic eruption:

"I knew it! In my fifteen years as Director of Student Affairs, what kind of delinquent haven't I seen?! You lawless little bastards weren't reforming at all—you were brewing a campus-wide riot! You staged a mass brawl right here in the classroom! You vandalized school property! You even smashed the exterior windows into pieces!"

His furious roar echoed endlessly down the long corridor, shaking loose fine clouds of dust from the concrete ceiling.

"Lin Jingtang! Get your ass off that pile!" Zhou Yan pointed a trembling finger directly at the boy's nose, flecks of spittle nearly striking Lin Jingtang's face. "Two major demerits! School-wide public reprimand! I'm calling your father to the school right now! Let's see if Old Lin, with his thirty-five-ton gravel truck, breaks your legs today!"

"Director Zhou... please... I beg of you, stop shouting..."

At that moment, a choked, tear-drenched plea emerged from beneath the teacher's podium.

Wen Yating hauled herself out, clutching the edges of the desk for support. This master's-degree homeroom teacher—who ordinarily wore immaculate suits and spoke with gentle cadence—now had hair disheveled like a madwoman, her knees blackened with soaked chalkboard dust, her modest makeup completely washed away by tears.

"Teacher Wen, do not plead for them!" Zhou Yan cut her off harshly, his glare piercing like a scalpel. "You just graduated from university; you don't understand the treacherous deceit of these little monsters! You think they're innocent children? They are openly defying the school administration! They are trampling upon school regulations!"

"No... Director... it's not like that..."

Wen Yating shook her head through sobs, but she was utterly shell-shocked by the surrounding carnage, unable to muster a single psychological theory to explain this apparent mass hysteria.

Not a single student in the classroom moved.

Even as Zhou Yan's bellows shook the walls like thunder, Fat Monkey, Lao Wu, and Dashenggong remained gritting their teeth on the floor, their backs braced firmly to support Lin Jingtang's weight.

No one retreated; not a single person offered an excuse.

Across twenty-six youthful faces smeared with blood, rain, and grime, there was only a tragic, unflinching silence.

"Are you deaf?! Lin Jingtang, what are you clutching in your hands?!"

Zhou Yan finally registered Lin Jingtang's bizarre posture—this boy, whose temper ordinarily resembled uncontained dynamite, hadn't hurled an insult back. Instead, as if shielding an invaluable celestial treasure, he clutched his blood-stained, calloused hands tightly against his chest.

Instinctive suspicion flared in Zhou Yan's eyes.

A switchblade? A slingshot wire? Or contraband firecrackers smuggled into the school?

"Open your hands!" Zhou Yan lunged forward, his heavy hand clamping onto Lin Jingtang's wrist. "I ordered you to open your hands!"

"DON'T TOUCH IT——!!"

Lin Jingtang snapped his head up, a ferocious, manic gleam erupting in his bloodshot eyes like a wounded wolf defending its den!

He paid zero heed to Zhou Yan's bruising grip pinching his flesh purple; rooted like an anvil welded to stone, the muscles of his arms locked in iron tension, his cupped hands refusing to yield by a fraction of a millimeter!

"You dare resist search?!" Zhou Yan's rage flared out of control as he reached to forcefully pry the boy's fingers apart.

"Director Zhou, please stop."

A clear, cold, yet impossibly calm voice suddenly cut through the chaos of the classroom.

Lu Shuqing rose to her feet beside an overturned desk.

Her pristine white uniform shirt was thoroughly soaked through with rainwater and blue ink; one corner of her spectacles had chipped away, and the short hair framing her cheek dripped steadily with rain. Yet she stood with impeccable posture, her expression as serene as if solving an elementary linear equation.

"Lu Shuqing?" Zhou Yan froze, his brow twisting into a hard knot. "You are the Class President! A countywide honor student! Don't tell me you had a hand in this riot too?!"

"This is not a riot, Director Zhou."

Lu Shuqing did not meet Zhou Yan's incandescent fury; she simply turned, bent down, and retrieved a bruised orange shoebox from a puddle in the debris.

That Nike shoebox was smeared with mud and soaked with water.

Yet the moment Lu Shuqing gently lifted the lid, an extraordinary, delicate fragrance drifted into the air.

It was the bittersweet, herbal scent of sun-dried pomelo peel, roasted tea leaves, and crushed camphor foliage.

At the very bottom of the shoebox lay three layers of neatly folded, pristine medical absorbent cotton. And dead center upon that cotton rested two slender splints whittled from orange-wood tongue depressors, bearing five crude ballpoint characters—[Lin Xiaoma Will Prevail].

Cradling the shoebox in both hands, Lu Shuqing took slow, measured steps toward Zhou Yan.

Then, bending down once more, she fished a pink, hardcover notebook out of the standing water.

The notebook had swollen thick from the water, the rhinestone bear on its cover missing an eye.

Lu Shuqing opened the pages, holding them directly before Zhou Yan's eyes.

"June 2nd, 07:00 AM: Wound suppuration observed; applied 0.3 grams of Yunnan Baiyao mixed with petroleum jelly;"

"June 5th, light rain: Loss of appetite; Lao Wu smuggled two drops of warm milk from home; heartbeat stabilized post-feeding;"

"June 9th, Black-Faced Judge... Student Affairs sanitation surprise inspection; emergency transfer to paper recycling bin; hypothermia noted; warmed with thermal pack for twenty minutes;"

"June 17th: Plumage 70% developed; talon grip restored; capable of perching atop pen holder for five seconds..."

Lu Shuqing read each line in an even, uninflected cadence.

As the words settled, the furious crimson draining from Zhou Yan's face gradually solidified into stony, stunned disbelief.

Those razor-sharp eyes, having scrutinized thousands of juvenile transgressions over fifteen years, stared fixedly at the dense, painstakingly neat, almost devout childhood handwriting upon the soaked pages, completely paralyzed.

Beside the podium, Wen Yating had already pressed both hands over her mouth, weeping inconsolably.

The suffocating silence she had misinterpreted as "collective passive aggression";

The unnatural quiet she had diagnosed as "post-traumatic neurotic hysteria";

The secret notes slipped under desks, the chairs raised two centimeters off the floor during self-study, the synchronized coughing cover-ups, the counseling tree tests all bearing hidden bird nests...

Every absurdity, every mystery, every incomprehensible contradiction—in this single crystalline second, like scattered jigsaw pieces, assembled into a complete, breathtaking tapestry before the two adults!

There was no conspiracy, no riot, no defiance of authority.

The reason this classroom of notorious troublemakers had sheathed their claws, enduring weeks of unnatural constraint and suffocating silence...

Was solely to nurture a dying little soul back to life, using their clumsy, childish devotion within the cold walls of this school.

The air fell into utter, breathless stillness.

Even the thunder outside seemed to have quietly receded, leaving only the gentle, persistent patter of summer rain tapping against the jagged shards of window glass.

The fingers Zhou Yan had clamped around Lin Jingtang's wrist slowly, inch by inch, uncoiled.

Those shoulders, perpetually squared in rigid authority, inexplicably slumped downward.

"Lin Jingtang..." Zhou Yan's voice was hoarse, entirely stripped of its earlier fury, trembling with an emotion even he could not name. "You... show me what is in your hands."

This time, Lin Jingtang did not roar.

The boy raised his face, covered in grime and blood, casting a deep, questioning glance toward Lu Shuqing beside him.

Lu Shuqing gave him a gentle nod.

Sniffing hard, Lin Jingtang finally let two fat tears slip free, splashing onto his blood-spattered forearms.

With infinite gentleness, with utmost reverence, he slowly uncurled his interlocked ten fingers toward the light.

At the center of those blood-streaked, mud-stained palms, a greyish-brown sparrow no larger than half a fist crouched quietly.

Its down was half-dampened by the blowing rain, its beak faintly smudged with a smear of bright crimson rubbed from the boy's split knuckle.

Yet it held its fluffy little head high, its glossy obsidian eyes gazing through the parting fingers, fearlessly inspecting the tall, stern, dark-faced middle-aged man standing before it.

Little Ma blinked.

In the deep quiet of Lu Shuqing's mind, the faint, haughty grumble of this proud little soul resonated once more:

*"Hey... you dark-faced giant ape... what are you glaring at? Have you never seen an Emperor traveling incognito...?"*

A bittersweet, tender smile touched the corners of Lu Shuqing's lips.

"Cheep..."

Little Ma let out a tiny peep from the hollow of Lin Jingtang's palm.

The sound was microscopic, tender, scarcely louder than a falling leaf striking the ground.

Yet that infinitesimal cry struck like a sledgehammer against Zhou Yan's ironclad heart, which had been armored with fifteen years of rigid disciplinary bylaws.

Zhou Yan stared at the tiny bird, looked at Lin Jingtang's lacerated shoulder, and finally took in the twenty-six soaked, battered children lying across the floor, holding their breath in solemn anticipation.

Across that weather-beaten, stone-hard countenance, the facial muscles twitched twice in profound defeat.

For the first time in five years, the students witnessed a flicker of bewildered vulnerability and overwhelming awe upon the face of the Black-Faced Judge.

Outside in the corridor, heavy, hurried footsteps echoed from the staircase.

It was the teachers from neighboring classrooms and the Academic Dean, drawn by the earlier thunderous crash and shattering glass.

"Director Zhou! What happened?! Did Class 6-1 cause another catastrophe?!"

Anxious queries drifted through the open doorway.

Paper, after all, could never wrap a feather.

Class 6-1's summer secret, guarded with devotion through weeks of silence, was now laid completely bare beneath the sunlight and shadows of the entire school.
