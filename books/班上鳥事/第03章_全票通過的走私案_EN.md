# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 3: The Unanimously Approved Smuggling Operation

At seven-forty-five in the morning, the grass-green wooden door of Class 6-1—marred by countless shoe prints—was violently kicked open with a thunderous clatter.

A-Tang swept into the classroom like a whirlwind.

His chest heaved violently, beads of sweat mingling with dark-brown mud streaks as they dripped steadily from the tip of his nose. Even more arresting was his left forearm—the sleeve of his pristine white uniform shirt had been savagely ripped away by half, his wrist tightly bound with a rag soaked in fresh blood. The crimson fluid had already crusted into a dark-brown casing along the cloth's edges, making him look as though he had just barely escaped with his life from some brutal Middle Eastern battlefield.

Yet, this youth, who habitually charged around with wild abandon and seemed intent on stomping craters into the concrete floor, now moved with an unnatural, chilling stealth.

His arms were clasped rigidly across his chest, his torso hunched forward like a fierce mother eagle guarding its prey, cradling the deep-blue canvas schoolbag securely in his embrace. With every step he took, his feet landed with agonizing lightness, his toes even testing the floor tentatively before bearing weight, as though the terrazzo tiles beneath him might collapse into an abyss at any given second.

"HOLY CRAP! A-Tang! Did you go wrestle a black bear on the back mountain?!"

Sitting atop a third-row desk munching on chocolate wafer rolls, physical education monitor Chen Dashan (Big Megaphone) sprang to his feet like an uncoiling spring the instant he saw A-Tang's battered state.

His 120-decibel, booming bass voice detonated across the classroom ceiling in an instant: "Look at all that blood! Even your sleeve's shredded! Did that gang of slackers from neighboring Zhongyi Elementary jump you by the rear gate?! Grab your weapons! Brothers, unscrew the broom handles! I'm marching over today and smashing their school's flagpole to pieces!"

"Dashan! Shut your damn... shut your mouth..." A-Tang's face was ashen, contorted with sheer panic, squeezing the words from the deepest recesses of his throat in a stifled hiss.

Yet it was already too late.

Big Megaphone's single roar was akin to tossing a stick of lit dynamite into a tranquil pond.

The class of rascals, who had been engrossed in their own separate antics, swarmed forward from their seats in an instant, like sharks catching the scent of blood.

"A-Tang's bleeding! Look, look! Those scratches are deep!"

"What're you clutching in your arms?! You didn't actually bag that one-meter-long stink rat snake from the back mountain alive, did you?!"

Thrusting his rotund belly forward, Old Wu—with half a steaming hot cabbage bun still stuffed in his cheek—shoved his way to the front with unbridled excitement, his greasy, plump hands lunging instinctively toward the bag in A-Tang's arms: "Let the brothers feast our eyes! Is this the blockbuster bomb we agreed yesterday to scare the new teacher with?!"

"Get lost! Don't touch it!"

A mixture of panic and fury seizing him, A-Tang recoiled violently, his spine slamming heavily against the wooden edge of the teacher's podium.

Yet how could seven or eight half-grown boys burning with curiosity possibly heed him? Amidst the chaotic scramble, Old Wu's elbow accidentally brushed against A-Tang's lacerated left wrist. A surge of blunt, excruciating agony flashed black before A-Tang's eyes, causing his fingers to involuntarily slacken for a fraction of a second.

Old Wu seized the strap of the schoolbag, yanking it outward in one swift motion—

"CLATTER!"

The bag's zipper burst open, and the deep-blue cardboard sneaker box tumbled out, landing heavily against the wooden podium floor with a crisp, hollow thud.

"What on earth is this? A pair of old sneakers?"

Several boys let out disappointed groans, gathering around the shoebox on the floor, pointing and laughing in derision.

Yet A-Tang's pupils shrank to the diameter of pinheads in that exact microsecond. His face turned whiter than the bloody bandage on his wrist, and he practically threw himself flat on the floor, his trembling hands grasping desperately toward the box:

"Quiet... I'm begging you... stop shouting..."

Before his plea could finish.

"BANG!"

To get a clearer view, Big Megaphone planted a heavy boot on the rim of an empty galvanized trash can nearby. The weighty metal bin screeched across the tiled floor in a piercing, two-meter-long slide, then collided violently into a wooden chair, toppling it with a resounding crash.

The grating screech of metal, the splintering crack of wood, and the overlapping clamor of over twenty boys coalesced into a terrifying acoustic storm within the enclosed classroom!

And right in the raging epicenter of that storm, the previously motionless sneaker box began to shudder violently!

From behind the dozen-odd pea-sized ventilation holes punched through the lid came the frantic, desperate scratching of tiny claws against stiff cardboard.

In sheer panic, A-Tang flipped open a corner of the lid.

The scene that met their eyes froze the grins on the faces of Old Wu and Big Megaphone in an instant.

Morning light cascaded through the crack in the box.

The soft white paper lining the bottom of the box was already soaked through with dark-red bloodstains. The fledgling sparrow, which had gradually stabilized out in the grass, was currently enduring an existential apocalypse.

This fragile spark of life was convulsing with heartbreaking violence amidst the crumpled paper!

A bird's eardrums, ten times more sensitive than a human's, had suffered catastrophic trauma under that sudden barrage of high-decibel screeches and crashing impacts. In its violent seizures, its fractured right wing slammed brutally against the rigid cardboard wall, shattering the newly formed scab once more. Tiny droplets of fresh blood splattered across the white paper in terrifying contrast.

Its two frail claws curled rigidly against its breast, every single tiny feather standing stiffly on end as though electrocuted. Its tender beak was wrenched wide, its neck arched backward at a grotesque angle, its throat only capable of producing disjointed, dying wheezes:

*“Krrr... krrr...”*

That black eye, scarcely larger than a mung bean, had begun to glaze over and lose its luster, the eyelid drooping weakly, poised to slide shut forever and plunge into eternal darkness.

This tiny life, which had miraculously survived the razor claws of a feral cat, was at this very second about to be shouted to death by a mob of raucous, oblivious schoolboys upon the teacher's podium.

"Th-This... how is it a bird?" Shock drained the color from Old Wu's face; the remaining half of his pork bun dropped from his limp fingers, landing *splat* in the dust.

"It... it looks like it's dying..." Big Megaphone's Adam's apple bobbed with immense difficulty, his usually booming voice stuttering like a broken radio receiver.

Kneeling on the floor with bloodshot eyes, A-Tang felt as cold as ice. His hands hovered in midair, yet he lacked the courage to even graze that trembling clump of gray down. He wanted to scream at everyone to shut up, yet he realized with crushing sorrow that any sound issuing from his throat would merely become the final nail in this fledgling's coffin.

Despair, like a freezing hand, clamped tight around the boy's heart.

At this razor-thin precipice between life and death—

"BAM—————!!!"

A deafening, thunderous slap against wood exploded like a bolt of lightning from the very front row of the classroom!

The sheer force of that palm strike was staggering; the heavy cypress teacher's desk, along with the box of chalk resting upon it, jumped half an inch off the floor!

Startled out of their wits by this sudden thunderclap, the entire class shuddered violently, their heads whipping around in unison.

There stood Lu Shuqing, having risen to her feet at some unknown second.

All traces of her usual detachment and cold apathy had vanished without a trace. Her neatly bound hair was slightly disheveled, and the eyes behind her thin black-framed glasses blazed with an all-consuming fury, their glare as vicious as twin poisoned ice daggers slicing across every face in the room.

Drawing a deep, shuddering breath that made her chest heave, Lu Shuqing forced a chilling, bone-piercing decree of absolute authority through clenched teeth:

**“EV—ER—Y—ONE—SHUT! UP!!”**

"If anyone dares draw another loud breath," Shuqing declared, snatching the gleaming stainless steel surgical scissors from her desk and pointing the sharp tip straight at the bridge of Big Megaphone's nose, enunciating every syllable, "I will personally snip out their tonsils and feed them to the stray dogs."

In that split second, the air solidified into solid granite.

Terrified out of his mind, Big Megaphone clapped both hands over his massive mouth, sliding down against the wall until he sat huddled on the floor; Old Wu held his breath so hard his chubby face flushed the color of a bruised purple eggplant; twenty-odd usually uncontrollable scoundrels stood petrified as if struck by an ancient binding curse, frozen in place, not daring to even roll their eyes.

Ten whole seconds.

The entire classroom of Class 6-1 plunged into an absolute, vacuum-like silence unprecedented in the thirty-year history of the school.

No one spoke, no one coughed; even the scuff of shoe soles against the floor vanished entirely.

The solitary sounds remaining were the dry rustle of the summer breeze sweeping through the banyan leaves outside the window and the faint, thread-thin humming of the fluorescent light ballast above the blackboard.

Within this suffocating tranquility, the fledgling sparrow's violently convulsing frame finally, miraculously, began to ease.

Its faint heartbeat, sheltered within this haven devoid of deafening noise, resumed its frail, rhythmic pulse with agonizing slowness.

Lu Shuqing stepped briskly around the podium.

Her footsteps were unimaginably light—toes touching down first, heels slightly aloft—gliding like a white crane skimming over fragile ice, without generating the slightest whisper of noise.

She approached A-Tang, looking down with a cold, haughty glare at the blood-drenched class ringleader.

"Move aside, you idiot."

A-Tang didn't dare utter a single syllable of protest, scrambling half a meter backward on hands and knees.

Dropping to one knee, Shuqing pushed her glasses up her nose with a delicate finger and peered into the depths of the shoebox.

Yet, the instant her slender, fair fingertips grazed the edge of the cardboard—

A faint, shrill, yet incomparably haughty and furious screech detonated directly within the cerebral cortex of her brain without the slightest warning!

*“Insolence! You crude, wretched, hairless giant primates!”*

*“The agony! My wing! My royal right wing! To think that I, the Seventh-Generation Legitimate Hereditary Lord of the Luyang Banyan Empire, should fall at the hands of gorillas who think with their lower extremities?!”*

*“Unhand me! Keep that slimy paw away! Even if I bleed my last drop of noble ichor, I shall never bow to you dull-witted mammals—OUCH! The pain, the pain, the pain! Murder! Mercy!”*

Lu Shuqing's outstretched hand froze dead in midair.

Her pupils contracted violently in a split second, her entire frame jolted as if struck by lightning, a cold sweat breaking out across her neck!

Th-This... What in the world was this?!

Shuqing stared fixedly at the miserable, half-dead fledgling—no larger than two marbles—nestled within the shoebox.

The little sparrow kept its eyes shut, its tiny head drooping limply against the paper shavings, appearing entirely feeble, harmless, and evoking profound pity. Yet as long as Shuqing's gaze remained anchored to the bird, that arrogant, sharp voice—chattering like a scolding fishwife—roared ceaselessly within the dome of her skull!

*“What are you gawking at, you female ape sporting two pieces of broken glass?! Have you never beheld fallen nobility before?!”*
*“I demand fresh morning dew! Go harvest the tender shoots of newly sprouted foxtail grass for me! Unpolluted! If there is a speck of dust, I shall not partake!”*
*“And that filthy male ape dripping with blood! How dared he strike my guardsman Black Charcoal with a brick... Granted, Black Charcoal is a traitorous rebel, but that is my imperial domestic affair! Do you baboons comprehend nothing of aristocratic politics?!”*

The blue veins on Shuqing's forehead throbbed with violent indignation.

Heaven alone knew what monumental reserves of willpower she exerted to keep from swatting this delusional "Banyan Empire Lord" into a paste right then and there!

Drawing a long breath, she marshaled every ounce of concentration in her consciousness and transmitted a razor-cold telepathic pulse:

*【Shut your beak, you gray grub weighing less than twenty-five grams. Squeak inside my head one more time, and I will pitch you into the artificial pond outside to feed the snapping turtles.】*

*“Vzzzt—!”*

In that fraction of a second, the fledgling in the box seemed choked by an invisible hand; all its arrogant squawking ceased abruptly.

That limp, downy head gave a violent jerk. Its liquid-black right eye cracked open with supreme effort, locking onto Lu Shuqing crouching before it.

Shuqing distinctly registered the gasp of horrified disbelief echoing from the tiny creature:

*“Y-You... You can understand my speech?!”*
*“Impossible! Cross-species spiritual communion was severed back in the Third Epoch! You lowly bipedal... Wait! Female long-haired ape, what did you just threaten to feed me to?! Turtles?! Those vile, unwashed reptiles who cannot even polish their own carapaces?! How dare you! The noble bloodline of the Archaeopteryx courses through my veins—”*

*【Try me and see if I dare,】* Shuqing sneered in the depths of her mind. *【Furthermore, you are fractured: proximal fractures of the radius and ulna, along with severe pectoral muscle tearing. If you have no desire to spend the rest of your days hopping on one foot eating trash off the dirt, keep your beak clamped tight.】*

The fledgling's yellow-rimmed beak twitched twice, seemingly coming to terms with the grim reality that it was the fish on the chopping block and she was the cleaver. It tucked its head grudgingly deep into the paper shavings, a faint whimper leaking from its throat:

*“...Barbarians. An uncultured realm of savages, devoid of all refinement.”*

Externally, Lu Shuqing maintained her mask of glacial, stone-cold severity.

Nobody present could have dreamed that within those fleeting three seconds, the most rational young prodigy of Luyang Elementary had concluded a covert interspecies duel within the psychic realm.

Turning briskly, she unzipped her backpack and retrieved the weighty tin first-aid kit.

"Lu Shuqing..." A-Tang knelt nearby, his voice trembling on the verge of tears, asking with immense caution, "Will... will it live?"

"That depends entirely on your collective IQ over the coming hour."

Donning a pair of latex gloves, Shuqing's voice was as dispassionate as an ER surgeon's:

"A fledgling's resting heart rate fluctuates between four hundred and six hundred beats per minute. Any sudden sonic trauma triggers an adrenaline surge, inducing ventricular fibrillation and acute cardiac failure. In layman's terms: you idiots came within an inch of liquefying its brain through sheer acoustics."

Hearing this, the entire class shrank their necks into their collars, drowning in guilt.

Big Megaphone wrung his massive hands together, not daring to lift his gaze.

Shuqing extracted two ultra-fine cotton swabs, moistening them with sterile saline. Her movements were devoid of the slightest tremor as she delicately cleansed the clotted blood and grit from the fledgling's broken wing. Next, she dabbed the wound with a minute quantity of povidone-iodine.

As the antiseptic met the raw tissue, Little Ma shrieked like a stuck pig within Shuqing's mind: *“YEEEOOOW! Burning! What vile poison is this?! My empire is fallen!”*

Shuqing's eyes narrowed, applying a fraction more pressure, and Little Ma promptly clamped its beak shut in terrified compliance.

Next, Shuqing selected two of the straightest, sturdiest wooden popsicle sticks from her pencil case, trimming them to two-centimeter splints with surgical scissors. Fetching breathable medical paper tape, she bound the fractured right wing securely against the bird's flank with breathtaking dexterity.

Finally, she drew half a dropper of lukewarm glucose electrolyte solution, holding the droplet poised delicately at the tip of the sparrow's beak.

A crystalline drop rolled down the crevice of the beak.

Little Ma smacked its chops.

*“...Hm? This morning dew... the flavor is passable. Consider yourselves marginally competent, you giant apes.”*

As warm vitality infused its weakened body, the fledgling's labored breathing finally settled into a serene rhythm. Closing its eyes, it curled up into a contented gray fluffball within the warm paper nest, even emitting an infinitesimally soft, whistling snore.

The emergency rescue was a complete success.

The cluster of boys encircling the podium let out a collective, synchronized exhale of relief.

"A miracle worker... Qingzi, you're literally the reincarnation of Hua Tuo!" Old Wu's cheeks quivered with joy as he clapped noiselessly with frantic enthusiasm.

"Be quiet, Wu Chenkai."

Stripping off the latex gloves, Shuqing returned each instrument to the tin box with crisp, orderly metallic clicks.

She stood upright, looking down upon the twenty-six students, her eyes behind the black frames radiating an icy judicial mandate:

"Its vital signs have stabilized for the moment. But this is merely step one. Avian bone regeneration requires at least three to four weeks. During this period, it must remain shielded from light, maintained at twenty-eight degrees Celsius, and immersed in absolute, unbroken silence."

Shuqing turned toward A-Tang: "Lin Jingtang, you brought a wild animal onto campus without authorization. According to Director Zhou Yan's code, that earns a minor demerit, and this bird will be carted to the security hut and incinerated as biohazard waste."

A-Tang slapped the floor, surging to his feet with gritted teeth: "I'll take the demerit! Let Director Zhou slap me with a major demerit for all I care! But whoever lays a finger on this bird will have to go through me!"

"Go through you with what? Your pencil eraser?" Shuqing mocked mercilessly. "The moment Director Zhou steps into this room and spots a living creature beneath your desk, he won't even need to touch it. A single blast from his brass whistle will scare this bird to death on the spot."

A-Tang froze. Gazing at the tiny, shivering creature in the box, a wave of unprecedented helplessness and panic washed over him: "Then... what do we do? If we put it back in the woods, Black Charcoal will swallow it whole!"

Shuqing pivoted slowly, her sharp gaze sweeping across the faces of all twenty-six classmates.

Morning light filtered through the window frame, bathing her poised silhouette in a subtle halo of gold.

"There is only one way."

Shuqing's voice dropped to a conspiratorial whisper, akin to plotting a regicide in the dead of night:

"From this second onward, this classroom is a sterile incubator. And every single one of us is an accomplice."

Every student's heart skipped a violent beat.

"First: Lin Jingtang's desk shall serve as the primary bunker. He is responsible for supplying ambient body warmth, but all feeding, medication, and wound care protocols must follow my clinical directives one hundred percent."

"Second: Xu Fangyu," Shuqing addressed the hygiene monitor in the back row, "you are in charge of environmental air quality and scent containment. You will bring fresh, dried citrus peels and spent tea leaves daily. This room must not emit the faintest whiff of bird droppings."

"Third: Chen Dashan," Shuqing fixed her stare on the physical education monitor, "your throat is officially sealed under martial law. If anyone exceeds forty decibels in the hallway, doorway, or windows, you are tasked with pacifying them through physical intervention. If your booming voice causes a relapse in its condition—"

A lethally sweet smile curved upon Shuqing's lips: "I will pay a personal visit to your mother and discuss in detail the three prank magazines hidden beneath your mattress."

Big Megaphone shuddered violently, nodding his head like a woodpecker on speed.

"Fourth: everyone in this room." Shuqing's tone turned gravestone-solemn. "Step lightly without shoes inside the room; communicate solely through whispers or lip-reading; lift your chairs two centimeters off the floor before moving them. If anyone informs Director Zhou, or exposes our operation through carelessness—"

Shuqing raised a slender hand, drawing a sharp slicing motion across her throat:

"Class 6-1 shall face collective liability, and everyone will be left to perish on the final exams."

A heavy, reverent hush fell over the room.

These scoundrels, who typically feared neither gods nor demons, exchanged solemn glances. Throughout their elementary school lives, their solidarity had only ever been harnessed to make teachers weep or tear down the plaster walls. Never before had they united to protect something so small.

Yet, as their eyes fell upon that humble shoebox on the podium, watching that fragile life breathing faintly amidst the crumpled white paper, a coarse yet scorching ember of devotion ignited within the depths of every unruly chest.

"All those in favor of the smuggling coalition, raise your hands," Shuqing declared.

*“SWOOSH—!”*

Without a fraction of a second's hesitation.

Twenty-six arms shot upward with ironclad resolve, straight as bamboo shoots piercing through fertile earth after a thunderstorm!

Even A-Tang hoisted his blood-stained arm high and true.

Old Wu even went so far as to retrieve his dropped half-bun, tucking it reverently into his pocket while raising his hand, swearing in a breath fainter than a mosquito's hum: "I... I will dedicate all my egg yolks to the Supreme Commander."

Within the shoebox, the fledgling sparrow shifted into a more comfortable posture.

Within Shuqing's consciousness echoed another faint, conceited hum:

*“...Hmph, barely adequate. At least these apes possess some sense of deference. From this day forward, I am your Supreme Commander.”*

Right at that heartbeat, Zhou Ziqiang—"Ziqiang Express"—slumped near the back door, suddenly jerked upright as though struck by an electric current.

He didn't dare utter a sound; he merely slipped both hands beneath his desk, using his knuckles to rap out an urgent, muffled telegraph code against the floor tiles—

*Thump, thump, thump!*

Danger incoming!

A collective gasp was swallowed down twenty-six throats as every pair of eyes darted toward the hallway.

There, around the corner illuminated by the early dawn, a slender figure clad in a beige knit cardigan, cradling a thick stack of psychological test packets, was stepping forward in low heels, a smile as warm as a spring breeze gracing her face as she approached the threshold of Class 6-1.

The new homeroom teacher, Wen Yating, had arrived on the battlefield.
