# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 9: Emergency Summons from the Counseling Office

The Counseling Office of Luyang Elementary School was situated in the most secluded basement level of the Ren'ai Building.

It was an oasis of gentle tranquility carpeted in pale-green acoustic felt, its walls painted a soothing blush-pink, the air perpetually scented with the delicate aroma of chamomile essential oils. In the eyes of the faculty and student body alike, this had long served as a safe harbor for battered spirits—appointed with an inflatable punching bag for venting aggression, a massive plush polar bear for weeping embrace, and countless illustrated volumes of chicken-soup stories emblazoned with rainbows and sunflowers.

Yet at two o'clock this afternoon, this sanctum of gentle murmurs was ripped apart by a chorus of near-hysterical sobs.

"Director Gao... I truly gave it my all..."

Cradling a mug decorated with wild daisies in both hands, Wen Yating curled into a ball upon the cream-white linen sofa, her dark locks spilling haphazardly across her shoulders, her eyelids swollen and red as overripe cherries.

Seated across from her was Gao Huimin, the most senior counseling director in the history of Luyang Elementary. Fifty years of age, wearing wide-rimmed reading glasses, Director Gao exuded the warm, maternal serenity of a living Bodhisattva, celebrated throughout the county's educational circles as the "Acupuncturist of the Soul."

"Yating, dear, don't rush; speak slowly." Director Gao pushed a box of Kleenex across the table, her tone as soothing as spring willows dancing in the breeze. "The situation in Class 6-1 is well-documented across the school. The departures of the three previous instructors were indeed catastrophic. How did they torment you today? Did they slip live bullfrogs into your lecture binders again?"

"No... there were no frogs..." Sniffling, Wen Yating's tears splashed steadily into her hot water. "If it had merely been a frog, I would not be drowning in such despair!"

"Then what was it? A spring-loaded trap? Or industrial glue smeared upon the podium chair?"

"It was a sentence." Wen Yating lifted her face, her eyes flashing with a terror bordering on delirium. "During the first period this morning... Lin Jingtang covered both his ears with his hands and told me, with absolute, unblemished sincerity... that the sound of chalk dust hitting the floor was too loud."

The silver stirring spoon in Director Gao's hand froze dead in midair.

Pushing up her reading glasses, her kindly brows furrowed ever so slightly: "Chalk dust? Hitting the floor? Too loud?"

"Yes!" Leaning forward as though grasping at a floating lifeline, Wen Yating's voice trembled: "And during the second-period mathematics class! For forty straight minutes, all twenty-six students—not a single soul whispered, not one looked out the window! They stared at my chalk with the wild fervor of religious martyrs! When Director Zhou Yan conducted a surprise raid at noon, they transformed the entire room into a botanical-scented abbey in under four seconds!"

Drawing a shuddering breath, Wen Yating lowered her voice to a chilling, blood-freezing whisper:

"Director Gao... this is neither mischief nor simple rebellion. This is the most dangerous manifestation of 'pathological emotional detachment' known to group psychology! Having had their sense of psychological safety obliterated by the harshness of the prior three teachers, they have collectively regressed into a state of hyper-vigilant, catatonic camouflage! If we do not intervene immediately, the psyches of those twenty-six children will be trapped forever inside a self-constructed black hole!"

Gazing at the young master's graduate teetering on the brink of a nervous breakdown, the smile faded entirely from Director Gao's face, her countenance hardening into unprecedented gravity.

Thirty years of counseling experience had taught her that the most perilous students were never the ruffians swinging broomsticks in the halls, but rather the "model children" who suddenly turned unnervingly quiet, impossibly obedient, their eyes as hollow and bottomless as stagnant cisterns.

"I understand."

Rising solemnly, Director Gao slid open the heavy metal filing cabinet behind her, extracting a hefty dossier from its lowest drawer.

"Since conventional verbal dialogue has failed, we must resort to subconscious projective instruments."

Director Gao dropped the dossier firmly onto the coffee table, its cover boldly bearing the most celebrated authoritative term in international clinical psychology—

**"House-Tree-Person Projective Test (HTP)"**.

Pushing up her spectacles, a professional gleam flared in Director Gao's eyes:

"This assessment circumvents all cognitive defenses of the cerebral cortex, extracting deep-seated subconscious traumas and terrors directly. The House represents home and belonging; the Tree reflects the vitality of the self and psychic energy; the Person projects self-image and interpersonal relations. No matter how impenetrable their camouflage, before a blank canvas and soft graphite, the demons buried in their hearts will have nowhere to hide."

Dabbing her tears, a fresh spark of hope ignited within Wen Yating's eyes: "Understood! We shall go this instant!"

Two-thirty in the afternoon.

As the bell chimed for the third period—integrated activities—Wen Yating and a benevolent-looking Director Gao strode solemnly into Class 6-1, bearing twenty-six sheets of imported acid-free drawing paper and twenty-six freshly sharpened soft graphite pencils.

"Class," Wen Yating announced from the podium, striving to make her smile radiate divine maternal warmth, "for this period, Director Gao from our school counseling department has come specially to visit us. We shall take no tests, nor shall we memorize texts. I will distribute a sheet of paper to each of you; please relax and draw three simple things upon it—a house, a tree, and a person. Draw them however you wish; there are no right or wrong answers, and no grades shall be assigned."

The drawing sheets and pencils were distributed down the aisles.

The instant Lu Shuqing's fingertips in the front row grazed the textured grain of the heavy art paper, her eyes narrowed into razor-sharp slits behind her dark frames.

As the daughter of a veterinarian and the school's undisputed intellectual ceiling, Lu Shuqing had memorized every single projective indicator of the House-Tree-Person test back when she was subjected to psychiatric evaluation at age seven. She knew precisely why these two women had arrived—they sought to dredge the secret "psychological trauma" the class had concealed for an entire week from these sheets.

Without lifting her chin, using the pretext of testing her pencil lead, Shuqing issued her command via micro-ventriloquism to the rear:

"Attention, everyone. Psychiatric audit. Draw freely, but all violent and destructive motifs are strictly prohibited. If anyone dares sketch an artillery cannon demolishing the school, I will ram that pencil up their nose."

In the back row, A-Tang gnawed on his eraser, cold sweat beading across his brow.

No cannons? No Black Charcoal the cat?

Then what was he supposed to draw?

Lowering his chin, peering through the gap in his desktop, the boy's gaze settled upon the tranquil shoebox deep within the compartment.

Afternoon light drifted through the ventilation holes, illuminating a fluffy, round gray silhouette—bellies full of yolk mash, head tucked peacefully into the tea-leaf bedding, snoozing serenely.

For reasons unknown, the tense, iron-hard knots in A-Tang's chest suddenly softened into mist.

In that hollow brick house with no mother, where his father was absent for weeks on end, he had never felt the meaning of "home." Yet within this cramped desk drawer, anchored to this fragile fledgling that twenty-six souls had sworn to guard with their lives, he tasted the warm, beating heartbeat of what a "home" truly meant for the very first time.

Laying graphite to paper, A-Tang began his sketch in the lower right quadrant.

And all around him, twenty-six wild scoundrels who typically only knew how to scribble turtles in textbooks found their minds converging upon the identical bird.

Fifteen minutes elapsed.

Standing before the podium, Director Gao and Wen Yating commenced gathering the twenty-six weighty "subconscious projective specimens."

Retreating to the corner of the podium, holding their breath in solemn anticipation, the two educators flipped open the topmost illustration.

It was Lin Jingtang's submission.

Director Gao pushed up her reading glasses; the moment her gaze fell upon the paper, her entire frame locked in petrified horror.

Dead center on the eight-gauge sheet stood a colossal, sprawling ancient banyan tree of terrifying proportions. Its limbs and foliage blotted out the heavens, consuming ninety percent of the canvas, every aerial root rendered as thick as an iron pylon.

And squarely in the thickest section of the primary trunk, A-Tang had hollowed out a massive rectangular sanctum with heavy charcoal strokes. Inside that hollow, rendered with sacred geometric symmetry, rested a pristine shoebox! A dozen neat perforations were sketched across the lid, and planted proudly atop the box in brilliant red crayon was a "Marshal's Battle Standard" emblazoned with five gold stars and a soaring feather!

As for the supposed "House," it had been shrunk into a matchbox-sized hovel, huddled pathetically in the mud at the banyan's roots, devoid even of a chimney.

And the "Person"—A-Tang had drawn a bare-chested boy, rippling with muscle, his left arm swathed in thick bandages, kneeling in devout prostration before the hollow of the banyan, hoisting both arms high overhead as if presenting a supreme, holy offering to the shoebox within the tree.

A choked gasp rattled from the depths of Director Gao's throat.

"Wh-What is this?" Director Gao's fingertips quivered violently. "The psychic vitality (Tree) has mutated to swallow the entire empirical world... while the home (House), which should represent baseline security, has been marginalized into an insignificant husk... And most terrifying of all is this central altar! He is prostrating before a perforated black cube in religious worship?!"

Her hands shaking, Wen Yating hastily flipped to the second sheet—Chen Dashan's drawing.

Big Megaphone's artistic style was brutally primitive and explosive.

The "Person" on the page was a colossal titan whose stature spanned the entire height of the paper. Yet what froze the blood in their veins was that this titan's mouth had been sealed shut by five thick, crosshatched iron bars, as though an iron muzzle had been welded across his visage! And perched proudly upon the broad shoulder of this gagged giant stood a diminutive gray sparrow sporting dark aviator sunglasses, radiating insufferable arrogance!

The little sparrow held a scepter crackling with lightning in its talons, planting its feet squarely atop the giant's skull!

"Mutism..." Director Gao's lips blanched. "Severe castration anxiety! Chen Dashan has completely severed his vocal apparatus... while the sovereign ruling his cerebral cortex is an avian totem exercising absolute dominion?!"

Wen Yating was descending into madness, tearing feverishly through the remaining sheets—

Old Wu's drawing: a towering citadel constructed of thirty colossal golden pork buns (House), its parapets occupied by a bloated, grease-dribbling sparrow;
Xiao Yu's drawing: a sheet densely carpeted with symmetrical pomelo rinds and dried foliage, where a sparrow perched upon a leaf was actively deploying an aerosol disinfectant canister;
Even the ultra-rational Class President, Lu Shuqing—her paper contained no landscape whatsoever! It was a millimeter-precise anatomical schematic of avian skeletal architecture! Flanking the fractured radius were two pristine popsicle-stick splints, annotated in exquisite fountain-pen script with the Latin binomial: *Passer montanus (Tree Sparrow) Callus Consolidation Phase*!

Twenty-six illustrations.

Twenty-six whole sheets!

In the center of every single drawing, no matter how bizarre the composition or distorted the lines, there appeared—without a solitary exception, one hundred percent of the time—the image of a bird!

Here a deity, there a despot; here a patient, there a tribal totem!

Director Gao's knees gave out, her back collapsing weakly against the blackboard frame, her spectacles nearly tumbling from her nose. She slowly raised her head, looking out upon the twenty-six children sitting as serenely as saints below, her eyes brimming with the tragic horror of an impending apocalypse:

"Yating... you were right..."

Director Gao's voice trembled like dry leaves in an autumn gale:

"This is no mere trauma... This is a profound, systemic mass-totemic cult... These children... have collectively constructed a miniature theocratic society worshipping an avian deity..."

And right at this absolute summit of psychological devastation—

Within the deepest shadows of the desk compartment, a sudden catastrophe detonated!

Having feasted upon rich egg yolk mash and enjoyed a luxurious slumber amidst the botanical bedding, Little Ma awoke brimming with boundless imperial vitality.

Its body temperature rising, the knitting bone fractures pulsing with a pleasant, tingling itch, Little Ma stretched its sound left wing luxuriantly within the paper nest. Rearing its head, crisp, ringing, and entirely devoid of inhibition, it unleashed a sky-shattering, euphoric morning bugle call from the depths of its tiny throat—

**“CHEEEEEP————!!”**

The sound was impossibly pure.

Impossibly crystalline.

Within this tomb-like classroom where respiration itself had been throttled to near-vacuum, that shrill, piercing, authentic avian bugle detonated like a high-explosive artillery shell in a silent cathedral!

*Clatter!* All twenty-six drawings slipped from Director Gao's nerveless fingers, scattering wildly across the podium!

Wen Yating's eyes dilated into massive saucers, her gaze snapping like a lightning bolt toward the epicenter of the blast—Lin Jingtang's desk in the fourth row!

"WHAT WAS THAT?!" Wen Yating shrieked in horror. "A bird chirp?! There is a bird in this classroom?!"

Upon that razor's edge of absolute annihilation—

Lin Jingtang's pupils contracted, Lu Shuqing's spine went cold, and Chen Dashan's entire visage contorted into an apoplectic, blood-engorged fury!

Exposed!

The Supreme Commander was exposed!

The second the box was found, it would be trashed, the class suspended, and the fragile life they had fought so hard to save crushed into dust!

There was no script, no eye contact, and not a microsecond for rational calculation.

Before Lin Jingtang's brain could fire an impulse, Big Megaphone Chen Dashan's primal athletic reflexes—honed across thousands of hours on the asphalt courts—detonated to save the realm in their hour of mortal peril!

*“HAAAACK——!!”*

Chen Dashan launched himself from his chair like an uncoiling spring, clamping both hands around his own throat, veins bulging across his crimson temples, his jaw dropping wide to unleash a ferocious, earth-splitting roar of a cough!

In the next heartbeat, A-Tang caught the signal!

Throwing his entire torso over his desk to smother the drawer, A-Tang reared his chin, tearing his throat raw in an agonizing, desperate barrage of hacking:

"HACK-HACK-COUGH-COUGH! HACK-COUGH-COUGH-HACK!"

In the subsequent microsecond, the entire roster of Class 6-1—like an ammunition depot caught in a chain-reaction detonation—bolted to their feet in unison, unleashing an unprecedented, apocalyptic hurricane of sonic biological warfare!

"COUGH-COUGH-HACK-HACK!"

"ACHOOO——! HACK-HACK-COUGH!"

"HURRRK——! COUGH-COUGH-HACK!"

Twenty-six boys and girls poured every ounce of their mortal strength into dry-heaving, sneezing, and pounding their chests!

Old Wu slapped his desk until the floorboards rattled; Big Megaphone coughed like an ancient water buffalo roaring into a monsoon, rattling the windowpanes in their casements; several usually demure girls coughed so hard real tears flooded their cheeks, and the entire room was thoroughly inundated beneath a raging tempest of respiratory convulsions!

*“Cheep-peep?!”*

Startled out of its wits by the sudden volcanic roar, Little Ma tucked its neck into its downy collar, its frail peep crushed into oblivion beneath the deafening acoustic storm.

Upon the podium, Wen Yating and Director Gao reeled backward before the terrifying wall of sound, halting only when their shoulder blades slammed flush against the chalkboard.

Staring in abject terror at the twenty-six students—faces flushed purple, veins bulging, tears and snot flowing freely, appearing as though they might vomit blood and drop dead en masse at any given second—the two educators were scared completely out of their wits!

"M-MERCIFUL HEAVENS!!"

Clutching her chest, tears streaming down her cheeks, Wen Yating unleashed a heart-wrenching wail of sheer hysteria:

"That wasn't a bird chirp... That was an acute stridor caused by bronchial spasms!!"

"Director Gao! Call an ambulance this instant! They aren't merely traumatized... They are suffering a collective outbreak of acute pulmonary tuberculosis! CALL AN AMBULANCE NOWWWWW————!!"
