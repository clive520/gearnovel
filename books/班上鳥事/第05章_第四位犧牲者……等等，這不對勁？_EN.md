# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 5: The Fourth Victim... Wait, Something's Wrong?

The Academic Affairs Director, who had taught at Luyang Elementary School for thirty full years, once coined a famous adage: in the Zhongxiao Building, you never need a wristwatch; simply listening to the commotion at the eastern end of the third floor will tell you the exact hour and minute.

Seven-fifty was customarily the hour when Class 6-1 punted their first or second class president's backpack out the window; eight o'clock was usually marked by the agonized wails of some unfortunate soul whose pants had been drenched by mop water down the corridor; and when the bell chimed for the first period at eight-ten, the entire room would reliably erupt into an earth-shaking tremor akin to thousands of wild oxen stampeding across a timber bridge in unison.

Yet today, when the golden morning rays of eight-ten sliced diagonally across the hallway, the third floor of the Zhongxiao Building resembled the airless, desolate surface of the moon.

Teacher Wen Yating stood rooted in the center of the podium, the stub of white chalk in her palm already reduced to a damp, crumbly pellet of slaked lime under her clammy cold sweat.

She drew a deep, ragged breath.

As an honors graduate of the Institute of Educational Psychology at Taiwan Normal University, Wen Yating had dedicated her master's thesis to researching "oppositional defiance mechanisms in adolescent group dynamics." Within her meticulously constructed cognitive model, these twenty-six children before her—though currently appearing as meek and docile as lambs—were unquestionably in a state of highly organized, deeply covert "pre-riot dormancy."

"Alright... class," Wen Yating strained to maintain a textbook-standard, forty-five-degree smile, her voice sweet as meltwater from early spring snow, "today is our very first day together. Before we commence our formal lesson, I would love to get acquainted with all of you. When I call your name, please stand up so I may see you, alright?"

Below the podium, a solemn, tomb-like hush prevailed.

There were no jeers, no mocking hoots, not even the faint rustle of bottoms shifting upon wooden seats.

Twenty-six youthful faces stared up at her in eerie synchrony, their eyes radiating a serene detachment and benevolence bordering on monastic asceticism.

Opening the attendance register, Wen Yating's fingertips trembled ever so faintly: "First, Lin Jingtang."

Seated dead center in the room, A-Tang's spine went rigid as a steel girder.

In an instant, the collective gazes of the entire class swiveled like searchlights, locking squarely onto A-Tang! In the front row, Lu Shuqing didn't even turn her head; she merely adjusted her spectacles upward by a solitary millimeter with near-imperceptible subtlety—a signal that, within Class 6-1's covert code, translated directly to "Maximum Red Alert."

A-Tang's Adam's apple bobbed with immense, painful difficulty.

He could not simply stand up like a normal human being, for nestled deep within his desk drawer lay the fragile, self-proclaimed imperial sovereign.

Clamping both hands firmly onto the desk surface, the core muscles of A-Tang's waist and thighs unleashed terrifying torque. Defying all known laws of gravitational physics, he wrenched his buttocks vertically off the chair seat! The inner adductor muscles of his thighs locked like iron clamps along the desk's edge, ensuring his standing posture appeared impeccably upright while simultaneously using his torso to wall off all line-of-sight into the drawer.

Even more unnerving was his motion—there was no draft of displaced air, no rustle of fabric, and not even the heels of his socks grazed the floor tiles.

"Student Lin Jingtang..." Wen Yating watched this legendary campus warlord—reputed to have sent Master Teacher Li into intensive care—with intense trepidation.

A-Tang's face was ashen, his hands pinned rigid against the seams of his shorts. Facing Wen Yating upon the podium, he executed a bow of solemn, agonizing deliberation.

A ninety-degree bow.

As impeccably standard as a seasoned concierge outside a five-star Japanese hot-spring ryokan who had undergone three solid years of hospitality drills.

Having completed the bow, A-Tang slowly raised his chin, parting his lips in a smile as pure and unblemished as a newly baptized Catholic choirboy, murmuring in an aspirated breath lighter than drifting down:

"G... Good morning... Teacher."

The sound was so delicate it threatened to scatter on the faintest draft.

As though struck by an electric bolt, Wen Yating stumbled backward half a step, her shoulder blades colliding heavily with the wooden molding of the blackboard!

Th-This was Lin Jingtang?!

This was the campus menace annotated in disciplinary archives as "exhibiting severe violent tendencies, attempted to rear a feral monitor lizard in the gymnasium, overturned three desks single-handedly"?!

Gazing upon this youth—his eyes crystal clear, his demeanor humble, his very breath disciplined to near-inaudibility during his bow—the logical processors of Wen Yating's brain collapsed into an infinite recursive loop.

"A-Alright... please take your seat." In a flurry of fluster, Wen Yating dabbed cold sweat from her brow.

Once more defying all mechanical intuition, A-Tang descended back into his seat in slow motion, settling upon the wood like weightless cotton lint, without surrendering a single micrometer of sound.

"Next... Student Chen Dashan."

Hearing his name, the abundant flesh across Big Megaphone's frame quivered violently.

Blue veins bulged across Big Megaphone's forehead, his neck swelling thick as a firehose on the verge of rupture. For eleven straight years, his physiological instinct had been to bellow an ear-splitting "HERE—!!" at one hundred decibels.

Yet at this moment, Big Megaphone sat staring in mortal terror at the gleaming surgical scissors resting by the corner of Class President Lu Shuqing's desk, teetering on the brink of an emotional breakdown.

He stood up.

Big Megaphone parted his cavernous maw, wide enough to swallow a whole duck egg.

Instinctively raising her hands to shield her breast, Wen Yating braced herself to receive the legendary sonic shockwave reputed to shatter window panes—

"...Er."

A sound—infinitesimally brief, agonizingly bashful, akin to a newly hatched pullet peeping in the spring breeze—squeaked feebly from the depths of Big Megaphone's throat.

Following this, the 160-centimeter-tall, sixty-five-kilogram juggernaut hung his head in mortified scarlet shame. Wringing the hem of his uniform shirt like a bashful three-year-old girl meeting a stranger for the first time, he squirmed back into his seat with dainty delicacy.

Wen Yating's hands hovered frozen in midair.

A sharp, throbbing pulse began hammering at her temples.

Something was wrong.

This was definitively not a normal student reaction! This was an impossible state of mind for a room full of psychologically sound sixth-graders!

Within Adlerian psychodynamics, extreme repression inevitably presaged catastrophic eruptive backlash. There was only one rational deduction for the spectacle before her—these students were waging a meticulously orchestrated, extraordinarily sophisticated "collective psychological war" against her! They were attempting to shatter her sanity through this cult-like, suffocating stillness, driving her into paranoid delirium!

"G-Good... excellent, you are all very disciplined."

Forcibly suppressing the churning dread within her chest, Wen Yating pivoted toward the blackboard:

"For our first language arts lesson today, we shall study the Tang Dynasty poet Bai Juyi's *Grass on the Ancient Plain: A Farewell*."

Picking up the chalk, she made her first deliberate stroke upon the slate.

*“SCREEECH—!!”*

It was the inevitable, grating screech produced when dry limestone chalk scraped across an aged, unmaintained chalkboard surface. The sound was not objectively deafening, yet within this room so quiet one could hear a dust mote settle, it struck like a rusted iron saw tearing violently across raw glass!

In that identical microsecond—

*“GASP—!!”*

All twenty-six students below, as if wired to a single terrifying tripwire, erupted into an eerie, synchronized paroxysm of dread!

In the front row, Lu Shuqing's entire frame convulsed, her fountain pen nearly punching clean through her notebook.

Because deep within her cerebral cortex, that "Imperial Lord," who had been teetering on the verge of acute distress, unleashed an unearthly, soul-shattering screech of agony:

*“AAARRRGH—! The pain! The torment!”*

*“What infernal torture is this?! Which demon is rending my soul asunder?! What venomous weapon is that female ape wielding?! Is she casting curses upon a black stone tablet?! My royal acoustic nerves are rupturing! Guards! Protect your Emperor!!”*

Little Ma thrashed its sound left wing frantically against the cardboard walls, its frantic protests translating into wave after wave of psychic shockwaves that drilled agonizing needles into Shuqing's temples!

Biting her lower lip until it nearly bled, a feral resolution flared in Shuqing's eyes. Without a second's thought, she whipped both hands up, clamping them tight over her ears!

Observing the class president's reaction, A-Tang, Big Megaphone, Old Wu, and the remaining scoundrels in the rear rows assumed Little Ma was on the verge of being vibrated to death by the chalk; all twenty-six entered a state of apocalyptic panic!

*“SWOOSH—!”*

In unbroken, chilling unison, twenty-six pairs of hands flew up, clamping down with white-knuckled force over their respective ears!

Furthermore, twenty-six boyish visages simultaneously contorted into expressions of profound agony and sheer horror. Big Megaphone ground his molars, Old Wu turned pale as milk, and A-Tang collapsed flat over his desk, using his ribcage to shield the drawer below, plunging the room into an atmosphere of impending cataclysmic annihilation!

When Wen Yating completed the character *“Fu”* and turned around with a gentle, benevolent smile, this was the spine-chilling tableau that met her eyes.

The entire classroom, hands clamped tight over their ears, staring in sheer, unadulterated terror at the chalk stub in her fingers—as though she had not written a classical word, but had rather carved a blood-dripping sigil summoning Satan himself onto the board.

The smile upon Wen Yating's face solidified into stone.

The chalk stub slipped from her nerveless fingers, tumbling to the podium with a brittle *clatter*, snapping in two.

"Wh-What is the matter with you all?" Wen Yating's voice cracked entirely, thick with an unshed sob. "Did my writing... hurt you?"

Clamping their ears without daring to loosen their grip, the students merely shook their heads with violent, synchronized intensity.

Twenty-six heads oscillated from side to side in eerie lockstep, resembling a field of sunflowers whipped by a soundless, spectral gale.

"Th-Then why are you covering your ears?!" A massive fault line finally tore through Wen Yating's psychological armor. Striding to the edge of the podium, propping both hands upon the wood, she cried out with brimming eyes: "Answer me! Lin Jingtang! You speak! What in heaven's name is happening?!"

Hearing his name summoned, A-Tang's torso gave a violent shudder.

Slowly lowering his right hand while keeping his left clamped firmly over his ear, he looked up at Wen Yating with an expression of unblemished sincerity and innocence, squeezing an answer from his throat that nearly caused her to vomit blood:

"Reporting to Teacher... the sound of the chalk dust... falling to the floor... was too loud."

Wen Yating froze in stunned disbelief.

The sound of chalk dust... falling to the floor... was too loud?!

A particulate of calcium carbonate, less than a millimeter in diameter and weighing fractions of a milligram, dropping one meter onto wooden boards, was too loud?!

Wen Yating stared unblinkingly at A-Tang's face—which radiated the pure, unadulterated message of *“I am earnest, I am innocent”*—as the grand, palatial edifice of psychology erected by Adler, Piaget, Freud, and countless titans came crashing down into smoldering rubble within her skull.

This was no prank.

This was no rebellion.

These children... were possessed.

Over the subsequent thirty minutes, Wen Yating endured the most agonizing, bizarre, and soul-chilling class period of her entire existence.

She dared not touch the blackboard again, nor did she dare draw a normal breath. She could only recite the classical verses line by line from her textbook like a wandering specter, speaking in barely audible, aspirated murmurs. Meanwhile, the twenty-six students below nodded and turned their pages with an unnatural, terrifying precision, like antique automatons stripped of their mortal souls.

The motion of turning a page had been slowed tenfold; each sheet flipped over without a sound, as imperceptible as a snowflake settling upon a dead leaf.

When the dismissal bell finally chimed melodiously along the third-floor corridor, that crisp *“ding-dong-ding-dong”* sounded to Wen Yating's ears no different from the heavenly chimes of papal absolution!

"Cl... Class dismissed."

Without even pausing to gather her notes, Wen Yating snatched up her canvas tote, abandoning all pretenses of ladylike poise, and fled through the doorway of Class 6-1 on wobbly heels like a refugee fleeing a war zone.

Only when her retreating silhouette had vanished entirely around the corridor corner did the classroom of Class 6-1 detonate into a wave of smothered, frantic, ragged gasps!

*“PHEWWWWW—!!”*

A-Tang collapsed beneath his desk like a puddle of wet clay, his uniform soaked through with cold sweat as if newly fished from a washing machine.

"I almost died..." Slumping in his chair, Big Megaphone clutched his chest with purple lips. "I thought I was gonna suffocate right in my seat!"

"Qingzi..." Sprawled on the floor, A-Tang reached into his drawer with trembling fingers, gently grazing the edge of the shoebox. "The Supreme Commander... the Commander's alright, isn't he?"

In the front row, Lu Shuqing massaged her temples with an emotionless expression.

Deep within her mind, that shrill, imperious voice had finally ebbed into faintness after its tantrums, laced with heavy drowsiness and disdain:

*“...Has that chalk-wielding female ape finally buggered off? Wise of her. I require rest. Tell that clumsy male ape not to squirm; if he dares nudge me with his gut again, I shall peck his navel to shreds...”*

Exhaling a long, weary breath, Shuqing pushed up her spectacles, sweeping a frigid glare across the surviving boys in the room:

"Round one is over. The Supreme Commander's vitals remain stable."

"However, our true troubles have only just begun."

Simultaneously, the heavy door of the Student Affairs Office on the ground floor was slammed violently open!

Pale as a ghost, hair disheveled, Wen Yating stumbled into the office, her trembling knees giving out as she collapsed to the floor beside Director Zhou Yan's desk.

Thermos in hand, Director Zhou jumped in terror at the sudden intrusion, hot tea splashing dangerously close to his trousers.

"Teacher Wen?! What happened?! Did they lay hands on you?! Did they trigger a spring trap?!" Zhou Yan snatched the iron disciplinary binder from his desk, murderous fury blazing in his eyes. "Insolence! Utterly unbridled insolence! I am calling security this instant to arrest Lin Jingtang and issue a major demerit!"

"N-No... no..."

Clutching Director Zhou's trouser leg with both hands, tears finally breached the levee of Wen Yating's eyes, her voice trembling in incoherent spasms:

"Director Zhou... there were no traps... no springs..."

"They... they aren't a demolition crew at all..."

Wen Yating lifted her face, those large, formerly luminous eyes now brimming with the suffocating terror of one standing on the precipice of madness:

"Director... they are controlled by a sinister cult!"

"They... every single one of them is possessed!!"
