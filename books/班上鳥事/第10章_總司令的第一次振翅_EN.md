# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 10: The Supreme Commander's First Wingbeat

As June arrived, the skies over Luyang Town looked as though they had been thoroughly drenched in an entire tub of viscous blue ink.

Upon the ancient banyan tree outside the Loyalty Building, tens of thousands of cicadas strained their raspy vocal cords, unleashing an unceasing acoustic bombardment across the scorching heat waves. By all standard metrics, this ought to have been Class 6-1's most rabid season—over the preceding five years, whenever sultry summer heat pushed temperatures toward thirty-six degrees Celsius, this room was either staging precision water-balloon sniper strikes or launching spit-soaked paper wads with plastic rulers until the ceiling resembled a sodden swamp.

Yet in June of this year, an eerie aura of solemnity and silence hung over Class 6-1's domain.

The doors and windows were deliberately kept ajar by only mere slivers, allowing just the slimmest ribbons of breeze to slip through. The ceiling fans had been dialed down to their lowest, gentlest setting, lazily rotating overhead with faint, rhythmic creaks.

Today was Wednesday during noon recess.

The novice homeroom teacher, Wen Yating, had been hauled off by the Office of Student Affairs to attend the countywide Earthquake Evacuation Drill Planning Conference, while Director Zhou Yan—the notorious Black-Faced Judge—was down at the gymnasium aggressively chewing out junior high slackers caught smoking behind the bleachers.

The entire east corridor on the fourth floor of the Loyalty Building was completely deserted.

Both the front and rear doors of Class 6-1 had already been noiselessly barricaded from within a minute earlier using two wooden brooms, manned by Fat Monkey and Dashenggong.

"How's the wind direction?" At the back of the classroom, Lin Jingtang lowered his voice, every syllable strung as taut as a fully drawn longbow.

Fat Monkey pressed his entire face against the frosted glass of the rear door, his round eyeballs darting frantically left and right, before turning to flash an exceptionally professional SWAT hand signal—two fingers aligned toward the far corridor, followed by a sharp, clenched fist.

"Reporting to Brother Tang: fifty-meter radius clear; the Black-Faced Judge is slamming his tactical clipboard in the gym, and Teacher Wen is currently swallowing her third antacid tablet."

Lin Jingtang drew a deep breath, turning his gaze toward the exact center of the classroom.

There, a sacred "sterile operating field" had already been cleared.

Four wooden desks had been pushed tightly together, their surfaces layered with three pristine sheets of snow-white tissue paper. Class President Lu Shuqing sat upright before the table, clear protective goggles perched on the bridge of her nose, her hands clad in latex surgical gloves requisitioned from her father's veterinary clinic, meticulously swabbing a pair of blunt-tipped stainless steel tweezers with an alcohol-soaked cotton ball.

Resting before her was the very vessel that had accompanied the entire class for twenty-five full days, exuding a faint herbal aroma of dried pomelo rind and roasted tea leaves—the Sovereign Supreme Headquarters: the Nike shoebox.

"Surgical preparations complete." Lu Shuqing lifted her eyelids, her gaze behind the protective goggles frosted with clinical detachment. "All units, report patient vital signs."

Sitting to her right, Xiaoyu immediately flipped open a hardcover pink notebook, pushing up her thick spectacles: "At 07:30 hours this morning, patient ingested 1.5 milliliters of cooked egg yolk mash blended with seven grains of hulled millet; fecal output well-formed, exhibiting healthy dark green with white streaks, zero indication of diarrhea; at 08:45 hours, patient underwent three right-foot weight-bearing trials inside the enclosure, none eliciting vocalized distress."

"Excellent." Lu Shuqing nodded curtly. "Next of kin, step forward and immobilize the patient."

The so-called "next of kin" was, naturally, Lin Jingtang.

This wild terror—who ordinarily bulldozed through basketball courts and whose colossal physical prowess had been forged by bouncing atop his father's thirty-five-ton Renault gravel truck—now looked like an utterly clumsy, terrified novice father stepping into a maternity delivery suite for the very first time.

He gingerly inserted his calloused palms into the shoebox, his palms slick with cold perspiration.

"Hey... y-you be gentle..." Dashenggong couldn't resist gnawing his fingernails nearby, hissing in an anxious whisper. "Last week Qingzi said its bone just fused and is more fragile than an egg roll! If you crush it, the whole class will hunt you down!"

"Shut your filthy dog mouth!" Lin Jingtang squeezed the words through clenched teeth, veins bulging along his forehead. "My hands are steadier than a Swiss watchmaker's!"

The moment Lin Jingtang's cupped hands withdrew from the shoebox, twenty-six pairs of eyes across Class 6-1 snapped wide in synchronized unison.

The once-feeble, blood-spattered, half-bald grey lump of trembling flesh that had teetered on the brink of death had now completely shed its pathetic nestling frailty.

Its body was adorned in a smooth, dense coat of rich greyish-brown plumage, its cheeks brushed with the delicate down unique to fledglings, its chest puffed high and proud, rounded like a plump, ripe kiwi fruit.

The sole blemish on this glorious portrait was its left leg, which remained bound by that miniature popsicle-stick splint secured with three layers of yellowed surgical micropore tape.

"Cheep—! (Unhand this Sovereign, you wretched, hairless mega-ape!)"

The instant it left the cozy warmth of its cotton nest, Little Ma thrashed indignantly within Lin Jingtang's palms, imperial fury blazing inside its pitch-black, obsidian bead-eyes.

Lu Shuqing's brow twitched imperceptibly.

In the eyes of every other student present, the little sparrow was merely wriggling with robust, youthful vitality; but inside Lu Shuqing's cranium, that shrill, piercing, insufferably haughty child's voice sounded like a miniature electric drill boring straight into her eardrums:

*"Insolence! This Sovereign is the seventh-generation rightful heir to the Luyang Banyan Empire! How dare lowly serfs constrain my sacred body with greasy paws! Should you harm so much as a single flight feather, I shall condemn your clan of upright monkeys to eternal perdition!"*

Lu Shuqing let out a cold smirk, the tweezers in her hand clicking with a metallic clack.

Lowering her gaze, she fired back an icy telepathic retort directly into the fledgling's mind:

*"Utter another word, and I'll ditch the scissors and use heavy pliers to pluck your leg down follicle by follicle."*

Within the boy's palms, Little Ma froze instantly.

That fluffy little head swiveled toward Lu Shuqing in an impossibly slow, electrified movement, pure and unadulterated horror flashing across its bead-like pupils.

*"You... you cold-blooded witch in white... you can actually comprehend this Sovereign's divine decrees?!"*

*"Stay still and shut your beak,"* Lu Shuqing warned ruthlessly in thought. *"Lin Jingtang, hold its wings down so it doesn't thrash. I'm removing the sutures."*

Lin Jingtang scarcely dared to inhale, extending two thick fingers to lightly, reverently rest over Little Ma's dorsal wing feathers.

The entire class held their collective breath, craning their necks on tiptoe, over twenty heads clustering into an airtight, impenetrable human fortress.

The air grew so silent that one could almost hear the alcohol evaporating from the cotton ball onto the metallic tweezers.

Holding her own breath, Lu Shuqing used the tweezer tips with her left hand to tease open a tiny seam along the edge of the micropore tape, while guiding the sharp scissors with her right, easing the blade in with surgical precision.

"Snip."

An almost imperceptible sigh of parting fibers.

The tape was cleanly severed down its midline. Next, gripping one edge of the popsicle stick with her tweezers as though disarming a volatile unexploded ordnance, Lu Shuqing gingerly peeled away the dressings encrusted with dried herbal residue.

It was the very splint she had whittled by hand from an orange-wood tongue depressor twenty-five days ago. Faintly visible upon its surface were five crooked, tiny ballpoint characters Lin Jingtang had once scrawled—"Lin Xiaoma Will Prevail."

"Snap... tap."

The final strip of adhesive relinquished its hold. That slender strip of wood, which had accompanied Little Ma for nearly a month, clattered softly onto the pristine white paper with a crisp, tiny ring.

The collective breath of the entire room froze solid.

There, the once-shackled left leg was at last laid completely bare to the summer air.

The previously fractured, warped joint had now completely remodeled, forming a smooth, healthy bony callus. Delicate pinkish scales coated the slender claw, its four tiny toes curling once before spreading wide and taut—as if savoring the pristine dawn of resurrected freedom!

"It moved..." Dashenggong clamped both hands over his mouth, his bulging eyes nearly popping out of their sockets as his voice choked in his throat. "The toes opened up!"

"Shh—!" Six or seven classmates glared daggers at him simultaneously.

"Set it down," Lu Shuqing commanded as she peeled off her latex gloves, a delicate sheen of perspiration glistening on her temples.

Lin Jingtang slowly, tremblingly flattened his palm.

Little Ma remained motionless for two stunned seconds.

First, it tentatively tapped the cool, lacquered surface of the desk with its right claw; then, in an act of profound discovery, its newly liberated left claw planted itself firmly down beside it.

Both feet rested perfectly even, solid as granite.

Little Ma appeared momentarily bewildered by its own balance. It hopped once on the spot, and then hopped again.

"Tap-tap, tap-tap."

Two light, crisply audible clutching sounds reverberated through the hushed classroom.

In that singular moment, Lin Jingtang's eyes welled with sudden, stinging redness. This notorious brawler—who had twice been issued formal disciplinary reprimands by the principal and had never shed a solitary tear even when whipped bloody by his father's leather belt—now swallowed hard against a convulsing lump in his throat, his mouth splitting into a goofy grin that looked uglier than weeping.

Yet the newly liberated "Seventh-Generation Rightful Heir" possessed zero intention of indulging in sentimental human drivel.

*"Gwahaha! My divine might has returned!"* Little Ma burst into a cackling, arrogant screech within Lu Shuqing's skull. *"Tremble, ignorant terrestrial vermin! Your Emperor shall once again reign supreme over the cosmos!"*

The feathered fluffball puffed its chest until it was spherical, strutting forward on two toothpick-slender legs to perform a pompous, textbook military goose-step across the joined desks.

It marched with immense majesty, pausing every three paces to jerk its tiny head skyward, casting a supremely haughty, down-turned sneer at the circle of emotionally overwhelmed human mega-apes.

The children watched in sheer, unadulterated astonishment.

"Holy crap..." Fat Monkey murmured under his breath. "Brother Tang, look at the way that little bastard struts... isn't that the exact swagger you use when walking into the Dean's office?"

"Shove off!" Lin Jingtang cursed with a muted laugh, though his eyes brimmed with helpless, overflowing adoration. "That's called majesty! That's supreme general material right there!"

Little Ma maintained its imperious imperial gait, parading all the way to the outer perimeter of Lin Jingtang's personal desk.

There, laid flat and fully exposed, was the fifth-grade mock math exam that had just been handed back this very morning.

At the very summit of that sea of red checkmarks and slashing corrections, rendered in bleeding scarlet ballpoint ink, sat a gargantuan, terrifying numeral—"07."

Teacher Wen Yating had clearly suffered a profound psychological unraveling while grading this particular paper; the "07" was violently encircled three times over in thick red loops, accented by an enormous, resentful exclamation point stabbing the margin.

Little Ma halted right before the test paper.

It cocked its head sideways, its glossy obsidian eye drilling into that hideous red emblem.

Lu Shuqing's mind was instantly bombarded by a squawk of violent revulsion:

*"What foul demonic blood-glyph is this?! Scarlet, grotesque, reeking of profound idiocy and abominable curses! How dare it defile the imperial boulevard of my royal promenade!"*

"Wait... Little Ma, what are you doing?" Lin Jingtang sensed an impending disaster.

Suddenly, Little Ma's feathers bristled outward like quills; the entire bird coiled like a spring compressed to its mechanical breaking point, and lunged violently forward!

"Thwack!"

A crisp, brutal strike rang out!

Little Ma's beak, hard and sharp as a forged steel awl, hammered with deadly accuracy right into the exact epicenter of that scarlet "07"!

"Rrrrip!"

Accompanied by the sharp shriek of tearing paper, Little Ma snapped its head backward, violently ripping away the shred bearing the "07" and its triple red halos, drilling a pristine, two-centimeter hole right through the header of the examination sheet!

"Pfft—!"

Fat Monkey nearly sprayed saliva across the desk, violently pinching his own thighs with both hands to keep from roaring with laughter.

Every boy in the room stared with eyes wide as saucers before erupting into a silent, convulsing tempest of manic celebration—pumping their fists in the air, soundlessly stomping, beating their chests, contorting their faces in ecstatic worship of this divine performance!

"A sacred bird..." Dashenggong mouthed in wordless frenzy, tears of mirth leaking from his eyes. "It just officially annulled Brother Tang's flunked exam!"

"That's not an annulment!" Lin Jingtang hissed in trembling, breathless ecstasy. "That is the Supreme Commander's Imperial Seal! That's rebirth from the ashes!"

Having pulverized the offensive paper, Little Ma was visibly thrilled with its conquest.

Spitting the paper fibers from its bill, it gave a spring with both legs, gracefully leaping onto the rim of a tall wooden cup stuffed with colored pens.

Deeming the vantage point insufficient, it bounded twice more in rapid succession, landing squarely atop the thirty-centimeter tower of Chinese dictionaries and reference tomes stacked beside Lin Jingtang.

Here, its height finally surpassed the level of the desks, bringing it level with the wide expanse of the classroom windows.

The scorching afternoon sunlight pierced through the window panes, casting dappled, golden highlights across the bird's lustrous coat of feathers.

Little Ma abruptly fell still.

That overbearing, despotic imperial bluster seemed to dissolve miraculously the instant the warm sunlight caressed its down.

It slowly turned its tiny head, gazing toward the open transom window pitched high on the wall.

Outside, the ancient banyan tree rolled in billows of deep, dark green foliage. Beyond it stretched an infinite, flawless canopy of radiant azure sky, where colossal summer thunderheads resembling snow-capped mountains were slowly massing along the distant horizon.

A warm summer breeze drifted through the transom, gently ruffling the soft, downy feathers upon Little Ma's chest.

A sudden, inexplicable ache tightened inside Lu Shuqing's chest.

In the deepest recesses of her consciousness, that comically noisy, childish voice had vanished, replaced by an ancient, primal resonance.

It was not language, but an instinctual calling etched into avian bloodlines across millions of years—the thirst for thermal air currents, the pursuit of shifting cloud shadows, the boundless longing to behold the terrestrial earth from thousands of meters above.

This classroom—fragrant with pomelo peel, tea leaves, chalk dust, and the scent of aged timber—no matter how safe, how warm, remained fundamentally a cage constructed of concrete and wood.

It belonged to the sky.

"Cheep..."

Little Ma whispered an almost inaudible sigh.

Then, beneath the breathless, reverent gaze of twenty-six children, Little Ma took a deep breath and unfurled the wings it had kept tucked away for an entire month!

They were two elegantly contoured wings, their margins etched with crisp black-and-white patterning. Under the brilliant sunlight, every single feather quill stood straight and robust, gleaming with the healthy luster of renewed vitality.

"Whir... whir..."

Little Ma began to beat its wings.

At first, it was merely a hesitant, rhythmic testing, but the frequency rapidly quickened, accelerating into a blur!

The rushing whistle of wings slicing through air echoed sharply across the room, generating a miniature, visible vortex upon the desk!

Eraser crumbs, pencil shavings, and brittle flakes of dried pomelo peel were swept into the air by the fierce downdraft, swirling in miniature cyclones above the table!

"It's going to fly..." Xiaoyu pressed her hands together in prayer against her chest, silent tears spilling over her eyelids.

Little Ma's claws kicked hard against the dictionary's hardbound cover!

In that breathtaking instant, its compact body surged aloft, hovering suspended in midair for an entire second! Its wings battered the atmosphere with furious strength, generating crisp, powerful flutters that resonated through the silence!

This was no accidental tumble, nor a panic-stricken scramble; this was a true creature of the air, having survived fracture and calamity, launching its very first solemn declaration of war against gravity!

"Flap."

A second later, its nascent endurance spent, Little Ma traced a gentle arc through the air, landing sure and steady right upon Lin Jingtang's outstretched thumb.

Its tiny claws gripped the boy's coarse, hardened knuckle with resolute strength.

Lin Jingtang froze, paralyzed with awe.

He could feel with crystalline clarity the vibration transmitting from those tiny talons—that minuscule heart thrumming at hundreds of beats per minute. It was not terror, but ecstasy, a burning fever yearning to pierce the clouds.

Lin Jingtang looked down at the tiny soul perched upon his thumb, his throat blocked by a thick, suffocating knot.

Not a single soul cheered; not a single person spoke.

Twenty-six unruly troublemakers who routinely drove deans to despair now stared with soft, shimmering lights glistening in their eyes.

It was boundless pride, the euphoria of witnessing a miracle resurrected within their own collective grasp.

Yet entwined with that joy, a faint, indefinable bittersweetness quietly bloomed within every young chest.

They had finally come to understand a simple truth.

When this bird no longer required splints, when its wings possessed the power to whip up gales... the time it had left to remain in this classroom, by their side, had officially begun its countdown.

"Qingzi..." Lin Jingtang did not look back, his voice thick with an unmistakable, congested tremor. "Is it... going to leave us soon?"

Lu Shuqing stood silently in place, quietly stowing away her tweezers and surgical scissors.

She looked toward the boundless azure expanse beyond the windowpane, releasing a soft, imperceptible sigh in her heart.

"Before it can clear the roof of the Loyalty Building in a single breath," Lu Shuqing pushed up her glasses, her voice calm and detached once more, though stripped of its usual razor edge, "let us first prepare a double ration of egg yolk millet for our Supreme Commander."

Little Ma thrust its head high, snapping its tail feathers with immense pride, as though thoroughly endorsing this exceptionally wise decree.

Outside the classroom window, however, that peacefully billowing summer thunderhead had, at some unnoticed moment, begun to take on a heavy, menacing shade of leaden grey.
