# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 1: The Third Earthquake of Zhongxiao Building

The Zhongxiao Building of Luyang Elementary School was an old, three-story building.

The construction artisans of thirty years ago evidently possessed little sense of aesthetics. The grayish-white pebble-dashed exterior walls were as coarse and rasping as an old ox's tongue, having absorbed the humid, sweltering rains of Southern Taiwan over countless seasons until patches of dark green moss sprawled across them like topographical map contours. A few dark red iron drainage pipes snaked down the corners of the building, their rusted mouths permanently draped with long-legged harvestmen spiders. By all rights, in an old-fashioned schoolhouse backed by an ancient banyan grove and facing a derelict ecological pond, time should have crept by with agonizing slowness—as slow as a green scarab beetle dozing on a window screen on an early summer afternoon, accompanied only by the lazy drifting of chalk dust in shafts of sunlight and the occasional crisp chirp of a cicada riding the breeze.

Yet, for any newcomer setting foot in this building for the first time, one merely had to climb the terrazzo staircase—polished to a gleaming mirror shine by tens of thousands of rubber-soled sneakers—up to the third floor, and all that pastoral serenity would be violently smashed to smithereens by a blast of high-pressure shockwaves hurtling head-on.

At the easternmost terminus of the corridor stood a classroom blessed with the finest sunlight and the closest proximity to the open sky.

Suspended above the lintel was a cypress plaque tilting fifteen degrees toward the lower right corner. The black lacquer on it had long since flaked and weathered away, yet three formerly dignified and upright characters could still be faintly discerned: "Class 6-1."

Rumor had it that when the masons poured the concrete slab for the third-floor ceiling back then, they could never have foreseen that thirty years down the road, this very classroom would evolve into the most active shallow seismic fault line in the entire township of Luyang.

Every single morning at exactly seven-thirty sharp, while the morning study periods of all other classes across the school hummed softly and orderly like silkworms nibbling mulberry leaves down the hallways, the seam between the two grass-green wooden doors of Class 6-1 was already spewing out billowing heatwaves of deafening decibels. That was by no means the carefree, innocent romp of ordinary children; that was an open-pit metal quarry operating at full, roaring capacity.

"A-Tang! To the left! Pass it back to Old Wu! He's still got half a pork bun in his mouth, he can't sprint!"

A deep bellow, resonating like an air-raid siren, ripped the morning air in two. That was Chen Dashan, the physical education monitor, known schoolwide as "Big Megaphone." The thoracic cavity of this eleven-year-old boy was clearly built unlike that of common mortals, boasting a lung capacity resembling a double-action blacksmith's forge bellows. He didn't even need to consciously draw breath; any casual shout from his throat was enough to induce a violent resonant rattle in the entire row of aluminum airtight windows.

Upon an impromptu "arena" cobbled together from eight desks in the dead center of the classroom, a colossal paper projectile—fashioned from over twenty crumpled discarded math test sheets and bound tight with seven layers of translucent yellow packing tape—howled through the morning rays, slicing a chaotic and savage arc across midair.

"Pass, my foot! Old Wu just swallowed, you trying to choke him to death?!"

Lin Jingtang, addressed as "A-Tang," let out a scornful laugh and sprang from the edge of the teacher's podium like an agile leopard uncoiling in mid-leap.

True to form, A-Tang had eschewed the school's mandatory white crew socks today. The legs of his deep-blue uniform shorts were rolled heroically all the way up to his groin, revealing a pair of wheat-tanned calves crisscrossed with scrapes of varying depth. His knees bore a perennial two-tiered crust of fresh and fading crimson scabs—the hard-earned badges of honor from scaling the rear mountain's perimeter wall to steal wild guavas yesterday. His eyes were as dark and gleaming as obsidian pebbles newly hauled from a roaring torrent, and in the depths of his pupils danced an untamable, almost feral vitality.

With a sudden explosive snap of his torso in midair, the warrior sneaker on A-Tang's right foot—its canvas toe already ripped with a gaping tear—whipped out an aerial volley!

"BAM—!!"

The dull impact thundered like a war drum. Striking at an impossibly treacherous angle, the paper sphere ricocheted violently off the iron blade of the ceiling fan and hurtled whistling straight toward the space above the blackboard.

"CLATTER!"

The bottom edge of the framed portrait of Sun Yat-sen hanging squarely in the center of the blackboard was clipped by the paper ball, instantly tilting forty-five degrees askew. The three solid weeks of white calcium carbonate chalk dust accumulated behind the picture frame immediately cascaded down in a magnificent avalanche, thoroughly blanketing the sign-in register on the podium.

"GREAT SHOT—!!"

A riotous wave of banging on tin pencil cases and slapping desks erupted from the rear rows of the classroom. Three boys even leaped onto their chairs, whirling their uniform jackets wildly overhead like a pack of buccaneers who had just ransacked a merchant galleon.

Amidst the swirling epicenter of this ear-shattering tumult, the seat by the window in the very first row maintained an uncanny, freezing stillness, akin to the dead eye of a hurricane.

The class president, Lu Shuqing, sat there without so much as fluttering an eyelash.

Morning rays filtered through the dappled canopy of the ancient banyan outside, casting faint shadows across her excessively pale and delicate face. She wore a pristine, long-sleeved white blouse without a single wrinkle on the collar, and her silky black hair was neatly bundled behind her neck with a navy-blue silk ribbon. Her desk surface was unnervingly immaculate. On the left stood a miniature mountain of books aligned as if measured with a drafting rule—*The Merck Veterinary Manual*, *Full-Color Comparative Anatomy*, and *Field Guide to the Wild Birds of Taiwan*—while the right held a stainless steel instrument tray meticulously arranged with tweezers, cotton swabs, saline solution, and three pairs of precision surgical scissors of varying calibers.

At this precise moment, Lu Shuqing extended two slender, porcelain-white fingers, holding a microscopic brass gear with an ultra-fine watchmaker's tweezer.

The deafening shrieks, rowdy guffaws, and clattering collisions all around her seemed thoroughly walled off by an invisible barrier of thick glass. Yet, upon the closest scrutiny, one would notice that the space between her brows was twitching at an infinitesimally faint frequency.

"Lin Jingtang," Shuqing said without lifting her head, her voice crisp and flat, yet carrying a chilling bite like a thin sheet of ice gliding across a porcelain plate. "The elevation of your leap just now was 1.12 meters, with a hang time of 0.4 seconds. Your soles brought in three grams of yellow clay from the back mountain, 1.5 grams of which dispersed onto the podium, while the remaining 1.5 grams are currently drifting toward the class's first ceiling fan in the form of suspended particulate matter."

Landing back on the floor, A-Tang propped his hands on his hips, grinning broadly to flash a mouthful of healthy, even white teeth, and laughed with utter nonchalance: "Qingzi, don't you get exhausted calculating those numbers all day long? Wasn't that kick brilliant? Curled right into the top corner! If Dr. Sun Yat-sen were alive today, he'd definitely award me the Golden Boot Trophy!"

"If Dr. Sun Yat-sen were alive today, he would simply summon the security guards to haul an incompletely evolved primate like you off to the zoo for quarantine."

With a crisp *click*, Shuqing snapped the tweezers shut and pushed the thin black-framed glasses up her nose, the lenses reflecting a cold glare in the sunlight:

"Furthermore, Chen Dashan's roaring volume just now peaked at ninety-two decibels. According to neurobiological research, prolonged exposure to such an auditory environment causes premature atrophy of the temporal lobes of the cerebral cortex. That explains splendidly why you only scored seventeen points on last week's monthly math exam—half your cranial neurons have already been shaken to death."

"Whoa! Qingzi, your tongue is seriously venomous!" In the back row, Big Megaphone scratched the back of his head aggrievedly and mumbled, "This is called natural lung power! My grandpa said having a booming voice is a sign of great fortune!"

"Your venerable grandfather likely failed to anticipate that this stroke of fortune would translate into the karmic retribution of our class's fourth collective deep-cleaning session this morning."

Shuqing spat out those words icily, then turned her head, her gaze drifting toward the swaying crown of the old banyan tree outside the window.

To outsiders, Lu Shuqing was merely an aloof, sharp-tongued, eccentric prodigy. Yet nobody knew that beneath this facade of icy, dispassionate rationality, she was enduring a torment beyond normal mortal imagination.

As the daughter of Lu Chongde—the sole rural veterinarian in Luyang Township—Lu Shuqing shouldered a terrifying secret that could easily have her branded a "severe paranoid schizophrenic": she could understand the speech of animals.

Not metaphorically, nor in any poetic, synesthetic sense, but through literal, verbatim linguistic translation.

Ever since her seventh year, her world had completely transformed. She could hear the coarse, grumbling complaints of stray dogs limping with arthritis; she could hear the foul-mouthed curses of hens laying eggs as they practically damned the grain mill boss to hell; she could even hear the old crested myna nesting beneath the eaves mocking the postman's receding hairline.

When she was seven, in her childlike innocence, she had relayed those remarks to her mother, and had even translated the neighboring white goose's scathing review of her aunt's looks in front of an entire gathering of relatives. The price she paid was catastrophic: her panic-stricken parents rushed her overnight to a premier medical center in Taipei, where twenty-four electrode pads were plastered all over her scalp in a freezing, claustrophobic EEG room, and doctors brandished thick psychiatric assessment questionnaires, solemnly counseling her parents to consider enrolling her in a closed pediatric psychiatric facility.

On that afternoon steeped in the stench of disinfectant and the terrified stares of adults, young Lu Shuqing learned two fundamental truths:

First, humankind's instinctive reaction to incomprehensible anomalies is always fear and destruction;
Second, if she did not wish to spend the rest of her life locked in a padded room swallowing antipsychotics, she had to become the most normal, rational, and unimpeachable "model human" on the planet.

Consequently, she learned to encase herself in a suit of impenetrable metal armor. She forced herself to memorize the entirety of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, weaponizing her tongue with the most clinical scientific jargon and Latin taxonomical names; she wielded razor-sharp sarcasm to repel anyone attempting to peer into her heart; and in this loudest classroom in the entire school, she compelled herself to maintain the stillness of a stone monument.

Yet God only knew how agonizing this was!

Whenever the wild rascals of Class 6-1 started howling and slamming their desks, the sparrows, Japanese white-eyes, and light-vented bulbuls nesting in the ancient banyan outside would be startled into a chorus of furious, foul-mouthed squawking:

*“Those loud-mouthed, hairless monkeys are in heat again!”*
*“Mercy! My eggs are going to crack from the vibration!”*
*“Who will drop a fresh turd on that alpha male monkey's (A-Tang's) head? I'll pay two green caterpillars!”*

The cacophony of human rowdiness and the furious curses of birds intertwined in a frenzied spiral against Shuqing's eardrums, feeling as though hundreds of pneumatic drills were excavating her temples simultaneously. Yet she could neither cover her ears nor shout at the treetops to shut up; she could only clench her jaw until her teeth ached and glare at Lin Jingtang with eyes of frozen slate.

And at this moment, the entire classroom was utterly oblivious to the hurricane their ceaseless din had stirred up at the far end of the hallway.

"SMACK—!!"

In the Office of Student Affairs on the ground floor of the Zhongxiao Building, a heavy navy-blue iron disciplinary binder was slammed violently onto the cypress desk, rattling the lid of the purple-clay teacup beside it.

Director of Student Affairs Zhou Yan surged to his feet.

Director Zhou was as emaciated as a dried bamboo pole, with sunken cheeks, prominent cheekbones, and perpetual scowls that had carved three deep, chisel-like furrows between his brows. Throughout Luyang Elementary School, from the first-graders to the cafeteria vegetable-washers, not a single soul did not dread this face. He was the most iron-fisted, ruthless enforcer of discipline since the school's founding, feared schoolwide as the "Black-Faced Judge."

Within the small eyes behind his thick spectacle lenses, a furious conflagration was currently blazing.

"Principal! Listen to that! Walk to the window and listen for yourself!"

Director Zhou's trembling finger jabbed toward the ceiling. The concrete slab overhead was shuddering with rhythmic regularity, accompanied by muffled, thunderous thuds of "THUMP! THUMP! THUMP!", laced faintly with the ear-piercing bellows of Big Megaphone Chen Dashan.

"This is morning study! This is sacred morning reading time!" Director Zhou's lips turned purple with rage, his Adam's apple bobbing violently. "All eleven other classes across the school are peacefully reciting classical prose and memorizing Tang poetry! Only the eastern end of the third floor! Only that damnable Class 6-1! They're demolishing the ceiling! They're staging guerrilla warfare on the roof!"

Slumped in his leather swivel chair, the elderly principal released a helpless sigh, reached out to pick up his cup of ink-black, bitterly stewed Kuding tea, and gently blew away the surface foam.

"Old Zhou, don't let your temper flare up so easily." The principal took a tiny sip of the bitter brew, the astringent taste puckering his liver-spotted face into a wrinkled walnut. "Children are blessed with overflowing vitality; it's only natural. It's early summer—puppies shed their coats, weeds shoot up from the dirt, and sixth-graders are on the verge of graduation. A little restlessness is understandable, quite understandable."

"Understandable?!" Director Zhou's pitch spiked, turning as shrill as fingernails scraping down a blackboard. "Principal, this is no longer a matter of shedding coats! This is outright rebellion! This is sheer terrorism!"

With a sharp snap, Director Zhou flung open the disciplinary binder in his hands. Its pages were packed with dense thickets of red crosses, the thick sheaf of paper nearly wrung dry under his white-knuckled grip:

"It's only been two months since the semester began! Two whole months! Do you have any idea how many outstanding educators we have already lost?!"

Director Zhou's finger stabbed into the roster like an iron spike:

"The first: Teacher Zhang! A star graduate straight out of teachers' college, such a bright and sunny young lad, stepping into that room with noble aspirations of teaching English with an acoustic guitar. And the result? On the very first morning, the moment he drew the curtains, a lifelike rubber rattlesnake was dangling from the sash! The snake's maw even held a slip of paper reading 'Welcome to Hell'! Teacher Zhang suffered acute shock on the spot and is currently sequestered in a Taoist temple in Changhua's Baguashan, drinking talisman water and chanting sutras to retrieve his scattered soul—he screams at the sight of a leather belt!"

The old principal mopped cold sweat from his brow, clearing his throat in awkward embarrassment: "Teacher Zhang's... psychological fortitude was indeed somewhat delicate."

"And what about Master Teacher Li?!" Director Zhou ground his teeth, his eyeballs bulging in their sockets. "Teacher Li is a county-level distinguished educator, an iron lady who led five consecutive honors-track graduating classes! On her very first day, she issued an ironclad decree to tame those hooligans with an iron fist of love. And the result? On the third afternoon, when she slid open the podium drawer to fetch a red pen, that damnable Lin Jingtang and Chen Dashan rigged a launcher using three discarded bicycle chains and industrial springs! Thirty thousand flakes of holographic rainbow glitter, Principal! Thirty! Thousand! Flakes!"

Director Zhou shut his eyes in agony, as if that tragic tableau were still replaying before him:

"Thirty thousand glitter flakes discharged at twenty meters per second! Teacher Li's stern countenance was gilded on the spot into a shimmering Thousand-Armed Guanyin! Even her eyelashes were dripping with reflective pink spangles! Her blood pressure spiked past 185, and an ambulance drove straight across the athletic field with sirens blaring! The first thing Teacher Li did upon discharge was apply for unpaid leave, swearing that she would rather slaughter pigs in an abattoir than ever set foot in Luyang Elementary again!"

The old principal's hand holding the teacup wobbled slightly, his gaze shifting guiltily toward the ceiling.

"And then Teacher He!" Director Zhou's voice now took on a mournful, weeping timbre. "Elder Master He is sixty-one years old this year, a master of Chinese classics and an exemplar of moral rectitude whom we coaxed out of retirement with great reverence! The gentleman has practiced Tai Chi for thirty years, adhering strictly to Laozi's philosophy of governing by non-action. Facing a storm of paper airplanes, he smiled and meditated; facing turtle graffiti on the blackboard, he closed his eyes and chanted scriptures. And then, during yesterday afternoon's study period, Lin Jingtang went so far as to place three plump caterpillars of the common green birdwing butterfly right onto the lid of Teacher He's purple-clay teacup!"

Director Zhou drew a ragged breath, his chest heaving violently:

"That wretched grub crawled with excruciating leisure straight onto the center of Teacher He's thread-bound copy of the *Dao De Jing*, and right between the six large characters 'Attain extreme emptiness; preserve profound stillness,' it dropped a perfectly round, dark-green pellet of caterpillar frass! Teacher He stared at that tiny pellet of dung for the duration of an entire incense stick. Finally, he rose, uttered not a single word, wrote the two words 'Follow Fate' on the blackboard, flicked his sleeves, and walked out! He quit the faculty chat group overnight, and word has it he has already purchased a one-way train ticket to the Zhongnan Mountains to become a hermit!"

The office fell into a tomb-like hush.

Only the muffled vibrations transmitting through the ceiling overhead seemed to mercilessly mock the utter impotence of the two school administrators.

Setting down his teacup, the old principal sank deeply into his broad leather sofa, appearing to age five years in a single instant. He rubbed the bridge of his sore nose and exhaled a long, ragged breath:

"Old Zhou, I understand everything you've said. But... this coming Wednesday, the County Education Bureau's inspector will personally lead a delegation to evaluate our school for the 'Model Moral Education Zone' honor. There are only two quotas in the whole county. If Class 6-1 is leaderless and brings down the roof in front of the inspectors, all of Luyang Elementary's facility renovation subsidies for the second half of the year will evaporate into thin air."

"Then what do you propose we do?!" Director Zhou threw his hands up in absolute despair. "Every single experienced teacher in this school turns pale as a sheet the moment they hear the words 'Class 6-1'! Teacher Wang, who teaches mathematics, suddenly claimed this morning that his severe osteoporosis has flared up and he can only walk with crutches; Teacher Chen from the science department even submitted a certificate proving his pet dog needs to be spayed! Who is willing to touch this powder keg?!"

The old principal did not reply.

He pulled open the bottom drawer on his right, revealing a brand-new resume without a single curled corner.

Using two fingers, the old principal pinched the document and slid it gently before Zhou Yan.

"Take a look at this."

Frowning, Zhou Yan leaned forward.

Pasted in the upper left corner was a two-inch color headshot. The woman in the photo appeared to be twenty-four or twenty-five, wearing her glossy black hair in a neat ponytail. Her features were soft and delicate, graced with an impeccably standard, sunny smile, and her gaze shone with the crystalline, sacred radiance unique to fresh graduates—resembling a wild daisy blooming on a spring hillside, untouched by harsh storms.

Neatly written in the name field were three elegant characters:

**Wen Yating.**

Education: Master of Education in Educational Psychology and Counseling, National Taiwan Normal University.

Specialties: Adlerian Psychology, Alpha Brainwave Guidance, Children's Projective Drawing Tests, Nonviolent Communication.

"Th-This... this is the substitute teacher who reported for duty this morning?" Director Zhou pushed up his spectacles, a flicker of bewilderment flashing through his eyes, swiftly overtaken by an even deeper pang of pity. "Principal, have you lost your mind? This girl looks frail enough to stumble backward three steps if a stray dog barks at her! What does she know about Class 6-1? Her textbook theories of 'loving education' and 'nonviolent communication' aren't worth a fart in Lin Jingtang's eyes! If you throw her in there, she'll be leaping out the third-floor window in tears within three days!"

"She is no ordinary college graduate, Old Zhou." The old principal pointed at the graduate school listed on the resume, his gaze deep as a dried well. "She holds a master's degree in educational psychology. She has analyzed a thousand case studies of troubled youth and penned a thirty-thousand-word thesis on behavioral intervention. Most importantly—she has only just graduated. The light in her eyes is still burning."

"Light? Ha!" Director Zhou let out a cold, rasping sneer. "Five minutes inside Class 6-1, and that little spark will be thoroughly snuffed out by chalk dust!"

"Treat a dead horse as if it were alive." The old principal waved his hand wearily. "She has already been waiting in the reception lounge for half an hour. I've spoken with her, merely telling her that Class 6-1 has 'a bit of unique personality.' She is brimming with confidence, even volunteering to tackle our toughest class, insisting that this alone embodies the true calling of education."

Gazing at the sweet-smiling young teacher in the photograph, the corner of Director Zhou's mouth twitched uncontrollably, a murmur steeped in profound sorrow escaping the depths of his throat:

"Namo Amitabha... Lord have mercy, what a sin."

At this very moment, in the classroom of Class 6-1 at the eastern end of the third floor, A-Tang was standing with one foot perched atop the teacher's desk, brandishing a blackboard eraser tied with a red scarf, surveying his domain like a triumphant warlord.

"Reporting to the Commander!" Zhou Ziqiang, "Ziqiang Express," sitting by the back door, suddenly jerked his head up, tugging down his oversized hoodie to reveal a pair of shifty, beady eyes. "The uncle at the ground-floor security booth just said a new teacher has arrived at the principal's office! Female! Young! Ponytail! Carrying a massive stack of papers printed with bizarre diagrams!"

Those words struck like a droplet of water plunging into boiling oil, instantly igniting the entire classroom into pandemonium!

"HOLY CRAP! A fourth sucker has arrived to die!"

"Is she strict? Does she look mean? Did she bring a disciplinary ruler?!"

Flushed with excitement, Big Megaphone slammed his schoolbag against his desk: "Who cares what she brought! A-Tang, what contraption are we using this time? I still have two packs of popping candy in my desk! Should we snatch the human skeleton model from the science lab and hang it behind her?!"

"Old hat! Utterly pedestrian!"

A-Tang smiled haughtily and waved the blackboard eraser in his hand:

"This time, we're going to welcome our new teacher with high artistic refinement. Old Wu, fetch half a bucket of fermented rag water from the wash sink; Big Megaphone, lock the transom window above the doorframe, leaving a gap of exactly three centimeters—"

A feverish gleam of mischief flared in A-Tang's eyes as he pumped his fist before the entire class:

"Brothers! Let the new teacher know who truly rules Class 6-1 of Luyang Elementary!"

"YEAH——!!"

Twenty-odd arms pumped wildly in the air, desks and chairs once again letting out an ear-splitting rumble, and the entire Zhongxiao Building genuinely seemed to tilt and sink under the weight of this tempestuous youth rebellion.

Nobody noticed Lu Shuqing, sitting by the window, quietly stowing her stainless steel tweezers back into their metal case.

Her delicate brows knit faintly as she pushed up her glasses, gazing out toward the sprawling ancient banyan tree that blotted out the sun. Deep within the impenetrable foliage, a few dark feathers flitted past, accompanied by the frail, agonizing cry of a sparrow.

For reasons unknown, Shuqing's heart gave a sudden, violent jolt.

She possessed a premonition—

Some absurd, chaotic, yet blood-soaked and burning catastrophe had already cast its web of fate beneath the old banyan outside the gates, waiting for this pack of reckless, ignorant boys to charge headlong into it.
