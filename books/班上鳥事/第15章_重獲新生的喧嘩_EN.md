# A Class of Feathered Troubles

## Chapter 15: The Resurrected Uproar

By early July at Luyang Elementary, the chorus of cicadas had reached a fever pitch of unmitigated delirium.

The ambient air simmered like an iron fresh from the forge, while the red polyurethane running track on the sports field shimmered beneath the scorching sun with visible waves of rising heat. Final examinations had just concluded; strewn along the fourth-floor corridor of the Loyalty Building were blank mock-exam sheets carried by passing breezes, amid the pre-dismissal restlessness tingling with sweat, watermelon rind, and the intoxicating scent of summer liberty.

And within the classroom of Class 6-1, that "Extreme Silence Operation" that had lasted for over a month had at last been officially dismantled.

"FAT MONKEY! GIVE ME BACK MY RULER——!"

A deafening roar rattled the newly repaired transom windowpanes so violently they threatened to hairline-fracture.

Lin Jingtang vaulted cleanly over a wooden desk with one hand braced against the edge, charging down the aisle like an unchained beast heading straight for the hallway.

Five meters ahead of him, Fat Monkey fled for his life, his rotund belly bouncing in undulating waves as he brandished a thirty-centimeter iron ruler, howling in terror: "Brother Tang! Just let me measure my bicep circumference! I'll give it right back when I'm done!"

"That's pure lard! Do you have a single muscle fiber on your body besides lard?! Halt right there, you bastard!"

Following the duo's frantic sprint, the classroom instantly dissolved into a whirlwind of uproarious chaos.

Dashenggong, holding half-melted ice cream in his mouth, stood atop the teacher's podium using a chalkboard eraser as a microphone, passionately broadcasting this "Pursuit of the Century" to the entire room; in the rear row, A-Bao and Lao Wu folded four colossal paper airplanes out of thick vocabulary notebooks, colliding them with pinpoint accuracy midair into a fluttering blizzard of white confetti.

Chairs screeched abrasively across the floor, sneaker soles squealed sharply against the tile in emergency braking maneuvers, laughter, curses, wrestling shouts, textbooks fluttering in the draft...

That very tumult—which had once driven three consecutive teachers to the brink of psychiatric collapse and terrorized the entire Loyalty Building—had, in this glorious instant, completely and triumphantly reclaimed this room.

Yet Teacher Wen Yating, standing beside the podium organizing the summer reading checklists, wore not the faintest hint of panic upon her face.

Today, she wore a refreshing light-blue cotton sundress, her long hair gathered into a tidy, buoyant ponytail. Watching the little monkeys somersaulting across the desks, Wen Yating merely shook her head with an affectionate smile, lightly tapping her stainless-steel tumbler against the podium:

"All right, that's enough! Stop dragging the chairs! Lin Jingtang, if you turn in a blank summer reading log again, I'm calling your father on the first day of next semester to pick you up in his gravel truck!"

"Teacher Wen! Old Lin drove out to Hualien hauling gravel today—he can't hear you!" Lin Jingtang snatched back the iron ruler, straddling his chair as he panted, grinning with shameless, carefree abandon.

Wen Yating pressed her lips together in amusement, lowering her head to resume grading the workbooks.

Just then, from the far end of the corridor, came the familiar, measured cadence of heavy, authoritarian footsteps.

"Clack, clack, clack."

It was the rhythm of hard leather heels striking the bare concrete floor.

Not a single student in the entire school could ever mistake this rhythm—Director of Student Affairs Zhou Yan, the Black-Faced Judge himself, was approaching with hands clasped behind his back in his signature patrol gait.

By all standard protocols of the past five years, the moment those footsteps were heard within twenty meters, the entire room would scatter like mice before a tomcat, plunging into terrified stillness within half a second.

Yet this time, when Zhou Yan's silhouette framed the rear doorway, the raucous uproar in the room merely eased downward by half a musical key.

Zhou Yan still wore that long face as black as the bottom of a cast-iron skillet.

His razor gaze swept across the confetti drifting across the floor, past Lin Jingtang straddling his chair, and finally came to rest upon Wen Yating.

"Teacher Wen." Zhou Yan paused in the doorway, his tone deadpan and cool. "For the end-of-term schoolwide sanitation review, your class sold off every scrap of paper in the recycling bins. It is currently so vacant not even a cockroach could survive inside. General Affairs has awarded your class 'Special Distinction.'"

With that, Zhou Yan produced a certificate stamped with a bold scarlet seal from his pocket, casually pinning it to the bulletin board beside the rear door.

"Holy crap! Special Distinction?!" Dashenggong's eyes widened.

"Thank you, Director," Wen Yating nodded with a gentle smile.

Zhou Yan did not smile. He turned, his gaze lingering for a solitary second upon the faint pink scar on Lin Jingtang's forehead where the scab had flaked away; an imperceptible twitch touched the corner of his lips, and with hands clasped behind his back, he marched away down the corridor without looking back.

Only when Zhou Yan's back vanished around the stairwell did Fat Monkey pat his chest, squawking in disbelief: "Did the Black-Faced Judge... just compliment us?"

"Don't flatter yourself, you fat pig!" Lin Jingtang chuckled, shoving the iron ruler into his desk drawer.

If anyone were to pull open Lin Jingtang's desk drawer at this very moment, they would uncover an astonishing secret.

Deep behind the chaotic clutter of comic books and snack wrappers, the corner that had once sheltered the Nike shoebox was now scrubbed immaculately clean. Resting there was a miniature wooden perch whittled from fragrant orange wood, beside a small handful of golden millet wrapped carefully in wax paper.

It was the permanent royal banquet Lin Jingtang maintained for the "Supreme Commander," who might drop by at any given hour.

And in the front row of the classroom, Class President Lu Shuqing remained seated at her desk.

Wearing new lenses in her black-rimmed spectacles, she held a comprehensive geographical atlas, her demeanor tranquil, composed, and razor-sharp.

To an outside observer, everything seemed to have returned to the original starting line.

Class 6-1 remained the rowdiest, wildest, most headache-inducing class in the entire school.

Yet if one listened closely with an attentive heart, one would discern that the very texture of this classroom's uproar had undergone a profound metamorphosis.

When Fat Monkey slipped on melted popsicle juice, what erupted was no longer cruel, spiteful mockery, but three hands extending instantly to seize his bulky frame before he could fall;

When an introverted boy in the back accidentally tipped over his water canteen, Xiaoyu didn't squeal with disgust, but quietly passed over half a pack of clean tissues;

Even the notoriously belligerent Lin Jingtang instinctively slowed his stride while sprinting down the corridor to steer clear of first-graders carrying thermoses of hot water.

Their clamor was no longer the feral howling of wild beasts tearing at one another, but a gathering of living, breathing human beings embracing each other's youth without armor.

Because deep within their souls, they had collectively guarded a fragile, miraculous spark of life.

"Whoosh—"

A refreshing cross-breeze swept through the room, rustling the dark green canopy of the ancient banyan tree outside the window.

Right in that instant.

Amid the blizzard of paper airplanes and the carefree laughter of the children, an exceptionally crisp, piercing chirp suddenly sliced through the ambient din, landing with pristine precision upon the eardrums of every single soul in Class 6-1.

"CHEEEEP—!"

The cry was impossibly high, impossibly arrogant, impossibly magnificent.

Like an emperor touring his territories incognito, standing atop the battlement to hurl down a haughty reprimand toward his subjects.

In that singular microsecond.

No one shouted an order; no one flashed a signal.

Fat Monkey froze mid-stride; Dashenggong halted with the eraser suspended in midair; the children chasing paper planes stopped dead in their tracks.

The entire raucous classroom, bustling like a morning marketplace, had its audio track severed by an invisible switch in the very same microsecond—

Plunging into a full, perfect second of absolute, unadulterated silence.

Twenty-six heads, joined by Teacher Wen Yating—twenty-seven souls in total—turned in flawless, telepathic synchronization toward the open transom window.

Perched upon the sunlit windowsill rested a plump, round, grey-brown fledgling.

Its plumage gleamed with robust health beneath the midsummer sun, the bold black-and-white patches upon its cheeks resembling the heraldic badges of an imperial general. It cocked its fluffy little head, its obsidian-bright bead-eyes surveying the classroom with supreme haughtiness, before hopping twice with agile, springy claws along the sill.

It dipped its beak down, precisely snatching up a solitary grain of golden millet left upon the ledge.

Then, as if sensing a presence, its gaze shifted toward the front row of the classroom.

Lu Shuqing sat there, a tender, shimmering ripple of light softening the gaze behind her spectacles.

Deep within her consciousness, that familiar, boisterous, insufferably pompous voice echoed with crystalline clarity:

*"Hairless mega-apes, what are you gaping at? This Sovereign is merely inspecting my imperial domain, dropping by to examine your tributes! My heavens are boundless; I haven't the leisure to play games with you daily!"*

The corners of Lu Shuqing's lips curved upward into a gentle, radiant smile as she whispered soundlessly in her heart:

*"Understood, Supreme Commander. Now get lost."*

As if comprehending the farewell, Little Ma tossed its head skyward in majestic pride, its broad wings snapping wide to either side!

*Whir-flut-flut-flutter——!*

The vigorous whistle of wind detonated across the windowsill once more.

Beneath the tear-glistening, smiling gaze of twenty-seven souls, Little Ma launched with both claws, transforming into a streak of grey lightning rocketing straight toward that boundless, flawless azure canopy above!

The one second of silence shattered.

"LITTLE MA——! IT'S LITTLE MA——!!"

"IT CAME BACK! IT ACTUALLY CAME BACK!"

"BROTHER TANG! YOUR MILLET WASN'T WASTED AAAAAAAAH!"

The entire classroom erupted into an incandescent volcano of joy!

Children surged toward the windows like a joyous tide, leaning over the sills, straddling the desks, waving uniform shirts skyward, sending the most triumphant, proud, and carefree cheers of their lives into the open blue!

Golden sunlight poured through the windowpanes, flooding the entire room in warm, dazzling luminance.

The sky outside was vast and deep—vast enough to cradle the migration and soaring flight of every wild bird under heaven.

And within this little classroom, the clamor continued to resonate with vibrant warmth.

Paper wads still flew, and recess was still filled with wild running.

They remained that very same Class 6-1 that drove the whole school to distraction.

Yet deep within every young chest, there now existed a tender, quiet sanctuary.

Where a single, warm, unfading feather would rest forever.
