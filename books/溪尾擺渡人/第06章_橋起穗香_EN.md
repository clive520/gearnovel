# Chapter 6: Epilogue: A Bridge Rises with Fragrant Grain—The Ferryman's Golden Grain

In the autumn of 2016, a radiant morning arrived that would be etched forever into the hundred-year history of Xiwei Village.

Banners fluttered along both banks of the Wu River, and crowds turned out by the thousands.

Following years of meticulous planning, arduous construction, and an investment of hundreds of millions of dollars, the Xiwei Bridge was inaugurated with grand ceremonies.

The red-and-white steel-arch structure soared like a magnificent rainbow, bridging the broad Wu River with monumental pride. Its massive caissons and earthquake-resilient flood dikes, anchored deep into the riverbed bedrock, were engineered to withstand once-in-a-century catastrophic deluges. A grueling journey that once required braving the rapids on a fragile bamboo raft—or spending nearly an hour detouring on motorbikes through Nantou and Changhua—could now be crossed in a mere five minutes by car, seamlessly stitching Xiwei to the vibrant heart of Taichung's Wuri District.

At the very front of the ribbon-cutting procession stood an elder with silvered hair, his posture tall and commanding.

That was Li Shun'an. Tempered by decades of professional toil, he had long become a renowned authority in bridge engineering across Taiwan. Over the years dedicated to planning and driving the construction of the Xiwei Bridge, he had stood countless times under the blazing sun upon these scorching gravel bars, surveying hydrology and mapping geological faults.

After the ribbon-cutting concluded, the bustling crowds gradually dispersed. Li Shun'an walked slowly and alone across the vast, smooth deck of the six-lane bridge.

He stopped at the highest apex of the main steel arch, resting his hands upon the gleaming railing to peer down.

Dozens of meters below, the waters of the Wu River still raced relentlessly westward. Beside the colossal modern piers, one could still catch faint glimpses of the mossy, broken stone steps of the long-abandoned ferry dock, and that centennial chinaberry tree scattering autumn leaves in the river breeze.

From inside his trench coat pocket, the elder produced a yellowed slip of red paper, immaculately preserved within a sealed protective film. It was that "Handwritten Ferry Ticket" that Uncle Rong had entrusted to someone to return to him before passing away forty years earlier. The brushstrokes remained unmistakable, and that tiny red thumbprint was as vivid as the day it was pressed.

Gazing at the roaring currents, his eyes grew warm with unshed tears. Leaning into the wind, Li Shun'an whispered softly to the river:

"Uncle Rong, do you see it? Ah-Shun did not break his word. Our Xiwei Bridge... has finally risen!"

The river wind whistling through the steel arches emitted a resonant hum, like a distant bamboo whistle answering him from the far bank of eternity.

Yet, while the bridge was built and the enclave's isolation conquered, a quiet sorrow had begun to stir within Ah-Shun's heart.

The swift convenience of modern transit had drawn the younger generation away into the concrete jungles of the cities; broad ribbons of asphalt brought traffic, yet also swept away the warmth once shared along the earthen field ridges. Would the ferryman's courage, the solidarity of the bamboo footbridge, and the profound reverence for the golden grains nurtured by the river's black silt be forgotten as the older generation faded away?

The answer to this question was delivered a few years later by his daughter, Lin Xiaotang.

In 2021, with the opening of the Xiwei Second Bridge spanning the Maoluo River to connect Changhua and Nantou directly, Xiwei Village formally became the vital crossroads linking three counties.

Having resigned from a lucrative marketing executive career in the city, Lin Xiaotang returned to Xiwei Village brimming with bold ideas and a bottomless affection for her roots.

"Dad, the bridge connects the roads between the shores, but we must use the memories of this land to connect the hearts of the new generation," Xiaotang said resolutely, standing beside the shimmering paddies.

Backed by the full support of the Wuri District Farmers' Association and Xiwei Elementary School, Xiaotang crafted a nationally celebrated agricultural-cultural brand named "The Ferryman's Golden Grain," centered upon Great-Grandpa Shuisheng's fertile black earth, Uncle Rong's legendary ferry, and her father Ah-Shun's legacy of the Wave-Calming Pole and the great bridge.

She promoted and refined the heirloom rice into nationally crowned champion varieties (Taikeng 16 and Kaohsiung 147); she turned the harvesting of sandbar watermelons into interactive family experiences; and most importantly, she transformed the century-old hydrology of the Wu River into a living, outdoor classroom beneath the children's feet.

This visionary initiative was formally honored as one of the Council of Agriculture's "Top Ten Agro-Tourism Experience Routes" nationwide.

Time softly looped back to that late autumn afternoon introduced in the Prologue.

"Sister Xiaotang, are we going to learn how to punt a bamboo raft across roaring waters just like Grandpa Ah-Shun did when he was little?"

The children of Xiwei Elementary School, gathered around the ancient thorny bamboo Wave-Calming Pole, finished listening to the half-century saga with shining eyes and thumping hearts.

"Of course we are!" Xiaotang smiled, gesturing with a sweep of her arm. "Though not by risking our lives in the wild river, but right here in our school's revitalized ecological canal! Follow me, everyone!"

Along the tranquil canal caressed by a gentle breeze, several small bamboo rafts—lashed in the ancient style by master craftsmen with slightly upturned bows—floated steadily atop the crystal-clear waters.

Shedding their sneakers, the children pulled on non-slip sandals woven from rushes. Guided by Xiaotang and the teachers, they stepped aboard in disciplined rhythm, one by one.

The buzz-cut boy carefully clasped the glossy, chestnut-burnished Wave-Calming Pole with both hands.

"Stand tall! Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly flexed!" he bellowed at the top of his lungs, reciting Uncle Rong's mantra from the story.

"Where should we look?" the children behind him chorused in unison.

"Look at Mount Dadu in the distance!"

The children burst into a cascade of crisp, joyful laughter. With a gentle push of the pole against the canal bed, the raft parted the water into shimmering ripples, gliding smoothly forward.

In the vast paddies beside the canal, another group of children wearing conical bamboo hats squatted barefoot in the warm, rich alluvial black mud. Clasping child-safe sickles alongside grandfatherly farmers wearing beaming smiles, they harvested golden, drooping bunches of rice that filled the air with sweet fragrance.

When the harvest ended, the children gathered beneath the shade of an ancient chinaberry tree along the ridge, eagerly unwrapping their steaming "Ferryman's Harvest Bamboo Lunchboxes."

Inside rested freshly steamed, naturally taro-scented champion rice from the Wu River granary, topped with slow-braised pork belly glistening in sweet soy reduction, crisp golden preserved radish omelets, and thick slices of refreshing, cold-tossed local luffa.

"Wow! This rice is so fragrant and naturally sweet, I could eat three bowls without any side dishes!"

"This is the very rice Sister Xiaotang talked about—grown in the black silt from the Central Mountain Range, packed with all the nutrition of the river!"

The children shoveled the rice hungrily into their mouths, grains clinging to the corners of their lips, their faces glowing with pure, unadulterated happiness.

Standing atop the high earthen ridge, seventy-something Li Shun'an rested on his cane, his daughter Lin Xiaotang at his side. Father and daughter gazed quietly upon the vibrant scene unfolding before them.

In the distance, traffic streamed across the grand arches of the Xiwei Bridge—the thrumming artery of modern civilization racing ahead. Near at hand, golden rice waves rolled endlessly beneath the autumn wind, while the joyful laughter of children echoed across the fields.

Standing silently in the center of the ridge was that five-meter thorny bamboo Wave-Calming Pole, seasoned by countless tempests. Sprinkled with golden husks of harvested rice, it radiated a warm, eternal gleam beneath the afternoon sun.

The great river rolls on; the great bridge soars high. Though the ferryman's paddle has rested, the courage, compassion, and reverence rooted within this black earth have transformed into an endless sea of golden grain—passed down from generation to generation, blossoming without end.
