# Chapter 2: Summer Floods and Churning Waves—The 6:00 AM School Crossing

With the onset of the fifth lunar month's plum-rain flood season, the Wu River—ordinarily flowing with meek grace—turned into an ancient behemoth completely enraged by days of torrential storms.

Relentless downpours cascaded across the Central Mountain Range, stampeding wildly down steep slopes. Within a brief span of three days, the broad gravel bars on the southern bank, once carpeted with wild reeds and stones, were devoured whole. The water level surged upward by more than three meters, transforming what had been a gentle, shallow brook into a furious expanse of roiling yellow mud over five hundred meters wide.

At 5:20 on a daybreak morning, the sky hung like a slab of heavy, leaden grey, the air saturated with the rank mineral stench of clay and the sodden chill of crushed vegetation.

Eleven-year-old Li Shun'an stood in the sticky mire below the ferry embankment, wrapped in a makeshift raincoat fashioned from an old plastic fertilizer sack, his hands clutching the straps of his blue cloth school satchel for dear life. Inside rested his dog-eared Mandarin and arithmetic textbooks, which his mother had wrapped meticulously in two layers of oiled paper the night before to keep the river spray from ruining them.

Beside Ah-Shun, a dozen schoolchildren hailing from every corner of Xiwei Village gradually assembled. Some stood barefoot, carrying their rubber shoes tied together with hemp twine; others, barely eight or nine years old, shrank shivering behind their older siblings.

Xiwei Village had no upper elementary school of its own. If the village youth wished to finish their primary education, their only path was to cross this broad, treacherous river every morning to reach Kali Elementary School atop the northern dike.

This dawn voyage across wind and water was what the villagers called the "School Crossing."

"Hey Ah-Shun, why is your face so ghostly pale? Are you so terrified you're about to pee your pants again?" A-Tai, a mischievous classmate, poked Ah-Shun in the ribs, cackling gleefully. "Look at those swells—they're taller than an ox's back! If the bamboo raft takes a roll, that flea-sized courage of yours will be swept straight into the Taiwan Strait to feed the sharks!"

Ah-Shun bit his lower lip and said nothing, yet his hands hidden inside his damp sleeves trembled violently against his will.

How could the expanse before them be called a river? It was a stampeding dragon of yellow mud. Monstrous waves laden with driftwood, weed clumps, and even torn boughs from uprooted chinaberry trees collided violently in midstream, churning up half-man-high froth of yellow foam and emitting a deafening, gut-shaking rumble. With a single glance, Ah-Shun's stomach heaved; that horrifying memory from last year—their yellow ox crying out in despair as it was dragged beneath the rapids—flashed once more before his eyes.

"Toot—toot—!"

A short, piercing note from a bamboo whistle cut through the howls of gale and water, sounding abruptly across the river beach.

The unruly clamor of the children went dead silent in an instant.

There, in the swirling backwater near the shore, a new raft lashed from twelve giant thorny bamboo trunks bobbed rhythmically against the swells. Uncle Rong stood barefoot, his veined, broad feet pinned like iron spikes to the center of the slick bamboo deck. He wore a broad, oiled conical hat and an ancient cape woven from coarse palm fiber, water trickling continuously from its frayed bristles.

Cradled firmly within the crook of his arm was that very five-meter-long thorny bamboo Wave-Calming Pole.

"The bigger ones stand along the sides, the little ones squat in the middle! Hold your bags tight against your chests—no moving about!" Uncle Rong's voice boomed like a bronze bell, barking instructions with unshakeable calm. "Board one by one. Plant your feet on the raised bamboo nodes; don't step into the gaps and slip into the river!"

Like disciplined ducklings led by the older children, the group carefully stepped across the gangplank onto the raft.

When it was Ah-Shun's turn, his right foot barely grazed the ribbed bamboo before an incoming surge pitched the raft sharply upward, only to drop it plummeting the next second! Ah-Shun let out a choked gasp as his knees gave way. He fell forward onto the deck, his hands clawing wildly at the bamboo stalks beside him, his knuckles stark white, his entire frame frozen stiff as granite.

A-Tai snickered from behind, but Uncle Rong's icy glare snapped his way, causing the prankster to yank his neck into his collar immediately.

Uncle Rong strode forward, hoisted Ah-Shun up by his collar with a single hand, and gave the boy's cold nape a firm, grounding squeeze.

"Stand tall! Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly flexed, just like planting rice seedlings in deep mud!" Uncle Rong's tone was heavy, yet possessed a mysterious power that cut through the tempest. "When the river sways, you sway with it. If your body learns to bend like bamboo, the waves will never knock you down."

Clenching his teeth, Ah-Shun followed Uncle Rong's mantra, shifting his bare feet onto the reinforced seams and rope lashes, willing his center of gravity to sink deep.

"Cast off! Raise the pole!"

With a sweep of his arm, Uncle Rong unhitched the thick hemp hawser from an old chinaberry tree ashore and hauled it onto the bow. Then, driving his arms forward with immense leverage, the Wave-Calming Pole screeched against the submerged riverbed gravel, and the entire raft—laden with more than a dozen children and their hopes—glided smoothly like a slender willow leaf, carried by the eddy out into the raging main current.

A mere twenty meters from the bank, the raft was seized violently by the savage, downward pull of the main channel.

"Crash—!"

A head-on wave battered the upturned bamboo prow, shattering into a spray of icy mud that rained down upon the children's heads and faces. Several lower-grade girls began to sob softly, while A-Tai fell dead silent, crouching pale-faced in the raft's belly, hugging the bundled bamboo underfoot.

Ah-Shun stood half a step behind Uncle Rong. He shut his eyes tightly, hearing only the hollow, booming thuds of brown rapids slamming against bamboo hulls. The raft was pitched violently upward and downward, banking side to side as though it might rip itself apart at any second.

"Ah-Shun! Open your eyes!"

A thunderous roar from Uncle Rong shattered the storm above.

Trembling, Ah-Shun was forced to open his eyes, blurred with grit and mud.

"Where are you looking? I told you to look at Mount Dadu!" Uncle Rong's feet were welded to the slippery bamboo skin. Though the gusts whipped his palm cape wildly, his spine remained as upright as an ancient pine. "You stare at the foam beneath your feet—apart from sludge and whirlpools, can you find a shred of a path out? Look up! Lift your chin!"

Shakily, Ah-Shun tilted his head upward.

Piercing through the curtains of driving rain and tumbling waves, the dark green, gentle ridge of Mount Dadu stood motionless and monumental beneath the stormy canopy of the northern bank.

Miraculously, the moment his eyes locked onto that unyielding mountain ridge, Ah-Shun's dizzying vertigo began to fade, and the chaotic din around him seemed to pull back into the distance.

"Do you see it clearly now? If the mountain doesn't move, you will know exactly where this river is trying to drag us!"

Uncle Rong shouted as he drove the Wave-Calming Pole deep into the torrential channel, sounding the riverbed several meters below. The pole entered the water with a high-pitched hum of tension. Uncle Rong leaned his weight to the left, utilizing the violent rebound of water striking bamboo to skillfully slip the raft along the edge of a massive, gaping whirlpool!

This was no reckless battle against nature; it was finding that sliver of momentum within the river's wild pulse to hitch a ride on its current. This was Uncle Rong's master technique honed over thirty years—"Harnessing the Current to Steer the Craft."

However, just as the raft sliced into the most perilous midstream trench, disaster struck without warning!

Several hundred meters upstream within the roiling brown waves, a waterlogged tree trunk as thick as a man's embrace came hurtling down the rapids at staggering speed, charging like a battering ram straight toward the raft's exposed flank!

"Uncle! Watch out for the log!" crouched in the middle, A-Tai shrieked in terror.

The raft was pinned squarely within the main chute; if they swung broadside to dodge, the current would roll them upside down in a heartbeat. If they held their course, several hundred pounds of waterlogged timber smashing into fragile bamboo would splinter the raft instantly!

Amid the frantic screams of the children, Uncle Rong's eyes turned sharp as cold steel.

"Ah-Shun! Come here—take the head of the pole! Hold it for half a breath!"

Without giving Ah-Shun a moment to hesitate, Uncle Rong's warm, iron-strong hand clamped onto the boy's chilled palms, thrusting the colossal, violently shuddering thorny bamboo Wave-Calming Pole straight into his grip!

The sheer torque transmitted through the bamboo nearly flung the scrawny boy headlong into the depths. Freezing water surged up the pole, the power of the torrent pulling against his small palms like a herd of wild horses. The rough bamboo rind scraped his skin raw in an instant, sending a searing flash of pain through his arms.

"Don't let go! Root your feet! Look at Mount Dadu!" Uncle Rong bellowed beside his ear.

In that split second, the image of that drowning ox flashed through Ah-Shun's mind, along with the memory of his mother's paper-wrapped books, and the terrified eyes of a dozen village children behind him.

"Ahhh—!"

The eleven-year-old boy erupted in a tearful roar. He did not let go. Throwing the entire weight of his body down onto the Wave-Calming Pole, his bare toes dug fiercely into the bamboo seams, holding out against the undertow that threatened to rip them sideways!

In those two agonizing seconds bought by Ah-Shun, Uncle Rong freed his hands. Grabbing the spare wooden paddle from the stern, the veteran sprang to the edge of the raft, his muscles bunching like iron, and shoved fiercely against the surging timber!

"Thump!"

A dull crash exploded with a burst of spray. The colossal log skimmed the curved bamboo tail by inches, tumbling harmlessly into the downstream rapids.

The raft rocked violently a few times before leveling out, finally cutting across the most murderous midstream chute and gliding softly into the gentle backwaters of the northern dike's mudflat.

"Scrape—"

The bamboo belly slid over the soft silt of the shallows, coming to a steady halt.

The entire world seemed to fall quiet in an instant, leaving only the crisp chirping of birds in the northern bamboo grove and the ragged gasps of the children who had cheated death.

Uncle Rong retrieved the pole and looked at Ah-Shun, who remained lunged forward, soaked to the bone and shivering, his hands still clutching the Wave-Calming Pole in a death grip. For the first time, the stern corners of the old ferryman's mouth curved upward into an expression of profound pride and approval.

"All right, Ah-Shun. You can let go now. We made it across," Uncle Rong said softly.

Only then did Ah-Shun awaken as if from a dream. Slowly prying his stiff, numb fingers apart, he saw the raw abrasions on both palms, faint traces of blood mingling with yellow river silt.

A-Tai and the other children stared at Ah-Shun, their eyes devoid of their usual mockery, replaced by genuine, heartfelt awe.

Uncle Rong bent down, using his rough thumb to wipe the mud from Ah-Shun's forehead, his voice deep and solemn: "Ah-Shun, if you hadn't held that pole for those two seconds, our raft wouldn't have made it. You weren't a coward today—you were the guardian of this boat."

Tears mingled with rainwater finally burst from Ah-Shun's eyes, but this time, it was not out of terror. It was from a newfound dignity and strength taking root deep within his bones.

One by one, the children hoisted their satchels and darted across the gangplank onto the stone steps of the northern dike. Morning sunlight pierced through the storm clouds, illuminating the red schoolhouse of Kali Elementary School in the distance.

Ah-Shun walked at the very rear of the group. Before cresting the levee, he couldn't help but look back across the vast river toward the slender raft bobbing gently in the shallows, and the old ferryman standing motionless like a monument with his Wave-Calming Pole.

At that exact moment, a resolute vow was silently forged within the young boy's heart:

When he grew up, he would master the greatest knowledge in the world to build an unshakeable, unfloodable bridge across this wild Wu River with his own two hands—so that no one would ever have to risk their lives to cross this water again!
