# Chapter 15: The Final Lecture in the Last 24 Hours

Late in the night of the sixth day following his death, the fluorescent lighting in the autopsy suite of the New Taipei City Mortuary was dialed down to its softest, warm amber hue.

Outside the window, the winter rain pattered rhythmically, tapping against the weathered iron window frames. Upon stainless steel table number one in the center of the suite, all pathological tissue sampling on Jiang Jingyuan's body had been completed. The internal organs had been neatly returned to the thoracic and abdominal cavities, silicone packing firmly supporting the sternum and abdominal wall, awaiting the final suturing and aesthetic restoration.

Upon the instrument tray lay a freshly unsealed pack of heavy curved cutting needles and thick black number three surgical braided silk.

Intern Ah-Da stood by holding a basin of warm sterile saline and clean white towels, his eyes red and swollen, his lips tightly pressed together, not daring to breathe too loudly.

Chen Nianzhou had changed into a clean, crisp set of surgical scrubs. With latex gloves on his hands, his right fingers held a heavy needle holder. Standing at the head of the table, he looked down at the long Y-shaped incision across his mentor's chest, his fingertips trembling ever so slightly.

"Nianzhou, to grip the needle holder properly, lock your thumb and ring finger into the rings, resting your index finger just ahead of the hinge—only then can the force translate cleanly through your wrist."

An aging, gentle voice drifted from beside the autopsy table.

Dressed in the dark gray wool jacket that had accompanied him through most of his life, hands in his pockets, Jiang Jingyuan's soul stood beside the dissection table, his eyes attentively watching the instruments in his student's hands.

Only twenty-four hours remained until the stroke of midnight—the final deadline of the Touqi crossing.

The old man's phantom was more translucent than in the days before, fringed with a faint, morning-mist halo, yet his discerning eyes remained as piercing as cold stars against the night sky.

"Master... I remember," Nianzhou answered softly, adjusting his grip on the instrument.

The needle holder firmly clamped the junction between the middle and posterior thirds of the curved needle. The sharp tip pierced perpendicular at ninety degrees through the skin margin along the mandibular midline, rotated smoothly with the wrist's torque, and emerged cleanly through the opposing skin edge.

The black silk traced a taut, straight arc in the air before being neatly secured with a surgeon's knot.

"Very good. Bite distance five millimeters from the margin, interval one centimeter—a textbook continuous baseball stitch," old Professor Jiang nodded gently, his eyes brimming with quiet satisfaction. "When you performed your very first post-autopsy suture in the NTU anatomy hall twenty years ago, your clumsy fingers snapped three threads in a row and nearly drove the needle straight through your own thumb."

Nianzhou focused intently on the needlework. The black stitches advanced like precision clockwork gears, drawing the wound edges together millimeter by millimeter, his lips moving softly: "That time... you punished me by making me copy the *Forensic Surgery Handbook* three times over. But that evening, you bought two bowls of angelica duck noodle soup at the Nanya Night Market in Banqiao, insisting that I eat both drumsticks."

"That was because you were far too gaunt," old Professor Jiang chuckled faintly, looking out into the rainy night beyond the window. "You were only twenty-two, having lost both parents to a mining collapse and a car crash, growing up in an orphanage, staying up every night in the morgue, thin as a bamboo reed. You fainted twice in the hallway that winter. If I hadn't fed you some duck meat to build your strength, who would be standing here today to carry the mantle of forensic medicine?"

The black thread danced steadily through Nianzhou's hands—one stitch, two stitches, three stitches...

Every stitch that landed carried echoes of decades past; every seam closed felt like repairing a memory etched deep into their shared bones.

Standing nearby, intern Ah-Da sniffled and asked in a hushed whisper: "Professor Jiang... I often hear senior doctors say that forensic pathologists face so much decay, brutality, and darkness every day, witnessing humanity's ugliest faces—doesn't your heart ever fall ill? What kept you and Master Nianzhou going for decades without ever giving up?"

Jiang Jingyuan turned around, looking kindly at the young junior colleague, gentle laugh lines crinkling the corners of his eyes:

"Foolish boy. Forensic doctors are not immune to fear and sorrow. But we know better than anyone that the dead lying on this table have lost all power to speak for themselves. If even the pathologist holding the scalpel turns away because he fears the dark, then no one left in this world will ever stand up to demand justice for them."

The old professor pointed toward the needle and thread gliding steadily through Nianzhou's hands: "Look at the needle in Nianzhou's hands. We are not merely stitching flesh; we are using our needle and thread to mend the final pieces of justice and peace for a fractured, cruel mortal world. As long as you hold reverence for life in your heart, the darkness will never consume you."

Ah-Da nodded heavily, his eyes reddening, engraving those words indelibly into his soul.

"Master, back then... why did you never question me about my psychic ability?" Nianzhou kept his head lowered, the needlework never ceasing, yet his voice carried a tremor held back for twenty years. "Every professor in the medical school called me insane, but from day one, you were the only one who believed me."

Jiang Jingyuan was silent for a moment. His soul slowly turned, gazing up at the antique wall clock that had hung above the autopsy table for twenty years, his eyes deepening into boundless distance.

"Thirty-five years ago, I had a son," the old man's voice was very soft, like a dry leaf falling upon still water. "When he was six, an expired antibiotic mistakenly administered at a clinic triggered anaphylactic shock, and he stopped breathing in my arms. I had just received my forensic license back then. I dissected countless bodies, yet I could not save my own flesh and blood."

Beside them, Ah-Da shuddered, tears spilling instantly over his cheeks.

"On that torrential rainy day, when I saw you huddled trembling in the hallway of NTU College of Medicine, whispering to the empty air, 'Uncle, it doesn't hurt anymore; I see the wound on your back,' " Jiang Jingyuan extended a translucent hand, gently brushing the gray strands at Nianzhou's temples. "In that moment, I knew you were no madman. You were a ferryman sent by heaven to bring back the final words of those who died with unavenged wrongs. Looking at you, I saw the child I never had the chance to raise."

A hot tear finally broke free from Nianzhou's eye, splashing heavily against the cold silk thread and sinking into the deceased's skin.

Nianzhou gritted his teeth, his hands never faltering for an instant. When the final stitch emerged from the pubic symphysis, the once-frightening Y-shaped incision had been restored into a seamless, symmetrical line, resembling an old scar that had peacefully healed in slumber.

Nianzhou severed the silk, tied the final locking triple knot, and set down the needle holder.

Taking the warm towel from Ah-Da, he gently washed away all traces of blood and formalin from his mentor's face and neck, then dressed the body in a brand-new black ceremonial gown belonging to a Lifetime Distinguished Professor of NTU Institute of Forensic Medicine, neatly draping the crimson doctoral hood over the shoulders.

Finally, Nianzhou carefully placed the gold-rimmed reading glasses that had accompanied the old man for half his life back upon the bridge of his nose.

Beneath the lamps, the old man appeared serene and dignified, his hands folded peacefully over his abdomen, wearing the First-Class Forensic Medal on his chest, looking as though he were merely preparing to step onto a podium to deliver a lifetime's academic address.

"You restored me well, Nianzhou," Jiang Jingyuan's soul stood by the table, inspecting his mortal shell with an appreciative nod. "A great forensic pathologist must know not only how to coldly cut open flesh to pursue truth, but also how to hold the highest reverence, stitching back the dead's final dignity and returning them honorably to this earth."

The old man walked over to the duty station, pulled open the heavy bottom drawer of the steel cabinet, and pointed to an old notebook bound in thick black cowhide with frayed edges inside.

"Bring it out," Jiang Jingyuan said.

Nianzhou stepped forward, lifting the brick-heavy black notebook with both hands.

The moment he opened the flyleaf, Nianzhou's chest clenched violently. On the yellowed first page, written in bold, powerful fountain pen script, were four characters: *The Notebook of the Silenced・Volume One*.

It was the very progenitor of the black notebook Nianzhou carried every day!

For forty years, Jiang Jingyuan had meticulously recorded the autopsy findings and final wishes of one thousand and eighty-two unnatural deaths, alongside secret ledgers of his own money spent burying impoverished victims and funding trusts for orphaned children!

"Forty years ago, shortly after I became a coroner, a corrupt high official tried to threaten me into ruling the suspicious drowning of a whistleblower as an accidental fall," old Professor Jiang recalled calmly, a tranquil light in his eyes. "I was suspended for six months, and the windows of my home were smashed. But I would rather haul bricks on a construction site than sign my name to a forged inquest report. The path of justice is never an easy avenue; it demands generation after generation using their spines to hold back the dark."

On the blank page at the very end of the notebook, two hours before his murder the previous night, old Professor Jiang had inscribed his final entry with a trembling pen:

"No. 1083. Name: Jiang Jingyuan. Case: Suspicion of murder committed to conceal multinational pharmaceutical trial malfeasance. Final Wish: Guard the conscience of Taiwanese forensic medicine; await my beloved student Nianzhou to hold the blade for me."

Nianzhou could restrain himself no longer; his knees gave out, slamming onto the concrete floor, clutching the heavy notebook tightly against his chest as he bowed his forehead against the ground, weeping with body-shaking grief.

Twenty years of fatherly love, twenty years of sacred legacy—in this moment, all poured forth in burning tears.

"Master..." Nianzhou choked out between heaving sobs, his shoulders shaking uncontrollably, "did Nianzhou... live up to your teachings?"

Jiang Jingyuan's soul walked over to Nianzhou and slowly knelt down before him.

He reached out both hands, like a father lifting up his son, gently steadying Nianzhou's shoulders, his eyes brimming with boundless pride and peace:

"Nianzhou, I have autopsied tens of thousands of bodies in my life, witnessing endless human darkness and greed. But the single greatest achievement of my life was not becoming institute director, nor how many textbooks I published."

The old man's voice echoed through the cavernous autopsy suite, warm and clear:

"The finest, most flawless masterpiece of my life—one I can present without shame before heaven and earth—is having taught you, Chen Nianzhou."

Outside the window, the drizzle had quietly ceased, and across the pitch-black sky, stars began to pierce through the parting clouds.

Only one day and night remained until the final tolling of the midnight bell for the Touqi crossing.

And in this autopsy room that had witnessed countless departures and sorrows, the torch of life had achieved its most sacred passing, ready to meet the final dawn and eternal rest.
