# Chapter 13: The Mentor on the Autopsy Table

At five o'clock in the morning at the New Taipei City Mortuary, the entire inquest corridor was engulfed in a deathly stillness.

The grinding rumble of the gurney's rubber wheels rolling over the epoxy floor echoed with jarring harshness through the empty hallway. Forensic intern Ah-Da pushed the heavy metallic gurney, both hands shaking violently, his tears already fogging up his safety goggles, splashing heavily in large droplets onto the blue waterproof shroud.

Following behind the gurney, Chen Nianzhou's footsteps were as heavy as if forged of lead.

He wore his washed-out green surgical scrub gown, his hands shoved deep into the pockets of his lab coat, balled into tight fists, his fingernails digging so hard into his palms that blood seeped out, yet his facial expression was as frozen and unyielding as a stone carving devoid of warmth.

Twenty years ago, when Chen Nianzhou was still a young intern struggling on an orphan subsidy scholarship at the medical school, he was ostracized by peers as a schizophrenic freak because he could often perceive the whispers of departed spirits, nearly being forcibly committed to a psychiatric ward by the department chair.

Back then, it was Jiang Jingyuan who pushed past all objections, pulling the rain-drenched Nianzhou to his feet in the pouring storm along the medical school corridor.

"Kid, hearing the voices of the dead isn't madness; it's a heavy burden bestowed on you by the heavens," the then-fifty-year-old Jiang Jingyuan had shoved a warm bag of wheel cakes into his hands, his gaze stern yet fiercely bright. "But in a court of law, the voices of the dead cannot save the living—only the scalpel held in your hands can. From today onward, learn the blade from me."

For twenty years, Jiang Jingyuan had personally placed that first number eleven scalpel into his hands, guiding him through Taiwan's most perilous homicide scenes, shielding him from countless political and underworld pressures. To Chen Nianzhou, who had neither father nor mother, Jiang Jingyuan was not merely his foundational mentor in forensic medicine, but the only biological father figure he knew in this callous mortal world.

The gurney slowly came to a halt beside stainless steel autopsy table number one.

Captain Zhang stood at the door of the autopsy suite, his eyes bloodshot, his lips bitten pale. The mouth that usually held a cigarette was clamped tight, looking at Nianzhou, his voice so raspy it was barely audible:

"Nianzhou... Banqiao Precinct and National Taiwan University Hospital... issued the administrative death certificate early this morning. The cause of death was preliminarily ruled as 'acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock.' Professor Jiang's remarried wife and the vice dean of the medical school are waiting in the outer hall with attorneys, demanding the body be transferred to the cremation furnace this morning."

Nianzhou said nothing. He walked forward slowly, extending hands accustomed to wielding scalpels for decades; when his fingertips touched the edge of the cold blue shroud, they trembled uncontrollably.

The shroud was slowly pulled back.

Lying upon the cold stainless steel table was an elderly man with snow-white hair and a thin, serene face.

The towering giant of Taiwan's forensic pathology, founding titan of the Ministry of Justice's Institute of Forensic Medicine, and Lifetime Distinguished Professor of Forensic Pathology at National Taiwan University—sixty-eight-year-old Jiang Jingyuan.

The elderly man was clad in a dark gray wool sweater, his eyes softly shut, a faint, peaceful smile lingering at the corners of his mouth, looking as though he had simply drifted off to sleep while reviewing case files late at night.

"Professor Jiang... was texting me from his laboratory at eight last night, saying he had reviewed a fifteen-year-old cold case involving a multinational pharmaceutical company's suspected poisoning, finding a crucial hidden metabolite..." Ah-Da fell to his knees beside the autopsy table, sobbing uncontrollably. "At nine thirty, news broke that he had suddenly died on the sofa in his lab... How could this be... How could it be acute myocardial infarction..."

Nianzhou closed his eyes and inhaled deeply, his chest heaving violently, forcing the tearing agony back down into his gut.

When he opened his eyes again, his gaze had transformed back into pure, almost merciless forensic scrutiny.

He donned double-layered sterile latex gloves, bending over to examine the twelve-hour post-mortem body of his mentor.

"If this truly were cardiogenic shock brought on by an acute coronary syndrome, the victim in the agonal phase of severe myocardial ischemia would suffer agonizing retrosternal crushing pain and a profound sense of impending doom; the facial expression would be contorted in agony, and the lips and nail beds would display severe, dark cyanosis," Nianzhou's voice was chillingly low, laden with surgical detachment. "Yet Professor Jiang's expression is far too serene. His lips are faint pink, and his nail beds display an unnatural pale rose."

Nianzhou gently parted old Professor Jiang's eyelids with his fingers.

Beneath the beam of the forensic examination lamp, scattered across the lower palpebral conjunctiva and fornix mucosa, were four or five tiny, pinpoint-sized dark red petechial hemorrhages.

"Look here," Nianzhou pointed at the hemorrhages, looking coldly toward Captain Zhang at the doorway. "Petechial hemorrhages of the palpebral conjunctiva. This is a classic sign of asphyxiation caused by an acute spike in intrathoracic pressure or central nervous system respiratory depression. A patient who dies purely of acute coronary occlusion from ruptured atheromatous plaque could never present with mechanical or toxic asphyxial signs."

Captain Zhang's chest jolted violently: "Nianzhou, you mean... Professor Jiang was murdered?!"

"And the killer is a master who understands pathology and toxicology intimately." Nianzhou straightened up, taking a high-power magnifying loupe and methodically scanning every centimeter of Jiang Jingyuan's hairline, behind the ears, the neck, and the interdigital webs of all four limbs. "The killer deliberately staged the scene as an acute cardiac death, pulling strings to rush a death certificate within twelve hours to cremate the body and destroy all physical evidence."

Just then, a sharp, arrogant clatter of leather shoes approached outside the airtight door.

The door was shoved wide open, and a middle-aged man in an expensive dark gray suit and gold-rimmed glasses barged inside flanked by two corporate attorneys in black, his face radiating undisguised fury.

Director of the Institute of Toxicology at National Taiwan University College of Medicine, and one of Jiang Jingyuan's proudest former students—Song Mingze.

"Chen Nianzhou! What kind of madness has possessed you?!" Song Mingze strode up to the autopsy table and pointed a finger sharply at Nianzhou's nose, shouting, "The professor suffered from hypertension and arteriosclerosis for over a decade! The chief of emergency medicine at NTUH and the Banqiao Precinct coroner jointly examined him and confirmed death by acute myocardial infarction triggered by overwork! Our mentor's wife is weeping in the lobby right now, and as his student, you defy the family's wishes and withhold his body in the autopsy room?! Do you realize this constitutes desecration of a corpse and unlawful coercion?!"

Nianzhou stared expressionless at his former senior classmate, his gaze as cold as looking at a soulless anatomical specimen.

"Director Song, as an expert in toxicology, you should know better than I do," Nianzhou slowly raised his hand, pointing at Professor Jiang's eyes. "Petechial conjunctival hemorrhages paired with non-cyanotic nail beds might be mistaken for cardiogenic shock in a chaotic emergency room, but in judicial inquest standards, it is an unequivocal indicator of suspicious death."

Song Mingze's expression twitched slightly, but he sneered and produced a document from his leather briefcase, slamming it onto the table: "Forensic Pathologist Chen, do not tarnish our mentor's honor with your paranoid neuroses! This cremation authorization bears the signature of our mentor's wife, Mrs. Lin. The professor performed countless autopsies in his life; in lectures, he repeatedly stated that upon death, he never wanted his own body to be cut open! As his students, we should honor his dying wish and allow him a dignified cremation!"

"Dignified?"

A deep, elderly voice tinged with sardonic mockery and sharp authority resonated faintly in the freezing air of the autopsy suite.

The temperature in the room plummeted several degrees, the surgical lamps flickering faintly as an ethereal bluish mist gathered in the air.

Song Mingze and the two lawyers felt an uncanny chill shoot down their spines, yet what they could not see was that directly opposite the stainless steel autopsy table, a translucent figure wearing an immaculate white lab coat and reading glasses had quietly seated himself upon the revolving iron stool.

It was Jiang Jingyuan.

Holding an ethereal bone-china pipe, the old man's frosted brows twitched as he looked with a faint, wry smile at Song Mingze, his spectral eyes laden with boundless disappointment and melancholy:

"Mingze, my boy... when I taught you toxicology all those years ago, I taught you how to use mass spectrometers to identify poisons and save lives, not how to use cryptic receptor blockers to disguise murder as a heart attack."

Song Mingze shivered violently, instinctively feeling the back of his icy neck and glancing around: "Who... who is talking?!"

Nianzhou took a deep breath, his gaze looking right through Song Mingze to meet the warm, razor-sharp spiritual eyes of his mentor.

"Director Song," Nianzhou's voice was as cold as iron, carrying undeniable authority, "under Article 218 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, whenever a judicial police officer or forensic pathologist suspects foul play in an unnatural death, they have the statutory authority to petition the prosecutor for an autopsy without family consent. Prosecutor Lin's formal autopsy directive was delivered to the New Taipei Criminal Investigation Corps three minutes ago."

Captain Zhang stepped forward immediately, his burly frame blocking Song Mingze like an iron wall, warning coldly: "Director Song, please vacate the autopsy suite with your lawyers immediately. Anyone obstructing a judicial autopsy can be arrested on the spot."

Song Mingze's face turned completely dark, his fists clenching tight as he glared at Nianzhou through gritted teeth: "Fine... Chen Nianzhou, you have guts! If you cut him open and find nothing, I guarantee you won't even keep your public forensic medical license!"

With that, Song Mingze spun around in fury and slammed the door behind him and his attorneys.

The heavy airtight door snapped shut with a resounding thud.

The autopsy room returned to dead silence, save for the monotonous drone of the ventilation fans.

Ah-Da wiped his tears, standing beside Nianzhou as he looked at their mentor on the table, his lips trembling: "Master Nianzhou... are we... are we truly going to perform an autopsy on Professor Jiang's body?"

Nianzhou did not answer. He stepped slowly to the instrument tray and reached out a trembling hand, picking up a cold, gleaming number eleven scalpel.

The fingertips holding the blade shook violently.

Twenty full years.

Ever since he first held this blade twenty years ago in the NTU anatomy hall, Chen Nianzhou's hand had never shaken a fraction of a millimeter, no matter how putrefied, charred, or mangled the corpse before him was.

Yet today, lying beneath the shadowless surgical lamp was the mentor who gave him a second life and passed down all his knowledge.

"Nianzhou."

The familiar, aging voice echoed beside his ear.

Jiang Jingyuan's soul floated gently to Nianzhou's side, laying a translucent hand over the back of Nianzhou's trembling hand. The touch was freezing, yet filled with a father's boundless majesty and warmth.

"I taught you for twenty years. When did I ever see your hand shake like this?"

Old Professor Jiang smiled gently, nudging his reading glasses, his gaze resting upon the mortal shell on the table: "What lies on that table is merely an aging husk of flesh; but standing before it, you are New Taipei's sole specialist forensic pathologist. In forensic science, even my corpse is simply your Seventh Subject."

The old man's eyes blazed with solemn conviction as he spoke deliberately, word by word:

"Cut, Nianzhou. Uncover the truth for your teacher. This is the final lesson I give you as a forensic pathologist."

Nianzhou's eyes reddened entirely, a hot tear spilling from his cheek to splash against the cold surgical steel.

He drew a deep breath, forcefully steadying his trembling fingers. When he lifted his head again, every trace of weakness and sorrow had vanished, replaced by an immovable, crystalline resolve.

"Ah-Da, start the audio recorder."

Nianzhou's steady voice rang out in the autopsy suite: "Autopsy Case Number: New Taipei District Prosecutors Office Jia-007. Deceased: Jiang Jingyuan. Pathologist: Chen Nianzhou. Commencing autopsy."

Day one after death. One hundred and forty-four hours remaining until Touqi.

A final duel of mentorship, cross-realm communion, and master-level toxicology began under the cold edge of the scalpel.
