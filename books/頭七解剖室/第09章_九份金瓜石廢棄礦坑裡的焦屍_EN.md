# Chapter 09: The Charred Body in the Abandoned Gold Mine of Jiufen-Jinguashi

The northeast monsoon swept thick sea fog down from the steep, grayish-black ridges of Keelung Mountain, shrouding the entire mountain towns of Jiufen and Jinguashi in a piercing, damp, bone-chilling white mist.

The iron gate of the abandoned Jinguashi Mine Shaft No. 6 had long rusted and collapsed. Murky yellowish-brown acidic mine water, pungent with a sharp acidic tang, trickled along the gravel rail bed, glinting with an eerie metallic sheen in the dark.

Yet amid the age-old stench of sulfur and heavy-metal mine runoff, there now drifted a sickening, cloying, heavy stench of burnt flesh. It was the unmistakable stench of death produced when infernal heat boils human subcutaneous fat and chars protein.

"Blech—!"

Scarcely ten meters into the mine tunnel, forensic intern Ah-Da suddenly grabbed onto a damp acacia timber support post, ripped off his activated carbon mask, and bent double, dry-heaving violently. The beam of his explosion-proof flashlight shook frantically in the gloom, illuminating the grotesque, twisted charred mass lying deeper in the drift.

"Ah-Da, put your mask back on," Chen Nianzhou said, carrying his heavy aluminum forensic field case as he stepped steadily through the cold, muddy mine water, his tone devoid of the slightest ripple. "Take deep breaths through your mouth. Press the tip of your tongue against the roof of your mouth. Vomiting at a forensic scene is not merely embarrassing—your stomach acid and vomitus will completely contaminate and destroy trace physical evidence."

Ah-Da's face was chalk-white, tears provoked by the acrid smoke brimming at the corners of his eyes as he scrambled to pull the mask back over his mouth and nose: "M-Master Nianzhou... this smell is too horrifying... It's like... like an entire pig thrown into a vat of boiling asphalt and torched to cinders..."

"That is because the fat content of the human body closely mirrors that of swine," Nianzhou said, crouching before the cordon and briskly donning double-layer sterile latex gloves and puncture-resistant shoe covers. "Moreover, he was wearing synthetic clothing before death. When plastic fibers melt and burn against skin, they release exceptionally high concentrations of dioxins and aromatic toxic gases."

Following behind them, Captain Zhang wiped rainwater from his face, chewing betel nut with his brow furrowed into a tight knot: "Nianzhou, the local precinct's initial report suggested this might be a homeless man seeking shelter from the cold in the abandoned shaft. He may have accidentally caused a gas explosion while cooking instant noodles with a portable butane stove and burned himself to death. They did find the mangled, exploded remnants of a butane canister right beside the body."

Nianzhou did not answer immediately. He flicked on two high-lumen, cold-light forensic lamps, the brilliant icy-white beams instantly illuminating the deceased at center stage with stark clarity.

It was a severely carbonized corpse categorized as level four on the Crow-Glassman Scale.

The limbs were curled tightly against the torso with fists clenched, elbows and knees locked in sharp flexion, presenting the classic "pugilistic stance" of forensic pathology. This posture was not born of any pre-mortem urge to fight, but resulted from muscle protein thermal coagulation—when exposed to temperatures exceeding two hundred degrees Celsius, the thicker, stronger flexor muscle groups contract far more forcefully than the extensors, locking into extreme thermal spasms as they dehydrate.

The epidermis was incinerated almost entirely away, the remaining dermis cracked into charred, hardened plates resembling alligator leather, while the thoracic and abdominal walls had split open from thermal shrinkage, exposing rib bones with a dull, calcified grayish-white gleam.

"Accidental gas explosion?" Nianzhou let out a soft, mocking scoff, his eyes glinting with razor-sharp coldness. "Old Zhang, in all your years working homicide, have you ever seen someone who accidentally triggers a gas explosion first take high-tensile copper wire and lash his own right wrist tightly to the acacia support timber behind him?"

Both Captain Zhang and Ah-Da froze at the same time.

Using long, slender stainless-steel forceps, Nianzhou gently nudged aside the charred, carbonized scapular region on the corpse's right side.

On the shadowed side of the timber post, a blackened yet resilient two-strand insulated copper wire was bound tightly around the distal epiphysis of the victim's wrist bones. Because the wrist had been lashed fast to the timber, the victim had been unable to flex his right arm when the inferno raged; his right arm had been wrenched into a dislocated, grotesque reverse angle, and the charred ends of the radius and ulna had even fractured from avulsion under extreme muscle contraction!

"Fuck..." The betel nut in Captain Zhang's mouth stopped moving entirely, his face darkening with fury. "Someone tied him alive to the pit timber and torched him..."

"Not merely tied," Nianzhou said, shifting the cold-light lamp toward the left side of the charred skull and lightly tracing the blackened cranial vault. "Look here. Along the left temporoparietal bone runs an eight-centimeter radiating linear fracture, with distinct inward beveling and bone plate depression along the fracture margins."

Ah-Da quickly leaned in and adjusted his glasses: "Master, isn't that just a 'heat fracture' where the bone naturally bursts from thermal stress in the inferno?"

"Good question, Ah-Da," Nianzhou murmured with a flicker of approval. He pointed a surgical probe precisely into the inner groove of the fracture. "Heat fractures typically occur on exposed bones where the soft tissue is completely incinerated—they are fine, curved, resembling spiderwebs or concentric delamination, and they never feature depressed bone fragments. But this fracture is a straight, radiating burst, and deep within the fissure lies a half-centimeter-thick dark red blood clot."

Nianzhou straightened up, looking directly at Captain Zhang: "That is an extradural hematoma resulting from blunt force trauma inflicted before death. In post-mortem thermal hematomas caused by fire, the blood boils into a chocolate-brown, spongy mass distributed along the sagittal sinuses. But this clot is dense, firm, with well-preserved erythrocytes. That proves the victim was struck violently over the skull with a heavy blunt instrument before death, lost consciousness, and was then dragged into the depths of the mine shaft, lashed to the timber, and burned alive."

"This is cold-blooded, sadistic premeditated murder," Captain Zhang growled through gritted teeth, slamming his fist against the tunnel wall, dislodging gravel and mud.

"What about time of death?" Captain Zhang pressed.

"Body temperature readings are completely meaningless in carbonized remains," Nianzhou observed, glancing at trace hydrolyzed free fat droplets in the murky water surrounding the corpse. "However, the mine shaft remains at a constant sixteen degrees Celsius year-round, with humidity above ninety-five percent. There are no signs of green putrefaction or maggots on the body, and the core liver temperature deep within the chest is still two degrees above ambient. Estimated time of death is between forty-eight and sixty hours ago."

"Meaning... two days ago?" Ah-Da counted on his fingers. "Today is the third day since death!"

The moment the words "third day" fell, the pungent stench of burnt flesh deep within the drift was suddenly joined by a strong scent of old mothballs and damp, sweet baked sweet potatoes.

The antique work lights strung along the tunnel ceiling began to flicker violently, their harsh white light fading into a dull, somber amber glow.

The hairs on the back of Ah-Da's neck stood on end instantly. He instinctively clutched at his chest, feeling that Mazu talisman freshly replenished with incense ash in Beigang warming once again—yet this time, the pouch did not burn; instead, it emitted a gentle, faint warmth.

"Master Nianzhou... d-do you hear that..." Ah-Da pointed with a trembling hand toward a narrow, abandoned side drift deep within the tunnel. "It sounds like someone is coughing over there... coughing so deep, so hard..."

From the cramped, damp shadows of the tunnel came a ragged, agonizing wheeze like broken bellows—"Hah... hack, hack... hooh..."

It was the miserable asthma of someone whose lungs were choked with phlegm and dust, fighting for air with every ounce of their remaining life.

Nianzhou slowly turned around, his gaze piercing through the dim beam of the explosion-proof lamp.

At the corner of the rock wall in the side drift, a translucent figure was curled weakly atop a pile of gravel.

It was an elderly man in his seventies, his gray hair sparse, his back severely hunched, wearing blue miner's overalls stained with black grease and covered in stitched patches. His cheeks were hollowed, the joints of his hands thick and deformed, his chest heaving violently. With every breath, a metallic rasp whined from deep in his throat.

The old man's spirit kept his head low, anxiously tapping his own chest with translucent hands, his cracked purple lips parting to squeeze out faint, broken murmurs in Taiwanese:

"Little girl... don't be scared... Grandpa's coming right back... Grandpa will cook rice porridge for you..."

Nianzhou watched the elderly man in silence, a wave of restrained ache washing through his chest.

As a forensic doctor, he had examined countless dead. But the deformed bulging chest and agonizing wheezing of this old miner's soul were all too familiar—it was the irreversible occupational disease left behind when Taiwan's older generation of miners traded their lives for coal and gold, breathing in deadly free silica dust: advanced stage-three silicosis.

"Elder," Nianzhou crouched down and spoke across the void in a gentle, low Taiwanese dialect, "take your time, breathe slowly. Within seven days, your killer will not escape. Whatever unfinished burden you carry in your heart, I will carry it for you."

The old man's phantom shuddered violently and slowly raised his head.

It was a weathered face etched with deep crevices, flecked with indelible specks of black quartz dust embedded deep within the skin. His eyes held no violent vengeance, only boundless pleading and hot, silent tears:

"Forensic Pathologist Chen... I beg of you... I don't matter... but the rice crock in my kitchen... my mute little granddaughter at home... hasn't eaten a bite in two whole days..."

Before his voice could fade, the old miner's figure dissolved into a wisp of vapor carrying the scent of mothballs and damp earth, disappearing into the chilly drafts of the tunnel.

Nianzhou closed his eyes, drew a deep breath, and let his chest heave once.

When he opened his eyes again, every trace of fatigue was gone, replaced by a cold, razor-sharp resolve akin to a scalpel's blade.

"Old Zhang," Nianzhou said, rising to his feet with chilling composure, "check the household registration for Tongshan Borough and Qitang Old Street here in Jinguashi. The deceased is a local retired miner, aged seventy to seventy-five, suffering from severe stage-three silicosis, with a severely hearing-impaired or mute little granddaughter at home."

Captain Zhang's chest jolted. He immediately whipped out his radio and barked urgent orders to the precinct.

Five minutes later, the precinct chief's astonished voice crackled over the radio: "Captain Zhang! Found him! Number 64 Qitang Road, Jinguashi—seventy-three-year-old retired miner Lin Fude! Neighbors say Uncle Ah-Fu has been missing for two days! He adopted a twelve-year-old mute orphan granddaughter, A-Mei. The little girl has been locked inside crying for two days!"

Nianzhou packed his tool case and turned toward Ah-Da, who stood frozen in place.

"Ah-Da, strip off your protective suit. Grab sterile saline and bread."

Nianzhou strode out of the mine shaft into the biting sea fog and lashing rain: "We're going to bring that little girl, who has waited two days in the dark, safely home."

The patrol car tore along the narrow, winding mountain roads of Jinguashi, its tires churning through water and moss-covered flagstones.

Number 64 Qitang Road was an old stone shack with a tar-paper roof clinging to the steep slope. The black asphalt tar-paper roofing had been battered and peeled back by coastal winds, held down by heavy green boulders, while the weathered wooden facade was streaked with dark, damp water stains.

Outside the entrance, a rusted iron chain and a brass padlock held the double wooden doors tightly shut. Along the bottom gap of the door were several tiny, frantic claw marks from fingernails, and from behind the wooden planks came the faint, injured whimpering of a wounded animal.

"Fuck! Who locks a twelve-year-old child in a place like this?!" Captain Zhang raged, drawing his telescopic baton and prying hard, snapping the corroded hasp with a loud crack.

The door was shoved wide open.

A gust of frigid air rushed out, carrying the smell of mildew, damp rainwater, and pungent fermented sweet potatoes.

No lights were on inside the cramped stone shack. Rainwater dripped steadily from leaks in the ceiling, plopping into an iron pot on the floor. Beneath a rudimentary bamboo cot against the far wall, a tiny, frail figure lay curled in a tight ball, trembling violently.

It was a twelve-year-old girl in an oversized, faded jacket. Her hair was tangled, her small face frozen into an ashen bluish-purple, her lips cracked and scabbed. Her thin, mud-stained arms were clutched tightly around a yellowed, dented plastic miner's helmet bearing the words "Taiwan Metal Mining Corp."

Beside the helmet lay a small flashlight whose batteries had long died, and an empty plastic water bottle without a single drop remaining.

When the glaring flashlight beams illuminated the room, the girl scrambled frantically toward the corner like a terrified deer, her throat emitting incoherent, frightened whimpers of "Ah... ooh...", massive tears rolling down her sunken cheeks.

"A-Mei, don't be afraid... don't be afraid. We are police officers, friends of your grandpa," Ah-Da said gently, dropping to his knees on the damp stone floor and slowly nudging forward the still-warm red bean bread and a thermos. "You're starving, aren't you? Eat something quickly..."

The moment she smelled the bread, A-Mei shuddered violently. She looked timidly into Ah-Da's round face, brimming with kindness and heartache, and finally reached out small hands marred with chilblains, grabbing the bread and shoveling it into her mouth, coughing hard from swallowing too fast.

With tears in his eyes, Ah-Da quickly poured a cup of warm water and held it to her lips, gently rubbing her frail, bony back.

Meanwhile, Chen Nianzhou swept his high-powered flashlight across the ransacked room with sharp, analytical eyes.

The wardrobe drawers had all been violently yanked out and overturned, floorboards near the cot bore pry marks from a crowbar, and the rice crock in the corner was overturned, grains scattered everywhere. Someone had conducted a brutal, thorough search before or after the murder!

Nianzhou walked to the hearth, his flashlight beam fixing upon a yellowed receipt and scattered mud prints along the stove edge.

It was a predatory loan "Property Mortgage Pledge Agreement." The borrower listed was an unfamiliar man, but the collateral was this very ancestral home on Qitang Road where the old miner had lived his entire life! At the bottom of the receipt was stamped a company seal: "Jinguashi Gold Panning Experience Center・Proprietor: Chen Jinbiao."

The muddy boot prints on the floor measured twenty-eight centimeters long, matching the work boot treads outside the mine ruins down to the millimeter!

"Old Zhang, come look," Nianzhou said, gesturing to the paper and footprints, his gaze freezing cold. "The killer wasn't looking for cash; he wanted this prime real estate bordering the Jiufen scenic trail. Chen Jinbiao tried to force Uncle Ah-Fu to surrender the deed. When Ah-Fu refused and threatened to expose his illegal encroachment on mining heritage sites, Jinbiao murdered him."

Just then, little A-Mei, having finished her bread by the bamboo cot, suddenly set down the water cup.

She reached out a tiny hand and tightly clutched the hem of Chen Nianzhou's trench coat. Her large eyes welled with tears, her mouth opening and closing soundlessly as her trembling hands gestured—clenching both fists and holding them beside her head like curving horns, then pointing out toward the black window, tears pouring down without restraint.

She was telling Nianzhou: Grandpa was taken away by a vicious man with hair like a bull's horns, and never came back.

Nianzhou slowly crouched down, extending a warm hand to gently wipe the freezing tears from the girl's cheeks.

"A-Mei, your grandpa asked me to come get you," Nianzhou said in a low, solemnly resolute voice. "Grandpa did not abandon you. Those who harmed him will answer for what they did. Chen Nianzhou will make sure justice is served."

Outside the window, the freezing sea wind howled past, and the distant silhouette of Keelung Mountain loomed like a slumbering beast.

In this night of bleak wind and bitter rain, the seven-day countdown for old miner Lin Fude entered its final four days. A forensic duel of fire camouflage, thermal bone fractures, and silent testimony was about to erupt upon the steep slopes of Jiufen.
