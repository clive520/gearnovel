# Chapter 10: The Last Breath in the Carbonized Trachea

Upon stainless steel autopsy table number one in the New Taipei City Mortuary autopsy suite, the shadowless surgical lamp radiated a stark, clinical white light that felt almost cruel.

Resting upon the table were the carbonized remains of retired miner Lin Fude, transported back from abandoned Mine Shaft No. 6 in Jinguashi. A suffocating stench of scorched flesh and burnt oil permeated the entire chamber; even with the exhaust fans churning at maximum capacity, the heavy, mournful stillness clinging to the tiles and surgical instruments refused to disperse.

Outside the heavy, airtight doors of the autopsy suite, a female officer from the Jinguashi Police Station sat on a long bench beside twelve-year-old mute girl A-Mei. The girl was dressed in a clean yet slightly oversized police jacket, her swollen, chilblain-scarred hands still clutching stubbornly to the yellowed, dented "Taiwan Metal Mining Corp" plastic helmet, her reddened eyes fixed upon the sealed autopsy doors, her tiny frame trembling from held-back fear.

Through the narrow double-paned acoustic observation window at the top of the door, intern Ah-Da glanced back at the small figure curled on the bench, a sharp ache tightening in the corners of his eyes.

"Master Nianzhou... A-Mei has been waiting out there the whole time, refusing to leave with the social workers," Ah-Da adjusted his dual-layer activated carbon mask, his voice weighed down by an unnamable sorrow. "She may not be able to speak, but she seems to understand everything in her heart... She knows that the one lying inside is the grandfather who cherished her for over a decade."

Sitting at the autopsy reporting station organizing instruments, Chen Nianzhou appeared composed, yet deep within his eyes rested an unyielding coldness.

"Among all unnatural death autopsies, post-mortem incineration is the most deceptive and tests a forensic pathologist's core acumen the most," Nianzhou said, holding a heavy bone saw and a number eleven pointed scalpel, his voice emerging muffled yet steady through his respirator. "Killers naively assume a roaring fire can burn every violent trace to ash. But they forget that the human respiratory system is the world's most faithful, and very last, black box to shut down."

In the shadows near the corner beside the autopsy table, the soul of retired miner Lin Fude floated silently.

The old man's spiritual chest heaved violently, his translucent, withered hands flecked with quartz dust pressed gently against his own throat. He spoke no words, merely gazing with sorrowful, tender eyes upon the blackened shell lying upon the table, then turning anxious glances toward his little granddaughter waiting outside the door.

Nianzhou glanced at the elderly spirit through his face shield, then withdrew his gaze as his scalpel cut with surgical precision into the midline of the corpse's cracked, charred chin.

The blade sliced through neck tissue charred as hard as charcoal, emitting a grating, skin-crawling friction sound. Nianzhou carefully performed blunt dissection along the margins of the thyroid cartilage and hyoid bone, liberating the entire visceral block of the neck along with the larynx, trachea, and bilateral carotid sheaths in one piece.

"Ah-Da, examine the tracheal mucosa closely," Nianzhou said, taking the stainless-steel scissors from Ah-Da and cutting straight down the posterior membranous wall of the trachea.

The moment the tracheal lumen was opened completely, Ah-Da sucked in a sharp, audible breath.

Although the exterior of the victim's neck had been seared to the bone by infernal heat, exposing the blackened anterior cervical ligament, the inner mucosa of that two-centimeter-wide airway was intensely hyperemic and swollen, its deeper branches lined with an exceptionally thick, sticky plug of dark grayish-black mucus.

Using a sterile swab, Nianzhou gently scraped a smear of mucus from deep within the airway, rolled it onto a glass slide, and slid it beneath a high-power polarizing microscope.

"Do you see it?" Nianzhou rotated the fine-focus knob of the microscope. "Trapped between the ciliated columnar epithelial cells of the bronchioles are massive quantities of microscopic, sharp-angled soot particles."

"This is... the fire's 'vital reaction'?" Ah-Da noted furiously on his clipboard.

"Precisely. This is one of the most lethal vital reactions recognized in a court of law," Nianzhou said, straightening his back as a glacial glint flared in his eyes. "The moment life ends, respiratory reflexes cease instantly. If the victim had been murdered before the fire was set, only negligible airborne ash would settle loosely upon the pharynx through passive physical sedimentation—it could never penetrate deep into secondary and tertiary bronchi. Only while a person is still alive, with their brain frantically commanding their lungs to draw breath, will desperate gasps force scalding, soot-laden smoke deep into bronchioles and alveoli, binding tightly with defensive mucus."

Nianzhou set down the swab and picked up the cardiac blood toxicology report just faxed over from the National Taiwan University Forensic Toxicology Laboratory.

"Carboxyhemoglobin saturation in the left ventricular blood residue reached fifty-six percent," Nianzhou pressed the sheet against Ah-Da's chest. "In healthy individuals, blood HbCO never exceeds two percent, and even heavy smokers rarely surpass five to eight percent. Exceeding fifty percent means that as the inferno consumed him, he suffered three to five agonizing minutes of violent asphyxiation in the smoke before death!"

Nianzhou then sectioned a histopathology block of the victim's lung tissue. Under crossed polarized light, the alveolar walls twinkled with dense, star-like birefringent hexagonal crystals glowing brilliant white.

"These are free silica crystals," Nianzhou murmured in profound solemnity. "He dug for gold in the sunless depths of Jinguashi for forty years. His lungs were riddled with fibrotic nodules, every inch hardened like stone. In such a compromised respiratory condition, inhaling scalding toxic smoke was a hundred times more agonizing than for an ordinary person."

Hearing this, the old miner's soul in the corner shuddered violently, as though that nightmare inferno was blazing once more within his spirit.

Amid the spiritual echoes of smoke and heat, the final memory of that night surged into Nianzhou's mind—

Deep in the pitch-black, freezing abandoned drift. The old man, skull fractured from a blunt blow, bleeding profusely, bound to the acacia timber with copper wire. A hulking brute with an oily slicked-back hairstyle resembling a bull's horns was splashing a bucket of accelerant—a blend of diesel and kerosene—across the old man's feet, chewing betel nut and sneering with sadistic malice:

"You stubborn old fart! I told you to sign over the stone house and you refused, even threatening to report my gold panning hall to the police for illegal land encroachment? Then stay down in this rotten pit and keep the dead miners company!"

The lighter was callously tossed down, and flames roared to life like an erupting dragon!

Amid the excruciating torment of flesh torn apart by fire, the old miner's lungs, already ravaged by silicosis, felt sliced by knives with every gasp. Yet he did not curse. In that final minute of suffocating smoke, the old man forced open eyes blinded by soot, gazing through burning tears toward the black mouth of the tunnel, his cracked lips squeezing out a final, faint murmur:

"A-Mei... if Grandpa dies... you must remember to eat your meals..."

The autopsy suite fell dead silent, save for Ah-Da's reddened eyes, struggling to swallow back the burning tears threatening to spill.

Just then, the heavy blast doors of the autopsy suite were shoved violently open.

Captain Zhang strode in alongside Prosecutor Lin from the New Taipei District Prosecutors Office, his expression grim: "Nianzhou, Chen Jinbiao came to the prosecutor's office with his mob lawyer! That bastard brought three lackeys, swearing up and down that he played mahjong all night with the tourism association at a teahouse on Jiufen Old Street, claiming an ironclad alibi! His lawyer even filed a formal complaint protesting the seizure of his work boots, threatening to hold a press conference accusing police of framing him!"

"Mahjong?" Nianzhou stripped off his soot-stained latex gloves, a razor-cold smile playing upon his lips. "Captain Zhang, Prosecutor Lin, show that suited beast into the interrogation room. I will personally teach him the most unforgettable forensic lesson of his life."

Twenty minutes later, in Interrogation Room Two of the New Taipei District Prosecutors Office.

Burly and arrogant, wearing a gold chain as thick as a pinky finger around his neck, Chen Jinbiao leaned back lazily in his chair picking his teeth with a toothpick, flanked by a young defense lawyer in a tailored suit.

"Prosecutor Lin, Captain Zhang, I've run my tourist gold panning hall in Jiufen for over a decade, paying over a million in local taxes every year. How have I wronged this town?" Chen Jinbiao spat out his toothpick with unmasked insolence. "That senile old fossil Lin Fude fell into debt and crawled into a mine to burn himself to death—what the hell does that have to do with me?! On what authority do you shut down my business and interrogate me like a murderer?!"

The young attorney cleared his throat and adjusted his gold-rimmed glasses: "Prosecutor, my client was playing tiles at the Mountain City Teahouse in Jiufen from eight in the evening until two in the morning. Six witnesses can testify under oath. For the police to seize my client's property without cause is a gross procedural violation!"

Just as Prosecutor Lin was about to slam the table in outrage, Chen Nianzhou pulled out an iron chair and sat down directly across from Chen Jinbiao, expressionless.

Nianzhou laid an evidence bag and a Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis report heavily upon the center of the table.

"Chen Jinbiao, you thought you were clever. You knew that in the damp humidity of a mine shaft, ordinary gasoline evaporates too quickly and fails to ignite timber, so you specifically mixed your own accelerant," Nianzhou laced his fingers together, his voice as matter-of-fact as reading a weather report. "You blended thirty percent high-viscosity industrial excavator hydraulic oil into ordinary kerosene. Hydraulic oil has a high flashpoint and tremendous viscosity; burning against synthetic clothing, it generates localized heat exceeding eight hundred degrees Celsius, ensuring the victim would be carbonized in the shortest time possible."

The sneer on Chen Jinbiao's face froze slightly, but his lawyer quickly jumped in: "Forensic Pathologist Chen, hydraulic oil is ubiquitous on construction sites! There are tens of thousands of excavators across Taiwan—that proves nothing regarding my client!"

"Hydraulic oil is indeed common, but this batch was very special," Nianzhou flipped to the second page of the report. "Because your 'Gold Panning Experience Center' has been illegally washing auriferous quartz sands in the upper Keelung River for years, your hydraulic oil reservoir sat beside the settling ponds, absorbing ultra-rare micro-flakes of native gold and accompanying pyrite crystal fragments."

Nianzhou looked up, his chilled gaze piercing like twin scalpels straight into Chen Jinbiao's chest: "From the soot mucus plugs deep inside Lin Fude's trachea, our Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS) tests detected identical isotopic ratios of native gold and pyrite trace metals! Out of thousands of excavators in the world, only the gold panning excavator in your shop could leave unique microscopic gold dust inside the dead man's lungs!"

Chen Jinbiao's face drained of all color, the thick rolls of flesh on his forehead twitching uncontrollably.

"And then there is this." Nianzhou produced the evidence bag containing the scorched copper wire, placing it before Chen Jinbiao's eyes. "The dual-strand copper wire binding Lin Fude's right wrist was secured with a 'Figure-eight loop with double hitch.' That is no electrician's knot—it is an antique miner's knot once used in Jinguashi to anchor heavy winch cables to ore carts. In all of Jiufen, aside from old-timers sleeping in the cemetery, only you—who worked three years as a winch operator under your father in the tunnels—still know how to tie it."

Nianzhou leaned forward, his icy breath bearing down upon Chen Jinbiao's deathly pale face:

"Even more unfortunate for you: when you hauled hard on that copper knot amidst the storm, your right thumb pressed too violently, leaving an abraded skin flake with sweat gland sebum against the plastic insulation. The DNA match came back half an hour ago."

Nianzhou tapped the table lightly: "All sixteen STR loci match completely. Chen Jinbiao, do you have any other alibis you'd care to share with the judge?"

Chen Jinbiao collapsed into his metal chair as though his bones had dissolved, his mouth agape, cold sweat pouring down his forehead like rain, dry, desperate gasps rattling in his throat, while his attorney prudently fell silent and shrank back several inches.

"I... I didn't mean to kill him!" Chen Jinbiao suddenly broke down in loud, blubbering sobs, snot and tears smearing his face. "He refused to cooperate! I offered him two hundred thousand dollars to buy his worthless stone shack, and he pulled out the old miners' union fund ledger, threatening to report me for illegally occupying state mining land! If he reported me, my gold panning hall would be shut down! I lost my temper... I only wanted to scare him!"

"Scare him?" Prosecutor Lin stood abruptly, fury blazing in his eyes. "Cracking a seventy-three-year-old man's skull, tying him to a timber, dousing him in fuel and burning him alive—you call that scaring him?! Chen Jinbiao, you are charged with murder under Article 271 of the Criminal Code with extreme brutality. I will seek the death penalty in court!"

When the handcuffs clicked shut once more, Ah-Da, standing outside the door, clenched his fists tightly, finally releasing the suffocating anger that had weighed on his chest for two full days.

Outside the interrogation room, Captain Zhang walked slowly forward leading little A-Mei.

Watching the burly, handcuffed Chen Jinbiao being escorted out in disgrace by two tall detectives, tears welled in the little girl's large eyes. Clutching her miner's helmet tightly, she trembled, yet did not take a single step backward.

As Chen Jinbiao passed her, A-Mei suddenly looked up, her pure, sorrowful gaze fixing straight upon the man who had murdered her grandfather, tears silently streaming down her face.

Chen Jinbiao did not dare meet the girl's eyes; he dropped his head in shame and terror, led away into the elevator like a defeated dog.

Ah-Da stepped forward, crouching down to gently pat A-Mei's hair: "A-Mei, the bad man has been caught. Master Nianzhou brought justice for Grandpa."

A-Mei sniffled, reaching out a tiny hand to gently tug at Chen Nianzhou's coat, then rested her small face against Nianzhou's wool coat, letting out a faint, tender whimper like a vulnerable young animal.

Nianzhou looked down, gently patting her small shoulder, a flicker of warmth warming his eyes.

Yet his gaze soon turned toward Keelung Mountain, still shrouded in heavy storm clouds outside the window—

Though the old man's murder was resolved, where was that "old miners' union fund ledger" Chen Jinbiao had blurted out? What was the true secret that old miner Lin Fude had guarded for thirty long years?

The countdown bell of the seventh day still echoed in the wind and rain. The final piece of the truth was about to emerge amidst the abandoned stone shacks and gold bars of Jinguashi.
