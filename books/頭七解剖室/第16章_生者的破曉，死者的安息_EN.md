# Chapter 16: Dawn of the Living, Rest of the Dead

The tolling of the midnight bell at the New Taipei City Mortuary resonated softly through the quiet night sky.

The winter gale gradually ceased, and the thick fog rolling outside the inquest corridor settled like a heavy shroud of white silk, draping silently over the hushed building. The heavy doors of the autopsy suite stood open. On stainless steel table number one, Jiang Jingyuan's body lay clad in the black ceremonial robe of a Lifetime Distinguished Professor of NTU Institute of Forensic Medicine, his face serene in slumber, the First-Class Forensic Medal on his chest gleaming with quiet silver radiance in the dim light.

On the bench outside the corridor, Captain Zhang and Prosecutor Lin stood shoulder to shoulder. Holding an urgent directive freshly delivered from the Ministry of Justice, Prosecutor Lin's voice carried a tired yet steadfast relief in the night air:

"Nianzhou, the Taipei District Prosecutors Office and the Ministry of Health and Welfare have jointly launched a formal criminal investigation into the thirty-seven fatal cover-ups in the illicit trials conducted by Swiss Novartis fifteen years ago. Song Mingze's secret Swiss bank accounts were frozen by the Swiss Federal Department of Justice, and all implicated hospital executives and pharmaceutical representatives have been barred from leaving the country and placed under pretrial detention tonight. The truth Professor Jiang defended with his life has been fully vindicated in the mortal world."

Captain Zhang wiped his face and exhaled a long sigh: "Breaking news alerts just went live across every major network. The entire medical and legal establishment in Taiwan is reeling. By giving his own life, the old professor ripped away fifteen years of deception, saving tens of thousands of future patients from harm. Such boundless karmic merit..."

Standing before the autopsy table, Chen Nianzhou nodded gently. He said nothing, merely looking up at the antique wall clock in the suite.

Eleven fifty in the evening.

Only ten minutes remained until the midnight stroke of the seventh day.

Just then, the air at the entrance of the autopsy suite warped violently. All ambient sound died away instantly, as though the heavens and earth had been muted.

Carried upon a faint, ethereal draft of chilled sandalwood fragrance, two tall, imposing figures stepped silently forward from the depths of the dense fog.

Tonight, Ox-Head and Horse-Face wore the ceremonial regalia of the underworld's highest order, unseen by mortal eyes. Ox-Head was clad in dark iron armor trimmed with gold filigree, his towering horns draped in solemn crimson silk ribbons; instead of cold chains, his hands reverently bore an antique, pure jade lantern radiating gentle azure light. Beside him, Horse-Face had tucked away his digital tablet, standing straight and tall like a stone sentinel guarding the celestial gates of the underworld.

"General Ox, General Horse," Nianzhou stepped forward, bowing in respect.

Ox-Head set the pure jade lantern gently upon the edge of the duty station, then raised eyes weathered by centuries, looking at Jiang Jingyuan's soul standing beside the autopsy table.

Ox-Head drew a deep breath, his sonorous voice booming softly in the quiet room:

"Jiang Jingyuan, founding titan of the Ministry of Justice's Institute of Forensic Medicine and Lifetime Distinguished Professor of Forensic Pathology at National Taiwan University. In your forty earthly years, you autopsied over ten thousand souls, untying endless knots of injustice, your merits filling the archives of the Ten Halls of the Underworld. Tonight at midnight, the Ten Kings of Hell have jointly decreed your honorable welcome, exempting you from all underworld trial. This Officer holds the pure jade lantern to escort you directly to the realm of the blessed."

Beside the table, Jiang Jingyuan's soul smiled faintly, clasping his hands in respectful gratitude: "I thank both Generals for their service."

Ox-Head turned to Chen Nianzhou, his iron-hard features softening with rare, paternal warmth:

"Forensic Pathologist Chen, the seven-day inquest term is complete. You cleared the elder's name and preserved human conscience. This old Ox has patrolled the living world for centuries and witnessed countless wretches betray and murder their teachers for coin, yet in you two, I have finally witnessed the unbending spine of humanity."

Just then, a soft, radiant golden halo blossomed across the inquest corridor outside the autopsy suite.

The cold, dim concrete hallway seemed draped in warm moonlight. Standing nearby, intern Ah-Da rubbed his eyes vigorously, taking an instinctive step backward, his mouth falling open in awe.

Down the length of the misty corridor, countless familiar ethereal figures had gathered silently along both walls—

At the front of the corridor stood mother Lin Xiuqin in her faded cleaner's uniform, holding the hand of her mailman son Zhou Zhiming in his secondhand suit and mail pouch; tears welled in their eyes, the faint, comforting aroma of scallion minced pork rice drifting once more in memory as they bowed deeply to Jiang Jingyuan from afar;

Beside them stood pianist Bai Zhiqing in her immaculate white swan evening gown, hands folded gracefully across her waist, the haunting melody of the unfinished *Mountain Cherry Lullaby* vibrating through the ethereal realm as she offered her most elegant curtsy to the mentor who had shaped Chen Nianzhou;

Further back, the forty-two old miners from the abandoned Jinguashi pit, led by Lin Fude and Chen Maosen, removed their hard hats and pressed them to their chests, smiling warm, honest smiles as their carbide lamps twinkled like stars in the distance.

They were all the departed souls whose unavenged deaths Chen Nianzhou had cleared and whose final wishes he had fulfilled in this seventh-day morgue.

On the threshold of reincarnation, bridging the divide between life and death, they had returned to the doors of this cold autopsy suite simply to escort this old pathologist—who had spent his entire life keeping the flames of justice alive—on his final journey.

For every one of them knew in their hearts: if Jiang Jingyuan had not rescued that orphan in the pouring rain twenty years ago, and if he had not placed that scalpel into Nianzhou's hands, this world would never have had the doctor who wiped away their tears in the dark.

Gazing at the smiling, bowing spirits lining the corridor, a brilliant light shone in Jiang Jingyuan's eyes. The old mentor inhaled deeply and turned back to look upon Chen Nianzhou and Ah-Da standing before him.

"Nianzhou, Ah-Da."

His voice was tranquil as still water, yet every syllable carried the weight of mountains:

"The path of a forensic pathologist is solitary and long. The night of the mortal world is dark, and greed and evil will never fully vanish. But you must remember—as long as there is light upon the scalpel, dawn will always come."

Jiang Jingyuan extended a translucent hand, gently patting Ah-Da's shoulder: "Young man, learn well from your Master Nianzhou. This bowl of rice is bitter to earn, but you will eat it with a clean conscience."

Tears streaming down his face, Ah-Da wiped them away and snapped to attention: "Professor Jiang, Ah-Da will remember!"

Then, the old professor turned to Chen Nianzhou, clasping his hand tightly.

"Go forward, child. It is time for your teacher to depart."

The final second of the midnight hour ticked away.

Ox-Head lifted the pure jade lantern, and Horse-Face stepped forward in solemn escort.

Straightening his white lab coat, Jiang Jingyuan strode peacefully out through the doors of the autopsy suite.

Guided by the gentle, warm radiance of the jade lantern, the old man walked between the rows of departed souls, amidst rising sandalwood fragrance and distant temple bells, slowly transforming into brilliant, pure golden light that ascended into the boundless night sky.

The spirits along the corridor dissolved into shimmering specks of light, drifting peacefully upon the wind toward the far shores of reincarnation.

The corridor returned to silence, with only the faint, crisp scent of pine lingering in the air.

After some time, across the eastern horizon over the sea, a seam cracked open in the leaden clouds of night.

A breathtaking burst of morning light spilled down from the heavens, piercing the haze over the mountain city of New Taipei, bathing the floor-to-ceiling windows of the autopsy suite in ten thousand beams of golden warmth.

The sunlight illuminated stainless steel table number one, the gleaming, tidy instruments beside it, and the serene, resolute peace upon Chen Nianzhou's face.

Wiping away his tears, Ah-Da stood straight beside the duty station clutching the clipboard, every trace of timidity gone from his eyes, replaced by the steadfast composure of a true forensic doctor.

Chen Nianzhou walked to the duty desk, opened the drawer, and took out the fresh notebook entrusted to him by his mentor: *The Notebook of the Silenced・Volume Two*.

Opening the pristine first page, he drew his black fountain pen and, beneath the first rays of morning sun, inscribed the very first entry with neat, deliberate reverence:

"No. 001. Name: Jiang Jingyuan. Age at Death: Sixty-eight.

Case: Homicide Committed to Conceal Multinational Pharmaceutical Trial Malfeasance.

Cause of Death: Acute Cardiac Conduction System Blockade via Transesophageal Micro-Puncture.

Final Wish: Guard the Conscience of Taiwanese Forensic Medicine; Pass on the Sacred Oath of the Scalpel.

Pathologist: Chen Nianzhou.

Status: Fully Completed; Master Rests in Peace."

Writing the final word, Nianzhou closed the notebook and released a long, deep breath.

Just then, the red telephone on the duty desk erupted with a sharp, urgent ring—

*Ring—!*

Ah-Da swiftly picked up the receiver, listened for a few moments, and turned toward Chen Nianzhou with solemn focus:

"Master Nianzhou, Xinzhuang Precinct just called in. An unidentified body was found in the reed marshes along the banks of Dahan Creek, suspected homicide. The prosecutor requests our immediate dispatch."

In the morning light, Chen Nianzhou turned around.

His expression was calm, his eyes as deep as an ocean reflecting the dawn. Reaching into the instrument case, he drew a brand-new pair of sterile latex gloves, snapped them over his hands, and gripped the number eleven scalpel that had accompanied him for twenty years.

"Ah-Da, grab the forensic field kit."

Nianzhou strode toward the doors of the autopsy suite, stepping out into the radiant morning sun, his voice as steady as mountains:

"Let's move."

The dawn of the living has arrived, and the journey to bring peace to the dead knows no end.
