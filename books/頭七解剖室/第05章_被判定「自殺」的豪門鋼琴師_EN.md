# Chapter 5: The Wealthy Pianist Deemed a "Suicide"

Tuesday, 3:15 PM. Qingshan Villa Estate, Xindian District, New Taipei City.

This was an ultra-exclusive, heavily guarded luxury residential enclave nestled against rolling hills and winding waters. Autumn sunlight filtered through the dense bald cypress groves, casting dappled leaf shadows across the broad, pristine asphalt road.

However, outside a modern minimalist three-and-a-half-story white villa, blinding yellow police cordon tape fluttered violently in the wind.

Down upon the granite landscaped courtyard below the villa, a dark, shocking pool of congealed blood had already dried, its edges soaking several trampled, ruined white rose petals.

Forensic Pathologist Chen Nian-Zhou gripped his heavy stainless-steel field investigation kit, ascending the stairs toward the rooftop terrace on the top floor.

This terrace had been converted into a panoramic glass piano conservatory boasting spectacular vistas. At the very center of the conservatory rested a Steinway concert grand piano worth millions, its ivory and ebony keys radiating a cool, aristocratic sheen in the sunlight.

The sliding glass doors of the music room were flung wide open, fronted by a tempered-glass balustrade approximately 1.2 meters high. Currently, the district prosecutor and Detective Investigator Xu Shu-Qing were inspecting the scene beside the railing.

"The preliminary scene findings are very straightforward,"

The prosecutor adjusted his rimless glasses, looking down at his notebook entries:

"The victim is Shen Yu-Xuan, twenty-six years old, a renowned young concert pianist. Around 11:00 AM this morning, she was discovered fallen in the ground-floor courtyard by a part-time cleaning lady arriving for her shift. On the tea table inside the conservatory lay three prescription bottles of Prozac, a half-empty bottle of red wine, and copious shredded sheet music scattered across the floor. In the wastebasket, investigators recovered a handwritten note reading: 'I can no longer play the sound you desire. These hands are so exhausted.'"

The prosecutor turned to gaze toward a young man standing just outside the perimeter, impeccably tailored in an expensive black bespoke suit with reddened eyes:

"Mr. Zhao, when was the last time you saw Miss Shen alive?"

The young man drew out a snow-white linen handkerchief, dabbing at non-existent tears at the corner of his eyes, his voice catching with practiced, pitch-perfect grief:

"Last night... around ten. She was a perfectionist artist who demanded the impossible from herself. Her solo recital at the National Concert Hall was scheduled for next month, and she spent over twelve hours a day practicing for the past half-year, frequently battling sleepless anxiety... Last night, her emotions spiraled into a furious row with me. To give her space to calm down, I returned to my downtown flat... I never imagined... I never dreamed she would take her own life like this..."

Clutching his handkerchief tight, his shoulders trembled faintly, painting the portrait of a heartbroken fiancé shattered by the loss of his beloved:

"Prosecutor, Yu-Xuan cherished elegance and dignity above all else in life... If the inquest confirms this was suicide by fall, please expedite the release of her remains. I wish to lay her to rest with utmost decorum, sparing her the indignity of an autopsy..."

"Rest assured, Mr. Zhao. We will proceed strictly by legal protocol," the prosecutor nodded.

Standing to one side with her case file, Xu Shu-Qing's brow was knit tight. Her instincts screamed that something was subtly discordant about this scene, yet between the latched interior doors, the half-drained wine glass, and the suicide note matching the victim's handwriting, every surface indicator pointed toward a textbook suicide driven by depression.

At that moment, Nian-Zhou approached, gloved in white.

He knelt by the glass balustrade, inspecting the subtle smudges along the railing rim, before glancing down at the shredded fragments of sheet music littering the floor.

Reaching down, Nian-Zhou picked up a single torn stave of manuscript.

It was not a commercial score, but an original composition painstakingly drafted in pencil. The musical notes were penned with delicate, elegant precision, and across the header, four faint pencil words were barely discernible through dried water stains: [Dedicated to Mother].

Lifting his eyes, Nian-Zhou cast an impassive glance at the grieving fiancé:

"Mr. Zhao, were the victim's hands insured for a substantial policy?"

The man froze for a fraction of a second, an ephemeral flash of wariness darting across his eyes before dissolving into a heavy sigh:

"Yu-Xuan was a concert pianist; the philharmonic orchestra indeed took out a fifty-million-dollar accidental disability policy on her hands. Doctor Chen, why do you ask?"

"Nothing. Just an occupational hazard," Nian-Zhou stood up, brushed his knees, and turned to Xu Shu-Qing and the prosecutor:

"Fall height twelve meters, blunt impact to the thorax and abdomen causing multiple rib fractures and internal organ lacerations. However, prior to an internal pathological autopsy, I will not check the 'Suicide' box on the death certificate. Transport the body to Autopsy Suite 4 at the Municipal Mortuary first."

One hour later. Basement level 2, New Taipei City Funeral Parlor.

Upon the cold stainless-steel dissection slab, the heavy black body bag was slowly unzipped.

The deceased, Shen Yu-Xuan, lay motionless upon the frigid metal.

Her long dark hair cascaded over her shoulders, her features delicate and refined, though her left frontal bone was depressed, and her pristine ivory silk dress was largely drenched in dark crimson stains.

Yet what commanded the eye above all were her hands.

They were a pair of slender, elongated, exquisitely articulated hands. Ten fingers graceful as twin white doves, her nails trimmed round, neat, and unpainted. Even resting lifeless upon cold steel, they resembled a masterpiece sculpted by the divine.

"Tsk, tsk, tsk, hands like that are truly blessed by the heavens,"

Standing three meters back, intern assistant Ah-Da stuffed fresh mint leaves into his surgical mask while muttering under his breath:

"That wealthy fiancé who brought the body in cried like the world was ending! Drives a Porsche, head-to-toe designer clothes, supposedly the sole heir to a mega-financial holding group... Sigh, a pianist this gorgeous and talented, on the brink of marrying into high society with a glittering future—what could possibly drive her to jump off a roof?"

"Ah-Da," Nian-Zhou pulled on his double layer of latex gloves, speaking coldly, "in an autopsy suite, all 'incomprehensible despair' is usually just a script concocted by the living to conceal the truth."

Approaching the table, Nian-Zhou bowed slightly toward the dead girl in customary respect:

"Forgive the intrusion, Miss Shen."

Then, Nian-Zhou extended his gloved right hand, gently taking the dead girl's cool, supple right wrist, pressing his index finger to the radial artery while rotating the palm to check the wrist joint and volar surface for defensive wounds.

Through the thin latex, his fingertips brushed the skin of her wrist—

“HUMMMMM————!”

The resonant toll of an ancient bronze bell detonated with shocking force right at the core of Nian-Zhou's skull for the third time!

Beneath his collarbone, the searing hourglass flared as if doused in molten magma—the third 168-hour countdown seamlessly ignited!

In the very next instant, a piercing, hysterical young woman's screech exploded right beside Nian-Zhou's ear:

"Aaaaaaaah——! Let go of me! Don't touch my hands!"

A translucent spirit of a young woman in an ivory silk gown sprang from the autopsy slab like a terrified fawn, hovering mid-air and clutching her hands frantically to her bosom!

Terror etched across her face as she beheld the gleaming scalpel in Nian-Zhou's grasp, her long hair whipping wildly in the phantom gale as her shrill cry resonated with piercing force:

"Doctor! What are you doing with that blade?! My hands are insured for fifty million! I am supposed to perform a solo recital at the National Concert Hall! You cannot sever my median nerve and tendons! You cannot cut them!"

Nian-Zhou's hand halted mid-air.

Rubbing his buzzing ear, he drew a deep, long-suffering breath and picked up the vernier caliper with unruffled calm:

"Miss Shen Yu-Xuan, first of all: you plummeted from a height of twelve meters, suffering a ruptured thoracic aorta and a comminuted fracture of the C2 vertebra. You are now thoroughly expired; the insurance company will only pay out accidental death benefits, not hand disability compensation. Second: all blood circulation to your nerves and tendons has ceased; even if I julienned them into noodles with my scalpel, you wouldn't feel an ounce of pain."

The girl hovering in the air froze solid.

She lowered her gaze to her flawless, transparent fingers, then looked blankly at her blood-soaked shell lying upon the cold metal slab.

"I... am I really..."

Her spectral eyes turned crimson in an instant, her translucent lips trembling violently.

Yet to Nian-Zhou's surprise, what surfaced upon her face was not the customary disorientation of the newly dead, but an incandescent, bone-deep fury and despair!

She threw herself directly before Nian-Zhou, her translucent hands clawing at empty space, crying out with hysterical anguish:

"Doctor! Listen to me! I did not commit suicide! I never jumped!"

"It was him... it was that monster Zhao Yu-Heng who shoved me off!"

Recording data nearby, Ah-Da watched Nian-Zhou staring blankly into empty air and shivered uncontrollably: "B-Brother Nian-Zhou? Why did you freeze up again? Is Miss Shen's ghost protesting against you..."

"Silence, Huang Zhi-Da. Document the integrity of the carpals," Nian-Zhou snapped without turning his head, his cold, razor-sharp gaze locked firmly upon Shen Yu-Xuan in mid-air.

"Zhao Yu-Heng pushed you?" Nian-Zhou murmured through gritted teeth.

"Yes! It was him!"

Shen Yu-Xuan wept, her entire form trembling violently as translucent tears dissolved into the cold air:

"He was never some devoted, gentle heir! He was cheating on me behind my back, and he lost tens of millions in underground futures trading! He set his sights on the ancestral house in Xindian left by my parents, and the overseas trust fund under my name!"

Clutching her phantom chest, her voice fractured with heart-wrenching agony:

"Last night, he cornered me in the conservatory, demanding that I sign a power of attorney transferring the trust. I refused, telling him I was breaking off our engagement... He flew into a psychotic rage, grabbed me by the throat, and dragged me toward the balcony!"

"What about the shredded sheet music on the floor?" Nian-Zhou pressed with clinical composure.

At the mention of the sheet music, Shen Yu-Xuan's soul seemed torn asunder, unleashing an agonizing wail of despair:

"That was... that was the only treasure my mother left me in this world..."

Kneeling in mid-air, Shen Yu-Xuan clutched her hair with trembling hands:

"My mother was a cleaning lady... When I was little, our family was desperately poor, living in a leaky shanty in Badu, Keelung. To pay for my piano lessons, my mother washed stairwells in commercial high-rises at four in the freezing winter mornings. Her hands were cracked with raw bloody sores, her knuckles swollen and blackened... She scraped and saved for ten relentless years just to buy me a second-hand upright piano..."

The sorrow in her eyes was vast as an ocean:

"My mother died of lung cancer three years ago. Before she passed, she hummed the lullaby she sang every night in my childhood from her hospital bed... I spent two whole years arranging that melody into a piano concerto called 'The Mountain Cherry Lullaby,' intending it as the grand finale of my recital at the National Concert Hall next month, playing it for my mother in heaven..."

Choking on her tears, her translucent form nearly dissolved under the weight of grief:

"Yet last night... that bastard Zhao Yu-Heng tore those manuscript sheets to shreds right in front of my eyes to break my resolve! He stamped on my mother's legacy, sneering that it was peasant trash! When I lunged forward to reclaim them... he shoved me violently over the balcony railing!"

Shen Yu-Xuan jerked her head up, those pure eyes that had once commanded world masterpieces now drowning in tears and desperate supplication:

"Doctor Chen... I cannot leave this world branded with the shame of 'suicide'... "

"My mother spent her entire life suffering for my sake; her greatest pride was seeing me live with head held high, courageous and strong... If she knew I died a 'suicide,' how shattered, how tortured by guilt would she be in heaven?!"

"I beg of you... you must help me find proof... prove to the entire world... that I never took my own life! I was murdered!"

Inside the silent autopsy suite, only the pungent stench of formalin drifted through the air.

Nian-Zhou gazed quietly at this sobbing young prodigy.

He knew all too well that the mortal world was merciless. In a court of law, a judge would never hear the testimony of the dead, nor would a prosecutor entertain the claims of a psychic coroner.

To overturn the ruling of suicide, there had to be irrefutable physical evidence.

"Before the fall, you had a violent physical struggle with him, didn't you?" Nian-Zhou asked with absolute calm.

Shen Yu-Xuan nodded through tears: "I... when I lunged to save the music, he grabbed my hair, and I fought back with everything I had..."

Nian-Zhou lowered his gaze, directing the concentrated beam of the shadowless surgical lamp directly onto the delicate hands hailed as "the fifty-million-dollar masterpiece."

Wearing a magnifying loupe, he took up a precision micro-dissection forceps, examining the margins of the deceased's fingernails inch by meticulous inch.

Deep beneath the nail folds of her right middle and ring fingers—

Under the intense illumination, Nian-Zhou's sharp eyes caught a minute, nearly imperceptible trace of dark grey wool fiber, alongside microscopic fragments of epithelial tissue lodged firmly under the subungual keratin layer!

"Found it."

A cold, resolute smirk curved the corner of Nian-Zhou's lips.

"The bespoke luxury suit Mr. Zhao wore today happens to be pure wool in dark grey."

Setting down the forceps, Nian-Zhou met the gaze of the spectral girl hovering above, her weeping stilled, her eyes overflowing with sudden hope. His voice rang out like an unsheathed blade:

"Miss Shen, your ten fingers were meant not only to play Chopin and Liszt."

"In the seven days after your death, they are also the lethal weapon that will personally drag your murderer behind prison bars."
