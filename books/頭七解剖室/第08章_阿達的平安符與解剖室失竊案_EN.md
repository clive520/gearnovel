# Chapter 08: Ah-Da's Peace Talisman and the Morgue Heist

In the basement autopsy suite of the New Taipei City Mortuary, the dead of night was always so quiet that only the monotonous droning of the exhaust fans remained.

The clock hands on the wall pointed to one thirty in the morning. Chen Jingda—known to his colleagues as "Ah-Da"—who had just completed two months as a forensic intern, was curled up on an iron folding chair beside the duty desk, holding a lukewarm bowl of Uni-President minced pork instant noodles in one hand while clutching desperately with the other at a Dajia Jenn Lann Temple Mazu peace talisman strung with red cord against his chest.

"Master Nianzhou," Ah-Da murmured in a hushed whisper, his Adam's apple bobbing nervously as his round, boyish face brimmed with awe, "that elderly grandmother who was just wheeled into Cold Locker Number Seven... was she really just an unidentified vagrant who froze to death in the demolition ruins on Datong South Road in Sanchong? Just now, when I was cataloging her personal effects, my neck felt icy cold, as if someone was standing right behind me exhaling chilled breath..."

Sitting at the autopsy reporting station, Chen Nianzhou did not even lift his head. His fountain pen drew a sharp red underline across the preliminary inquest file: "Deaths from severe hypothermia are frequently accompanied by paradoxical undressing, but when this elderly woman was found, she was bundled in three layers of ragged yet neatly buttoned heavy padded coats. More crucially, there is a three-centimeter laceration-contusion on the occipital bone at the back of her skull, surrounded by trace intravital hemorrhage."

"Meaning... she didn't just collapse on the street and freeze on her own?" The plastic fork in Ah-Da's hand froze mid-air.

"She was pushed and struck a hard object, losing mobility, before being abandoned alive amidst the rubble on a damp, freezing eight-degree rainy night to die of hypothermia." Nianzhou set down his pen, rubbed his bloodshot eyes, and gently tapped his fingertip against the yellowed forensic photo. "Furthermore, the red wedding-cookie tin box tucked inside the inner lining of her coat bears marks of having been forcefully pried open. This is no ordinary accidental collapse; this is an intentional abandonment resulting in death disguised as a homeless freeze."

Before the words had fully landed, the red embroidered talisman worn against Ah-Da's skin suddenly turned scalding hot without the slightest warning.

It was not the gentle warmth of warm water, but the searing sting of glowing charcoal branded directly onto his flesh. Ah-Da cried out in pain and yanked the talisman out from beneath his shirt, only to see a faint wisp of pale green smoke rising from the edge of the silk pouch, the golden embroidery swiftly consumed by a circle of blackened incense-ash char.

Simultaneously, at the far end of the hallway, the heavy stainless-steel latch of Cold Locker Number Seven emitted a distinct, crisp snapping click.

The main fluorescent tubes of the autopsy room let out a shrill buzzing whine of electric current, and in the next instant, the entire row of lights abruptly went out, leaving only the greenish emergency exit fixtures at the corners of the ceiling, casting the subterranean chamber in an eerie, pale spectral glow.

In the darkness, heavy, shuffling footsteps dragged forward step by step from the depths of the cold storage, accompanied by the rustling scrape of plastic slippers against the concrete floor and the faint, raspy weeping of an elderly woman.

"G-G-Ghost! Master Nianzhou! Dajia Mazu is manifesting! The talisman caught fire!" Terrified, Ah-Da bounded out of his chair and slammed headfirst into the metal cabinet behind Nianzhou, noodle broth sloshing all over his clothes as he trembled like a leaf in a gale.

Nianzhou did not budge, his brow not even twitching. He fished a Long Life cigarette from his pocket, placed it between his lips, and flicked his lighter with his left thumb, a small orange flame flickering in the dim green room.

Illuminated by the lighter's faint glow, a frail, stooped phantom had materialized unnoticed by the iron doors of the autopsy room.

It was an elderly woman with wild, withered white hair like dry grass, her back severely hunched, dressed in a faded, pilled floral padded jacket and wearing blue-and-white plastic flip-flops with soles worn completely through. Her soul was translucent and feeble. Her withered, wrinkled hands, dotted with age spots and cracked fissures, clutched frantically at the empty air, her lips murmuring in endless distress: "My tin box... Ah-Di's photo... Don't take it away... That's Ah-Di's only photograph..."

Nianzhou exhaled a stream of blueish-gray smoke, his gaze piercing through the apparition, and spoke in a low voice: "Elder, within seven days, in any place where Chen Nianzhou stands, no one can steal what belongs to you."

The elderly woman's spirit shuddered violently. Her clouded, hollow spectral eyes turned toward Nianzhou, and then, trembling, she fell to her knees with a thud, letting out a silent, heartbreaking sob.

"Ah-Da, turn on the backup flashlight," Nianzhou instructed calmly, though a razor-sharp glint flashed across his eyes. "Someone has broken into the autopsy suite."

Trembling, Ah-Da produced the high-intensity flashlight. Its beam of white light slashed through the darkness, illuminating the temporary evidence locker behind the duty station.

Locker Number Three, originally secured with a padlock, had been severed clean through with hydraulic bolt cutters. The metal door hung wide open; the rusted cookie tin and the tattered blue canvas backpack retrieved from the elderly woman's inner coat were gone!

On the concrete dust of the floor were several prominent, fresh muddy footprints. The soles displayed a distinct chevron herringbone tread pattern, leading straight toward the gurney ramp escape door at the rear of the autopsy room.

"Fuck! Someone dares to steal personal effects from the dead inside a morgue?!" Ah-Da's terror was instantly replaced by a surge of fiery indignation, his fists clenching tight. "That granny didn't have ten dollars on her—just a few scraps of paper and a broken tin box! Is this bastard insane from poverty or utterly devoid of conscience?!"

"That was no ordinary thief." Nianzhou pulled on latex gloves, crouched down, and shone the beam over the footprints and scuff marks on the floor. "The footprint depth is heavier on the left than the right, stride length approximately seventy-five centimeters, body weight around eighty-five kilograms. Adhering to the edges of the tread are ultra-fine yellow-brown clay and concentrated asphalt binder particles—sediment unique to the foundation mud of the unauthorized building demolition site beneath Sanchong's Chongxin Bridge."

Nianzhou dabbed a sterile swab against trace powder on the door handle and brought it to his nose: "Also industrial-grade diesel and abraded metallic dust from high-hardness stone crushers. The intruder was the site foreman or excavator operator from that demolition site."

"Why would he steal that battered tin box?" Ah-Da asked, bewildered.

"Because what was inside that tin box was not cash, but the original land deed and household registration transcript proving the elderly woman's identity and her lawful inheritance rights to that ancestral property." Nianzhou stood up, his voice sharp as a blade. "To forcibly demolish old structures and claim lucrative floor area bonuses, the developer has spent the past two years driving out unyielding residents. The elderly woman had no surviving family and suffered from dementia. If she died as an 'unidentified vagrant,' her ancestral home would be bulldozed as ownerless debris, and tens of millions in urban renewal profits would flow straight into the developer's pockets."

Nianzhou turned toward the kneeling spirit of the elderly woman, his gaze softening with profound compassion: "What that foreman didn't expect was that although she was knocked unconscious, she gritted her teeth, crawled dozens of meters, and hid the tin box deep within her padded coat until patrolling officers found her and brought her here."

Hearing this, the elderly woman's spirit trembled violently, her withered lips quivering as she transmitted a wave of memory directly into Nianzhou's mind through spiritual resonance.

Twenty-five years ago, outside the Beigang Chaotian Temple in Yunlin.

It was a bustling Lantern Festival evening thick with temple incense. Her three-year-old grandson, "Ah-Di," was quietly snatched away by an unfamiliar hand amidst the festive crowd, vanishing from the world without a trace. The grandmother searched frantically through every corner of Taiwan. Her husband passed away early from grief and exhaustion, so she sold the family property, slung a worn canvas bag over her shoulder, and supported herself by collecting scrap cardboard and doing odd jobs on construction sites, eventually drifting all the way to Sanchong in New Taipei, living in a ramshackle tin shack in the redevelopment zone for twenty years.

Every morning, the first thing she did was tape missing-person flyers onto utility poles; every evening before sleep, she would open that cookie tin to touch the only remaining one-month baby photo of her grandson and the two matching mother-and-child peace talismans she had prayed for at Chaotian Temple all those years ago.

"Ah-Di... Granny lost you..." The elderly woman's wail sounded as if echoing from the bottom of a deep well, desolate enough to bring tears to anyone's eyes. "Granny can't leave... If Granny leaves, and Ah-Di finds his way home... he won't be able to find Granny..."

Standing to the side, Ah-Da's eyes welled with tears.

He looked down at the half-charred talisman clutched in his hand, veins bulging along the back of his fist.

"Master Nianzhou," Ah-Da's voice was choked yet resolute, "this case—I am seeing it through to the end. My hometown is in Beigang... When I was a kid playing in the plaza of Chaotian Temple, there was always a hunched granny sitting beneath the banyan tree by the temple gate. No matter how hard it rained or how bitter the cold wave was, she would hand cups of hot ginger tea to passing kids, showing everyone a yellowed photo and asking, 'Have you seen my Ah-Di?'..."

Ah-Da looked up, tears brimming in his eyes: "That was her! She's the 'Ginger Tea Granny' I used to see when I was little!"

Nianzhou patted Ah-Da's shaking shoulder, his gaze deep: "Since you recognized her, don't let this elderly lady remain nameless on the road to the underworld. Old Zhang happens to be patrolling nearby. Let's go take back what belongs to her."

Two fifteen in the morning, at the demolition site beneath Sanchong's Chongxin Bridge.

Cold wind and lashing rain whipped against twisted rebar and concrete rubble. A massive excavator sat idle in the center of the muddy site, behind which a corrugated metal site office glowed with the dim amber light of a tungsten bulb, accompanied by the faint sounds of counting cash and clinking metal.

The wooden door of the site office was kicked open with a booming thud by Captain Zhang.

The burly site manager, Liu Daqiang, sat at his desk holding a handheld blowtorch, attempting to melt down the pried-open cookie tin into scrap metal. Scattered across the desk were several yellowed property deeds, a household registration transcript bearing the official seal of the Beigang Township Office, and a baby photo with yellowed laminate and worn edges.

"Police! Make one move and you're in cuffs!" Captain Zhang barked, drawing his service weapon, while two plainclothes detectives swiftly tackled the foreman as he attempted to lunge toward the window.

"Officer! It's a misunderstanding! This is scrap metal I picked out of the trash on the job site!" Liu Daqiang bellowed, pinned against the desk as his fleshy jowls twitched frantically. "Is picking up trash a crime?! What right do you have to arrest me?!"

Chen Nianzhou stepped deliberately into the office, with Ah-Da following close behind.

Ignoring Liu Daqiang's howling, Nianzhou walked up to the desk, picked up the baby photo slightly singed by the blowtorch, gently blew the ash off its surface, and turned toward Liu Daqiang.

"Scrap collectors don't break into the New Taipei Mortuary autopsy suite at one in the morning and sever public evidence lockers with hydraulic shears." Nianzhou retrieved a handheld UV lamp from his pocket and aimed its beam at Liu Daqiang's grease-stained palms.

Under the ultraviolet light, the tips of Liu Daqiang's fingers and his cuffs immediately flared with a blazing, unmistakable neon-green luminescence!

"The handles and locker surfaces in the forensic evidence room are routinely coated with forensic luminescent transfer powder," Nianzhou said, his gaze fixing coldly upon the pale-faced foreman. "The moment bare hands touch it, the powder embeds deep within fingerprint ridges and sebaceous pores. You couldn't wash it off with soap in three days."

Staring down at his glowing hands, Liu Daqiang's lips began to tremble uncontrollably.

"Furthermore," Nianzhou pointed to the steel-toed work boots on Liu Daqiang's feet, caked with yellow mud, "the chevron tread on your soles matches the mud impressions on the autopsy room floor down to the millimeter. Even more lethal: from deep within the laceration on the back of the elderly woman's head, we retrieved heavy-duty anti-corrosion epoxy paint chips identical in composition to the bucket edge of your excavator. During last night's rainstorm, you shoved her down, watched her skull crack against the ground in the mud, and callously padlocked the site gates, leaving her to freeze to death."

Nianzhou's words landed like a verdict from the underworld: "Intentional illegal demolition, manslaughter escalating to abandonment causing death, plus burglary of public evidence by breaking into a government office. Liu Daqiang, prepare to rot in prison for the rest of your days."

Liu Daqiang's knees buckled, and he collapsed entirely into the muddy floor, incapable of uttering another word of defense.

Captain Zhang escorted the handcuffed suspect out of the office, while Ah-Da stepped forward in silence, gathering the scattered deeds, the household transcript, and that precious baby photo, carefully returning each one into the rusted cookie tin.

Outside the site office, the drizzle had stopped, and the eastern sky began to show the pale gray tint of dawn.

Beside the muddy mound of rubble, the spirit of the elderly grandmother stood quietly amidst the morning mist.

Ah-Da stepped forward, reverently cradling the tin box against his chest with both hands. Tears finally breached all restraint, and he sank to his knees before her phantom.

"Granny... your things have been found," Ah-Da choked out, his voice echoing across the silent ruins. "The deed is back, the household registry is back... and Ah-Di's photo is safely right here."

The elderly woman looked down at the intact tin box. The fear and agony that had etched her face slowly dissolved into an immensely tender smile.

From the void, accompanied by ancient hoofbeats, Ox-Head in his black trench coat and Horse-Face stepped out from the morning mist.

For once, Ox-Head held no cigarette between his lips, looking down in quiet dignity at Ah-Da and the elderly grandmother kneeling on the earth.

"Ah-Da," Nianzhou prompted softly, "take out your peace talisman."

Ah-Da paused, then quickly pulled the half-charred Dajia Mazu talisman from his chest and raised it high alongside the cookie tin.

The elderly woman extended her translucent hand, stroking Ah-Da's head with a touch that was icy yet filled with the boundless warmth and comfort of a grandmother.

"Good boy... thank you..." Her voice was no longer raspy, turning as gentle as a spring breeze. "If Ah-Di... had grown up, he would probably be your age by now..."

Sunlight broke through the cloud cover, casting golden morning rays across the grandmother's form.

Her tattered floral coat and worn slippers transformed into clean, tidy blue linen robes. Her hunched spine straightened, and the age spots and weathered wrinkles melted away, revealing a kind, warm, serene countenance.

Ox-Head stepped forward holding an antique bronze guiding lantern, bowing slightly to her: "Honorable elder, after twenty-five years of waiting, your earthly trials are ended. Your son and husband have prepared hot tea at the foot of the Naihe Bridge. Let us go."

"Uncle Ox," Ah-Da wiped his tears and looked up, "what about Ah-Di? Where is the little boy she spent her whole life searching for?"

Horse-Face checked his glowing digital registry and let out a soft sigh, his voice surprisingly gentle: "Ah-Di fell gravely ill after being abducted and passed away when he was seven. But he refused to reincarnate; he has been sitting obediently by the banks of the Naihe Bridge waiting for his grandmother... Finally, their family will be reunited."

The grandmother turned, bowed deeply to Nianzhou and Ah-Da, and with tears of pure peace and happiness, followed the warm lantern light, stepping lightly into the golden dawn.

As the morning mist dissipated, only a faint wisp of sandalwood fragrance lingered above the ruins.

Nianzhou knelt down, helped up the tear-drenched Ah-Da, and pressed a pack of tissues into his hand: "Once you've cried enough, stand up. As a forensic doctor, let your tears fall into your heart. When your hand holds the scalpel, it must never shake."

"Master Nianzhou... I understand." Ah-Da sniffled, wiping his tears away with determination as he clutched the tin box tightly against his chest. "I will personally suture Granny's body and send her off on her final journey."

Nianzhou looked out at the rising sun and lit the Long Life cigarette between his lips.

A new day had begun, and in the forensic pathologist's notebook, yet another wandering soul had finally found her way home.
