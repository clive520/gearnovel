# Chapter 3: Minced Pork Rice with Scallions at the Night Market Corner

6:15 AM. Zhong 4th Road, Ren'ai District, Keelung City.

The harbor dawn was draped in a veil of grayish, humid sea mist. From the distant container terminal came the deep, lingering horn of a freighter, while fine drizzles drifted silently over the slick asphalt pavement.

The vintage Wild Wolf 125 rumbled to a halt before a dilapidated five-story walk-up whose exterior was mottled with dark mildew spots.

Forensic Pathologist Chen Nian-Zhou removed his helmet, cradling in his arms the Danish butter cookie tin wrapped inside a clean canvas tote.

Beside him, old Lin Fu-Lai from the Dahan River drowning case drifted with an ethereal, flickering presence in the morning light. The old man was as visibly anxious as an elementary schooler stepping onto a stage for the first time, his translucent hands clutching the frayed hem of his windbreaker, not daring even to breathe loudly before the rusty green iron door of the apartment.

"Y-Young punk, are you really gonna knock?" The old ghost's voice trembled violently. "What if her fiancé answers? What if... what if she chucks the tin out like common rubbish?"

"If she chucks it, I'll pick it up and sell it for recycling; that's still worth five bucks."

Nian-Zhou gave him a deadpan eye roll, then lifted his hand and rapped his slender, firm knuckles three times against the cold iron door.

"Thump, thump, thump."

The muffled knocking reverberated down the silent stairwell.

A few seconds later, the faint shuffling of house slippers sounded from within, followed by the crisp metallic clack of an unbolting lock.

The iron door slowly cracked open.

Standing behind the threshold was a young woman in her late twenties. Draped in a loose beige knit sweater, her unadorned face was delicate yet exhausted, her eyelids slightly reddened as if she had recently wept. In the living room hallway just behind her hung a snow-white bridal gown draped in a clear dustproof garment bag.

Her brow and eyes bore an uncanny, seventy-percent resemblance to old Lin Fu-Lai hovering in the air beside her.

The moment the old ghost saw that face, his entire spirit stiffened in shock. Clamping both hands tightly over his mouth, the corners of his eyes collapsed as silent phantom tears spilled uncontrollably down his cheeks.

"Hello, may I help you?" The young woman looked with confusion at this cold, severe young man with dark, hollow circles under his eyes.

Nian-Zhou wasted no words, raising the heavy canvas bag with both hands and offering it calmly before her:

"You are Miss Lin Ya-Ting, correct? Fulfilling the final dying wish of an elderly gentleman, I am delivering this to you."

The woman blinked in bewilderment. Subconsciously receiving the canvas tote, she pulled open the zipper.

The moment that rectangular Danish butter cookie tin—its four corners pitted with mottled yellow-brown rust—met her eyes, her pupils contracted violently!

"Th-This box..." Her fingers began to tremble with uncontrollable force.

She pried open the rusted tin lid.

Resting right on top was a Chunghwa Post savings passbook with frayed, dog-eared edges, and that thick, pristine white envelope inscribed with [To Miss Lin Ya-Ting].

The woman's tears burst forth in that instant without warning.

With trembling hands, she tore open the envelope and drew out the five yellowed grid sheets. When her eyes fell upon the first line of clumsy, crooked handwriting crowded with phonetic Zhuyin symbols, and the dark brownish blood thumbprint pressed at the very end of the final page, all the strength drained from her body.

"Thud."

Lin Ya-Ting's knees gave out completely, and she collapsed heavily onto the cold concrete threshold.

Hugging those five sweat-stained, tear-streaked sheets tightly against her bosom, she curled into a trembling ball and unleashed a heart-wrenching sob suppressed for thirty agonizing years:

"Daddy..."

"Why were you so foolish... you big idiot..."

Huge tears splattered heavily across the rusted cookie tin as she wept uncontrollably:

"I never blamed you... Even when I was six years old, I wanted to tell you... losing the house didn't matter, Mama being gone didn't matter... as long as you came home, everything would be okay... Why didn't you come back to see me for thirty years..."

"Three hundred thousand... saving it dollar by single dollar, torturing yourself like this... all I ever wanted was for my daddy to hug me just once..."

At the corner of the stairwell outside the doorway.

The faint dawn light filtered through the courtyard opening, casting a soft glow over the floating old phantom.

Looking upon his daughter sobbing on the floor, looking upon that beautiful white wedding dress hanging in the living room, the final trace of liberated tears slipped down the old man's translucent face.

He did not step forward; instead, standing where he was, he slowly bowed the spine that had been stooped with labor all his life, bending deeply toward his daughter upon the threshold.

"Ya-Ting... my good girl... may you find lifelong happiness."

Straightening up, the old man wore the cleanest, gentlest smile he had possessed in thirty years. Then, his form dissolved into a soft bluish-grey luminescence, drifting quietly into the front compartment of Nian-Zhou's canvas backpack, slipping into a long, sweet, and tranquil slumber.

Nian-Zhou cast a quiet glance at the woman inside the doorway, set a pack of tissues onto the shoe cabinet, turned around, and walked down the dim stairs.

11:20 AM. The parking lot of the New Taipei City Funeral Parlor.

"Putt-putt-putt—hiss."

After sprinting continuously for over a hundred kilometers, the ancient Wild Wolf 125 finally exhausted its last drop of vitality, sagging limply within the parking space amidst a cloud of bluish-white exhaust smoke.

Forensic Pathologist Chen Nian-Zhou dismounted, dragging legs heavy as lead pipes, his bloodshot eyes swollen like overripe lychees.

"Phew... sleep, at last."

Pinching the throbbing bridge of his nose, Nian-Zhou turned to head toward the folding cot in the coroner's office, when a sharp siren and the crisp clatter of leather heels suddenly cut down the corridor!

"Doctor Chen! Hold it right there!"

A crisp, assertive woman's voice laced with urgency resonated down the tiled hallway.

Nian-Zhou turned to see a tall young woman with a sharp bob cut and a black tactical windbreaker vest marching swiftly toward him. Tucking a thick manila case file beneath her arm, her refined, capable features were taut with solemn resolve.

Detective Investigator Xu Shu-Qing of the New Taipei City Police Department's Criminal Investigation Corps.

She was also Nian-Zhou's former classmate from Yang Ming Medical University—while Nian-Zhou had specialized in forensic pathology, she had majored in criminalistics and forensic investigation.

"Detective Xu," Nian-Zhou twitched the corner of his mouth without enthusiasm. "If this is about stamping autopsy warrants, drop them on Ah-Da's desk. If you're treating me to coffee, just hand me fifty bucks in cash; right now, my soul requires an intimate communion with my folding cot."

"Cut the wisecracks. We have a crisis."

Xu Shu-Qing pressed the autopsy dossier firmly against Nian-Zhou's chest, her expression grave:

"At 4:00 AM this morning, a horrific collision occurred at the intersection of Wenhua and Minsheng Roads in Banqiao. A speeding Mercedes ran a red light at over a hundred kilometers per hour, plowing straight into a food delivery scooter on an active run. The drunk driver blew a zero-point-eight-five on the breathalyzer and is currently throwing a drunken tantrum at the precinct."

"The rider died instantly?" Nian-Zhou flipped open the file.

"Multiple comminuted fractures throughout his body, severe cranial depression, dead on arrival," Xu Shu-Qing bit her lower lip, indignation flashing in her eyes. "He was barely twenty-two, name's Jiang Cheng-Kai. What makes it worse... the defense is already angling for a legal brawl between vehicular manslaughter and reckless endangerment, while the victim's sole guardian is in hospice care at Far Eastern Memorial Hospital, completely unable to identify the body."

Nian-Zhou paused for a beat, tucked the file under his arm, and pivoted back toward the eternally chilled Autopsy Suite 4:

"Wheel him in."

Ten minutes later. Upon the cold stainless-steel table.

The moment the body bag opened, even intern assistant Ah-Da—hardened as he was to death—gasped in horror, frantically pressing his palms together to chant Buddhist mantras.

The deceased was a sturdy, athletic youth, his yellow delivery uniform shredded into tattered ribbons, his forearms and torso mangled with shocking contusions and compound deformities.

Donning his double latex gloves, Nian-Zhou laid his right palm gently upon the crushed right shoulder joint, palpating the bony crepitus and the depth of subcutaneous hemorrhagic infiltration.

“HUMMMMM————!”

The resonant bronze chime chimed shrilly once again in the recesses of Nian-Zhou's mind!

The searing hourglass over his heart began trickling its first grain of scalding star-sand—the second 168-hour countdown seamlessly ignited.

In the very next second, a panicked, youthful roar nearly ruptured Nian-Zhou's eardrums:

"Crap, crap, CRAP! Holy hell! My delivery order is massively overdue, aaaaaargh!"

A translucent, youthful spirit sprang three feet into the air from the steel table, frantically swiping his thumbs across an imaginary smartphone in thin air:

"I'm doomed! It's already forty minutes late! The customer is definitely giving me a one-star review! The platform is going to ban my account permanently! There goes my thirty-thousand-dollar perfect attendance bonus, nooooo!"

Nian-Zhou withdrew his hand impassively, picked up a vernier caliper, and let his dry baritone voice drift out:

"Student Jiang Cheng-Kai, you have seven fractured ribs on your right side, a punctured lung, and an eight-centimeter stellate fracture on your cranium. You have been forcibly and permanently deregistered by King Yanluo; the platform won't have the opportunity to dock your bonus."

The young ghost froze solid. He looked down at his own see-through hands, then whipped his head around to stare at the mangled, bloody corpse upon the slab:

"I... did I really... get murdered by that bastard in the Mercedes?!"

Holding the examination clipboard nearby, Ah-Da watched Nian-Zhou talking to empty space, tears of terror welling in his eyes: "B-Brother Nian-Zhou! Were you just holding a conversation with the dead body?! Stop doing that, man! Every time you mumble to yourself, I feel like a demon on the ceiling is staring down my neck..."

"I'm reciting preliminary gross pathology findings. Focus on your notes, and stop scrawling like you're drawing Taoist hexes," Nian-Zhou scolded without looking back.

Ah-Da ducked his chin, furiously scribbling on his clipboard.

Meanwhile, after a fleeting moment of numbness, ghost Ah-Kai suddenly remembered something of monumental importance, lunging directly before Nian-Zhou's face in sheer, unadulterated desperation:

"Mr. Medical Examiner! You can hear me, can't you?! Help me! That delivery cannot be delayed! It really, truly cannot!"

Nian-Zhou replied evenly while measuring the trauma, "As I stated, the dead cannot be resurrected, and late deliveries certainly cannot."

"It wasn't a customer's order! It was my mom's!"

Ah-Kai broke down with a shattered cry, sobbing uncontrollably as he hovered:

"My mom is in the palliative hospice ward at Far Eastern Memorial! End-stage liver cancer... the doctors said she could slip away at any moment... I never had a father growing up; my mom raised me all by herself stitching garments at an apparel factory..."

Gritting his teeth, his spectral eyes turned a furious crimson:

"I work as an auto mechanic apprentice by day with hands black with grease, and deliver food at night, sleeping barely four hours a day just to earn enough for her targeted cancer therapies and out-of-pocket painkillers... At one in the morning, she called me, her voice so faint, so weak..."

"She whispered... 'Ah-Kai, Mama's stomach hurts so much today, and my mouth tastes so bitter. Mama really craves that old stall's minced pork rice with scallions from the corner of Nanya Night Market... with a braised egg, and extra savory gravy poured over the top...'"`

The scalpel in Nian-Zhou's fingers hesitated ever so slightly once more.

"I queued for half an hour in the dead of night just to get that piping hot bowl!" Ah-Kai wept, curling his knees into his chest and clenching his fists:

"I was so terrified it would get cold that I hugged the thermal bag tight against my ribs and gunned the throttle. I just wanted to reach the ward before visiting hours locked down... and then at that crossing, that red-light runner smashed straight into me..."

Ah-Kai jerked his head up, clawing desperately at Nian-Zhou's white coat, his fingers passing right through the fabric:

"Doctor! Where is my delivery bag?! What about the minced pork rice?! Was it destroyed?! My mom has been waiting for that meal since midnight! If it never arrives, she'll think I was slacking off getting into trouble again... she'll leave this world with a broken heart!"

Just then, the heavy steel door of the morgue burst open with a resounding bang.

Detective Xu Shu-Qing walked in briskly, carrying a yellow delivery thermal bag splattered with road grime and severe abrasions. Her eyes were rimmed with red, her expression heavy with sorrow:

"Doctor Chen, the evidence recovered from the crash scene is here. When the forensics team examined the site photos earlier, every single investigator had tears in their eyes..."

Stepping forward, Shu-Qing took a deep breath, her voice trembling slightly:

"The instant that Mercedes plowed into him, the boy was launched over a dozen meters through the air. Yet at the final moment of landing, he curled his entire body into a fetal ball, using his chest, ribs, and forearms to shield this insulated bag like a human cushion..."

Shu-Qing gently unzipped the thermal bag.

Though the heat-resistant paper container inside was slightly deformed from the crushing impact, the plastic seal remained wrapped with airtight precision.

A generous bowl of rice saturated with rich savory gravy, smothered in crispy golden fried shallots, accompanied by that intact, plump braised egg—it had survived such a catastrophic crash without a single drop spilled, miraculously radiating a lingering trace of faint warmth!

A dense, mouthwatering aroma of slow-braised pork belly, rich soy sauce, and fragrant lard fried shallots quietly suffused the cold, clinical autopsy suite.

Floating in mid-air, Ah-Kai stared at that pristine paper bowl, collapsing to his ethereal knees and letting out a long, shattered wail of relief and agony.

Nian-Zhou set down his surgical instruments, slowly peeled off his blood-spattered latex gloves, and tossed them into the biohazard bin.

Walking over to Xu Shu-Qing, he took the heavy delivery bag from her hands.

"Chen Nian-Zhou? What are you doing?" Xu Shu-Qing dabbed the corner of her eyes, looking at him in confusion. "That's key crash evidence; forensics still needs to photograph it for the case file..."

Without answering, Nian-Zhou retrieved his green leather notebook, flipped to a fresh page, and uncapped his pen:

[Client: Jiang Cheng-Kai (Banqiao Wenhua Road Collision Case, Day 1 Post-Mortem)]

[Errand: Deliver Final Bowl of Minced Pork Rice with Scallions (Extra Gravy, Braised Egg)]

[Delivery Destination: Far Eastern Memorial Hospital, Hospice Ward Room 702]

[Time Remaining: 6 Days and 22 Hours]

Snapping the notebook shut and tucking the insulated bag under his arm, Nian-Zhou locked eyes with the dumbfounded detective, his voice utterly flat:

"If this meal isn't delivered right now, it will curdle sour by the time forensics finishes snapping photos."

"Detective Xu, lend me your squad car."

Pulling down his surgical mask, Nian-Zhou revealed a face haunted by sleepless dark circles, yet burning with unyielding conviction:

"There is a dying mother who has been waiting for this delivery for seven agonizing hours."
