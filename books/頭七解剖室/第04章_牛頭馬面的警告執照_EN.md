# Chapter 4: The Warning License from Bull-Head and Horse-Face

12:10 PM.

Outside Room 702 of the Palliative Hospice Ward, 7th Floor, Far Eastern Memorial Hospital.

The corridor was suffocatingly quiet. The air was saturated with strong disinfectant and chamomile aromatics, punctuated only by the faint, muffled hum of rubber casters as nurses wheeled IV stands along the tiled floor.

The microwave at the nursing station chimed with a crisp "ding."

Forensic Pathologist Chen Nian-Zhou wore insulated gloves, delicately lifting out the steaming, reheated paper container.

Standing nearby, the head nurse looked at the scuffed yellow delivery thermal bag in Nian-Zhou's grasp and then at the bowl overflowing with minced pork and fried shallots. Her eyes abruptly welled with tears, and she muffled a quiet sob into her hand:

"Ah-Kai really bought this... That boy... Whenever he had a day off, he'd rush to this ward reeking of motor oil, wiping his mother's back with a sponge while grinning about how he managed to deliver a few extra orders today..."

Taking a ragged breath, the head nurse's voice trembled:

"Her blood pressure started plummeting early this morning, slipping into a deep coma. The attending physician said... it's likely a matter of hours..."

Carrying the warm bowl, Nian-Zhou walked with somber steps into Room 702. Behind him, Detective Xu Shu-Qing pressed her lips tight, her footsteps as noiseless as shadows.

Inside the room, the cardiac monitor emitted slow, frail rhythmic beeps.

Upon the bed lay an emaciated woman. Prolonged chemotherapy had claimed nearly all her hair, leaving parchment-thin, jaundiced skin drawn taut over hollow cheekbones. Her eyes were closed, oxygen prongs resting in her nostrils, her respiration as faint as gossamer strands waiting to scatter in the wind.

"Creak—"

The door shut softly behind them.

Hovering in mid-air, the translucent spirit of young delivery rider Jiang Cheng-Kai clutched his spectral chest, tears streaming like a burst dam. He drifted clumsily to the bedside, falling hard onto phantom knees in thin air, reaching frantically for his mother's withered fingers only to watch his see-through hands pass powerlessly through her cold skin.

"Mom... I'm home..."

Ah-Kai wept, trembling so violently his words splintered into fragments:

"I'm sorry... Ah-Kai got into an accident on the road... Ah-Kai is late..."

As though summoned by this inaudible spiritual anguish, the dying mother's eyelashes fluttered with sudden intensity. Her chest heaved, and with agonizing effort, she slowly opened her cloudy, sunken eyes.

"Hello, Mrs. Jiang,"

Nian-Zhou stepped forward, gently cranking the head of the bed upward by thirty degrees.

He brought the steaming paper bowl—drenched in rich, savory gravy and golden fried shallots—right beneath her nose.

That familiar, comforting aroma of lard-sautéed shallots and slow-simmered soy sauce drifted tenderly through the tranquil room.

It was the most indelible memory binding mother and son across twenty years—the day Ah-Kai scored a hundred on his elementary school exam, when his mother collected her meager factory overtime pay, took his little hand, and ordered that special bowl of minced pork rice at the entrance of Nanya Night Market.

"Th-This bowl of rice is..." The woman's parched lips parted, her voice as faint as a dying sigh.

"Ah-Kai delivered it,"

Nian-Zhou fabricated the lie impassively, his voice steady, calm, and resolute:

"He had too many orders backed up today, and his scooter blew a tire on the bridge, trapping him at the repair shop. Terrified the food would grow cold, he specifically begged the police and forensic examiners to rush it over for him."

A sudden, miraculous spark of lucidity flared within her dim, exhausted eyes.

She rolled her gaze with great effort, taking in the plump, glossy braised egg soaked dark in braising broth, beholding the thick layer of savory minced pork, her lips curving into an exceedingly faint, tender smile:

"I knew it... I always knew my Ah-Kai... would never forget..."

Picking up a plastic spoon, Nian-Zhou carefully scooped a small bite of soft rice drenched in savory sauce, blew it cool at his lips, and offered it gently to her mouth.

The mother parted her withered lips with immense effort, taking in that single bite.

The rice was tender, the gravy rich and savory.

As she chewed with slow, reverent grace, two scalding tears rolled silently down her hollow eye sockets, splashing heavily onto the snow-white linen:

"It smells so good... just like when he was little... That boy... with rain pouring so hard last night... he must have been soaked to the bone again..."

Floating beside the pillow, Ah-Kai had collapsed into a ball of sobbing light, shaking his head frantically while clutching empty air:

"No... Mom! I didn't get soaked! I hugged the thermal bag tight against my chest! The food didn't get wet at all! It didn't get cold!"

"Mom... in our next life... I still want to be your son... I won't ever ride fast again... Mom..."

The mother held on long enough to swallow three small spoonfuls of rice.

Those three mouthfuls seemed to exhaust the last reserve of her mortal life.

She feebly raised her trembling fingertips, resting them lightly against Nian-Zhou's wrist, her chilled touch conveying a mother's final earthly devotion:

"Thank you both... please tell Ah-Kai... Mama is full... my stomach doesn't hurt anymore..."

"Tell him not to run delivery orders so desperately... he needs to sleep more... he must remember to eat breakfast..."

The instant those words drifted away.

The faint rhythm on the cardiac monitor gradually flattened, stretching into a single, piercing, continuous tone—

"BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP————"

Outside the window, a warm autumn breeze gently stirred the white curtains.

Wearing an expression of absolute peace, devoid of any further pain, the mother gently closed her eyes and passed away.

A long, broken sob tore from Ah-Kai in mid-air. He collapsed against the edge of the bed, resting his transparent forehead against his mother's cooling hand, never lifting it again.

Stepping out of the ward, the sunlight along the corridor was warm yet dazzling.

Leaning against the cool tiled wall with reddened eyes, Xu Shu-Qing remained silent for a long time. She turned to look at Chen Nian-Zhou beside her, who had quietly pulled down his mask with exhausted eyes:

"Chen Nian-Zhou... the way you lied just now made you look entirely like a hopeless con artist."

"A medical examiner's duty is to seek truth for the dead. Moonlighting as a benevolent liar occasionally doesn't violate civil service regulations."

Nian-Zhou handed the empty yellow delivery bag back to her impassively:

"I will have the vehicular accident reconstruction and autopsy report on your desk before tomorrow afternoon. Now, I am heading back to the mortuary."

11:40 PM.

Rooftop terrace of the morgue facility, New Taipei City Funeral Parlor.

The night wind howled, the late autumn chill biting like frost. In the distance, the streaming headlights along the elevated expressway flowed like an endless river of molten gold.

Wrapped in his heavy black windbreaker parka, Nian-Zhou cupped a steaming can of sugar-free black coffee fresh from the vending machine, breathing pale plumes of fog into the air.

Beside him, old Lin Fu-Lai from the Dahan River drowning case had faded to an ethereal transparency, his edges glowing with a fragile luminescence that seemed ready to scatter with the wind.

Barely ten minutes remained before the one hundred and sixty-eight hours since the old man's drowning reached total completion.

"Young fellow..."

The old man turned, smiling warmly at Nian-Zhou with a serene peace that had washed away all earthly guilt:

"I owe everything to you for Keelung. Today in my dreams... I saw Ya-Ting wearing that white bridal gown. She looked so breathtaking... just like her mother when she was young."

"Glad you saw it," Nian-Zhou took a sip of the bitter coffee. "When you get down there, remember to take a number and wait in line. Don't fight for a spot with those vicious spirits."

The old man chuckled softly and nodded with profound gratitude.

The exact second the clock struck 11:55 PM—

"WHOOOOOSH————!!"

The howling wind ceased with uncanny, dead silence in an instant!

Across the chain-link perimeter fence, a white layer of biting frost crystallized at terrifying speed! The temperature plummeted below zero, and the sensor lights in the stairwell buzzed erratically, flickering violently amidst sharp electrical pops!

Then, the heavy fireproof steel door to the rooftop was slowly pushed open by a large hand clad in a black leather glove.

Through rolling waves of inky, freezing mist, two tall silhouettes emerged with thunderous, steady strides.

The figure on the left was hulking and broad-shouldered, well over six feet tall, draped in a tailored long black trench coat with a toothpick rolled in the corner of his mouth, an ancient dark-gold bull-horn sigil gleaming faintly across the center of his forehead;

The one on the right was lithe and lean, sporting a sharp crew cut and gold-rimmed spectacles, head bowed as his fingers tapped across an ultra-slim black-jade tablet glowing with ethereal blue light, a pale white celestial stallion glyph pulsing behind his ear.

Bull-Head and Horse-Face.

Specialized enforcers of the Netherworld's Seventh Constabulary Unit.

"Aiya, Old Bull! I told you this mortal household registration digital system is an absolute half-baked joke!"

The bespectacled man—Horse-Face Ah-Ma—scrolled frantically through digital talisman records while griping aloud:

"What 'Section 2, Lixing Road, Alley this, Lane that, Sanchong, New Taipei City'?! When I patrolled here a few centuries ago, this was all paddy fields! Those old fossils upstairs keep pushing for digital transformation, and our yin-yang GPS navigation nearly steered me headfirst into the mud of the Tamsui River!"

"Shut it, Ah-Ma. Duty hours. Maintain dignity."

The hulking man—Old Bull—crushed the toothpick between his teeth with a muted crunch, his bronze-bell eyes scanning the rooftop before locking with sovereign weight onto Chen Nian-Zhou.

Halting in his tracks, Old Bull drew an obsidian token from his coat—its surface licking with dark nether-flames—his voice rumbling like distant thunder:

"Deceased spirit Lin Fu-Lai: mortal ledger terminated, seven-day grace period expired. By decree of the Great Emperor of Mount Dongyue, this court arrives to strike your name from the register of the living and escort you below."

Looking upon the two celestial marshals, old Lin Fu-Lai showed not the slightest hint of terror.

Smoothing down his ragged windbreaker, he bowed deeply toward Chen Nian-Zhou in one final, solemn gesture of reverence:

"Doctor Chen, for your immense grace... this old soul departs now."

"Safe journey. I won't see you off," Nian-Zhou waved his hand impassively.

Ah-Ma stroked his slender finger across the dark jade screen.

A black chain forged of pure nether death-energy coiled around the old man's wrists like a gliding serpent. Lin Fu-Lai's form dissolved into gentle, warm golden motes, streaming down the links into Ah-Ma's leather dossier bag.

With the official arrest concluded, Old Bull did not leave.

He strode forward with ponderous steps before Nian-Zhou, his towering bulk casting an imposing, chilled shadow like a mountain peak.

Gazing down at the coroner, Old Bull drew a cicada-wing parchment scroll from his lapel—glowing with dark crimson light and the sharp scent of cinnabar—slapping it down onto the concrete parapet with a sharp smack.

Across the header blazed the scarlet imperial seal of the Underworld: [Yin-Yang Special Dispensation: Impermanence Courier Oversight Warrant].

"Chen Nian-Zhou,"

Old Bull's voice was gravitas incarnate, reverberating like spiritual thunder:

"Over these years, exploiting the convenience of your post-mortem examinations, you have tied up earthly loose ends for countless departed souls. Neutralizing their grievances and obsessions on mortal soil has spared our judicial tribunals considerable interrogation grief."

Old Bull jammed a thick, iron-like finger onto the ancient parchment:

"However, look upon this 'Warning License' with open eyes."

"Birth, age, sickness, and death are the inexorable cycle of heaven. A soul departs, seven days unfold, and each bears their own karma. Running errands and passing messages is your personal mercy; but if you ever dare cross the absolute crimson line—meddling with mortal lifespans or sheltering wicked spirits from judgment..."

A terrifying flare of divine dark-gold brilliance detonated within Old Bull's eyes:

"This soul-binding black iron chain will be the next thing to pierce through Chen Nian-Zhou's collarbones!"

Facing the suffocating aura of the Netherworld titan capable of crushing mortal nerves, Nian-Zhou didn't so much as blink an eyelid.

He merely took out a handkerchief from his pocket, dabbing at a droplet of black coffee that had slopped over the rim in the gust, speaking as placid as still water:

"Marshal Bull, first of all: I'm just a civil servant drawing seventy thousand a month from the District Prosecutors Office. I haven't the slightest leisure to defy heaven. Second: if the Underworld's digital overhaul is truly running that short of manpower, remember to calculate my courier overtime into karmic merit points and deposit them into my spiritual account next month."

Old Bull froze in surprise, then threw back his head, unleashing a deep, wall-shaking roar of laughter:

"Hahahahaha! What a reckless little mortician! That tongue of yours remains as venomous as ever!"

Beside him, Horse-Face Ah-Ma adjusted his gold-rimmed glasses, snapping the jade tablet shut with a meaningful smirk:

"Doctor Chen, don't hold Old Bull's harsh words against him. That 'Seventh-Day Hourglass' beneath your collarbone—every time it rotates, a shred of your primordial essence burns away. Exercise caution; we'd hate to examine your remains on our autopsy schedule too early."

"We depart!"

Flourishing his trench coat, Old Bull and Ah-Ma dissolved into two icy bluish-grey whirlwinds, vanishing in the blink of an eye into the boundless night sky.

The bone-chilling frost retreated swiftly from the rooftop as the distant murmur of city traffic returned to the world of the living.

Nian-Zhou lowered his head, watching the dark crimson warrant fade into nothingness against the concrete, before reaching into his coat to touch the green leather notebook resting against his chest.

On the newest page, the delivery errand for Jiang Cheng-Kai's minced pork rice was recorded in neat handwriting, stamped with a final pen stroke: [Errand Completed].

Lifting his head, Nian-Zhou drained the last dregs of cold black coffee from the can.

The night breeze was crisp, brushing across his exhausted yet unwavering face.

Upon the cold dissection table, the first wisp of smoke had scattered.

Yet the warmest bonds of the human world were only just beginning to unfold.
