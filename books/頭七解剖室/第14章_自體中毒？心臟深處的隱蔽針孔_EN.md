# Chapter 14: Endogenous Poisoning? The Hidden Puncture Deep Within the Heart

Under the shadowless surgical lamp of the autopsy suite, the crisp clinking of surgical instruments sounded exceptionally chilling.

The number eleven scalpel in Chen Nianzhou's hand sliced with extreme steadiness across his mentor's skin, starting along the anterior midline of the mandible down to the pubic symphysis, executing a textbook Y-shaped thoracoabdominal incision with consummate precision.

The blade severed the aged, withered soft tissue overlying the sternum, revealing a pale yellowish subcutaneous adipose layer. There was no redundant clotting, no contusive extravasation; everything bore the deceptive appearance of a normal mortal frame that had come to a peaceful halt from advanced age.

Standing beside him handling suction and recording, forensic intern Ah-Da's forehead was beaded with sweat, his hand holding the bone shears trembling slightly: "Master Nianzhou... to the naked eye, the coronary arteries appear patent, and the epicardium shows no overt transmural infarction or necrosis..."

"Never trust the superficial veneer your eyes first behold," Nianzhou said, taking the rib shears, his voice steady without a tremor of emotion. "The cause of death always hides within the deepest shadows. Snip the costal cartilages; lift the sternal plate."

*Snap, crunch—*

Muffled cracking of bone echoed throughout the cavernous autopsy chamber. Nianzhou carefully peeled back Professor Jiang's sternal shield, laying bare the mediastinal viscera of the thoracic cavity beneath the cold glare of the lamps.

The pericardial sac was intact, glistening with a faint, translucent sheen. Using delicate toothless forceps, Nianzhou pinched the anterior pericardial wall, made a two-centimeter incision with tissue scissors, and aspirated all ten milliliters of clear, pale-yellow pericardial fluid with a sterile syringe, sealing it in a chilled anticoagulant tube.

Just as Nianzhou cupped his mentor's three-hundred-and-fifty-gram heart in both hands, a soft cough resonated from the empty air beside the table.

Clad in his familiar white coat, Jiang Jingyuan's soul leaned against the instrument tray, an ethereal pipe between his lips, looking down with keen amusement at his own heart resting in his student's hands.

"Nianzhou, my boy," old Professor Jiang's frosted brows arched slightly, his tone laden with a forensic pathologist's dark humor, "my heart looks reasonably well-maintained, wouldn't you say? The left anterior descending branch has some lipid deposition, but luminal stenosis doesn't exceed forty percent. I quit smoking for five years and jogged every morning—on this coronary artery alone, I should have had another decade easily."

The scissors in Nianzhou's hand hesitated for a fraction of a second.

Without looking up, an ache surged deep in his chest as he answered quietly: "The wall of the anterior descending artery is smooth, without plaque rupture, and devoid of acute red thrombus. Song Mingze's diagnosis of 'coronary artery disease with acute myocardial infarction' collapses under pathological scrutiny."

"Yet if it wasn't a heart attack, how did I suffer sudden cardiac arrest on the couch in under two minutes last night?" Professor Jiang exhaled a puff of spiritual smoke, his gaze turning razor-sharp. "Mingze handed me a cup of Longjing tea he had brewed himself. After two sips, there was no crushing chest pain—only an icy chill spreading from the pit of my chest across my limbs, followed by total paralysis of all skeletal muscles, leaving me unable to even reach the emergency bell."

"That is the hallmark of simultaneous central nervous system and cardiac conduction blockade," Nianzhou placed the heart in the dissection tray, beginning millimeter-by-millimeter cross-sectional slicing along the atrioventricular groove and coronary tree.

In routine cardiac examinations, pathologists typically perform bread-loaf slicing along the ventricular long axis. But Nianzhou did not rush to slice the myocardial walls; instead, using high-power surgical loupes, he cradled the heart gently and turned it to inspect the posterior pericardial reflection and the deep recess of the coronary sinus.

At the junction of the left atrium and left ventricle on the posterior cardiac wall, buried deep within the thick adipose pad, lay a microscopic dark red hemorrhage point scarcely zero point three millimeters wide!

"Ah-Da, high-intensity cold light source; hand me number thirty-two micro-forceps," Nianzhou's voice tautened suddenly.

Ah-Da hurriedly focused the microscope's illumination onto the posterior cardiac wall.

Under the high magnification of the lens, that tiny speck hidden in the adipose tissue of the coronary sinus was dramatically enlarged—it was no spontaneous capillary rupture, but a clean, smooth mechanical puncture mark with an outer diameter of approximately zero point two millimeters!

The needle had penetrated the epicardial fat, piercing precisely into the deep cardiac vagal nerve plexus running alongside the circumflex branch of the left coronary artery!

"A needle mark?!" Ah-Da gasped, eyes widening in shock. "The killer drove a needle directly into the posterior wall of Professor Jiang's heart?! H-How could that be done in a living person? The ribs and sternum protect the entire anterior chest!"

"No need to penetrate the chest wall," Nianzhou's gaze was as frigid as ice, his probe pointing precisely to the anatomical interface between the posterior esophageal wall and the pericardium. "The killer didn't strike from the front. By administering tea laced with high-potency topical anesthetic and central muscle relaxants, during the golden window when the victim is in deep coma but the heart has not yet stopped, an ultra-thin flexible endoscope can be introduced through the oral cavity into the esophagus. At the anatomical weak point where the mid-esophagus abuts the posterior left atrium, an endoscopic micro-puncture needle can pierce cleanly through the esophageal mucosa and posterior pericardium straight into the cardiac conduction system!"

"Transesophageal ultrasound-guided cardiac micro-puncture!" Ah-Da's knees went weak, cold sweat drenching his brow. "T-That is the latest minimally invasive drug delivery patent developed jointly by NTU Cardiac Surgery and the Toxicology Lab!"

"And listed first among the inventors on that patent," Nianzhou set down his forceps, his voice freezing as tundra, "is none other than Song Mingze."

Standing beside them, Jiang Jingyuan's soul fell silent. He lowered his bone-china pipe, the playful glint in his spectral eyes vanishing, replaced by profound, bone-deep sorrow.

"Mingze..." Jiang Jingyuan closed his eyes, exhaling a long, mournful sigh. "I took him as my final doctoral student, cherishing his brilliant gifts in molecular toxicology, fighting opposition to send him to Roche in Switzerland. To think... he would turn a life-saving micro-delivery technique into a murder weapon against his own teacher's heart."

"Professor Jiang," Nianzhou looked at the spirit, his voice gravelly, "what toxin did he inject? Standard toxicology screens at NTUH emergency couldn't detect a single microgram."

"It is code-named 'NZ-407,' an ultra-short-acting synthetic conotoxin analogue," old Professor Jiang opened his eyes, molecular structures flickering in his spiritual gaze. "An extreme neurotoxin developed fifteen years ago during illicit third-world clinical trials by that multinational pharma giant. Inside the human body, it blocks Purkinje fiber conduction in thirty seconds, triggering ventricular fibrillation and cardiac arrest. Deadliest of all, its half-life is barely ninety seconds—in the bloodstream, pseudocholinesterase hydrolyzes it into harmless glycine and water, rendering the parent molecule invisible to standard mass specs."

"Hydrolyzed?" Nianzhou scoffed coldly. "There is no perfect poison in this world. As long as it traverses living cells, it inevitably leaves irreversible molecular footprints."

Nianzhou swiftly excised a two-millimeter rim of myocardial and pericardial tissue around the puncture site, sealing it alongside the pericardial fluid in a dry-ice thermostat box: "Ah-Da, alert the CIB Forensic Chemistry Lab. Spin up Taiwan's only ultra-high-resolution quadrupole Orbitrap LC-MS/MS system."

"Master, what metabolite are we scanning for?" Ah-Da asked urgently.

"Specifically modified peptide fragments and trace lanthanide metal catalyst residues," Nianzhou's eyes flared with brilliant forensic intellect. "Chemical synthesis of conotoxin is immensely difficult; to preserve its disulfide bond stereochemistry, neodymium trichloride must be used as a metal coordination catalyst. By measuring the ultra-trace neodymium isotope ratio in the pericardial fluid, we can pinpoint the sole laboratory in Taiwan that procured that catalyst!"

Two hours later, in the rooftop cleanroom of the Criminal Investigation Bureau Forensic Sciences Building.

The indicator lights of the quadrupole Orbitrap mass spectrometer blinked rhythmically in the darkened cleanroom. Accompanying the steady hum of high-pressure liquid pumps, the printer rolled out a three-meter-long multidimensional spectrum.

"We got it!" Ah-Da leaped in jubilation, sprinting to Nianzhou with the sheet. "Master Nianzhou! From the puncture tissue extract, an anomalous enrichment peak of four hundred and fifty picograms of Neodymium-142 was detected! Furthermore, cross-linked fluoro-glycine fragments unique to conotoxin were isolated from myocardial receptor proteins!"

"Move," Nianzhou stripped off his lab coat, gripping the embossed Ministry of Justice forensic certificate like a drawn broadsword. "Captain Zhang has deployed outside NTU College of Medicine. We close the net."

Ten o'clock in the morning, Auditorium One of the International Conference Hall at NTU College of Medicine.

Draped across the gilded podium was a massive crimson banner: *NTU Institute of Toxicology & Swiss Novartis Multinational R&D Signing Ceremony and Academic Symposium*.

The hall was packed to capacity, cameras from dozens of major news outlets focused on the stage, flashbulbs popping incessantly. Impeccably dressed in a bespoke suit, Song Mingze stood at the center of the lectern with laser pointer in hand, addressing medical luminaries and foreign executives:

"Distinguished colleagues, this three-billion-NTD new drug development fund will revolutionize Taiwan's standing in targeted molecular therapies. I wish to express my deepest gratitude to my late mentor, Professor Jiang Jingyuan. Though he tragically passed away last night, his uncompromising dedication to truth will forever inspire our team..."

"Director Song, I suspect the single greatest regret of Professor Jiang's life was admitting you to that team."

A razor-cold male voice detonated from the rear of the auditorium, broadcasting with crystalline clarity over the PA system.

Dead silence clamped down upon the room as every lens swung around toward the rear entrance.

Clad in his black trench coat, Chen Nianzhou strode down the center aisle, flanked by an iron-faced Captain Zhang and Prosecutor Lin, while plainclothes detectives carrying tactical handcuffs sealed all emergency exits flanking the stage.

The smile on Song Mingze's face froze solid as he barked furiously: "Chen Nianzhou! This is a top-level international symposium! What right do you have to cause a scene here?! Security! Escort these people out!"

"Security cannot touch us, but the Criminal Code can touch you," Prosecutor Lin stepped onto the stage, presenting an arrest warrant. "Song Mingze, the New Taipei District Prosecutors Office has issued a warrant for your arrest on the charge of homicide. Step down."

Uproar erupted across the floor, flashes strobing frantically!

"Preposterous! Slander!" Song Mingze's face flushed crimson as he roared at the press corps, "Professor Jiang died of acute myocardial infarction! Emergency physicians and precinct coroners issued the death certificate! Chen Nianzhou is merely a disgruntled field pathologist maliciously framing me out of petty professional jealousy!"

Chen Nianzhou strode up to the lectern, plugging the flash drive containing the multidimensional mass spectra and myocardial pathology slides directly into the master console.

Across the massive projection screen flashed high-resolution micrographs of the micro-puncture on the posterior cardiac wall, alongside the sharp absorption peaks of Neodymium-142 and synthetic conotoxin!

"Song Mingze, you believed that using transesophageal endoscopic puncture into the coronary sinus plexus would leave you above suspicion?" Nianzhou leaned against the lectern, his voice cracking like breaking ice. "You thought that by injecting synthetic conotoxin NZ-407 with its ninety-second half-life, post-mortem hydrolysis would erase your tracks? But you forgot that the neodymium trichloride catalyst you personally procured left four hundred and fifty picograms of isotopic residue in his pericardial fluid!"

Nianzhou locked eyes onto Song Mingze's contorting features: "In all of Taiwan, only you hold the patent for this transesophageal cardiac drug delivery method; and in all of Asia, only you imported five hundred milligrams of neodymium catalyst from Merck Germany last month under the name of the NTU Toxicology Lab! The recipient's signature reads Song Mingze!"

Every sentence struck like a sledgehammer onto Song Mingze's skull.

Foreign pharmaceutical executives blanched and packed their briefcases in haste to leave; press photographers frantically captured Song Mingze drenched in sweat, crumbling behind the lectern.

"I... I did it for science! I did it for that three-billion-dollar fund!" Song Mingze broke down completely, ripping off his necktie and howling like a cornered beast, "That stubborn old relic! He got hold of the raw clinical trial data from fifteen years ago and insisted on reporting the pharma company to the Ministry of Health and prosecutors for covering up fatal toxicities! If he blew the whistle, the entire fund would vanish! My academician title, my directorship—everything gone! He was sixty-eight years old, on the verge of retirement—why couldn't he just look the other way?! Why did he have to stand in my way?!"

"Because he was a forensic pathologist."

Nianzhou's voice was not loud, yet it resonated like a great bronze bell throughout the hall.

"A forensic pathologist's blade is forged to cut away lies and guard truth—not to serve as a cosmetic brush for corporate greed." Nianzhou's eyes glistened with tears as he looked coldly upon him. "Song Mingze, you calculated every molecule and mass spectrum in the world, yet you failed to account for the human conscience."

Song Mingze's legs gave out, and he collapsed heavily onto the stage, his gold-rimmed glasses shattering against the floor.

Captain Zhang strode forward, and the cold handcuffs snapped shut around Song Mingze's shaking wrists with a heavy click.

Behind the flashing lights and chaotic press throng, high up in the shadows of the mezzanine, Jiang Jingyuan's soul stood in his white coat. Gazing down at his handcuffed former star pupil, the old mentor sighed softly, then turned toward Chen Nianzhou on stage with a proud, comforting smile.

Bathed in the glare of the spotlights, Nianzhou stood straight as an iron pillar, meeting his mentor's gaze.

Day two after death, the truth was unveiled. Yet for this bond between teacher and student spanning two decades, one final, sacred rite remained as the Touqi countdown ticked on.
