# Chapter 11: The Old Miner's Thirty-Year Gold Bars and Testament

In the afternoon, the mountain towns of Jiufen and Jinguashi were once again swallowed by a damp, leaden sea fog.

A persistent drizzle pattered against the mottled tar-paper roof of Number 64 Qitang Road, whispering like hushed murmurs. Built during the Japanese colonial era and weathered by more than half a century of wind and rain, this old stone house felt all the more desolate and bleak following the murder of retired miner Lin Fude.

Yet today, this quiet ruin was surrounded by curious neighbors from the lane, alongside several sharp-suited, ill-intentioned strangers.

"Captain Zhang, we are legal representatives of Hongyuan Asset Development Company," a young man wearing gold-rimmed glasses stood arrogantly at the cordon outside the entrance, waving a counterfeit authorization letter. "During his lifetime, Lin Fude owed our client Mr. Chen Jinbiao two million dollars. Under the mortgage provisions of the Civil Code, we have the right to inventory and impound this ownerless stone house and all personal effects within!"

Behind him stood two burly thugs gripping iron pry bars and pickaxes, eager to barge inside and smash open the floorboards.

"Ownerless stone house?" Captain Zhang chewed his betel nut and sneered, resting his heavy palm squarely against his service pistol holster. "Chen Jinbiao was detained in custody without bail last night as a suspect in first-degree murder! You scavengers sure get the news fast—your boss just landed in the detention center, and you're already scrambling to loot an old man's funeral urn?!"

"Captain Zhang, you cannot speak like that. We hold lawful debt instruments..."

"Lawful instruments?"

A bone-chilling voice resonated from below the stone steps. Chen Nianzhou strode up the stairs accompanied by Ah-Da, flanked by a female officer from the Jinguashi Police Station holding the hand of twelve-year-old mute girl A-Mei. Dressed in a thick winter coat, the little girl still clutched the yellowed "Taiwan Metal Mining Corp" plastic helmet tightly against her chest, shrinking behind Nianzhou in terror.

Nianzhou held a formal document bearing the embossed seal of the Keelung District Court and slapped it squarely against the legal representative's chest: "This is an 'Order for Evidence Preservation and Mandatory Appointment of Estate Administrator' petitioned by Prosecutor Lin from the court this morning. Prior to the conclusion of the criminal trial and lawful inheritance proceedings, anyone trespassing to tamper with physical evidence will be arrested on the spot for obstruction of justice and destruction of evidence."

A blade-sharp chill flashed in Nianzhou's eyes: "Still want to go in? Put your hands together for the cuffs, and I'll escort you straight to the detention center."

The men's faces changed drastically. Exchanging uneasy glances, they spat on the ground and retreated down the stone steps in disgrace under Captain Zhang's stern glare.

"Bastards, trying to tear off a dead man's last roof tile," Ah-Da cursed under his breath, stepping forward to push open the battered wooden door of the stone house.

The interior of the stone shack remained in a state of violent disarray from Chen Jinbiao's ransacking. Shreds of old cotton batting and broken tiles were strewn across the floor, and the earth around the hearth had been pitted with spade marks. Chen Jinbiao had tortured the old miner before his death precisely because he was convinced a fortune in gold was hidden inside this very shack.

"Master Nianzhou, Chen Jinbiao pried open almost every flagstone in this place last night, smashing the hearth and the bottom of the water cistern, but found nothing," Ah-Da swept his flashlight across the debris, scratching his head in confusion. "Did the old grandpa truly hide gold bars? Or is this just a miner's myth passed down in Jiufen over the decades?"

Nianzhou did not answer right away. He crouched down, gently drawing little A-Mei to his side, his tone softening as if he were an entirely different man: "A-Mei, what was the first thing Grandpa did every day when he came home?"

The girl blinked her reddened eyes. Seeing the gentle warmth in Nianzhou's gaze, her fear slowly dissolved. She raised two small fingers, deftly gesturing in the air with tapping and twisting motions, then pointed toward the blackened, crude acacia-timber-supported earth stove in the corner of the room.

"Chen Jinbiao already smashed that earth stove to bits..." Ah-Da muttered.

Nianzhou walked slowly over to the stove. The masonry had been pulverized by pickaxes, black wood ash scattered everywhere. But Nianzhou's gaze did not settle upon the hearth floor; instead, it drifted upward, locking onto the mud-and-straw coated flue pipe rising above the stove.

It was a stout, crude black earthenware flue pipe extending straight up into the ceiling rafters. The surface was coated in a layer of soot and coal tar centimeters thick, looking indistinguishable from any ordinary rustic chimney.

Donning sterile latex gloves, Nianzhou extended his index finger and tapped lightly against three nodal points on the earthenware flue near the roof timber.

*Thud, thud, clink—*

When he tapped the highest blind spot concealed behind a patch of black tar paper, a deep, heavy metallic resonance echoed from within the earthenware pipe!

"It's hollow!" Ah-Da gasped in surprise.

Taking a flat chisel and rubber mallet from Ah-Da, Nianzhou meticulously chipped away along the carbonized mortar seam around the pipe's exterior. Moments later, a removable baffle cleverly disguised as an earthenware tile was lifted out, revealing a square iron box sealed tightly in heavy-duty rubber moisture-proof sheeting deep within the flue.

The box had been wrapped in five layers of fireproof tape and coated in anti-corrosion asphalt pitch. When Nianzhou slit the tape with his scalpel, the dry scent of brass and vintage printing ink preserved for thirty years drifted into the room.

The iron box was gently opened.

Under the beam of the high-powered flashlight, Ah-Da and Captain Zhang held their breath simultaneously.

Inside lay no gleaming piles of modern bullion, but three heavy, rustic, antique "999" fine gold bars, their surfaces stamped with the vintage official seal of "Taiwan Metal Mining Corp・Benshan Mine," each weighing approximately ten taels; beneath the gold bars rested a thick, yellowed, brittle ledger, alongside dozens of letters bearing red thumbprints.

It was a handwritten ledger entitled *General Ledger of Receipts and Disbursements of the Benevolent Mutual Aid Fund of Jinguashi Benshan Shaft No. 6 Miners*.

Wearing his gloves, Nianzhou turned open the first page with solemn reverence.

Upon the fragile, yellowed paper, alternating brush and pen calligraphy recorded modest yet fiery entries: *"August, 63rd Year of the Republic: Chen Ashui died in the line of duty from a rockfall; fellow miners contributed five hundred dollars to the principal fund..." "69th Year of the Republic: Lin Wangfa passed away from silicosis; funeral expenses disbursed from fund..." "76th Year of the Republic: Taiwan Metal Mining Corp ceased operations and sealed the shafts; remaining mutual aid fund of thirty taels of gold entrusted to Lin Fude as permanent representative..."*

On the final page of the ledger were the signatures and crimson thumbprints of more than forty retired miners. Beside nearly every name, a neat, stark black box had been drawn with a pen—in Taiwanese documentary tradition, a boxed name signified that the person had departed from this world.

Forty-two names in total, forty-one enclosed in black boxes!

Lin Fude was the very last living soul upon this yellowed covenant of life and death.

"So... this gold never belonged to Grandpa Lin alone," Ah-Da's eyes reddened completely, his throat choked as if blocked by cotton. "This was the lifeline saved penny by penny by those old miners, setting aside their meager wages to care for fallen brothers, widows, and orphans..."

"Taiwan Metal Mining Corp closed thirty years ago, and those old workers suffering from silicosis and crippling injuries passed away one after another," Captain Zhang removed his police cap and bowed his head deeply, his voice raspy. "For thirty years, Uncle Ah-Fu guarded these thirty taels of gold all by himself, living in a leaking stone shack and eating sweet potato mash, yet never touching a single cent..."

Nianzhou picked up a yellowed sheet of paper resting at the very bottom of the ledger.

It was a last will and testament written in trembling calligraphy by Lin Fude shortly before his death. Portions of the paper were stained with dried sweat and tears, the handwriting uneven, yet carrying the ultimate dignity and devotion of an honorable man:

"To the future Judge or person of good conscience:

I am Lin Fude, retired miner from Benshan Shaft No. 5 of Taiwan Metal Mining Corp. I am past seventy, and my silicosis has reached its terminal stage; I know my time is short.

These thirty taels of gold represent the mutual aid fund left behind by forty-two miner brothers. For thirty years, I have administered this gold, arranging funerals and caring for the widows of twenty-seven fallen brothers. Today, all my brothers have departed, leaving only A-Mei, the deaf-mute granddaughter of my closest sworn brother Chen Maosen, to live by my side.

Upon my death, I wish for these thirty taels of gold to be liquidated entirely: half to be donated to the New Taipei Miners' Silicosis Welfare Association to honor the memory of my brothers sleeping beneath the earth; the other half to be placed into a court-supervised trust fund to provide for A-Mei's hearing treatment, cochlear implant surgery, and lifelong living expenses.

Our ancestral house at Number 64 Qitang Road was built together by my brothers in our youth; under no circumstances must it fall into the hands of gangster Chen Jinbiao. I plead with you in tears of blood."

At the bottom of the letter was pressed Lin Fude's aged thumbprint scarred by quartz dust, alongside a tiny red thumbprint pressed by a little girl's hand with stamping ink.

After reading the testament, every officer in the room fell into prolonged silence, only the sound of rain tapping the roof echoing in the quiet.

"A-Mei... isn't even Uncle Ah-Fu's biological granddaughter?" Ah-Da wiped his tears, looking at the quiet, obedient little girl beside them. "She is the bloodline of the brother who saved his life all those years ago..."

"Beneath the earth in a mine shaft, blood ties do not matter—only the brothers to whom you entrust your life," Nianzhou carefully placed the letter into a sterile evidence pouch, his tone heavy with reverence. "These thirty taels of gold are heavier than all the wealth of any billionaire family."

Just then, the chilly air inside the stone shack began to glow with a gentle, warm luminescence.

The flame of the windproof candle by the table flickered softly, casting an ethereal silhouette upon the yellowed plaster wall.

The soul of retired miner Lin Fude materialized silently beside A-Mei. His hunched spine remained habitually curved, his eyes filled with boundless devotion and longing, as he reached out translucent hands in a trembling gesture to hold his granddaughter one last time.

Twelve-year-old A-Mei seemed to sense something profound.

She jerked her head up, her large eyes searching through the empty stone room. Then, as if catching sight of a familiar light, tears spilled from her eyes; dropping the miner's helmet from her arms, she threw her small arms wide and leaped forward into the phantom in the air!

Though her tiny body passed straight through the translucent spirit, A-Mei knelt stubbornly on the floor, lifting her tear-streaked face and gently caressing the old man's phantom features with her small hands in the empty air.

Parting her voiceless lips, her throat trembled violently as she summoned every ounce of strength from deep within her small chest, tearing out a faint, hoarse, yet thunderous cry:

"Ah... Ah... Gong...!"

It was the very first word this deaf-mute girl had spoken in all twelve years of her life!

The old miner's soul shuddered violently, two streams of hot spiritual tears streaming down his sunken cheeks. He bowed his head, gently pressing his spectral forehead against hers, his translucent lips parting in endless tenderness:

"A-Mei... Grandpa's precious girl... Grandpa heard you... Grandpa heard every word..."

Inside the stone shack, Ah-Da could hold back no longer, crouching on the ground and weeping openly; Captain Zhang turned his back, brushing his sleeve roughly across his eyes.

Chen Nianzhou stood straight and tall, his eyes burning with emotion. He stepped forward, reverently lifting the handwritten ledger and testament in both hands, and bowed deeply toward the old miner's spirit.

"Senior Lin," Nianzhou's voice was low and unshakably firm, "Prosecutor Lin has already initiated judicial trust proceedings for these thirty taels of gold, which will be placed under the full supervision of the Keelung District Court. Next week, ENT specialists at National Taiwan University Hospital will evaluate A-Mei for a cochlear implant; and this ancestral house on Qitang Road will be designated a protected historic heritage site by the Cultural Affairs Bureau—neither Chen Jinbiao nor his mob backers will ever touch a single stone."

Nianzhou drew a deep, steady breath, his voice ringing with absolute certainty: "The loyalty and brotherhood shared between you and your forty-one fellow miners—Chen Nianzhou gives you his word: this era will never forget it."

The old miner's soul slowly looked up, gazing upon the heavy ledger in Chen Nianzhou's hands. In that moment, all the burdens, grief, and agony that had weighed upon his heart vanished into peace.

He pressed his hands together in gratitude toward Chen Nianzhou, then turned to cast one final, lingering look upon his granddaughter.

A-Mei seemed to understand that her grandfather was about to embark on his final journey; wiping her tears, she took a red bean candy that Ah-Da had given her earlier from her pocket, laid it carefully upon the edge of the stone hearth, and offered her grandfather's phantom a pure, radiant, understanding smile.

The old man's silhouette dissolved into a warm golden glow, accompanied by the faint fragrance of sandalwood and damp earth, ascending gently into the wooden rafters of the stone shack.

Outside, the sea fog began to disperse, and across the rugged slopes of Jinguashi, a beam of evening sunlight broke through the clouds, illuminating the black tar-paper roof with a shimmer like scattered gold dust.

The final day of the seven-day countdown was about to dawn, and upon this mountain city that had buried so much blood, sweat, and glory, a justice and redemption thirty years in the making had finally arrived.
