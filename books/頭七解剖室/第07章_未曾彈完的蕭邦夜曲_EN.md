# Chapter 07: The Unfinished Chopin Nocturne

The air in the first interrogation room of the New Taipei Criminal Investigation Corps was as cold as frost.

The acoustic foam walls showed a faded grayish-white tint, and behind the one-way glass stood Inspector Zhang with a solemn, stern expression. Directly opposite the interrogation table sat Zhao Lingyun, eldest son of the prestigious family, now changed into a crisp, navy-blue tailored shirt. Flanking him on either side were two top-dollar defense attorneys, seated upright with an arrogant demeanor, as if they had merely dropped by to inspect their private social club.

"Captain Zhang, I reiterate: the marks on my client Mr. Zhao Lingyun's arm were accidental scrapes from rose thorns while trimming bushes on his balcony a few days ago." The senior attorney on the left pushed a thick legal brief forward, a seasoned sneer glinting behind his spectacles. "You have detained Mr. Zhao without grounds for over six hours, and even forcibly restricted his personal freedom before producing any formal forensic report. This will not hold up in court for a second. If you do not release him within ten minutes, we will immediately petition the District Court for habeas corpus review and file state compensation and defamation lawsuits against the New Taipei District Prosecutors Office."

Sitting in the center, Zhao Lingyun appeared completely at ease, even casually smoothing down the creases of his cuff with a faint, arrogant smirk playing across his lips. His well-groomed fingers tapped lightly against the table in a steady, dull rhythm, his gaze dripping with disdain, as though watching ants struggle in vain before death.

The heavy iron door of the interrogation room creaked open.

The person who stepped inside was not an intimidated detective, but Chen Nianzhou, dressed back in his wrinkled khaki trousers and jacket. He carried no baton or handcuffs in his hands—only a stack of freshly printed lab sheets still warm from the laser printer at the Criminal Investigation Bureau's forensic laboratory, and a black flash drive sealed inside a transparent moisture-proof case.

Nianzhou pulled out an iron chair and sat down. Instantly, the thick smell of old morgue formalin and cheap Long Life cigarettes spread through the sealed interrogation room. He glanced neither at the lawyers nor at the lavish legal brief; he simply lifted his bloodshot eyes and stared straight into Zhao Lingyun's hypocritical face.

"Trimming roses?" Nianzhou's voice was low and gravelly, like sandpaper scraping across raw lumber. "Mr. Zhao's garden variety must be truly extraordinary. The rose thorns it grows even know how to precisely secrete human male sebaceous epithelial cells."

The two attorneys' expressions shifted abruptly.

Nianzhou slapped the first lab report down onto the steel table.

"The expedited forensic match from the Ministry of Justice's Institute of Forensic Medicine and the CIB DNA Laboratory came out fifteen minutes ago." Nianzhou extended a tobacco-stained index finger and pressed it heavily against the sixteen STR locus profiles at the bottom of the sheet. "Extracted via sterile saline and ultrasonic agitation from deep within the mangled nail bed of victim Bai Zhiqing's right middle finger were trace epithelial cells. All sixteen STR loci match completely. The statistical probability of these cells originating from anyone other than Zhao Lingyun is one in one hundred quintillion."

Zhao Lingyun's rhythmic tapping froze instantly, and the corner of his eye twitched.

"Forensic Pathologist Chen, DNA contamination is commonplace in forensic practice!" the younger attorney on the right immediately countered in a sharp, raised voice. "Zhiqing was Mr. Zhao's fiancée! They cohabited and had intimate contact. For dead skin flakes from her fiancé to remain under her fingernails is entirely plausible and reasonable. That cannot possibly stand as evidence of murder!"

"Don't be in such a rush, counselor. I'm not finished," Nianzhou cut him off coldly, sliding the second sheet—a cross-sectional wound profile taken under a high-power stereomicroscope—across the table.

"In casual daily intimacy, what collects under fingernails is merely loose, sloughed-off epidermal stratum corneum dead cells. But scraped from Bai Zhiqing's nail bed were tissue fragments containing dermal papillary capillaries with red blood cells and live stratum basale cells." Nianzhou pointed to the dark red cluster of tissue on the micrograph. "In other words, this was fresh, living flesh violently gouged from a living person by someone using every ounce of their dying strength amidst agonizing struggle and asphyxiation."

The interrogation room fell dead silent, save for the low hum of the air conditioner.

"Next, regarding that so-called 'rose thorn scratch' on Mr. Zhao's wrist." Nianzhou flipped to the next page, revealing a forensic macro photograph of Zhao Lingyun's wrist taken during compulsory sampling. "The physical characteristics of plant thorn injuries are puncture tears or unidirectional linear scratches accompanied by epidermal indentation caused by plant sap and back-curved barbs. But the wound on your wrist features retro-curling epidermal flaps and an asymmetrical undulating groove. That is the hallmark of human fingernails digging in, met by the violent twisting of wrist tendons during a life-or-death struggle. Under a 3D optical profilometer, the contour and width of that scratch groove match Bai Zhiqing's snapped middle fingernail with an error margin of less than zero point zero two millimeters."

Zhao Lingyun's complexion began to shift from pale to sickly green, and fine beads of cold sweat broke out across his temples.

"Finally, there is this." Nianzhou plugged the flash drive into a laptop and rotated the screen toward Zhao Lingyun. "You claimed you were already back asleep in your Xinyi District penthouse before eleven on the night of the incident. While you unplugged the private road surveillance camera at the Yangmingshan villa, the flood-control high-resolution infrared camera at the water treatment plant at the base of the hill captured your silver-gray Porsche heading down at one fourteen in the morning. Even more damning: the wool fibers recovered from Bai Zhiqing's neck are a patented Super 180s cashmere-wool blend from a Savile Row bespoke tailor in London, dyed in a proprietary batch limited to only three suits globally. One of them is currently hanging on the rack in your walk-in closet under Captain Zhang's seizure order—and its collar even tested positive for the exact white tea perfume Bai Zhiqing wore in life."

Nianzhou shut the file folder with a crisp, echoing snap, sounding just like a judge's gavel striking the bench.

"Zhao Lingyun, she clawed into you before dying not to beg for her life, but to use her own fingernails and your flesh to write her own final death certificate." Nianzhou's voice was chillingly sharp. "You strangled her with your bare hands, staged it as a hanging, and used the silk cord of the piano chandelier to desecrate the music she held dearer than life itself. You believed your wealth and lawyers could purchase any lie. But in forensic science, a corpse never lies."

"Enough! Shut up!"

Zhao Lingyun sprang abruptly from his iron chair, slamming both palms down onto the metal table. His polished aristocratic facade shattered entirely, his eyes bloodshot with frantic rage as he roared hysterically: "That ungrateful, worthless bitch! What did she know?! That thirty-million-dollar music foundation was my family's money to begin with! What was wrong with using it for company cash flow?! How dare she take the ledgers and threaten to call a press conference to cancel the engagement! Who did she think she was?! Just a dirt-poor street rat raised on temple fair night markets in Sanchong! She only stood on the stage of the National Concert Hall because I groomed and promoted her! I gave her status, I gave her a name, and she wanted to destroy me?!"

Both attorneys sank back into their chairs, their faces ash-white, lacking even the strength to restrain their client.

The steel door behind the one-way glass burst open, and Captain Zhang strode in accompanied by two armed detectives, gleaming steel handcuffs reflecting coldly under the fluorescent lights.

"Zhao Lingyun, you are under arrest as a flagrant offender for homicide. No bail." Captain Zhang's voice boomed like thunder as the cuffs snapped shut around Zhao Lingyun's trembling wrists with a sharp, merciless click.

Watching Zhao Lingyun get dragged out of the interrogation room in disgrace, Chen Nianzhou merely closed his eyes in exhaustion and rubbed his throbbing temples.

At the corner of the corridor, a faint chill stirred in the air.

Nianzhou opened his eyes and saw Bai Zhiqing's slender figure standing silently in the shadows. The moment the handcuffs locked onto the killer, the dark grudge-mist shrouding her soul had mostly dispersed, yet her eyes still brimmed with crystalline spiritual tears, her mangled, broken fingers hanging helplessly at her sides.

"Nianzhou... thank you," Zhiqing whispered, her voice as light as a breeze, filled with release yet carrying boundless sorrow. "But I... I so wish I could play the piano one last time..."

Nianzhou looked down at his wristwatch.

Nine fifteen in the evening.

Only two hours and forty-five minutes remained until the midnight Zi hour of the seventh day—the absolute deadline when Ox-Head and Horse-Face would claim her soul.

"Old Zhang," Nianzhou turned toward Inspector Zhang, who had just walked out, his expression grave to the point of pleading, "can you help me borrow the keys to the New Taipei Arts Center concert hall tonight? Just half an hour."

Captain Zhang froze for a moment. He looked into Nianzhou's bloodshot yet resolutely determined eyes, then glanced at the sheet music folder inside the evidence bag—worn by tears and creases. He sighed, fished out his patrol car keys, and said, "You crazy bastard... Let's go. The center director was my classmate at Central Police University. I'll pledge my badge in the Bureau Chief's name."

A cold drizzle drifted through the night over the streets of New Taipei. The patrol car sped through the darkness with sirens flashing, pulling up at the empty, silent Arts Center at ten o'clock sharp.

The cavernous auditorium was pitch black, save for a single maintenance worklight hanging from the dome, casting a solitary beam of cold light squarely upon the black Steinway concert grand piano at center stage.

The piano lid reflected the empty rows of red velvet seats, as silent and solemn as a colossal obsidian monument.

"Nianzhou, this piano... Mom and I could never come in here." Zhiqing hovered beside the grand piano, reaching out translucent fingers in a trembling effort to touch the keys, only for her fingertips to pass through the black and white ivory again and again. "When I was little, we lived in a leaky tin-roof shack beneath Chongxin Bridge in Sanchong. Mom worked three jobs all by herself—hauling produce at the wholesale vegetable market at dawn, operating sewing machines at the garment factory in the afternoon, washing dishes at the night market until midnight..."

Nianzhou stepped onto the stage, pulled out the piano bench, sat down, and gently unfolded the carefully plastic-sealed, yellowed sheet music onto the music stand.

It was music handwritten by Bai Zhiqing. The score began with Chopin's famed *Nocturne in E-flat Major, Op. 9 No. 2*, but at the climax of the ornate ornamentation in the middle section, the notes ingeniously modulated, blending seamlessly into an immensely tender and rustic Taiwanese lullaby melody.

In the bottom-right corner of the score, a delicate pencil note was written in neat, elegant handwriting: *A Lullaby Dedicated to Mom: Mountain Cherries and Nocturne*.

"Mom's hands were covered in thick calluses, her finger joints swollen and deformed from washing vegetables in the bitter winter cold." Zhiqing's soul sat beside Nianzhou on the bench, tears dripping one by one onto the keys and dissolving into ethereal flecks of light. "She pinched every penny, saving every ten-dollar coin and wrinkled hundred-dollar bill inside an old tin cookie box decorated with wedding designs. When I reached third grade, she finally gathered thirty thousand dollars and bought a yellowed, secondhand Yamaha upright piano..."

"She didn't know how to read sheet music. But whenever I cried from frustration while practicing, she would sit on the stool beside me, massaging my sore fingers while softly humming this *Mountain Cherry Lullaby* to soothe me to sleep." Zhiqing choked up, her voice breaking with heartbreaking sorrow. "Before she passed away in the hospice ward, she told me the proudest moment of her entire life was seeing me in a beautiful gown playing on stage... She said she would be listening from heaven."

In the shadows on both flanks of the stage, the air suddenly warped violently.

Accompanied by the heavy, ancient clatter of hooves and the clinking of iron chains, Ox-Head in his black trench coat and Horse-Face clutching his glowing soul tablet materialized silently from the void.

Ox-Head carried an unprecedented solemnity upon his countenance. For once, he did not reach for a cigarette, merely gazing down at the faint green glow of the underworld registry.

"Forensic Pathologist Chen," Horse-Face's synthesized voice dropped unusually low, laced with a rare sigh, "thirty-eight minutes remain before the Zi hour claiming."

Nianzhou took a deep breath. He raised his hands—hands calloused from years of gripping scalpels—and hovered his fingers above the black and white keys.

"I haven't touched a piano since graduating from high school," Nianzhou murmured with a self-deprecating smile, turning toward Bai Zhiqing beside him. "My fingering is bound to be terrible. Please bear with me."

"You'll do wonderfully..." Zhiqing smiled through her tears. She gently laid her ethereal, translucent hands with their broken fingernails over the backs of Nianzhou's rough hands. "Nianzhou, close your eyes and feel the breath of the notes... I will guide your fingers."

The first chord descended softly.

The gentle, mellifluous melody of the *E-flat Major Nocturne* began to flow quietly through the vast, hollow stillness of the concert hall.

At first, Nianzhou's keystrokes were somewhat stiff and unpracticed, but guided by Zhiqing's soul, the music grew steadily warmer, deeper, like moonlight rippling across a tranquil lake at midnight.

As the music developed and reached the transition in the score, the classical romantic melancholy of Chopin quietly gave way, replaced by the earthy Taiwanese melody that carried the scent of home and the warmth of a mother's embrace.

In that melody lived the steady patter of rain against the corrugated tin roofs of Sanchong alleys; the damp, chilly mist of the fruit and vegetable market before daybreak; the enduring silence of hands submerged in freezing dishwater; the crisp metallic chime of coins dropping into a cookie tin; and above all, the boundless devotion of an ordinary, frail mother who lifted her daughter's musical dreams with the entirety of her humble life.

In the empty auditorium, the piano notes felt like a tangible wave of warmth, washing through every shadowy corner.

In the shadows of the wings, Ox-Head held his soul chains and slowly closed his eyes in solemn respect, while the normally boisterous Horse-Face watched silently as the emerald light of his tablet dimmed, both listening in reverence to this farewell song spanning the realms of the living and the dead.

The melody gently drifted toward its final movement.

It was a sustained, gentle concluding note, soft as a mother's calloused palm caressing her daughter's hair for the very last time, and like a mountain cherry blossom blooming bravely in the freezing winter wind, finally greeting the first warm rays of spring sunlight.

The music faded. The final resonance hovered beneath the high vaulted ceiling of the hall for a long time before dissolving into profound peace.

Center stage, the second hand of the clock quietly ticked past eleven o'clock. The Zi hour had arrived.

Bai Zhiqing stood up from the piano bench.

At that very instant, a miraculous luminescence enveloped her entire form. The hideous black strangulation marks around her throat vanished, her broken, ripped fingernails turned whole, lustrous, and smooth once more, and the cold morgue cloth draped over her dissolved into a pure white evening gown, elegant and proud like a swan in flight.

She turned toward Chen Nianzhou, crossed her hands gracefully in front of her waist, and bowed deeply with solemn grace.

"Forensic Pathologist Chen, thank you for clearing my name, and thank you for helping me finish this piece." Pain and resentment had completely vanished from Bai Zhiqing's face, replaced by a radiant, serene smile of release. "Mom... must have heard it."

Ox-Head and Horse-Face stepped slowly forward.

This time, Ox-Head did not draw his heavy, frigid iron chains. Instead, he drew from inside his coat a soft ribbon of dark red silk, resting it gently around Bai Zhiqing's fair wrist.

"Miss Bai, your hour has come," Ox-Head said in a deep, gentle voice. "Your mother has waited for you by the Sansheng Rock at the banks of the Wangchuan River for three years. She has a hot bowl of soup ready for you. Let us go."

Zhiqing's eyes welled with tears as she nodded, turning back to cast one final look at Nianzhou: "Forensic Pathologist Chen, please... never stop being a forensic doctor. Because of you, the dead do not depart carrying unavenged wrongs."

"Have a safe journey, Miss Bai," Nianzhou said, rising to stand straight beside the piano and bowing his head with deep respect.

Amidst wisps of blue vapor and a warm, fading halo, the three figures gradually dissolved into the boundless void.

The vast concert hall returned to quiet stillness, with only a solitary spotlight shining down upon the black Steinway grand piano.

Nianzhou slowly sat back down on the bench and placed an unlit Long Life cigarette between his lips. From his jacket, he pulled his ever-present black leather notebook, and in the blank space beside Bai Zhiqing's name, he wrote down a neat, deliberate entry:

"Case Type: Apparent Suicide (Amended to Homicide). Cause of Death: Manual Strangulation Asphyxia. Final Dying Wish: Perform *Mountain Cherries and Nocturne* for her mother. Status: Completed."

Closing the notebook, Nianzhou gazed out at the empty auditorium and let out a long breath mingling the scent of tobacco and faint traces of formalin.

The night is long, but the dawn will always pierce the darkness.
