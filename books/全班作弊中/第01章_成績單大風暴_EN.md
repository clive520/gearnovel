# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 1: The Report Card Storm

On Wednesday afternoon, an announcement more terrifying than a typhoon warning was being held in the classroom of Class 6-1 at Luyang Elementary School.

On the podium, Teacher Gao Zhixian pushed up his black-rimmed glasses, a faint azure glint flashing in his pupils behind the lenses:

"Attention, everyone. According to the announcement from the Bureau of Education, the nationwide standardized monthly exam for sixth grade will arrive next Wednesday. Scope: Chinese Lessons 1-5, Math Units 1-4, Science Units 1-3, Social Studies..."

"Clank!"

The pork bun in Old Wu's mouth dropped straight onto his desk. His eyes went glassy, his voice trembling like a dead leaf in winter:

"T-Teacher... just hearing the words 'Social Studies' makes me feel like the whole world isn't 'social' anymore..."

A-Xin collapsed onto his desk, wailing miserably: "We're doomed! The monthly exam! My mom said if I'm at the bottom of the class again, she'll lock my game console in a safe and make me best friends with a cram school for the whole summer!"

That remark was like a lit fuse. All twenty-six faces in the class instantly twisted into bitter gourd shapes.

"Silence!"

At the classroom door, a low, stern voice poured in like a blast of cold air, and the whole class was instantly pressed onto the mute button.

Director of Student Affairs Zhou Yan strolled in with his hands clasped behind his back, his hawk-sharp gaze sweeping across the class, his infraction notebook already flipped to the newest page:

"During the exam period, this school will enforce the strictest examination rules in history! Anyone caught with hidden notes, passing papers, or signaling will be penalized in accordance with school regulations! Class 6-1, you little trouble-makers, listen carefully—don't let me, Zhou Yan, see your names on the violation list!"

No sooner had Director Zhou left than a mouse-and-fox-like little head poked out of the ceiling ventilation duct with a "whoosh."

Liu-Liu, the class's AI smart pet, flicked its metallic-lustered tail and blinked its electronic eyes with frantic red flashes:

"Beep beep! Black-Faced Judge intimidation index: ninety-nine! Negative energy blanketing the entire corridor! Recommend the whole class retreat to a safe distance of fifty meters or more!"

"Retreat my foot!" A-Xin grabbed a pencil stub from his drawer and tossed it at Liu-Liu. "The exam isn't going to disappear just because we retreat!"

The dismissal bell rang overhead of Class 6-1.

If the monthly exam was the storm, then the string of events after school was the last few muffled thunderclaps before the storm.

The first muffled thunderclap came from the school gate.

Mama Ke—vice president of the parent association, and the town's most accomplished mother at slamming tables with designer handbags—was blocking the pickup zone, one hand holding her phone, the other on her hip, her volume louder than the class bell:

"Teacher Gao! I only let my child stay in Class 6-1 out of respect for the parent association! This time, if our class doesn't even get half a class's worth of students into the top ten, I'll mail the report cards straight to the Bureau of Education! Let all of Taiwan know that Class 6-1 of Luyang Elementary is the 'class of trouble-makers that even cram schools can't save'!"

"Mama Ke, grades are meant to assess learning progress, not..." Teacher Gao barely got half the sentence out.

"I don't care about assessing anything! I only look at scores!"

The second muffled thunderclap came from the home contact notebook.

A-Xin fished the notebook out of his backpack, flipped to the last page, and saw his mother's large, bold words written in red pen:

"A-Xin: Mom has locked your game console in the safe. Pass every subject on the monthly exam, and I'll give you the password. Fail, and I'll send you to be a professional exam-taker over the summer."

"What's a professional exam-taker? It sounds kind of impressive, but when you think about it, it's terrifying!" A-Xin wailed.

The third muffled thunderclap came from the scooter carrying Old Wu's father just outside the school gate.

Old Wu's father, one foot on the scooter pedal, held a fragrant fried chicken drumstick and waved it smugly:

"Little Wu! Dad wants to make a bet with you—if you pass every subject on this monthly exam, I'll treat you to a whole month of drumsticks! But if you fail even one subject—heh heh, no drumsticks for a month. You can't even smell them!"

Old Wu's face instantly turned paler than the grayish color of his lunchbox.

"Dad, threatening me like that is no different from tying me up in front of a drumstick stall and starving me for three days!" Old Wu said, his eyes welling up.

"This is a father's love, got it!"

Just as the twenty-six students were being bowled over by their parents' muffled thunderclaps, A-Xin quietly stopped his wailing, a strange light igniting in his eyes.

He hopped onto the podium, pulled out a stub of chalk he'd picked up from who-knows-where, and forcefully drew a big circle in the center of the blackboard:

"Everyone! Listen up!"

All eyes in the class snapped over at once.

"The monthly exam is coming, the parents are opening fire, and the director has laid down the law—tell me, are we just supposed to sit back and wait to be slaughtered?"

"Yeah... I guess?" Old Wu bit into his pork bun, looking utterly lost.

"Wrong!" A-Xin stabbed a dot into the center of the circle. "When one person gets picked off, that's an accident; when twenty-six people get picked off, that's a battle! I hereby declare—the 'Exam Mutual Aid Society' is now officially established!"

"A mutual aid society? Is it for studying together?" Lin Yunyun timidly raised her hand.

"Studying?" A-Xin widened his eyes as if he'd just heard some alien language. "Yunyun, you're so naive! Mutual aid is, of course—you help me, I help you, everyone 'rescues' each other through the monthly exam!"

The air fell silent for three seconds.

Old Wu was the first to react: "So it's... cheating?"

"Keep your voice down!" A-Xin clapped a hand over Old Wu's mouth, glancing nervously at the doorway. "Do you want the Black-Faced Judge to be the first one to catch us?!"

Old Wu nodded hard, then shook his head hard, then nodded once more, finally flashing a complicated expression that was equal parts scared and excited.

Qingqing sat in the first row, twirling a small screwdriver in her hand, speaking coolly:

"From a technical standpoint, the questions on a nationally standardized monthly exam have never been seen by anyone. Unless we get the answer key, the real substance of 'mutual aid' is just stitching together whatever bits each of us remembers—which, in statistics, is called 'cutting the long to patch the short.'"

"Yes! That's exactly it! Qingqing, you're so smart!" A-Xin said, his eyes welling with emotion. "So you're joining, right?"

"I didn't say I'd join." Qingqing pushed up her glasses. "I was just objectively analyzing the feasibility."

"Objective analysis = joining! Didn't your mom ever teach you that?!" A-Xin, overjoyed, threw his arm around Qingqing's shoulders.

In the corner, Weixi stood quietly, the inside of the chrono bracelet on her left wrist glowing faintly. She shook her head gently:

"I'm not joining."

A-Xin was taken aback: "Weixi, with grades as good as yours, you don't need to—"

"It's not about the grades." Weixi's voice was very soft, yet extraordinarily clear. "It's because... cheating is something that will have to be paid back someday."

"Paid back? To whom?" A-Xin was full of question marks.

Weixi didn't answer. She only touched the glowing ring on her wrist, a fleeting shadow from a distant future flickering across her eyes.

"Anyway!" A-Xin clapped the blackboard. "The Exam Mutual Aid Society—twenty-six members minus one, that's twenty-five, officially established! Starting tomorrow, we're going to come up with the most amazing cheating methods in the whole world, so powerful that neither Teacher Gao nor Director Zhou can catch us!"

"But..." Old Wu raised his hand timidly. "A-Xin, have you forgotten who our teacher is?"

"No matter how strong Teacher Gao is, he's still human!" A-Xin declared with heroic bravado. "And humans have weaknesses!"

The next morning, during morning self-study, A-Xin's grand declaration met its ruthless live test.

"Classmates," Teacher Gao Zhixian stood on the podium, pushing up his black-rimmed glasses, "there are still five days until the monthly exam. To ensure fairness on the exam, today I'll demonstrate firsthand—what is called a 'cheating scan.'"

He closed his eyes, and when he opened them again, a golden ring of light appeared faintly in his pupils, zooming and shrinking.

In the next second, twenty-six desks, twenty-six backpacks, twenty-six palms, and twenty-six pencil-case compartments all became transparent X-ray plates beneath his gaze.

"Wu Chenkai," Teacher Gao's gaze locked precisely onto Old Wu, "on the left side of your eraser is written 'circumference = 2πr,' and on the right side 'area = πr².' Half of the writing is smudged by the sweat on your palm—it looks like you've been nervously rubbing it for three days."

Old Wu slid right off his chair with a "thump," frantically stuffing the eraser into his mouth and mumbling: "Teacher, I didn't! This is, this is, I'm using it as a talisman!"

"A talisman?" Teacher Gao pushed up his glasses. "According to examination rules, a talisman with formulas written on it is treated as a cheating tool. Mr. Wu, please recite the contents of your talisman aloud, right here, as an honesty exercise."

Old Wu, hands trembling, fished the eraser out of his mouth. Glancing at the formulas smeared crookedly by sweat, he stammered:

"The circ-circumference equals two pi r... the circle's area equals pi r squared..."

"Very good." Teacher Gao nodded. "The writing is blurry, but you recited it perfectly. Remember this feeling—in thirty seconds just now, you absorbed all the knowledge that was on one eraser."

Old Wu was stunned.

He looked down at the eraser, then up at the blackboard, and suddenly realized that maybe he had really... remembered it?

"Next," Teacher Gao's gaze turned to Wang Zhuankai, "on the palm of your left hand, written in mechanical pencil, are three lines of annotations from Lesson 4 of Chinese. Are you sure you want to put them somewhere that smudges so easily?"

"Pfft!" Wang Zhuankai, frightened, frantically wiped his palm against his pants.

"Lin Yunyun, the folded paper in your pencil case—that's your review notes for Science Unit 1, isn't it?"

Lin Yunyun turned pale, silently hugging her pencil case to her chest.

Teacher Gao's scan was like a precise patrol satellite, naming one student after another, exposing one scheme after another. Out of the twenty-six students, twenty-three had hidden "backup materials" of varying sizes on them—from erasers, palms, and pencil cases, all the way down to the soles of their shoes. None escaped.

The entire classroom was dead silent. Liu-Liu hung on the wire outside the window, electronic eyes wide as saucers, all the fur on its tail bristling:

"Beep, beep... target combat assessment revision: infinite! Absolutely infinite!"

A-Xin sat in the last row, the corners of his mouth twitching.

He had originally wanted to say "no matter how strong Teacher Gao is, he's still human," but now, watching Teacher Gao's eyes that seemed able to see straight through the desks, he felt for the first time—this teacher might really not be human.

"To sum up," Teacher Gao concluded, "I scanned twenty-three sets of prepared cheating materials. Honesty index: eleven and a half percent for the whole class. This number makes me very sorry to hear."

The whole class lowered their heads, not daring to breathe.

"However," Teacher Gao shifted his tone, the light behind his lenses suddenly softening, "I discovered something very interesting—every student who was scanned can recite the contents they were hiding, completely."

A faint stir rippled through the classroom. Old Wu scratched the back of his head: "Huh? It seems... really, yeah?"

"This proves that, in the process of preparing their 'backup materials,' you all actually already stored the knowledge in your heads." Teacher Gao smiled faintly. "Classmates, before the monthly exam, consider this question—if you've already remembered it, why risk getting written up to hide it inside an eraser?"

After school, the first formal meeting of the Exam Mutual Aid Society was convened in a circle at the back of the classroom.

"Everyone!" A-Xin slapped the table hard. "We all saw it today! Teacher Gao is not human at all! He can see through things! He can scan! Even a note in the sole of your shoe can't escape!"

"So... so should we still cheat?" Old Wu asked timidly.

"Cheat! Of course we'll cheat!" The fighting spirit blazed in A-Xin's eyes. "Precisely because he's so strong, we have to cheat even more! As long as we come up with a cheating method stronger than his detection, nobody can catch us!"

"But A-Xin..." Qingqing spoke calmly, as if delivering a finishing blow. "You heard it just now. Teacher Gao said everyone has actually already remembered the material."

"That must be a coincidence!" A-Xin said firmly. "Stuff like 'once you remember it, you don't need to cheat'—only goody-goodies believe that! I'm a man who's getting his game console back!"

"And my drumsticks..." Old Wu added quietly on the side.

A-Xin pulled out the chalk stub he'd picked up and, at the corner of the blackboard, solemnly drew the first tally mark:

"Starting tomorrow, we'll figure out a way—Cheating Plan No. 1, officially launched!"

Night fell, and the classroom of Class 6-1 quietly went dark.

Nobody noticed that, in the shadows at the corner of the hallway, Director Zhou Yan quietly set down his infraction notebook, a cold smile curling at the corner of his mouth:

"Exam Mutual Aid Society? Hmph... Class 6-1, I've known all along that you little trouble-makers wouldn't stay quiet."

He flipped open his notebook and, on the blank first page, wrote two words in pen:

"Keep a close watch."
