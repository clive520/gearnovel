# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 3: Paper Planes and Rubber Band Conveyor Belt

"Everyone! The Miniature Library has fallen!"

In the classroom after school, A-Xin snapped a piece of chalk in two and slapped it on the podium in righteous indignation:

"But failure doesn't mean the end! Teacher Gao has X-ray vision, so we just—don't hide anything anymore! We'll go with 'transporting'!"

"Transporting? Transport what?" Old Wu asked through a mouthful of pork bun.

"Transport—the answers!" A-Xin drew three big characters on the blackboard. "Plan No. 2: The Aerial Delivery System!"

He unrolled a design drawing full of scribbles, covered with dense arrows, dashed lines, and mysterious circles:

"Everyone, take a look! Instead of having everyone risk being scanned to hide cheat sheets, let's have the smartest person among us 'know the answers,' and then—air-drop them to everyone!"

"So we need a 'main unit'?" Qingqing pushed up her glasses.

"Yes! And the main unit is—" A-Xin's gaze swept the class and finally landed on the girl in the front row who was busy doing homework, "Weixi!"

Lin Weixi raised her head, a hint of confusion in her eyes: "Me?"

"Your grades are the best!" A-Xin leaned in, palms pressed together. "Weixi, if you'll just transmit the answers to everyone during the mock exam, the whole class is saved!"

"I don't cheat." Weixi's answer was clean and crisp.

"But..."

"And besides," Weixi shook her head gently, "A-Xin, answers delivered by 'passing' will always be passed wrong, passed short, or passed slow someday. The only truly reliable answers are the ones already in your own head."

"That's because your head is good!" A-Xin clutched his own head and wailed.

"Then—make everyone's heads good too." Weixi left those gentle words and went back to her homework.

"Fine! If you won't be the main unit, we'll build our own!" A-Xin turned to the class, pointing at the design drawing. "Airdrop system, three routes in parallel! Route one—paper planes! Route two—rubber band slingshot! Route three—chalk box airdrop, piloted by Liu-Liu in person!"

"Beep?" Liu-Liu poked its head out of the ventilation duct, its electronic eyes plainly saying "What's it got to do with me?"

"Liu-Liu! The whole class is entrusting you with a great responsibility!" A-Xin pulled out a can of lubricating oil and waved it in front of Liu-Liu. "Mission accomplished, and this can of 'top-grade space lubricant' is yours!"

Liu-Liu's electronic eyes instantly blazed like two little suns, its tail standing straight up, letting out an excited electronic chirp:

"Beep beep beep! Roger! Liu-Liu Airlines, ready for takeoff!"

"Good! Everyone, get to work!"

And so, the underground factory of the Exam Mutual Aid Society started production in full swing.

Wang Zhuankai was in charge of folding paper planes. He folded a dozen in a row, every one crooked, some with wings of mismatched lengths, some with noses flattened as if they'd been stepped on. A-Xin stared at them for a long while before he couldn't help complaining:

"Your planes... can they even fly?"

"Whether they fly or not is one thing," Wang Zhuankai said with full confidence, "the point is—they're cool!"

"Cool? This is 'cool'? I'd call it 'drool'!" A-Xin held his forehead helplessly.

Over on the other side, Old Wu was frowning at a thick rubber band. He slipped it over his fingers, pulled it hard—"whoosh"—the rubber band shot out and hit a paper plane dead-on, snapping its nose in two.

"Wow! Old Wu! Are you testing force or destruction?" A-Xin jumped up and yelled.

"I-I'm just testing the strength!" Old Wu rubbed his fingers aggrievedly. "Who knew it would be this powerful..."

"You used the plane I folded myself to test it!" Wang Zhuankai cradled the wreckage lovingly.

In the corner, Qingqing was silently assembling the third route—the chalk box airdrop pod. She disassembled three chalk boxes, taped them into a simple hanging gondola, strapped Liu-Liu's special harness in the middle, and fixed two balance wings cut from rulers on the front and back.

"Airdrop pod complete." Qingqing pushed up her glasses. "In theory, Liu-Liu takes off carrying the answer sheet, flies to the target's airspace, releases the pod, and the answer sheet lands precisely at the designated spot. Estimated margin of error: within three centimeters."

"A three-centimeter error?" A-Xin widened his eyes. "That's basically the distance of 'dropping onto Old Wu's pork bun'!"

"Precisely." Qingqing nodded calmly. "Which is why I've set the target as—the center of Old Wu's desk."

"Why me?" Old Wu screamed.

"Because you're Customer No. 1." Qingqing didn't even look up.

The next day, the school-wide mock exam before the monthly exam, right on schedule.

As the test papers were handed out, A-Xin sat in the first row as the "answer relay station"; Old Wu sat in the fifth row as "Customer No. 1"; outside the window, Liu-Liu, clutching a paper full of answers, was already in position on the eave.

"Everyone!" A-Xin recited silently in his head. "Everything hinges on this! With three routes of airdrops, at least one has to succeed!"

"Chirp—"

Route one, the paper plane, took off.

Wang Zhuankai tossed his painstakingly folded "cool fighter" diagonally backward. The paper plane, dragging a crooked arc, wobbled toward Old Wu—

"Slap."

Two fingers caught the paper plane's wing midair, steady as can be.

Teacher Gao Zhixian stood on the podium, a golden ring appearing in his pupils behind his lenses, his tone as flat as a weather report:

"Wang Zhuankai, the flight parameters of this paper plane of yours: initial velocity two point one meters per second, elevation angle thirty-one degrees, affected by the airflow from the desk fan on the podium, it would have drifted forty-five degrees off target. Incidentally—even if it had landed successfully, the writing on it isn't the answer you wanted."

The whole class gasped.

"Three-dimensional trajectory tracking system activated." Teacher Gao pushed up his glasses. "Everyone, please don't test your teacher's computational abilities."

A-Xin gritted his teeth: "Never mind! There's still route two!"

Old Wu, trembling, slipped the rubber band over his fingers, aimed at A-Xin's direction, and pulled hard—"whoosh"—the rubber band, carrying a square of folded answer paper, whistled out!

"Slap!"

Caught again.

Without even turning his head, Teacher Gao casually raised his hand and precisely intercepted the paper between his fingers, while the rubber band snapped back and "slapped" Old Wu right on his own forehead, leaving a red mark.

"Mr. Wu," Teacher Gao let out a sigh, "this rubber band of yours has an elasticity modulus of zero point eight and a rebound rate of ninety-three percent. Your chances of hitting yourself are six times higher than your chances of hitting me."

"Ow..." Old Wu held his forehead, eyes brimming with tears.

"There's still the last route!" A-Xin steeled himself and gave the signal. "Liu-Liu! Go!"

Outside the window, Liu-Liu, clutching the chalk box pod full of answer papers, swooped down from the eave like a silver-white little rocket! Its ears pressed flat, its tail carving a graceful arc through the air, aiming straight for Old Wu's desk—

"Liu-Liu Airlines, final sprint!"

"Whoosh—!"

Just as the pod was about to reach its target, Teacher Gao's right hand shot out at a speed that defied human ergonomics, grabbed the pod's suspension line midair, and with his left hand caught Liu-Liu, holding it up in his palm like a caught baseball.

Liu-Liu lay in Teacher Gao's palm, four paws in the air, tail rigid, electronic eyes blank.

"Liu-Liu," Teacher Gao looked down at the little pet in his hand, "according to school rules, passing answers during an exam is a serious violation. Although you're the class's collective pet, I'll still record this on your 'behavior observation log' first."

He pulled a small notebook from his pocket, flipped to the page labeled "Liu-Liu," and meticulously drew a big "X" with his pen.

Liu-Liu's tail drooped limply, as if its power had been cut off.

"Ow... beep..." Liu-Liu let out a pitiful electronic whimper, and A-Xin's eyes reddened with heartache.

"Slap!"

The classroom door was pushed open again, and Director Zhou Yan barged in triumphantly, waving his notebook: "I heard 'whoosh whoosh whoosh' sounds from the hallway! You must be—"

He stopped mid-sentence, seeing the paper planes all over the floor, the rubber band that had snapped into the corner of the wall, and the chalk box pod hanging from Teacher Gao's hand. He froze.

"Uh... what are you doing?"

"Director Zhou," Teacher Gao said calmly, holding up the intercepted paper plane, "our class is conducting a 'flight principles' experiment class. This paper plane is Wang's 'lift model'; this rubber band is Wu's 'catapult launcher'; and this chalk box pod is our class's 'Liu-Liu Airlines' airdrop experiment. The latest Ministry of Education curriculum encourages cross-disciplinary hands-on learning."

"Fl-Flight principles?" Director Zhou stared wide-eyed, glancing left and right, and finally scanned the room suspiciously. "Then why does the paper plane... seem to have writing on it?"

"That's the 'design annotation.'" Teacher Gao said without changing his expression. "For recording the experimental data."

"..." Director Zhou opened his mouth, unable to find any flaw. In the end, he grudgingly snapped his notebook shut. "Hmph... hands-on learning, huh? If you do it well, I'll sign you up for the city-wide science fair!"

"Really?!" A-Xin's eyes lit up instantly.

After Director Zhou left, the whole class collapsed limply into their chairs.

Teacher Gao collected all the paper planes, rubber bands, and chalk box pods neatly into a cardboard box and set it at the corner of the podium:

"Everyone, I'll keep these 'experimental apparatus' safe for now. Starting tomorrow, we formally enter the monthly exam sprint—I hope everyone will put the energy they used 'flying' just now into 'studying.'"

He paused, then added: "Incidentally, in that mock exam just now, the class average improved by twelve points compared to last week."

The whole class was dead silent.

"Up twelve points?!" A-Xin couldn't believe it. "How is that possible? We were all busy passing answers!"

"Because in the process of preparing to 'pass answers,'" Qingqing pushed up her glasses, her tone calm, "you went through all the review key points again."

A-Xin froze.

He looked at the box of confiscated "experimental apparatus" on the podium, then at his own mock exam paper, filled with writing—he'd spent the whole exam directing the deliveries, and yet he realized... he seemed to know how to answer every question?

"Strange..." A-Xin murmured to himself. "Why do I feel like... I didn't even need to pass answers?"

"A-Xin," Old Wu leaned over and asked quietly, "so... do we still cheat?"

A-Xin was silent for three seconds.

Then, he "slapped" the table and stood up, fresh fighting spirit blazing in his eyes:

"Cheat! Of course we cheat! Three routes wiped out is nothing! I declare—Plan No. 3, the Code Signaling Operation!"

Outside the window, Liu-Liu lay sprawled on Teacher Gao's "behavior observation log," wearily flicking its tail and letting out a long electronic sigh:

"Beep... human stubbornness really has no limits..."
