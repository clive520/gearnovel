# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 4: Cough Codes and Morse Gestures

"Everyone! Paper planes are down, rubber bands are down, and Liu-Liu Airlines is down too!"

In the classroom after school, A-Xin stood on his desk, hands on his hips, radiating momentum:

"But just now I realized the most important thing—in the first three battles, we all lost because we were 'carrying things'! As long as you carry something, it can be scanned!"

"So?" Old Wu chewed his pork bun vaguely.

"So this time!" A-Xin "slapped" his palms together. "We don't carry anything! We use 'signals'!"

He turned and drew a dense jumble of symbols on the blackboard:

"Plan No. 3: The Code Signaling Operation! Rules as follows—"

"Touch your left ear, answer A!"
"Touch your right ear, answer B!"
"Touch your nose, answer C!"
"Touch your chin, answer D!"
"Cough N times, that's question N!"

"Which means—" A-Xin gestured excitedly, "you cough twice, telling everyone: question two is in play! Then you touch your left ear once, and everyone knows: the answer to question two is A!"

"Wow—!" Old Wu's eyes went wide as saucers. "Isn't this like kung-fu movie codes?!"

"Of course!" A-Xin said smugly. "This time we're carrying no paper at all. No matter how many times Teacher Gao scans, he can't find anything! Because the answers are hidden between us!"

"From an information-theory standpoint..." Qingqing pushed up her glasses, "this system has one fatal flaw."

"What flaw?" A-Xin asked.

"Coughing is an auditory signal; touching your ear is a visual signal." Qingqing analyzed calmly. "Both signals require the 'transmitter' and the 'receiver' to pay attention at the same time. If a single person misses a look or a sound, the whole message chain breaks."

"So we train!" A-Xin waved his arm. "Everyone! Begin rehearsal!"

And so, an absurd "code-signal drill" unfolded in full swing in Class 6-1's underground classroom.

"Cough!"

A-Xin cleared his throat, then touched his left ear.

"Question two... no, wait!" Old Wu jumped up in a panic. "A-Xin, you coughed once. Is that question one, or question two?"

"Coughing once means question one!" A-Xin sighed helplessly. "Old Wu, has your memory been stuffed with pork buns?"

"But I just heard you cough twice!" Old Wu said aggrievedly.

"That cough was me clearing my throat for real! Not a code!" A-Xin stamped his foot.

"Then... then how are we supposed to tell the difference?" Old Wu wailed.

"A real cough and a code cough have different rhythms!" A-Xin demonstrated. "A code cough has to be short and forceful—'cough'! A real cough drags—'cough-cough-cough'..."

Before he could finish, Wang Zhuankai sneezed for real:

"Hah—choo!"

The whole class "snapped" simultaneously to touch their left ears, right ears, noses, and chins, turning into total chaos.

"Who sneezed! Who! Serious injury!" A-Xin hopped in fury. "A sneeze doesn't count as a code! No sneezing in the exam room!"

"I-I can't help it!" Wang Zhuankai rubbed his nose aggrievedly.

Qingqing quietly wrote a note in the corner and handed it to A-Xin:

"Recommendation: establish a 'signal filter system.' Every action must be repeated twice to count. For example, touching your ear twice means answer B."

"Repeated twice?" A-Xin pondered. "Doesn't that make it slower?"

"But at least it won't be disturbed by sneezes, coughs, or blinks." Qingqing said. "Accuracy matters more than speed."

"Fine! We'll do it your way!" A-Xin made the call. "Everyone, remember! From now on, every code has to be repeated twice!"

One week before the monthly exam, the third mock exam arrived on schedule.

A-Xin sat in the last row as the "switchboard"; Old Wu sat in the first row as the receiver. To avoid exposure, all twenty-five of them (Weixi wasn't participating) wore masks.

The test paper was handed out.

Question one. A-Xin counted silently in his head: "One!" He cleared his throat: "Cough—cough—"

Then, he quickly touched his left ear twice.

Old Wu held his breath, translating in his head: "Coughing twice = question two... touching the left ear twice = answer A!"

He was just about to lower his head and fill in the answer—

"Wu Chenkai."

Teacher Gao Zhixian's voice came down from the podium, flat as a weather report:

"Just now, you blinked three times total, touched your ear twice, and your gaze lingered on A-Xin's seat for six seconds. According to voice waveform analysis, the signal you just received, translated, is—question two, answer A. Correct?"

Twenty-six faces, all under masks, froze at once.

Teacher Gao stepped off the podium and walked to A-Xin, a golden ring in his pupils behind his lenses:

"Lu Lixin, the waveform of your cough just now: frequency four hundred and twenty hertz, duration zero point three seconds, sound pressure eighty-five decibels. This waveform is exactly the same as the cough you used while practicing codes."

He pulled a pen from his pocket and drew a waveform curve on the blackboard:

"Everyone, this is the 'code recognition system.' The sound waves of coughs, the motion of ear-touching, the angle of noses, the curve of chins—every signal gets recorded, compared, and decoded."

"So we..." A-Xin's voice began to tremble.

"Decoding result as follows." Teacher Gao wrote on the blackboard, stroke by stroke:

```
Touch left ear x2 = Answer A
Touch right ear x2 = Answer B
Touch nose x2 = Answer C
Touch chin x2 = Answer D
Cough N times = Question N
```

The whole class, seeing the "code comparison table" on the blackboard, gasped collectively.

Old Wu was so startled he pulled his mask up over his whole face, his voice muffled: "T-Teacher, how did you know we were using this?"

"Because," Teacher Gao put down the chalk, his tone sincere, "when you were rehearsing the codes, I happened to pass by and heard the complete 'intelligence.'"

"—!" A-Xin stood frozen on the spot, his expression as if he'd been struck by lightning.

He never imagined that the code system he'd painstakingly designed had been fully recorded by Teacher Gao the very day of the rehearsal!

"Also," Teacher Gao added the finishing blow, "when you cough while wearing masks, the sound waves get filtered by the fabric, making the audio even clearer—in acoustics, this is called a 'filter.' Next time you want to send codes, I suggest not wearing masks."

"Ow..." A-Xin slumped limply into his chair.

"Bang!"

The classroom door was pushed open again, and Director Zhou Yan rushed in, his face tense:

"What's going on! I heard 'cough cough cough' coming from the classroom! Has the flu broken out?!"

He scanned the room, saw all twenty-six of them (including Weixi) wearing masks, and his face instantly went white: "Everyone... has masks?!"

"Director Zhou," Teacher Gao said calmly, "our class is currently conducting 'Chinese recitation vocal training.' The coughing is the practice of pronouncing the consonant 'k'."

"P-Pronouncing the consonant?" Director Zhou stared wide-eyed at Teacher Gao, then at the group of students with masks and evasive eyes. "'K'?!"

"Please listen," Teacher Gao demonstrated, "k—k—this is the initial consonant of the word 'ke' (lesson). The students are doing a collective vocal exercise."

The whole class, in perfect unison, chanted: "K—! K—!"

Director Zhou's mouth twitched. He looked at his notebook, and in the end, he shuffled out grumbling: "...You people are crazy!"

After Director Zhou left, Teacher Gao erased the "code comparison table" from the blackboard and wrote a few large words in its place:

"Everyone, the code system failed, but it actually has value—because it proves you've learned 'classification and organization.'"

He pointed at the blackboard: "Touch left ear for A, touch right ear for B... this is actually a memory method of 'classifying options.' Instead of using codes to pass answers, why not use this classification method to organize your review points?"

"Teacher," Old Wu raised his hand, "so this code table... is it still useful?"

"It is." Teacher Gao smiled faintly, handing each student a printed "key-point classification memory card." "Starting today, this is our review mnemonic. Touch your left ear to remember the answer to A; touch your right ear to remember the answer to B. On the exam, just touch your own ear, and you'll recall the answer."

"Touch my own ear..." Old Wu half-believing, half-doubting, touched his left ear. "Huh? Can I actually recall it this way?"

"The key is 'remembering,' not 'touching.'" Teacher Gao said.

After school, the emergency meeting of the Exam Mutual Aid Society was as heavy as a funeral.

"Everyone..." A-Xin clutched his head, groaning in pain, slumped over his desk. "We lost... completely lost... all three of our battle plans are dead..."

"A-Xin," Qingqing spoke, "have you noticed something? Every time we prepare to cheat, our grades improve anyway?"

"That's just a coincidence!" A-Xin jumped up. "A coincidence! You understand?"

"Then what about this time?" Qingqing asked. "In this mock exam, do you think you passed?"

A-Xin opened his mouth, but found he couldn't answer at all.

He thought back. In that mock exam, although he'd spent the whole time directing the codes, those questions... he seemed to understand all of them?

"Th-This isn't right!" A-Xin grabbed his hair. "I was thinking about codes the whole time, so how could I understand the questions?"

"Because while you were thinking about codes," Qingqing delivered the finishing blow calmly, "you read the questions too."

"..." A-Xin was silent for a full ten seconds.

Then, he abruptly stood up, clenched his fists, fighting spirit blazing in his eyes:

"Since human cheating gets caught, we'll use—technology! Plan No. 4: The Cheating Technology Revolution, officially launched!"

"But A-Xin..." Old Wu weakly raised his hand. "The monthly exam... is in two days."

A-Xin's movements froze.

He stiffly turned his head and looked at the calendar on the wall—in two days, the monthly exam!

"...Let's set technology aside for now." A-Xin took a deep breath, his tone suddenly solemn. "Since there's no time, we'll have to use the final trump card!"

"What trump card?" Old Wu asked nervously.

A-Xin smiled mysteriously, leaning in close to the class, his voice low:

"The human puzzle. Twenty-six people, each memorizing one piece. On exam day, we piece together the complete answer!"
