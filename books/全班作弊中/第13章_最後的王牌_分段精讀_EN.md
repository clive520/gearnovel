# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 13: The Final Trump Card: Segmented Intensive Reading

"Everyone! All twenty-eight tools are gone, but—we still have one final weapon that can never be confiscated!"

In the classroom of Class 6-1 after school, A-Xin stood on his desk, pointing at his temple, eyes blazing:

"—our brains!"

"Brains?" Old Wu chewed his pork bun, full of question marks. "How do you cheat with brains?"

"Write the answers directly 'into the brain'!" A-Xin gestured excitedly. "Plan No. 8: The Ultimate Human Puzzle! Twenty-six people, one unit each, memorize the entire final exam question bank! During the exam, we're twenty-six 'living dictionaries'!"

"Twenty-six living dictionaries..." Qingqing pushed up her glasses. "It sounds reasonable, but something feels off."

"What's off?" A-Xin asked.

"The 'living dictionaries' are memorized," Qingqing said, "but during the exam, how do you 'piece them together'? We can't each write one question and then swap papers, right?"

"Swapping papers gets confiscated!" A-Xin said firmly. "So we'll use—'telepathy'!"

"Telepathy?" The whole class cried out in unison.

"Yes!" A-Xin leaned in mysteriously and lowered his voice. "When you come across a question you don't know, use telepathy to ask the classmate in charge!"

"..." Qingqing was silent for two seconds. "A-Xin, according to neuroscience, telepathy—doesn't exist."

"Then we'll use lip-reading!" A-Xin refused to give up.

"Teacher Gao has voice waveform analysis." Qingqing replied coldly.

"Then we'll use brainwaves!" A-Xin jumped up.

"..." Qingqing sighed. "A-Xin, why don't you first memorize 'the unit you're responsible for'?"

"Fine!" A-Xin clenched his fist. "Whatever! We'll memorize first! Once memorized, we'll naturally find a way to piece things together!"

And so, a "most heroic" segmented intensive reading operation unfolded in Class 6-1 in full swing.

A-Xin divided the final exam scope into twenty-six portions, assigning them one by one:

"Old Wu, you're in charge of social studies' 'Taiwan geography'!"
"Wang Zhuankai, you're in charge of science's 'plants'!"
"Lin Yunyun, you're in charge of Chinese 'Lessons Five to Eight'!"
"I'm in charge of math's 'circle area and circumference'!"

"Why do I have to be in charge of Taiwan geography?" Old Wu wailed. "I hate place names the most!"

"Because your social studies is the worst!" A-Xin said with full justification. "The worst means the most room for improvement!"

"..." Old Wu was crying without tears.

"Weixi," A-Xin turned to the quiet figure in the corner, "you're not participating, but can you be our 'examiner'?"

"Examiner?" Weixi looked up.

"Yes!" A-Xin pressed his palms together. "Your grades are the best. Help us check! See whether we've really memorized our portions!"

"..." Weixi was silent for a moment, then gently nodded. "...Fine. I'll check you all."

"Excellent!" A-Xin rejoiced. "Then we—start memorizing!"

So, over the following days, every single person in Class 6-1 turned into a "memorizing machine."

Old Wu was in charge of "Taiwan geography." He turned the place names into recipes: "Alishan is 'Chicken Mountain'—because drumsticks taste the best! Yushan is 'Meat Mountain'—because meat tastes the best! Sun Moon Lake is 'Drumstick Lake'—because there's a roasted drumstick stand by the lake!"

"Old Wu," Weixi gently quizzed him, "next to Alishan, which mountain is it?"

"It's..." Old Wu thought nervously, then his eyes lit up: "It's Meat Mountain! No wait! It's Yushan! Yushan! Because there's lots of meat on the mountain!"

"...Are you memorizing geography, or memorizing a menu?" Weixi laughed helplessly.

Wang Zhuankai was in charge of "plants." He muttered to the potted plant on the classroom windowsill every day:

"Photosynthesis... requires light... chlorophyll... turns carbon dioxide and water... into oxygen and glucose..."

"Wang Zhuankai," Liu-Liu lay on the windowsill, quizzing him with its newly upgraded "textbook database," "what conditions does photosynthesis require?"

"Light!" Wang Zhuankai said firmly. "And chlorophyll!"

"Very good." Liu-Liu nodded. "Then what if there's no light?"

"Then... there's no photosynthesis." Wang Zhuankai froze for a second. "...Huh? How do I know that?"

"Because you've memorized it many times." Liu-Liu flicked its tail smugly.

Lin Yunyun was in charge of "Chinese." She recited the text even while walking, reciting as she walked, until even her dreams were full of recitation:

"The north wind blows, whoosh whoosh... the leaves rustle, shasha..."

"Yunyun!" Old Wu walked over and patted her shoulder. "At this rate, you'll lose your mind!"

"I won't!" Lin Yunyun's eyes were glassy. "I've memorized Lessons Five through Eight completely! Even the punctuation!"

"Even the punctuation?!" Old Wu marveled.

And A-Xin was in charge of "math."

He turned the circle area formula into a rap: "Pi! Pi! Pi! Circle area! Pi r squared! Circumference! Two pi r! Go around once! Round, round, round!"

"A-Xin," Qingqing listened, then was silent for a moment, "if you take this rap to a street dance competition, you might win an award."

"Really?!" A-Xin rejoiced.

"But if you take it to a math exam," Qingqing added, "the teacher might invite you out for tea."

"..." A-Xin's smile froze.

And so, twenty-six people (Weixi excluded), each memorizing the unit they were responsible for, memorized for an entire week.

The day before the final exam, a "final acceptance ceremony" was held in the classroom of Class 6-1.

Weixi stood on the podium, holding a piece of paper, gently scanning the whole class:

"Everyone, the acceptance check begins. First quiz—Old Wu, in Taiwan's geography, where is the Jianan Plain?"

"The Jianan Plain..." Old Wu swallowed nervously. "It's... in southwestern Taiwan! Because there are lots of rice paddies there, and lots of drumsticks!"

"..." Weixi sighed softly. "...Correct. Next—Wang Zhuankai, plant classification, the difference between 'monocots' and 'dicots'?"

"Monocots... have parallel leaf veins! Dicots... have netted leaf veins!" Wang Zhuankai answered fluently.

"Very good." Weixi nodded. "Next—Lin Yunyun, Lesson Eight of Chinese, what is the last sentence of the text?"

"'As long as we don't give up, spring will surely come.'" Lin Yunyun answered without hesitation, not even missing the punctuation.

"Very good." Weixi turned to A-Xin. "A-Xin, the formula for circle area?"

"Circle area equals pi times radius squared! πr²!" A-Xin patted his chest, full of confidence. "I can do this question with my eyes closed!"

"Very good." Weixi smiled faintly, then suddenly shifted tack. "Then... A-Xin, since you've memorized math, let me ask you—what conditions does science's 'photosynthesis' require?"

"Photosynthesis..." A-Xin froze. "Isn't that Wang Zhuankai's section?"

"Right, it is Wang Zhuankai's section." Weixi said gently. "But you sit next to him and listen to him recite every day, for a whole week—do you think you don't know it?"

"..." A-Xin opened his mouth, then blurted out, "Light! And chlorophyll!"

He froze for a second: "...Huh? How do I know that?"

"Because you listened for a whole week." Weixi laughed softly. "Just like you listen to Old Wu recite 'Alishan, Yushan, Sun Moon Lake' every day—you've long since memorized Taiwan geography too."

"!" A-Xin stared wide-eyed.

"This is what 'piecing together' means." Weixi said gently. "You thought you only knew your own portion. But actually—each of you has taken in the whole class's content."

"..." The whole class instantly fell silent.

"Then... then..." Old Wu stammered, "do we still... need 'telepathy'?"

"No longer." Weixi shook her head gently. "Because the things you should know—you already know all of them."

"—!"

A-Xin stood frozen on the spot, stunned for a long, long time.

He lowered his head, looked at his own hands, then raised it to look at the group of classmates who were both excited and confused.

"...Cheating..." he murmured. "...have we... after all... been cheating?"

"A-Xin," Qingqing walked over, her tone calm, "tomorrow, you only need to do one thing."

"What?"

"Write the exam paper." Qingqing said.

"..." A-Xin was silent for a second, then abruptly raised his head, fresh fighting spirit blazing in his eyes:

"Fine! Tomorrow, we'll piece the 'human puzzle' to the very end! In the exam room, we'll use 'telepathy' to write out all the answers!"

"..." Qingqing sighed. "...Fine. I wish you—'telepathy success.'"

"You jinx! This time it'll definitely succeed!"

Outside the window, Liu-Liu lay at the ventilation duct, watching this group of "trouble-makers about to succeed without knowing it," its tail swaying gently, letting out a tender electronic chirp:

"Beep beep... detecting... 'tomorrow's exam'... seems... like a miracle is about to happen..."
