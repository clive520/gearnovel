# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 5: The Perfect Division of the Twenty-Six

On the day of the monthly exam, the sky was as gray as a soaked rag.

In the classroom of Class 6-1, twenty-six desks had been pulled into the standard single-seat exam layout, the surface cleared of books and clutter, leaving only pencil cases, desk pads, and a ruler.

A-Xin sat in the last row. Seizing the moment when Teacher Gao was sorting the exam papers at his desk, he hissed the final orders to Old Wu in the front row:

"Old Wu! Your part is the 'gas exchange' unit in science! Got it?!"

"Got it!" Old Wu nodded nervously. "Gas exchange... gas exchange... I'm even exchanging gases in my dreams!"

"Also, you're in charge of six questions total, remember, six questions! Leave everything else blank, and wait to copy from us!"

"But A-Xin..." Old Wu asked trembling, "what if Teacher Gao won't let us copy?"

"He'll let us copy!" A-Xin said firmly. "Because we have the 'gap tactic'! He's bound to turn his head at some point!"

"Everyone," Teacher Gao Zhixian's voice came from the podium, "before the monthly exam officially begins, allow me to remind you—"

He paused and pushed up his black-rimmed glasses:

"According to the Ministry of Education's exam room regulations, proctors must watch the candidates at all times to ensure fairness. So today, I will stand here, from the start of the exam to the end, and I will not turn my head for a single second."

"He won't turn his head for a single second?!" A-Xin's heart sank with a "thump."

"Yes." Teacher Gao stood still, hands clasped behind his back, like a statue, his gaze sweeping the class like two laser beams. "Please answer with peace of mind."

The exam began.

A-Xin counted silently in his head: "In a moment he's bound to blink, wipe his glasses, or look out the window—as long as there's a one-second gap, we strike!"

He waited ten full minutes.

Teacher Gao stood nailed to the podium like a surveillance camera, not blinking once.

"How is that possible..." sweat beaded on A-Xin's forehead. "How can a human not blink?!"

He decided to manufacture a gap.

"Clatter!" A-Xin shoved his pencil case off the desk with a loud crash. "Oops! Teacher! I dropped my pencil case!"

"Mm." Teacher Gao's gaze didn't move an inch. "Please pick it up and continue answering."

"I-I'll also wipe my tears!" A-Xin crouched down, secretly gesturing to Old Wu to "get ready to move."

Teacher Gao's gaze stuck to A-Xin like glue, utterly motionless.

"Teacher! There's a bird outside the window!" Wang Zhuankai suddenly shouted.

"The bird outside the window," Teacher Gao said without turning his head, "is a gray-faced buzzard, migrating season, flying up from the south. Please continue answering."

"Teacher! The ceiling is dripping!" Lin Yunyun screamed.

"That's condensation," Teacher Gao replied calmly, "caused by the high relative humidity in the classroom, water droplets forming at the air conditioner outlet. Please continue answering."

A-Xin was utterly in despair.

This teacher... was simply a wall in human form! No matter how many gaps A-Xin manufactured, Teacher Gao blocked them all as if he'd calculated them in advance!

"No choice..." A-Xin gritted his teeth and hissed forward, "We'll use the 'final measure' directly! Old Wu! Pass your exam paper to Wang Zhuankai under the desk!"

Old Wu, hands trembling, took advantage of Teacher Gao "pretending" to look at the papers on the podium (while actually watching them all along)—

"Slap."

"Mr. Wu," Teacher Gao's voice came from the front, "you stuffed your exam paper into your backpack, then tried to pass it to Wang under the desk—this is my third reminder. According to exam room regulations, all movement below the desk surface is treated as cheating."

Old Wu froze mid-motion, the paper still stuck in his backpack, standing like a wax figure.

"Old Wu! Put it back quickly!" A-Xin hissed.

"I-I'm stuck!" Old Wu wailed. "The backpack zipper is caught on the paper!"

"You!" A-Xin was nearly spitting blood.

And so the whole exam descended into chaos. When the collection bell rang, the exam papers of all twenty-six students were covered in large stretches of blank space.

"Everyone," Teacher Gao gathered the papers, his tone even, "tomorrow is math, the day after is science, and the day after that is social studies. I hope everyone adjusts their mindset and answers well."

The whole class collapsed into their chairs, eyes dead.

"We're doomed..." Old Wu muttered. "This monthly exam... I'm finished..."

"It's not over yet!" A-Xin abruptly stood up, the flame of defiance burning in his eyes. "Tomorrow's math! The day after's science! We still have a chance! We still have the final trump card of the human puzzle—'mutual assistance, mutual answers'!"

"But A-Xin," Qingqing spoke calmly, "do you think, in front of a teacher who doesn't even blink his eyes, we have any chance of 'mutual assistance'?"

"..." A-Xin fell silent.

The next three days went exactly the same way.

For math, A-Xin's carefully planned "segmented answering"—everyone writing only the questions they were assigned and leaving the rest blank to be "pieced together"—fell apart because Teacher Gao's all-round supervision left no gaps to piece anything together. Half of the papers the class submitted were blank.

For science, Old Wu had memorized his six "gas exchange" questions inside and out, but he didn't write a single other question. After the exam, he wept to A-Xin: "A-Xin, I know gas exchange, but the paper asked about photosynthesis!"

"Then if you don't know photosynthesis, why didn't you write it? You've studied it before!" A-Xin stamped his foot in fury.

"Because it's not my section!" Old Wu burst into tears. "My section is gas exchange! Everything else is yours!"

"You!" A-Xin's hair was practically standing on end.

For social studies, A-Xin divided the class into four groups, respectively in charge of "Taiwan's geography," "Taiwan's history," "Taiwan's culture," and "Taiwan's customs," planning "intra-group mutual supplementation" during the exam. As a result, because of Teacher Gao's surveillance, not even a single meaningful glance could pass between the groups.

The day the monthly exam ended, all twenty-six students drifted into the classroom like twenty-six zombies.

"Everyone..." A-Xin slumped into his chair, his voice weak. "We... have completely failed..."

"A-Xin," Qingqing suddenly spoke, her tone unusually calm, "have you noticed something?"

"What?"

"The questions each of us was responsible for," Qingqing said, "didn't we all get them right?"

A-Xin was taken aback: "How do you know?"

"Because I looked." Qingqing pushed up her glasses. "Your 'gas exchange'—"

"That was Old Wu's!"

"Your 'Chinese new words,' your 'math geometry,'" Qingqing delivered the finishing blow, "you got all of them right."

A-Xin opened his mouth but couldn't say a word.

Just then, Teacher Gao walked in carrying a stack of notebooks.

"Everyone," he set the notebooks on the podium, "the grading results are out. Before I announce them, I'd like to show you something first."

He turned and drew a dense web of lines on the blackboard:

"This is the result of comparing all twenty-six of your exam papers using the 'homework similarity algorithm.'"

"Every line in this diagram represents two people who had an identical segment of answers."

The whole class gasped.

"After comparison," Teacher Gao pointed at the blackboard, "across all twenty-six exam papers, thirty-seven pairs of 'completely identical answers' were found."

"And of those—" he paused, "twenty-three pairs are wrong answers."

"Wrong?!" A-Xin widened his eyes.

"Yes." Teacher Gao said calmly. "Because the parts you 'copied from each other' were copying each other's 'half-baked' answers. Whoever remembered their section well had high accuracy; whoever only remembered half had high error rates."

He picked up Old Wu's paper and pointed at the red marks:

"Mr. Wu, your six 'gas exchange' questions—all correct. But the 'photosynthesis' and 'plant classification' you copied—all wrong. Moreover, your wrong answers are wrong in exactly the same way as twelve other students in the class."

"Twelve people wrong the same way?!" Old Wu exclaimed.

"Because you copied from the same person." Teacher Gao smiled faintly. "Wang Zhuankai remembered 'photosynthesis' as 'plants take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen'—"

"Right!" Wang Zhuankai puffed out his chest smugly.

"—but you missed the condition 'under light.'" Teacher Gao added. "So thirteen people copied the wrong answer 'no light needed' onto their papers together."

A chorus of agonized wails rippled through the classroom.

"So," Teacher Gao put down the paper, his tone sincere, "in this monthly exam, the whole class's scores are exactly equal to the scores of 'the parts each person remembered themselves.' If you had treated the entire exam as the 'section you were responsible for' and written it—"

He swept his gaze across the class and smiled faintly:

"—this monthly exam, the whole class would already have passed."

The classroom was utterly silent.

A-Xin sat in the last row, looking at the red "similarity web" on the blackboard, looking at all those marks of "wrong in exactly the same way," and for the first time, he couldn't produce any grand declaration.

He lowered his head and looked at his own paper covered in red marks—the parts he'd written himself were all correct; the blanks waiting to be "pieced together" were all wrong.

"...Is cheating really more useful than studying?" A-Xin muttered to himself.

Outside the window, Liu-Liu lay at the ventilation duct, watching this group of crestfallen trouble-makers, its tail swaying gently, letting out an electronic sound that was part sympathy, part mockery:

"Beep beep... human 'division of labor'... really should be used in the right place..."

Far away, Director Zhou Yan stood by the office window, clutching the infraction notebook, a cold smile slowly curling at the corner of his mouth:

"The 'homework similarity algorithm,' huh... hmph, Class 6-1, you don't think this is over, do you?"

He flipped open his notebook and, beneath the words "Keep a close watch," added another line:

"Prepare for the monthly exam make-up test."
