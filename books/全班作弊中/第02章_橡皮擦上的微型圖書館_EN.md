# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 2: The Miniature Library on an Eraser

After school, the curtains in the classroom of Class 6-1 were drawn tight, and half the lights were turned off.

Twenty-six contact notebooks were piled into a small mountain on the desks in the back row. A-Xin stood on top of the desk, chalk in hand, scanning the room like a commander:

"Everyone! You all saw yesterday's scan! Teacher Gao's pair of eyes can see even a paper note hidden in the sole of your shoe!"

"So... so should we still cheat?" Old Wu raised his hand, his voice trembling.

"Cheat! Of course we'll cheat!" The fighting spirit blazed in A-Xin's eyes. "Precisely because he's so strong, we have to be stronger! Yesterday he scanned out twenty-three 'backup materials'—that's because everyone prepared too crudely!"

He turned and wrote several large words on the blackboard:

**"Plan No. 1: Miniature Library"**

"Everyone! Traditional cheat sheets are big and easy to expose! So we have to concentrate, concentrate, concentrate—concentrate the material until it's 'hidden anywhere and looks like nothing'! Even if Teacher Gao uses his X-ray vision, he won't find it!"

"But A-Xin..." Qingqing pushed up her glasses, her tone as calm as a weather report, "you saw with your own eyes yesterday that Teacher Gao can scan even the blurred pencil words on an eraser. Do you really think 'making it smaller' will work?"

"Qingqing, that's where you don't get it!" A-Xin put his hands on his hips. "Yesterday was 'scattered individual preparation'; today is 'whole-class systematic warfare'! If we can't win fighting solo, we'll win fighting as a team!"

"From a statistical standpoint..." Qingqing began.

"Shut up! This time, statistics listens to me!" A-Xin snatched the conversation.

And so, the first systematic operation of the Exam Mutual Aid Society officially began.

A-Xin divided the whole class of twenty-six into four groups, each claiming one subject:

"Chinese group—condense each lesson's new-word annotations into three lines!"
"Math group—shrink all formulas and unit conversions to the size of a business card!"
"Science group—use code names for experiments and key points!"
"Social group—condense all the place names, dates, and people!"

"Reporting!" Old Wu raised his hand. "After we condense them, where do we put them?"

"Now that's the right question!" A-Xin smiled mysteriously. "Everyone—take a look!"

He pulled a pile of odds and ends out of his backpack: erasers, pencils, rulers, desk pads, mechanical-lead boxes, correction tape, a bottle of nail polish... and each one had a label stuck on it.

"This is our class's 'Miniature Library'!" A-Xin announced proudly. "Eraser = Math Library! Desk pad = Science Library! Pencil case = Chinese Library! Palm = Social Library! Every book is hidden in the least conspicuous place!"

"But A-Xin," Lin Yunyun timidly raised her hand, "who can possibly write letters that small?"

"That's what our tech director is for!"

A-Xin "slapped" an engineering pen with a needle-fine tip onto Qingqing's desk:

"Qingqing! Handing the baton to you!"

"I refuse." Qingqing said flatly. "I only research, I don't use."

"Who said you'd use it?" A-Xin widened his eyes. "I mean you 'make' it! Making isn't using! That's a scientist's sacred duty!"

"...Your twisted logic somehow sounds kind of reasonable." Qingqing sighed, and in the end, she picked up the engineering pen, set up the magnifier, put on her grandmother's reading glasses, and began carving ant-sized characters onto erasers under the lens.

"Wow!" Old Wu leaned in to look, then stumbled backward. "Qingqing! Your writing is smaller than my dad's signature!"

"That's the point." Qingqing didn't look up. "Small enough that Teacher Gao's X-ray vision will overlook its existence."

"Good! Very good!" A-Xin nodded with satisfaction. "Everyone, let's assign roles! Old Wu, you're 'Customer No. 1'—tomorrow's science quiz, you test it first!"

"M-Me first?" Old Wu stuffed a whole pork bun into his mouth. "Why me?"

"Because you need it the most!" A-Xin patted his shoulder. "Pass, and the drumsticks are yours!"

Old Wu's eyes shifted from fear to a kind of heroic resolve: "...Fine! For the drumsticks! I'll do it!"

At ten o'clock that night, the lights in the classroom of Class 6-1 finally went out.

In the darkness, Liu-Liu poked its head out of the ventilation duct, its electronic eyes sweeping over the table full of "Miniature Library" items, letting out a worried electronic beep:

"Beep beep... detecting a large quantity of high-risk items... according to historical data, the average survival time of this type of item: one morning."

The next morning, before the bell for the science quiz, Old Wu gripped the eraser covered with formulas, sweat beading on his forehead.

"Old Wu, the science quiz is all multiple choice. You just need to remember three key points—photosynthesis! gas exchange! plant classification!" A-Xin whispered urgently.

"But A-Xin, I'm nervous..." Old Wu's hand was shaking.

"Nervous about what! You're the man who's getting his drumsticks back!" A-Xin patted him firmly, then quickly returned to his own seat.

The quiz began.

As the test papers were handed out, Old Wu picked up his pen with trembling hands and secretly glanced at the eraser on his desk.

—The formulas were all right there, clear as day!

He was just about to lower his head and copy them down—

"Wu Chenkai."

Teacher Gao Zhixian's voice exploded like a clap of thunder directly above Old Wu's head.

Old Wu froze on the spot. The eraser "slapped" out of his hand, rolled to the front of the podium, and stopped precisely at the toe of Teacher Gao's leather shoe.

"It seems..." Teacher Gao bent down, elegantly picking up the eraser with two fingers and examining it against the light, "the 'Math Library' of this 'Miniature Library' has opened for business today?"

The faces of the entire class turned white in an instant.

"And—" Teacher Gao's gaze swept across the class, a golden ring appearing in his pupils behind the lenses, "the Chinese Library, the Science Library, the Social Library... as well as the 'palm book vaults' in your hands—they've all opened for business too, haven't they?"

He closed his eyes, then opened them again:

"Mr. Wu, math formulas on the left side of the eraser; Wang Zhuankai, three characters written in correction fluid on the pinky nail of your left hand; Lin Yunyun, the paper in your pencil case, science key points—and, everyone in the class is carrying traces of the same special ink on their hands."

Qingqing's pen "clicked" and fell to the floor.

"That ink is blended from a special ratio of engineering-pen core." Teacher Gao slowly stepped off the podium, walked to Qingqing's desk, lowered his head, the light behind his lenses falling straight on her. "There is exactly one person in this whole school who writes with that ratio of ink. Lin Yunqing—can you explain why the hands of the entire class all carry your 'handwriting'?"

Qingqing opened her mouth, then finally closed her eyes, her tone as calm as if she were handing in homework:

"Reporting to the teacher. I only make them. I don't use them."

"Very good." Teacher Gao nodded. "Then let's talk about the 'making' part."

Just as the whole class thought they were all going to go down together—

"Bang!"

The classroom door was pushed open, and Director Zhou Yan strode in, his hawk-sharp gaze sweeping the room:

"I just saw this kid sneakily staring at his eraser from the hallway! Caught red-handed! I've already got my infraction notebook ready!"

He smugly waved his notebook—only to see all twenty-six students looking at him with expressions that clearly said "you came too late."

Director Zhou froze: "...What's going on?"

"Director Zhou." Teacher Gao turned, his expression calm. "Mr. Wu was just performing an 'honesty exercise'—reciting the formulas on his eraser to confirm he truly remembered them. This is the 'active memorization method' promoted by the Ministry of Education."

"Act-Active memorization method?"

"Look," Teacher Gao held the eraser up in front of Director Zhou, "written here are Mr. Wu's own organized key points. Not only can he recite them, he recites them perfectly. This is a textbook demonstration of 'organizing key points and memorizing through repetition.'"

Director Zhou stared wide-eyed, glancing left at the eraser, right at the sweat-soaked Old Wu, then back at the imperturbable Gao in the middle. Finally, he "snapped" his notebook shut:

"Hmph... consider yourselves lucky! But remember—if I catch you on the monthly exam, it won't be as simple as reciting an eraser!"

He stormed off, slamming the door.

No sooner had Director Zhou left than the classroom let out a collective sigh of relief. Old Wu collapsed into his chair, looking as if every last drop of strength had been drained from him.

"Everyone," Teacher Gao returned to the podium, his tone unexpectedly gentle, "that 'Miniature Library' was very thoughtfully designed. The eraser, the desk pad, the fingernail—every hiding place was clever. If you could use this ability in the right place, it would actually be a wonderful piece of 'key-point organization.'"

He stepped down from the podium and laid the confiscated eraser, desk pad, and pencil-case paper in a neat row on the blackboard:

"So these 'hidden books,' starting today, are officially donated to the whole class—posted on the bulletin board beside the blackboard, becoming our 'review zone.' Any student may look at them anytime."

"Do-Donated?" A-Xin's mouth fell open.

"This is what the teacher meant by 'used in the right place.'" Teacher Gao smiled faintly, a glint flashing across his lenses. "Mr. Wu, how many questions did you answer correctly on that quiz just now?"

"Uh..." Old Wu looked down at his own quiz paper and froze for a few seconds. "It seems... I got all of them right?"

"Because you memorized the formulas on the eraser." Teacher Gao said. "Remember, once you've memorized it, you don't need to look at the eraser—I said that last week."

The classroom fell silent.

Old Wu stared blankly at his all-correct quiz paper, then at the donated eraser on the bulletin board, and suddenly discovered something very strange:

"Strange... why do I feel like... my math has been getting better for no reason since yesterday?"

"That's your illusion!" A-Xin "slapped" the table and stood up, refusing to accept it. "Everyone! Failing once means nothing! Teacher Gao has X-ray vision, so we'll just stay out of his eyes! I declare—Plan No. 2 starts tomorrow!"

"But A-Xin..." Qingqing slowly added, a hint of a sting in her words, "you just watched the whole class get every quiz answer right using the 'Miniature Library,' with your own eyes. So... why do we still need to cheat?"

A-Xin stood frozen on the spot, opening his mouth, yet unable to answer even a single word.

Outside the window, Liu-Liu poked its head out of the ventilation duct, flicked its tail, and muttered in a tiny electronic voice that only it could hear:

"Beep beep... statistics show: human stubbornness is, indeed, the highest among all species..."
