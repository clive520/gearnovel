# *The Whole Class Is Cheating*

## Chapter 16: The Twenty-Six Who Took the Exam Honestly

On the second day of the final exam, the exam room of Class 6-1 was, as promised, "open."

"Teacher, today... is it open too?" A-Xin raised his hand, his tone full of deep helplessness.

"Open." Teacher Gao Zhixian nodded, his tone flat. "Welcome, everyone—to cheat."

"..." All twenty-six sighed in unison.

"Then... can I not bring my tools?" Old Wu asked weakly. "I brought them all day yesterday, and didn't use a single one."

"You can choose not to bring them." Teacher Gao said. "But 'open' means—you have a choice."

"..." Old Wu thought it over, and in the end, still patted the eraser in his pocket. "...I'll bring it anyway. Better safe than sorry."

"Right! Better safe than sorry!" A-Xin echoed. "Even if we know everything, we still bring the tools! That's a matter of principle!"

"..." Qingqing pushed up her glasses, quietly adding, "Is it 'the principle of cheating'?"

"Qingqing! You shut up!" A-Xin shouted.

The exam paper was handed out.

A-Xin took a deep breath, picked up his pen, and was just about to answer—when he suddenly realized that he wasn't nervous at all.

"Huh?" He froze, looking at the questions on the paper. "Why... am I not nervous at all?"

"Because you know how to write everything." Qingqing said calmly from the side.

"..." A-Xin was silent for a second, then lowered his head and began to answer.

Question one, he knew it. Question two, he knew it. Question three, he knew it...

The more he wrote, the smoother it went, more and more fluid, and before he knew it, he'd written half the paper.

Old Wu, sitting diagonally behind him, wasn't so composed.

Old Wu clutched the formula-covered eraser, his palms soaking wet. He sneaked a glance at Teacher Gao—Teacher Gao was standing in front of the podium, back turned to the class, head bowed as he corrected homework.

"Good opportunity!" Old Wu thought. "If I sneak a peek at the eraser, he probably won't notice..."

With trembling hands, he flipped the eraser over and looked down—

"!" Old Wu's eyes went instantly wide as saucers.

"Old Wu!" A-Xin hissed. "What are you doing?"

"A-Xin! My, my eraser..." Old Wu raised the eraser in shock. "What I wrote here is more detailed than the exam paper!"

"What do you mean?" A-Xin leaned over.

"Look!" Old Wu pointed at the dense rows of characters on the eraser. "My 'photosynthesis' here has 'light,' 'chlorophyll,' 'carbon dioxide,' 'water,' 'oxygen,' 'glucose'—everything! More detailed than the questions on the paper!"

"..." A-Xin looked at Old Wu's eraser, then at Old Wu's exam paper. "Then... shouldn't you know how to write all of it?"

"I..." Old Wu froze. "I... seems like... I really do know how to write all of it?"

"Then why are you peeking at the eraser?" A-Xin asked.

"Because... because I'm used to it!" Old Wu said aggrievedly. "I've been living off this eraser all semester!"

"..." A-Xin was silent for a second. "Old Wu, have you noticed that you've long since memorized what's on this eraser?"

"..." Old Wu lowered his head, looked at the eraser, then raised it to look at his own exam paper, and finally murmured, "Seems like... really... I just wrote it all out just now without even looking at the eraser..."

"This is what's called 'the highest state of the cheat sheet.'" Qingqing added from the side. "When you made the cheat sheet, you copied the textbook from beginning to end—and once you copied it, you stored the textbook in your brain."

"..." Old Wu stared wide-eyed. "So... when I make a cheat sheet... I'm actually making notes?"

"Yes." Qingqing nodded. "And they're—'the finest notes.'"

"..." Old Wu lowered his head, looked at the little eraser in his hand, was silent for a long time, and finally, gently, put the eraser into his pencil case and picked up his pen to answer.

"Old Wu!" A-Xin hissed. "You're not using the eraser?"

"No need." Old Wu shook his head, a kind of indefinable lightness in his tone. "Because... I already know it."

"..." A-Xin watched Old Wu's back and fell silent.

He lowered his head, looked at the pile of "insurance tools" in his pocket, then at his own densely written exam paper—he realized that he too... hadn't used a single one.

"..."

Just then—

"Sssss-crackle—!"

On the podium, a blinding light suddenly burst from Teacher Gao Zhixian's eyes!

"Another projection!" Old Wu was the first to jump up. "Answers! The answers are being projected!"

"Old Wu! Calm down!" A-Xin grabbed Old Wu's hand. "Look!"

On the blackboard, the "standard answers" to the entire exam paper appeared in projection!

"This, this is..." The whole class held their breath.

"The answers... are all on the blackboard..." Wang Zhuankai murmured. "Just look up... and you can see them..."

"..." A-Xin raised his head, looked at the row of clear answers on the blackboard, then at his own densely written exam paper.

Then, he lowered his head and continued writing.

"A-Xin!" Old Wu hissed. "The answers are right in front! Why aren't you looking?!"

"No need to look." A-Xin's tone was unusually calm. "I... know how to write everything."

"You know how to write everything?!" Old Wu stared wide-eyed. "Are you sure?!"

"Sure." A-Xin nodded. "I've already finished writing the whole paper just now."

"—!" Old Wu froze, lowered his head, and looked at his own exam paper. "I... I've finished writing too?"

"..." None of the twenty-six in the class raised their heads to look at the answers on the blackboard.

Lin Yunyun even closed her eyes and continued writing: "I'm not looking! I'm not looking! My own answers are the best answers!"

"Yunyun!" Wang Zhuankai shouted. "If you write with your eyes closed, won't you make mistakes?"

"I won't!" Lin Yunyun said firmly. "The text I memorized is clearer than the answers on the blackboard!"

"..." The pen tips of the whole class rustled across the paper.

No one raised their head.

The row of "standard answers" hung silently on the blackboard for three seconds.

Then, Teacher Gao himself shut it off with a "click."

"My apologies," Teacher Gao pushed up his glasses, his tone flat, "the projection system has malfunctioned again. However—"

He scanned the class and smiled faintly: "it seems everyone... doesn't really need it."

"..." All twenty-six remained silent, but the corners of everyone's mouths involuntarily curved upward.

"Bang!"

The classroom door was pushed open again, and Director Zhou Yan barged in triumphantly, waving his notebook:

"I saw it! I saw it! There were answers on the blackboard just now! It was a projection! You were—"

He stopped mid-sentence, only to find all twenty-six students, each with their head bowed writing their exam papers, not a single one looking up at the blackboard.

"Huh?" Director Zhou froze. "There are answers on the blackboard... why aren't any of you looking?"

"Reporting to the director," A-Xin raised his hand, a kind of indefinable pride in his tone, "we all know how to write, so we don't need to look."

"You all know how to write?!" Director Zhou couldn't believe it. "You trouble-makers, know how to write?!"

"..." All twenty-six raised their heads in unison, looking at Director Zhou, and spoke in one voice:

"Yes."

"—!!!" Director Zhou's mouth twitched, and finally, he grudgingly snapped his notebook shut and stormed out.

"You... you'd better pray that tomorrow's social studies, you know how to write too!"

"Reporting to the director!" Old Wu shouted. "I know how to write social studies too!"

"—!" Director Zhou's footsteps paused for a moment, and finally, he left without looking back.

The collection bell rang.

All twenty-six students handed in twenty-six fully written exam papers.

"Teacher!" Old Wu raised his hand excitedly. "This time, I never looked at the eraser! I wrote everything myself!"

"Very good." Teacher Gao smiled faintly. "Then tomorrow's social studies—"

"Reporting to the teacher!" A-Xin said loudly. "Tomorrow's social studies, we're going to—'write it ourselves'!"

"..." Teacher Gao looked at A-Xin, was silent for a second, then smiled softly. "Very good."

After school, A-Xin stood alone in the empty classroom.

He pulled out the pile of "insurance tools" from his pocket—erasers, slips of paper, invisible pens, the calculator...

"..." He lowered his head, looked at these tools, and murmured, "These... seem like... they'll never be used again..."

He thought it over, and finally, stuffed all these tools back into the deepest corner of his backpack.

"...Tomorrow," he raised his head, looking at the orange-red sky outside the window, his voice light as a feather, "let's pretend... we really did... cheat successfully."

Outside the window, Liu-Liu lay at the ventilation duct, its tail swaying gently, a tender light flickering in its electronic eyes:

"Beep beep... detecting... 'the last day'... is about to arrive..."
