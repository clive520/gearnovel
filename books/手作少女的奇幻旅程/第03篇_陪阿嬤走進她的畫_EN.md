# The Handmade Girl's Fantastic Journey

## Story 3: Walking into Grandma's Painting

There was a sixth-grade girl at Luyang Elementary named Liu Qianyun. There were always traces of paint she couldn't wash off on her fingertips; her backpack was always stuffed with paintbrushes, colored pencils, and half-shaped pieces of clay. Rumor had it, whenever she finished a piece of work poured with genuine emotion, that work would carry her away to another world. And in that world, she would always see certain "hearts" that even time could not carry away.

In December, at Luyang Elementary, the final exams were over.

The sky was gray and gloomy, as if it were about to rain.

After school, Qianyun didn't go straight home.

She slung her backpack over her shoulder and headed toward the back gate of the school.

Outside the back gate stood a bicycle.

On the bicycle sat an old lady with gray hair.

"Grandma!" Qianyun ran over with a smile.

"Oh my, my Qianyun!" Grandma smiled, taking a packet of hot roasted sweet potatoes out of the bicycle basket, "Here, eat them while they're hot!"

"Thank you, Grandma!" Qianyun took the sweet potatoes and held them in her palms, "So hot!"

"Just blow on them a little." Grandma said with a chuckle.

Qianyun took a bite of the sweet potato.

Sweet, soft, and so fragrant.

"Grandma's roasted sweet potatoes are the best!" she said.

"Of course!" Grandma chuckled, "Grandma has been roasting sweet potatoes for sixty years!"

"Sixty years?" Qianyun's eyes went wide.

"That's right!" Grandma said, "Ever since Grandma was a little girl!"

"Hahaha!" Qianyun laughed.

"..." She bit into the sweet potato, looking at Grandma's smiling face, and her heart felt warm.

This was Grandma's habit.

"Grandma," Mom once said to Qianyun on the phone, "Grandma has been... forgetful lately."

"If you have time, go keep her company more often, okay?"

"..." Qianyun said, "Okay."

"And..." Mom's voice grew soft, "The doctor said Grandma's..."

"..." Mom didn't finish.

"Mom?" Qianyun asked, "What did the doctor say?"

"..." Mom was silent for a moment.

"The doctor said," Mom said in a low voice, "Grandma's memories will get fewer and fewer."

"Fewer and fewer?" Qianyun's heart sank.

"Mhm." Mom said, "Like... sand slipping through your fingers, little by little."

"..." Qianyun held the phone, silent.

"So..." Mom said, "we should, while Grandma still remembers..."

"keep her company more." Mom said, "listen to her talk more."

"and..." Mom's voice choked a little, "remember more of the things she talks about."

"..." Qianyun's eyes reddened.

"I understand, Mom." she said softly.

"I will..." she said, "keep Grandma good company."

Every Wednesday, Grandma would ride her bicycle from Luxi Village to Luyang Elementary to pick Qianyun up after school.

"Grandma, today let me give you a ride back!" Qianyun said.

"You know how to ride?" Grandma asked with a smile.

"I do!" Qianyun patted her chest, "I'll give you a ride!"

"Alright, alright," Grandma smiled, her eyes narrowing, "let our Qianyun give me a ride."

The bicycle wobbled along the road to Luxi Village.

The wind was cool.

The roasted sweet potato was warm.

"Grandma," Qianyun asked while pedaling, "how did you know school let out early today?"

"..." Grandma was silent for a moment.

"Ah...

"Grandma?" Qianyun asked again.

"Oh!" Grandma snapped back to reality, "What did you say?"

"I said... how did you know school let out early today?"

"..." Grandma was silent again.

After a moment, Grandma finally said: "I... I forgot."

"Forgot?" Qianyun was startled, "What did you forget?"

"I forgot..." Grandma said in a low voice, "why I'm here."

"..." Qianyun's hands on the handlebars tightened a little.

"It's okay, Grandma!" she said loudly, "You came, so we can go home together!"

"That's right, that's right!" Grandma smiled again, "Let's go home together!"

Qianyun didn't say anything more.

But in her heart, there was a little... sour feeling.

Because this wasn't the first time.

Lately, Grandma had been forgetting things often.

She would forget where she put her keys.

She would forget what day of the week it was.

She would stare at an old photo for a very, very long time.

Last month, Grandma went to the village shop at the entrance to buy soy sauce, and on her way home, she took the wrong road.

She walked all the way to the neighboring village before the shopkeeper found her and brought her home.

"Grandma, how did you end up here?" Mom asked worriedly.

"I..." Grandma looked around, dazed, "I thought... this was our house."

"This isn't our house!" Mom said.

"Oh..." Grandma lowered her head, "I... I forgot again."

At that moment, Qianyun stood at the door, her eyes red.

She remembered that when she was little, Grandma would hold her hand and walk with her through every road in the village.

Grandma always said: "This road, Grandma has walked it for decades; I can find my way even with my eyes closed!"

But now...

Grandma... was about to forget even the road home.

"Grandma..." Qianyun called out softly in her heart.

Sometimes, she would even... mix up names.

"Qianyun..." she would sometimes call her, "A-Tian? You're back?"

A-Tian.

That was Grandpa's name.

Grandpa had been gone for three years now.

Every time she heard Grandma mix up names, Qianyun would pretend she hadn't heard.

Then she would softly answer: "Mhm, I'm back."

"..." Qianyun looked at the gray sky ahead, and her heart felt sour.

Grandma... was she about to forget Grandpa?

About to forget... those warm, warm memories?

That night, Qianyun stayed at Grandma's house.

Grandma's house was an old house built of red brick.

In front of the house stood a huge, huge phoenix tree.

After dinner, Grandma brought out a sewing box.

"Qianyun," she said, "come, let Grandma teach you to embroider."

"Embroider?" Qianyun leaned in curiously.

"Look," Grandma picked up needle and thread, "first, thread the needle."

"Then..." her hands worked lightly on the cloth, "like this... a flower appears."

"Wow!" Qianyun gasped at the little flower Grandma had embroidered, "so pretty!"

"Grandma used to be a seamstress at an embroidery shop." Grandma said.

"I know!" Qianyun said, "You told me!"

"..." Grandma was silent for a moment.

"I told you?" she asked.

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded, "You said that when you were young, you worked at an embroidery shop."

"..." Grandma tilted her head and thought for a long time.

"..." Finally, she smiled, "I forgot."

"..." Qianyun's heart clenched.

But she didn't show it.

"It's okay!" she said with a smile, "Then I'll tell you again!"

"..." Grandma looked at her, smiling.

"Alright, alright." she said, "You tell me."

"Grandma used to be a seamstress at an embroidery shop!" Qianyun said loudly.

"And then..." she thought for a moment, "there was a young man on a bicycle who passed by the embroidery shop door every day!"

"Ring-ring! Ring-ring!"

"..." Grandma listened and smiled.

"And then?" she asked.

"And then..." Qianyun smiled, "he became Grandpa!"

"Hahaha!" Grandma laughed until she couldn't close her mouth.

"You little one..." she shook her head, laughing, "you really can tell stories."

"..." Qianyun looked at Grandma's smiling face, and her heart felt warm.

"Grandma..." she said softly, "when you smile, you look just like in the painting."

"Grandma," after dinner, Qianyun said, "let me wash the dishes!"

"No need, no need!" Grandma waved her hand, "You go watch TV!"

"Let me help!" Qianyun snatched the bowl from Grandma's hands, "You sit down, Grandma!"

"..." Grandma looked at her and smiled.

"Our Qianyun has grown up." she said softly.

After washing the dishes, Qianyun walked into Grandma's room.

The room had an old, camphor-wood smell.

On Grandma's bed were neatly folded quilts, washed until they were pale.

At the head of the bed sat a photo.

It was a photo of Grandpa and Grandma when they were young.

In the photo, Grandpa wore a white shirt, and Grandma wore a floral blouse.

The two of them stood under the phoenix tree, smiling so happily.

"..." Qianyun picked up the photo and looked at it for a long time.

"Grandpa..." she said in a low voice, "do you... miss Grandma too?"

She gently put the photo back.

Then she saw it.

Under the bed, a corner of an old, cloth-wrapped bundle was sticking out.

"What's this?" Qianyun crouched down and dragged the cloth bundle out.

The cloth bundle opened—

It was an old, thick sketchbook.

"A sketchbook?" Qianyun opened it.

On the first page of the sketchbook was a drawing of a phoenix tree.

Under the phoenix tree stood a red-brick house.

"It's Grandma's house!" Qianyun exclaimed, "Grandma drew it!"

She kept flipping.

The second page drew a small stream.

By the stream, a few children were playing in the water.

"Is this Luxi?" Qianyun looked at the drawing, "Grandma, you used to draw?"

She flipped to the third page.

On the third page, a person was drawn.

A young man.

Wearing a white shirt, standing under the phoenix tree, smiling.

"This is..." Qianyun's eyes went wide, "it's Grandpa!"

"Grandma drew... a portrait of Grandpa!"

She kept flipping.

The fourth page.

The fifth page.

The sixth page.

The sketchbook was full of so many drawings.

There were the old houses of the village.

There were the rice paddies in the fields.

There was the stage at the temple square.

There was a whole stretch of phoenix tree in full bloom.

Every page was drawn with such care.

Qianyun looked through them slowly, page by page.

On one page, there was a drawing of the bridge over Luxi.

On the bridge stood a figure with an umbrella.

"Is this Grandma?" Qianyun wondered.

On another page, there was the stage at the temple square.

On the stage stood an actor in costume.

Below the stage were drawn a crowd of tiny figures.

"Is this... during the temple fair?" Qianyun looked at the drawing and smiled softly.

"Grandma..." she said in a low voice, "you used to... keep a diary with your drawings."

Every page of drawing was like a small moment, saved forever.

As if... Grandma was afraid she would forget, so she drew them all down.

"Grandma..." Qianyun touched the sketchbook, "you used to... draw so well..."

"..." Suddenly, she froze.

Because she had flipped to the last page.

On the last page, there was also a drawing.

But it was a drawing... that had not been finished.

In the drawing was Grandma's red-brick house.

In front of the house was that phoenix tree.

Under the phoenix tree were drawn two figures.

The person on the left was already finished.

It was Grandpa.

He stood there in a white shirt, smiling.

But the person on the right—was only half drawn.

The body was drawn.

The hair was drawn.

But the face had not been drawn yet.

"This drawing..." Qianyun said softly, "was never finished."

"Why..." she asked in a low voice, "why wasn't it finished?"

"..." She thought for a moment, then carried the sketchbook out of the room.

"Grandma!" she said, "I found a sketchbook!"

"A sketchbook?" Grandma sat in the rattan chair in the living room, looking at her blankly.

"Look!" Qianyun flipped the sketchbook to the last page, "This drawing... did you draw it?"

"..." Grandma looked at the drawing and fell silent.

For a long, long time.

So long that Qianyun thought Grandma had fallen asleep.

"..." Grandma reached out and gently touched the drawing.

"This is..." her voice trembled slightly, "me and A-Tian..."

"This drawing," Grandma said in a low voice, "is one I drew before we got married."

"Before you got married?" Qianyun asked.

"Mhm." Grandma smiled softly, "At that time, I was still working as a helper at the embroidery shop."

"A-Tian, you see..." she said, "he rode past the embroidery shop door every day."

"He rode his bicycle, ringing the bell."

"Ring-ring, ring-ring—"

"Every time I heard the bell, I knew he was coming."

"..." Qianyun listened, her eyes feeling a little warm.

"I told him," Grandma went on, "that I wanted to draw a picture of the two of us standing under the phoenix tree."

"He said: 'Okay.'"

"He said: 'When it's done, we'll hang it in the most visible place in the house.'"

"But..." Grandma's voice dropped, "before I could finish it..."

"He..." she paused, "he got sick."

"..." Qianyun's heart clenched.

"I planned to wait until he was better, then keep drawing." Grandma said.

"But..." she said in a low voice, "he never got better."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head, her eyes red.

"Later," Grandma said softly, closing the sketchbook, "I put the sketchbook away."

"And... never opened it again."

"..." Qianyun looked at Grandma.

She saw that there was a thin layer of tears in Grandma's eyes.

"Grandma..." Qianyun gently took Grandma's hand, "let me... finish the drawing for you, okay?"

"..." Grandma was stunned.

"Finish it?" she repeated, "finish it..."

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded, "I can draw!"

"I'll finish this drawing for you!"

"I'll draw Grandma..." she said with a smile, "into it too!"

"..." Grandma looked at her and was silent for a long time.

Then Grandma smiled.

"Okay." she said softly, "then... I'll leave it to our Qianyun."

That night, Qianyun brought the sketchbook into her own room.

She spread the last page on the desk.

"This drawing..." she picked up her pencil, "how should I finish it?"

"In the drawing..." she closed her eyes, "is Grandma's red-brick house."

"It's the phoenix tree."

"It's Grandpa."

"And..." she opened her eyes, "Grandma, who hasn't been drawn yet."

"When Grandma was young..." she thought, "what did she look like?"

She remembered that in the photo, Grandma wore a floral blouse.

She wore two braids.

When she smiled, her eyes narrowed into slits.

"Okay..." Qianyun picked up her brush, "let me draw!"

She began to draw.

She drew Grandma's braids.

She drew Grandma's floral blouse.

She drew Grandma's smile.

"..." As she drew, she stopped.

"No." She looked at the drawing, "Grandma... doesn't smile like that."

"In the photo, Grandma is smiling at Grandpa."

"Then..." she closed her eyes, "what expression does Grandma have when she looks at Grandpa?"

She thought about it.

She remembered the light in Grandma's eyes, every time she mentioned Grandpa.

"It's..." she said softly, "a very, very gentle smile."

"Like..." she said, "she's seen the happiest thing in the whole world."

She picked up her brush and kept drawing.

This stroke was drawn very, very slowly.

Because she had to draw that kind of smile—the smile of "seeing the happiest thing in the whole world."

"..." She finished the last stroke and put down her brush.

"Done." She smiled softly.

"Grandma and Grandpa..." she looked at the drawing, "finally... drawn together."

"..." She looked at that drawing for a very, very long time.

"I wonder..." she said in a low voice, "when Grandpa sees this drawing, will he be happy?"

At that very moment—

"Woom—!"

A warm, pale golden light suddenly surged up from the drawing paper!

"This..." Qianyun's eyes went wide, "this is..."

The light gently wrapped around her.

"This drawing..." she murmured, "I... I finished it?"

"..." She lowered her head and looked at the drawing.

"I finished it." She smiled softly.

"I... drew Grandma and Grandpa together."

"So..."

"Swish—!"

The whole world spun before her eyes.

When she came back to her senses—

She found herself standing—

Under a phoenix tree.

A huge, huge phoenix tree.

The tree was covered in fiery red blossoms.

The wind blew gently.

Fiery red petals fell like snowflakes.

"This place is..." Qianyun looked around, stunned.

Behind her stood a red-brick house.

In front of the red-brick house was a small path paved with stones.

Flowers were planted on both sides of the path.

Farther away, there was a sparkling little stream.

By the stream, a few fishing nets were hung out to dry.

The stream water babbled as it flowed.

On the surface of the water, the light of the setting sun was reflected.

A flock of ducks swam around in the stream.

"Quack, quack, quack!"

"..." Qianyun watched the ducks in the stream and smiled.

"Did Grandpa... used to fish here too?" she asked in a low voice.

"He did!" A voice answered.

Qianyun was startled and turned around.

It was the young Grandpa.

He had walked up beside her without her noticing.

"When I was little," the young Grandpa said, smiling at the stream, "I loved fishing here most of all."

"When I caught a fish, I'd take it home and have Grandma cook soup."

"..." Qianyun looked at him, her eyes warm.

"Grandpa..." she said in a low voice, "the fish soup you cooked... I've never had it."

"..." The young Grandpa froze for a moment.

Then he smiled.

"Well then..." he said, "next time you come, Grandpa will cook it for you."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and smiled.

But tears fell.

"Okay." she choked, "it's a promise."

"Here..." Qianyun gently reached out her hand, "it's exactly the same as in the painting..."

"No." She looked at the little flowers at her feet, "it's even more beautiful than the painting."

She looked down at herself.

She was still wearing her school uniform.

"..." She looked up at the fiery red petals dancing all over the sky.

"Here..." she murmured, "is this Grandma's painting?"

"Is this..." she asked in a low voice, "the world where Grandma and Grandpa were, before they got married?"

"Who's there?"

Suddenly, a voice sounded from under the phoenix tree.

Qianyun was startled and turned around.

Under the phoenix tree stood two people.

A young man and a young woman.

The young man wore a white shirt, smiling.

The young woman wore two braids and a floral blouse.

"..." Qianyun's eyes went wide.

"Gr... Grandpa?" she looked at the young man.

"Gr... Grandma?" she looked at the young woman.

"Who are you?" The young man—the young Grandpa—looked at her and smiled, "whose child are you?"

"..." Qianyun was stunned.

"I..." she stammered, "I'm Qianyun."

"Qianyun?" the young Grandma tilted her head, "what a special name."

"..." Qianyun looked at them, and her heart felt warm.

She remembered the story Grandma had told her.

When they were young, Grandpa rode his bicycle past the embroidery shop door every day.

Ring-ring, ring-ring.

"A-Tian!" the young Grandma would always poke her head out of the embroidery shop.

"A-Lan!" the young Grandpa would always stop his bicycle, "let me take you to the stream!"

"..." Qianyun thought of that scene and smiled softly.

"Grandpa..." she said in her heart, "the way you took Grandma to the stream... must have been so handsome."

"..." Qianyun opened her mouth, not knowing how to explain.

How could she say it?

"I'm your granddaughter"?

"I came from many, many years later"?

"..." She thought for a moment, and in the end, she only said: "I... I came to see you two."

"To see us?" the young Grandpa smiled, "Great!"

"Come, come," he beckoned, "sit under the tree!"

"..." Qianyun stepped closer and looked at the young Grandma carefully.

The young Grandma was holding a handkerchief that was half embroidered.

On the handkerchief, a red phoenix flower was embroidered.

"Grandma..." Qianyun asked softly, "are you embroidering... a phoenix flower?"

"Mhm!" The young Grandma's eyes sparkled, "A-Tian said phoenix flowers are his favorite!"

"So..." she looked down at the handkerchief, "I want to embroider a phoenix-flower handkerchief to give him."

"..." Qianyun looked at the handkerchief, her eyes warm.

"Grandma..." she said softly in her heart, "when you were young... you were so gentle."

"..." She smiled softly.

"Phoenix flowers..." she said in a low voice, "must be... the most beautiful flowers in the whole world."

"We were just..." he smiled and glanced at the young Grandma, "thinking about something here."

"Thinking about what?" Qianyun asked.

"..." The young Grandpa and Grandma exchanged a glance.

"I," the young Grandpa said, "want to propose to A-Lan."

"A-Lan?" Qianyun was stunned.

"That's her!" The young Grandpa pointed at the young Grandma, "Chen Guilan!"

"..." The young Grandma blushed and pushed him, "Don't talk nonsense!"

"I'm not talking nonsense!" the young Grandpa said, smiling, "I even drew the picture already!"

"A picture?" Qianyun's heart suddenly skipped a beat.

"That picture!" the young Grandpa said, "the one of the two of us standing under the phoenix tree!"

"..." Qianyun's breath stopped for a moment.

"That picture..." she said in a low voice, "was it... left unfinished halfway?"

"..." Both the young Grandpa and the young Grandma froze.

"How do you know?" the young Grandma asked.

"Because..." Qianyun lowered her head and said softly, "many, many years later, I saw that picture."

"Many, many years later?" the young Grandpa asked.

"..." Qianyun was silent for a moment.

"Mhm." She nodded softly.

"Then..." the young Grandpa looked at her, "many years later, how did we... do?"

"..." Qianyun looked up, her eyes red.

"We did well." she choked, "You two... did very well."

"You got married." she said.

"You had a son." she said.

"You..." she lowered her head, and tears fell, "you spent a very, very long time together."

"..." The young Grandpa and Grandma listened quietly.

"Then..." the young Grandpa asked gently, "many years later... am I still here?"

"..." Qianyun's tears couldn't stop flowing.

"..." She shook her head, then nodded.

"You're here." she choked, "You've always been here."

"You're in Grandma's painting." she said.

"You're in Grandma's photo." she said.

"You..." she cried, "you're in Grandma's heart."

"..." The young Grandpa looked at her quietly.

Then he smiled.

"Then that's good." he said softly.

"That's... good."

"..." The young Grandma stood beside him, her eyes red too.

"A-Tian..." she said in a low voice, "what are you talking about?"

"I'm not talking about anything." The young Grandpa smiled, taking her hand, "I just think... it's wonderful to know you."

"..." The young Grandma blushed and lowered her head.

"..." Qianyun watched them, tears streaming down.

"Grandpa..." she said softly in her heart, "do you know... how much Grandma has missed you since you left?"

"Grandma..." she choked, "she often stares at your photo, lost in thought."

"She often... forgets the way home."

"She often..." she cried, "calls me by the wrong name, calls me 'A-Tian.'"

"..." She looked up at the young Grandpa, "because... the person she misses most is you."

"..." The young Grandpa listened to her quietly.

"..." He gently let go of Grandma's hand and walked to Qianyun.

"Child." He crouched down and looked at her.

"Grandpa..." Qianyun cried, "I miss you... I miss you so, so much..."

"..." The young Grandpa gently touched her head.

"Silly child." he said gently, "Grandpa... has always been here."

"In Grandma's painting."

"In Grandma's photo."

"In every place... you remember."

"..." Qianyun cried and shook her head, "But... Grandma... she's about to forget you..."

"She often..." she choked, "can't remember your face..."

"..." The young Grandpa was silent for a moment.

Then he smiled.

"It's okay." he said.

"It's okay?" Qianyun was stunned, "How can it be okay?"

"Because..." the young Grandpa said softly, "what Grandma forgets is Grandpa's 'face.'"

"But..." he said gently, "what she will never forget is the feeling of 'us being together.'"

"..." Qianyun was stunned.

"Think about it," the young Grandpa said, "doesn't Grandma still cook the dishes Grandpa likes?"

"..." Qianyun thought, "She does."

"Doesn't she still brew a cup of hot tea when it gets cold?"

"She does."

"Doesn't she..." the young Grandpa smiled, "still tell people: 'My A-Tian...'?"

"..." Qianyun's eyes reddened again.

"She does." she choked, "She... she says it all the time."

"Then that's it." The young Grandpa gently patted her shoulder.

"Memories, you see," he said, "sometimes get lost."

"But feelings don't."

"Feelings..." Qianyun repeated, "don't get lost?"

"They don't." The young Grandpa smiled and looked under the phoenix tree.

"Look." he said.

Qianyun followed his gaze.

Under the phoenix tree, the young Grandma was bending over, picking up petals from the ground.

"She..." the young Grandpa said gently, watching the young Grandma, "even if she forgot the whole world, she would never forget... the person she loves."

"..." Qianyun watched the young Grandma.

She saw the young Grandma holding a handful of fiery red petals, smiling.

Smiling so gently.

"..." Qianyun lowered her head, and tears fell, drop by drop, onto the grass.

"Grandpa..." she choked, "then what should I do?"

"What should I do..." she cried, "to help Grandma find those memories back?"

"..." The young Grandpa was silent for a moment.

"Help me..." he said softly, "finish that painting."

"Finish that painting..." he looked at her, "and give it to Grandma."

"Finish it?" Qianyun said, "I... I've already finished it."

"..." The young Grandpa shook his head.

"You finished the painting." he said.

"But..." he said gently, "Grandma hasn't 'seen' it yet."

"Seen it?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." The young Grandpa said, "You have to deliver that painting to Grandma with your own hands."

"You have to let Grandma..." he said, "see with her own eyes—"

"the two of them, her and A-Tian, standing together under the phoenix tree."

"..." Qianyun looked at him.

"Can I..." she choked, "can I really do it?"

"Of course you can." The young Grandpa smiled, "Because you... are our most wonderful child."

"..." Qianyun's tears fell again.

"Grandpa..." she cried, "how did you know... I'm your child?"

"..." The young Grandpa smiled.

"Silly child." he said, "Didn't you... just say it yourself?"

"You said..." he said gently, "you came from many, many years later."

"You said..." he looked at her, "you came to see us."

"You said..." he smiled, "we... did very well."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and smiled.

Tears were still flowing.

But the corners of her mouth were smiling.

"Grandpa..." she said softly, "thank you."

"Thank you..." she choked, "for letting me know that Grandma won't forget you."

"..." The young Grandpa smiled and gently touched her head.

"It's about time." he said.

"Time?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." The young Grandpa looked up at the sky, "the sun... is about to set."

"..." Qianyun followed his gaze.

In the sky, the setting sun was red.

Like a whole, ripe orange.

"The sunset..." she murmured, "is so beautiful."

"Yes." The young Grandpa said, "Grandma loves watching the sunset most of all."

"She says..." he said gently, "the sunset is the gentlest moment of the day."

"..." Qianyun looked at that sunset, her eyes warm.

"Grandpa..." she said in a low voice, "when I go back... I'll finish that painting."

"I will..." she choked, "deliver it to Grandma's hands."

"I will..." she cried, "let Grandma see, with her own eyes, the two of you standing together."

"..." The young Grandpa looked at her quietly.

Then he smiled.

"Good." he said softly.

"..." He reached out his hand, making a "promise" gesture.

"Then..." he said, "we've agreed."

"..." Qianyun looked at his gesture.

She reached out her hand and gently hooked fingers with him.

"We've agreed." she choked.

"..." The young Grandma walked over from under the phoenix tree.

She was holding a handful of fiery red petals.

"Child," she said gently, "these... are for you."

"..." Qianyun took the petals and froze.

"These..." she said in a low voice, "are phoenix blossoms..."

"Mhm." The young Grandma smiled softly.

"Phoenix blossoms are the flowers A-Tian loves most." she said.

"They are also..." she said gently, "the most beautiful flowers in our Luxi Village."

"..." Qianyun held those petals, her eyes red.

"Thank you, Grandma." she choked.

"..." The young Grandma looked at her and smiled.

"You and our A-Tian..." she said softly, "are so alike."

"In what way?" Qianyun asked.

"You both have eyes..." the young Grandma said gently, "that can see into people."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and smiled.

"Woom—!"

The warm light surged up once more.

"..." Qianyun held the petals and looked at the two people under the phoenix tree.

"Grandpa..." she choked, "Grandma..."

"Goodbye."

"..." The young Grandpa and Grandma waved to her together.

"Go back!" the young Grandpa called out with a smile, "Finish the painting!"

"Go back..." the young Grandma said gently, "and draw well!"

"..." Qianyun closed her eyes.

"I will." she said softly.

"I will... draw well."

"..." When she opened her eyes again—

She found herself lying on the desk.

The sketchbook lay open in front of her.

That drawing lay quietly on the last page.

Outside the window, the sky had already brightened.

It was dawn.

"..." Qianyun looked at that drawing.

In the drawing, under the phoenix tree, stood two people.

Grandpa, smiling.

Grandma, looking at him gently.

"..." Qianyun reached out and gently touched the people in the drawing.

"Grandpa..." she said in a low voice, "I'm back."

"I..." she choked, "I finished the drawing."

She took a deep breath and stood up.

"Grandma!" she ran out of the room, "Grandma!"

"What's the matter?" Grandma was sitting in the living room, shelling peanuts.

"Look!" Qianyun spread the sketchbook in front of Grandma, "I finished the drawing!"

"..." Grandma looked at that drawing and froze.

Under the phoenix tree stood two people.

A-Tian on the left, smiling.

On the right... it was herself.

A young her.

Wearing braids.

Wearing a floral blouse.

Looking at A-Tian gently.

"This..." Grandma's lips trembled, "this is me..."

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded, "I drew it!"

"I drew..." she said softly, "the smile Grandma has when she looks at Grandpa—that look of 'seeing the happiest thing in the whole world.'"

"..." Grandma's eyes reddened instantly.

"You..." she touched the person in the drawing, trembling, "how did you know..."

"..." Qianyun looked at Grandma and smiled.

"Because..." she said softly, "every time Grandma mentions Grandpa, that's the expression you have."

"..." Grandma's tears fell.

"..." She lowered her head and looked at that drawing for a very, very long time.

"A-Tian..." she choked, "look..."

"Do... do you see?"

"..." She gently hugged the sketchbook to her chest.

"We..." she cried, then smiled, "we're finally... drawn together."

"..." Qianyun stood beside her, her eyes red too.

"Grandma..." she said in a low voice, "Grandpa said..."

"Grandpa said..." she choked, "he will always be by our side."

"..." Grandma looked up at her.

"You..." she asked, trembling, "you saw A-Tian?"

"..." Qianyun was silent for a moment.

"Mhm." She smiled softly, "I dreamed of Grandpa."

"In the dream..." she said, "Grandpa was still the same, smiling."

"He wore a white shirt and stood under the phoenix tree." she said.

"He told me..." she choked, "to draw well."

"He wanted me..." she said softly, "to finish the painting and give it to Grandma."

"..." Grandma listened, and her tears couldn't stop flowing.

"..." She lowered her head and looked at the sketchbook in her arms.

"A-Tian..." she said in a low voice, "you silly man..."

"I can draw it myself too..."

"Why..." she cried, "why didn't you come back and draw it yourself?"

"..." Qianyun crouched in front of Grandma and gently took her hand.

"Grandma..." she said gently, "Grandpa can't come back."

"But..." she said, "he has always been in your heart."

"He has always been..." she said softly, "in the places where you remember him."

"..." Grandma was silent for a long time.

"..." She lowered her head, and tears fell, drop by drop, onto the sketchbook.

"I know." she choked.

"I know..." she cried, "he's always been there."

"I've... always known."

"..." Qianyun reached out and gently hugged Grandma.

"Grandma..." she said in a low voice, "let's... hang this painting up, okay?"

"Hang it up?" Grandma looked up.

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded, "Hang it in the living room, in the most visible place!"

"Just like Grandpa said..." she said softly, "when it's done, hang it in the most visible place in the house."

"..." Grandma froze.

"You..." she trembled, "how do you know... he said that?"

"..." Qianyun smiled.

"Because..." she said, "Grandpa told me in my dream."

"..." Grandma looked at her.

Looking and looking, Grandma smiled.

"Okay." she said softly, "let's... hang it up."

That morning, Qianyun and Grandma framed the painting together.

Then they hung it on the most visible wall in the living room.

"..." Grandma stepped back two steps and looked at the painting.

"A-Tian..." she said in a low voice, "look... our house is so pretty."

"..." She smiled.

"The phoenix tree..." she said softly, "is blooming."

"..." Qianyun stood beside Grandma, her eyes warm.

"Grandma..." she said softly, "from now on, I'll come every week to draw with you, okay?"

"Draw with me?" Grandma turned around.

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded, "I can draw all sorts of things!"

"I can draw..." she thought for a moment, "the phoenix tree!"

"I can draw..." she said, "Luxi!"

"I can draw..." she smiled, "Grandma's house!"

"..." Grandma looked at her, smiling.

"Okay." she said softly, "then... we've agreed."

"We've agreed!" Qianyun held out her hand, "Pinky promise!"

"Pinky promise!" Grandma held out her hand and hooked pinkies with her.

"..." Qianyun looked at Grandma and smiled.

Grandma looked at her and smiled too.

At that moment, sunlight streamed in through the window.

It fell on the painting on the wall.

In the painting, under the phoenix tree, the two people were smiling so happily.

In the days that followed, Qianyun went to Grandma's house every Wednesday.

She drew with Grandma.

She helped Grandma organize old photos.

She listened to Grandma tell stories about Grandpa.

Once, Qianyun asked: "Grandma, how did you learn to draw?"

"Me..." Grandma thought for a moment, "when I was little, a painter lived next door."

"Every day, I'd crouch by the wall and watch him paint."

"Watching and watching..." she smiled, "I learned it."

"Wow!" Qianyun said, "Grandma is amazing!"

"No, no!" Grandma waved her hand, "just drawing for fun."

"But..." Qianyun looked at the painting on the wall, "the phoenix tree you drew is so beautiful."

"Is it?" Grandma looked at the painting and smiled.

"It's beautiful." she said softly, "because... it's my favorite tree."

"Because..." she said, "A-Tian loved waiting for me under the tree most of all."

"..." Qianyun listened, her eyes warm.

"Grandma..." she said in a low voice, "you and Grandpa... were so happy."

"..." Grandma smiled.

"Yes." she said softly, "we... were very happy."

"..." Qianyun looked at the painting on the wall and smiled.

"Then..." she said, "I want to be like Grandma, and cherish the people around me."

"..." Grandma looked at her, smiling, and gently touched her head.

"Our Qianyun..." she said gently, "will surely be very happy too."

"Grandma," Qianyun asked once, "do you still... forget what Grandpa looks like?"

"..." Grandma thought for a moment, then smiled.

"No more." she said.

"Why?" Qianyun asked.

"Because..." Grandma pointed at the painting on the wall, "he's right there."

"..." Qianyun followed her hand.

In the painting on the wall, under the phoenix tree, Grandpa stood, smiling.

"..." Qianyun lowered her head, her eyes red.

"Mhm." She smiled softly, "Grandpa... has always been here."

"..." She looked at that painting and said softly in her heart: "Grandpa, thank you."

"Thank you, for letting me know..."

"Memories can get lost."

"But feelings... can't."

Grandma would still occasionally forget things after that.

She would still forget where she put her keys.

She would still forget what day of the week it was.

But she never again... forgot Grandpa.

"A-Tian," she would still say, "loves the braised pork I make most of all."

"A-Tian," she would still say, "hates the heat, and sleeps in the yard in summer."

"A-Tian..." she would still say, "has been waiting for me."

"Waiting for me... to finish the painting."

"..." Whenever Qianyun heard this, her eyes would always feel warm.

"Grandma..." she would gently take Grandma's hand.

"I'll draw with you." she said.

"Let's... draw together."

"..." Grandma would look at her, smiling.

"Okay." she would say, "let's... draw together."

The wind blew gently.

The phoenix tree outside the window rustled.

—As if to say: "Yes."

"Feelings..."

"never get lost."
