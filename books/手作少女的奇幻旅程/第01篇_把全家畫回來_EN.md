# The Handmade Girl's Fantastic Journey

## Story 1: Painting the Family Back

There was a sixth-grade girl at Luyang Elementary named Liu Qianyun. There were always traces of paint she couldn't wash off on her fingertips; her backpack was always stuffed with paintbrushes, colored pencils, and half-shaped pieces of clay. Rumor had it she had a secret—whenever she finished a piece of work poured with genuine emotion, that work would carry her away to another world. And that world was where she mended "broken hearts."

In October, at Luyang Elementary, the leaves of the phoenix tree were beginning to turn yellow.

Inside the classroom of Class 6-1, the students huddled together in twos and threes, discussing what booth they should set up for next month's school fair. Only Liu Qianyun sat alone by the window in the corner, quietly drawing.

"Qianyun is drawing again." a girl whispered.

"She never talks to us, does she?" A boy lowered his voice, "Does she think we're boring?"

"I think she's just arrogant!"

"Actually... she hasn't done anything bad." Someone spoke up for her quietly, "She just doesn't talk much."

"Whatever. She's just weird."

Qianyun actually heard all of these words.

She didn't look up; the hand holding her pen paused slightly.

Then she lowered her head and continued drawing.

The sunlight outside the window fell on her drawing paper through the glass.

She was drawing a phoenix tree.

Under the tree, there were three figures.

They were herself, and... her father and mother.

But halfway through, her hand stopped.

"...Forget it." she said in a low voice, "I can't draw it anyway."

The school dismissal bell rang.

The students rushed out noisily like a flock of sparrows released from a cage. Only Qianyun slowly packed her backpack, carefully tucking the half-drawn phoenix tree back into her sketchbook.

"Qianyun!" A voice called from the classroom door.

It was the class president, a girl named A-Cheng.

"What's up?" Qianyun looked up, a little hesitant.

"Have you finished the piece we need to hand in for art class tomorrow?" A-Cheng asked.

"I'm done." Qianyun nodded.

"Then..." A-Cheng paused, as if about to say something, but finally only said, "then... that's good. I'll go first. Bye."

"...Bye." Qianyun said softly.

A-Cheng turned and ran off.

Watching her back, a nameless feeling welled up in Qianyun's heart.

She knew A-Cheng was actually a very kind person. But she didn't know how to talk to others. Rather than say the wrong thing and make the atmosphere awkward, it was better... to stay quietly by herself, drawing.

"Anyway..." she slung her backpack over her shoulder, "drawing is simpler."

Actually, Qianyun didn't not want to play with everyone.

She also wanted, like the other girls, to go to the school store with a group after class, buy a bag of snacks, and share them bite by bite.

She also wanted, like the other boys, to run and jump around on the playground, working up a sweat.

But she didn't know how to ask.

"That... can I join you?"

She had practiced that sentence in her heart a hundred times, a thousand times.

But every time it reached her lips, she swallowed it back.

Because she always felt that everyone definitely wouldn't want to play with her.

"Look, Liu Qianyun is alone again." "Does she think we're too loud?" "She seems to only like drawing."

She had heard these words too many times.

After hearing them enough, she began to believe that she really was just "a person who only likes drawing."

And so, she grew more and more used to being alone.

When she was alone, the world was quiet.

So quiet... that she could hear the sound of her own heartbeat.

So quiet... that she could completely hide herself inside her drawings.

On the way home, Qianyun passed by that phoenix tree at the school gate.

It was already autumn; the flowers on the tree had long since withered, leaving only a canopy of green leaves.

"The flowers bloomed so early this year." she said in a low voice, "Before I could draw them, they'd already fallen."

She stood under the tree and looked up for a very, very long time.

Then she lowered her head and gently touched her sketchbook.

"If only..." she murmured, "the flowers could bloom forever."

"Just like..."

She didn't finish.

Because she didn't know how to describe the hollow feeling in her heart.

Qianyun's home was in a small alley not far from the school.

It was an old apartment building, the unit on the right on the third floor.

"I'm home." She opened the door and changed into her slippers.

The living room was empty.

On the dining table was a plate of food wrapped in plastic wrap, and a note.

On the note, in her mother's hurried handwriting, was written:

"Qianyun: Mom has to work overtime today, will be home late. The food is on the table, remember to heat it up. Dad is on a business trip, will be back tomorrow.—Mom"

"..." Qianyun stared at that note for a long, long time.

Silently, she put the food into the microwave, heated it up, and sat alone at the empty dining table, eating.

At the table, there was only her.

The sound of chopsticks clinking against the bowl echoed loudly in the quiet living room.

"...Eating alone isn't so bad, I guess." she told herself, "Anyway, that's how it's always been."

But she couldn't fool herself.

Because she still remembered that before—when she was very, very little—her father and mother would sit together at the dining table, the three of them, around a table of steaming hot food.

Back then, her mother would put food in her bowl, her father would tell funny jokes, and she would laugh until her eyes squinted shut.

Back then, the dining table was full.

Now, the dining table was empty.

Her father was busy with work and often away on business trips.

Her mother was busy with overtime and often came home very late.

When they were home, they were always... arguing.

"Where did this money even go?" "What do you think I want?" "How long has it been since we talked properly?" "You have no idea how tired I am!"

Those words were like little knives, stabbing into Qianyun's heart.

That was an evening a week ago.

Qianyun was drawing in her room when, suddenly, the sound of her parents arguing came from the living room.

"Where did this money even go?" Her father's voice carried anger.

"What do you think I want?" Her mother's voice was urgent and aggrieved, "Our household expenses are so high—if I don't budget carefully, how can we manage?"

"I didn't say to spend money recklessly!"

"I'm not spending recklessly! Count for yourself—how long has it been since you came home for a meal?!"

"I'm busy making money to support this family!"

"Money, money, money! All you know is money! Our Qianyun... how long has it been since she even saw you?"

"..."

The living room suddenly fell silent.

Qianyun, hiding behind her bedroom door, her hand gripping the doorknob, trembled slightly.

"..." She didn't dare go out.

She was afraid that if she went out, her parents would stop arguing.

She was afraid that if she went out, she'd see her parents with red eyes and frazzled looks.

And even more, she was afraid... that they were arguing because of her.

"It's all my fault..." she said in a low voice, "If I were a better kid... would they stop fighting..."

She retreated back into her room, closed the door, and curled up under her blanket.

That night, she cried for a very, very long time.

But after crying, she would still wipe away her tears and pretend nothing had happened.

Because she didn't want to make her parents worry.

She didn't dare say anything, and she didn't know what to say.

She could only, alone, hide in her own room, drawing.

Drawing was the only place where she felt safe.

That night, Qianyun sat at her desk and opened her sketchbook.

She flipped to the page she had started drawing yesterday—that phoenix tree, with three figures beneath it.

She looked at that drawing for a very, very long time.

"Dad, Mom..." she said in a low voice, "when will we be able to smile together, like in this drawing?"

She picked up her pen, but couldn't bring herself to draw.

Because she didn't know what kind of smile to draw.

That was a kind of expression... she could almost no longer remember.

"I can't draw it..." she put down her pen, "because... I've already forgotten what it looks like when the three of us smile together."

She lay down on the desk, burying her face in her arms.

The room was quiet.

Only the wind outside the window stirred the curtains.

After a very, very long time, she lifted her head.

Her eyes were red.

She looked down at the unfinished drawing and, suddenly, made a decision.

"...Alright." she picked up her pen, "Then I'll draw what it looks like when we 'smile together.'"

"Even if... it hasn't happened yet."

"I'll draw 'that day' first."

She began to draw.

She drew very, very slowly.

Each stroke was like her whispering softly to that "ideal home."

She drew the phoenix tree more lush and greener than the one at the school gate.

She drew the sky bluer and clearer than the one outside the window.

She drew her own face happier and brighter than the one in the mirror.

She drew her father as a father who would draw with her and tell jokes.

She drew her mother as a mother who would pat her head and sit at the dining table for a proper meal.

As she drew, tears fell drop by drop.

But her pen didn't stop.

"I'm not drawing a 'perfect home'..." she choked, "because I don't know what a perfect home looks like."

"But..."

"I'll draw the home I 'wish for.'"

"I wish... the three of us could smile together under the phoenix tree."

"I wish..." she gently laid down the final stroke, "that day would come soon."

At that moment, she finished.

She put down her pen and looked at the drawing.

In the drawing, a family of three smiled brighter than the sunlight under the phoenix tree.

Just then—

"Woom—!"

A warm, pale golden light suddenly surged up from the drawing paper!

"This..." Qianyun's eyes went wide, "this is going to..."

She couldn't react in time.

That light gently wrapped around her.

Then—

"Swish!"

The whole world spun before her eyes.

When she came back to her senses, she found herself standing—

Beneath a phoenix tree.

A phoenix tree covered in fiery red blossoms.

The wind blew gently.

Fiery red petals fell like snowflakes.

"This place is..." Qianyun looked around, her voice trembling slightly, "the world I drew..."

This world was exactly like her drawing.

In fact, it was even more real than her drawing.

She looked down at the grass beneath her feet—soft, green, still carrying the scent of morning dew.

She reached out and touched the trunk of the phoenix tree—rough, cool, with a few tiny ants crawling on it.

"Even ants..." Qianyun marveled, "exist here?"

"Of course!" A crisp voice sounded.

Qianyun was startled and looked up—

It was a round little bird, perched on the branch in front of her, tilting its head as it looked at her.

"You..." Qianyun's eyes went wide, "you, you can talk?!"

"I'm something you drew!" The little bird flapped its wings matter-of-factly, "When you drew me, weren't you thinking you wanted me to keep you company?"

"I..." Qianyun was stunned.

She thought about it. And... it seemed like that was true.

When she drew this picture, she felt the scene under the tree was too quiet, so she casually drew a little bird.

"Everything you drew," the little bird circled above her head, "is alive here!"

"Look!" the little bird called toward the distance, "That cloud! You drew that too!"

Qianyun looked up—that fluffy, cotton-candy-like cloud in the sky was indeed one she had drawn.

"And those flowers!" the little bird called again, "That row—you drew all of them!"

Qianyun looked at the flower bed under the tree—neat rows of colorful little flowers, exactly the ones she had drawn stroke by stroke with her colored pencils yesterday.

"This..." Qianyun's eyes welled up, "this is the world I drew..."

"Every flower, every leaf, every gust of wind here," the little bird landed on her shoulder, saying softly, "were all drawn by you, with your true heart."

"That's why they're so beautiful."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and gently stroked the little bird's feathers.

"Thank you." she said.

"You're welcome!" the little bird chirped, "Let's go! Your father and mother are waiting for you under the tree!"

"Father and mother..." Qianyun's footsteps paused slightly.

She thought of her father and mother in reality.

Would they... be waiting for her here too?

She looked down at herself.

She was still wearing her school uniform. But everything here seemed... as if it had stepped out of her drawing.

The sky was impossibly blue.

The grass was so soft and green.

The phoenix tree bloomed more beautifully than any painting.

And under the tree—

Two people stood.

A man, and a woman.

"Dad... Mom..." Qianyun looked at them, stunned.

Because that man and woman were exactly the two in her drawing—the "ideal father" and "ideal mother."

"Qianyun!" the mother in the drawing opened her arms wide to her, smiling tenderly, "You're here! We've been waiting for you!"

"Come! We made all your favorite dishes!" the father in the drawing took her hand and led her to the picnic mat under the tree, "Today, the three of us will have a proper meal together!"

Qianyun was led, dazed, to sit on the picnic mat.

On the picnic mat were all her favorite dishes.

Braised pork belly, tomato scrambled eggs, braised chicken legs, and... a pot of steaming hot soup.

"Come, come, eat more!" the mother in the drawing put a piece of braised pork into her bowl, "Your favorite braised pork!"

"I... I..." Qianyun looked at the braised pork in her bowl, and her eyes suddenly welled up.

"What's wrong?" the father in the drawing asked worriedly, "Is it not tasty?"

"No..." Qianyun shook her head, her voice choked, "It's just..."

"It's just..." she lowered her head, tears falling drop by drop into the bowl, "it's been so long... so long since I had a meal with my father and mother..."

"Silly child," the mother in the drawing gently stroked her head, "aren't we always here?"

"But..." Qianyun looked up, "the father and mother in reality... they're so busy... they argue... they..."

"What about them?" the father in the drawing asked.

"They..." Qianyun's voice grew smaller and smaller, "they... seem to have forgotten how to have a proper meal..."

"..." The father and mother in the drawing were quiet for a moment.

Then, the mother in the drawing gently drew her into her arms.

"Qianyun ah," she said tenderly, "do you know? The father and mother in reality... actually miss you very much too."

"Miss me?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." the mother in the drawing nodded, "They argue because they're both tired, both worried. Father works so hard because he wants to give this family a better life. Mother works overtime so desperately because she wants..." she said softly, "she wants you to live a better life."

"But..." Qianyun choked, "I'd rather... they didn't work so hard. I just want... them to keep me company."

"..." the father in the drawing crouched down, looked at her seriously, and said, "Qianyun, would you be willing to help your father and mother with something?"

"What?"

"Help us," the father in the drawing said, "draw 'having a meal together' back."

"Draw it back?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." the mother in the drawing nodded, "The real us are busy every day, so busy we forgot... the most important thing."

"The most important thing?" Qianyun asked.

"It's..." the father and mother in the drawing said in unison, looking at her.

"You."

"You are the most important person to your father and mother."

"..." Qianyun's nose suddenly stung.

She lowered her head, and tears flowed uncontrollably.

"So," the mother in the drawing gently wiped away her tears, "when you go back, take this drawing you made, and go find your father and mother."

"Tell them—" the father in the drawing said, "you want to have a meal with them."

"You..." the mother in the drawing smiled, "will help us draw the family back, right?"

"..." Qianyun looked at the father and mother in the drawing and nodded hard.

"I will." she choked, "I will definitely... draw our family back."

At that moment, the phoenix tree in the drawing suddenly rained down a warm shower of petals.

"Time's up." the mother in the drawing gently stroked her head, "Qianyun, it's time to go back."

"Go back?" Qianyun was stunned, "But... I..."

"Go back." the father in the drawing smiled, "The 'us' here will always be waiting for you here. Waiting for you... to turn the home in reality into the home in this drawing too."

"..." Qianyun looked at them, her vision blurred with tears.

"I'll come back." she said, "After I draw the family back... I'll bring my father and mother here with me."

"Mhm." the father and mother in the drawing waved to her together, "We'll be waiting."

"Woom—!"

The warm light surged up once more.

Qianyun closed her eyes.

When she opened her eyes again—

She found herself lying on the desk.

Her sketchbook lay open in front of her.

That "family portrait" lay quietly on the paper.

The sky outside the window had already brightened.

It was dawn.

"I..." Qianyun looked at the drawing and gently touched the smiling faces of the three people in it, "I'm back."

She took a deep breath, stood up, and walked to the window.

"Dad, Mom..." she said in a low voice, "I'm going to... draw our family back."

That morning, Qianyun left home earlier than usual.

She slung her backpack over her shoulder, hugging that rolled-up "family portrait" to her chest, as if carrying a huge secret.

"I'll keep it here for now," she walked into the art classroom and carefully placed the drawing in her locker, "until Friday... when both Dad and Mom will be home."

She had made up her mind.

On Friday, Dad would be back from his business trip, and Mom would get off work on time.

She would take out the drawing that day and tell them:

"Let's... have a meal together."

But time passed more slowly than she had imagined.

On Tuesday, Mom worked overtime until ten at night.

On Wednesday, Dad called, saying his trip was delayed again, and he might not make it back by Friday.

"What?!" Qianyun gripped the phone, her voice suddenly anxious, "Dad, you said..."

"I'm sorry, Qianyun," Dad's voice was tired, "something came up at work... Dad really wants to come back too..."

"..." Qianyun gripped the phone, her eyes reddening.

"But... I..." she choked, "I have something very important I want to show you..."

"What is it?" Dad asked, "Can you wait until Dad gets back?"

"..." Qianyun fell silent.

She looked down at the drawing tucked in her backpack.

"...Okay." she finally said softly, "I'll wait for you."

After hanging up, Qianyun returned to her room and opened her sketchbook.

She looked at that family portrait for a very, very long time.

"Dad, Mom..." she said in a low voice, "when will you remember... to come back and see me?"

She lay down, burying her face in her arms.

After a long time, she lifted her head.

"No." she took a deep breath, "I can't... just wait."

"The father and mother in the drawing said—"

"'Help us draw 'having a meal together' back.'"

"Then..." she stood up, "I'll start working with my own hands."

That night, Qianyun didn't draw.

She brought out a small toolbox containing colored paper, scissors, glue, and... a stack of beautiful wrapping paper she had saved for a long time.

She was going to make a card.

A "family card" for her father and mother.

While making the card, Qianyun suddenly remembered something.

She rummaged through the deepest part of her drawer and pulled out an old photo album.

It was a family album her father and mother had organized a long, long time ago.

She flipped through it, page by page.

She came to a photo of herself as a child—

In the photo, she was still a tiny, round baby.

Her father held her in his arms, her mother stood beside them, and both of them were laughing so, so happily.

"This..." Qianyun's finger gently traced the smiles of her father and mother in the photo, "is us..."

She kept flipping.

Another photo—

That year, she entered first grade and won her very first award.

Both her father and mother came, the three of them standing at the school gate, taking a photo.

"Back then..." she said in a low voice, "my father and mother would come take photos with me..."

She flipped another page.

After this page, there were fewer and fewer photos.

The last page was empty.

"..." Qianyun was silent for a moment.

Then she gently pulled that "family portrait from childhood" out of the album and placed it on the desk.

"I'm going to..." she picked up the colored paper, "draw a 'family portrait of now,' based on this photo."

"Draw... a photo of 'tomorrow' that belongs to the three of us."

"Draw a photo... that when my father and mother see it, they'll remember that we are a family."

She began to draw.

This time, she drew even more carefully than before.

She drew her father's hair a little messy—because every time Dad came back from a business trip, his hair was messy.

She drew her mother's eyes bright and shining—because when Mom smiled, her eyes sparkled.

She drew herself in the very middle, holding her father's hand on the left and her mother's hand on the right.

"This..." she finished drawing and looked at it gently, "is the 'gift' I want to give my father and mother."

"How should I make the card..." Qianyun rested her chin on her hand and thought for a very, very long time.

"The cover..." she gently folded a phoenix tree shape out of colored paper, "will have a phoenix tree."

"When you open the card..." she folded two sheets of colored paper in half and cut out three little figures holding hands, "there'll be the three of us, holding hands."

"And then..." she picked up her pen and wrote, word by word, inside the card—

"To my dear father and mother:

I know you're busy. I know you're tired.

I know you're both working for this family, so that I can live better.

But I actually... don't want anything.

I don't want new toys, or allowance, or lots and lots of things.

I just want... the three of us to sit together at the dining table and have a proper meal.

Just like before.

Just like... the drawing I made.

Love, Qianyun"

After writing the last word, Qianyun's nose tingled.

She gently closed the card.

"Dad, Mom..." she said in a low voice, "will you... like it?"

Time passed, day by day.

Every day, Qianyun took that drawing and that card out of the cabinet, looked at them, touched them, then put them away again.

She was like a person waiting for the "right moment."

Friday finally came.

But Friday was also the day Qianyun feared most.

Because she didn't know whether her father and mother would both be home today.

After school, Qianyun didn't go straight home.

She sat alone under the phoenix tree at the school gate, looking at the sky, lost in thought.

"Today..." she said in a low voice, "will it be that day?"

Just then, her phone rang.

It was her mother.

"Qianyun!" Mom's voice sounded a little anxious, "Mom might have to work overtime today too..."

"..." Qianyun's heart sank, little by little.

"Mom..." she gathered her courage and said softly, "can you... not work overtime today?"

"..." There was silence on the other end of the line.

"Mom wants to come home early too," Mom sighed, "but..."

"Mom," Qianyun interrupted her, her voice soft yet unusually firm, "I have something very important I want to show you and Dad."

"Something very important?" Mom was taken aback, "What is it?"

"..." Qianyun paused, then said, "It's... something that can bring our family back."

"..." There was silence on the other end again.

After a long moment, Mom spoke, her voice a little hoarse: "...Okay. Mom will come home early today."

"Really?" Qianyun's eyes instantly lit up.

"Really." Mom said, "Mom promises you."

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded hard, "Then I'll hurry home too!"

She hung up, slung her backpack over her shoulder, and sprinted toward home.

She was going to decorate the dining table beautifully.

That evening, Qianyun bustled around the kitchen like a little magician.

She didn't know how to cook, but she knew how to decorate.

She polished the dining table until it sparkled, spreading a tablecloth printed with little flowers—her favorite.

She gently taped that "family portrait" to the wall directly across from the dining table.

She placed that "family card" neatly in the center of the table.

Then she sat down in her chair, hands on her knees, waiting.

"Knock, knock."

A knock sounded at the door.

"Qianyun! Mom's home!"

"I'm home too!"

It was the voices of her father and mother!

Qianyun jumped up, dashed to the door, and opened it—

Outside the door stood her father, travel-worn, and her mother, carrying a big bag of groceries.

"Dad! Mom!" Qianyun's eyes instantly welled up, "You... you really..."

"Silly child," Mom set down the groceries, crouched down, and stroked her head, "when Mom says she'll come home early, she comes home early."

"Dad too." Dad smiled, setting his suitcase on the floor, "No matter how important the trip is, nothing is more important than our Qianyun."

"..." Qianyun's tears burst out.

"Dad... Mom..." she choked, "I... I have something I want to show you."

She took her parents' hands and led them into the living room.

"Look..."

She pointed at that "family portrait" on the wall across from the dining table.

"This is what I drew..." Qianyun's voice trembled slightly, "the 'us' I drew..."

Her father and mother froze.

They looked at that drawing.

In the drawing, the phoenix tree bloomed with fiery red flowers.

In the drawing, a family of three smiled brighter than the sunlight.

"This..." Mom's lips trembled, "this is... us?"

"Mhm." Qianyun nodded, tears falling drop by drop, "I drew the 'us' in my heart. That's... the way I most wish to see us."

"..." Dad was silent, but his eyes reddened.

"And this." Qianyun picked up the "family card" from the table and gently handed it to her father and mother, "This is... what I made for you."

Trembling, her father and mother opened the card.

They read those crooked words:

"I actually... don't want anything. I just want... the three of us to sit together at the dining table and have a proper meal. Just like before. Just like... the drawing I made."

Reading this, Mom's tears could no longer be held back and fell.

"Qianyun..." Mom drew her into her arms, "I'm sorry... I'm sorry... Mom... Mom's been so busy... so busy she forgot..."

"Mom..." Qianyun cried in her mother's arms, "It's okay... we... we're together now..."

"..." Dad stood to the side, his eyes red, and gently drew both his wife and daughter into his arms.

"Dad is sorry too." Dad's voice was hoarse, "Dad was so busy with work, wanting to give you a better life. But Dad forgot..." he lowered his head, "the best life is the three of us being together."

"..." The family of three hugged each other in the living room, crying together.

But crying and crying, they all smiled again.

"Alright, alright," Mom wiped her tears, stood up, "since Qianyun has made the home so beautiful, then..." she rolled up her sleeves, "tonight, Mom will cook a table full of good food!"

"I'll help!" Dad said.

"Me too!" Qianyun raised her hand.

And so, that Friday evening, the small kitchen echoed with the long-missed sound of bustling warmth.

"Dad! The tomato scrambled eggs need salt!" "I added it! I added it!" "Mom! Is the braised pork ready to come out?" "Wait another five minutes!"

"Hahaha!"

Qianyun stood at the kitchen door, watching her father and mother bustle about, her nose tingling.

But her heart was warm.

Half an hour later, a table of steaming hot dishes filled the dining table.

Braised pork. Tomato scrambled eggs. Braised chicken legs. And a pot of steaming hot soup.

"Come, come, let's eat!" Dad announced loudly.

"Wait!" Qianyun suddenly said, "We... we have to take a photo!"

"A photo?" Her father and mother froze.

"Mhm!" Qianyun ran to the other side of the table and picked up her phone, "I want to take a photo of the three of us eating together!"

"Just like..." she smiled, "just like in the drawing I made!"

"Alright!" Her father and mother smiled.

"Three, two, one—"

"Click!"

In the photo, at the dining table, a family of three smiled.

That meal, they ate very, very slowly.

They talked about so many things.

Dad told funny stories from his business trip, Mom shared amusing stories from work, and Qianyun talked about the drawing she made, the card she made, and... the little wish in her heart.

"Dad, Mom," after the meal, Qianyun suddenly said, "from now on... can we have a meal together every week?"

"Every week?" Her father and mother exchanged a glance.

"Mhm!" Qianyun nodded hard, "It doesn't have to be long, just... the time of one meal."

"..." Her father and mother looked at her, then smiled together.

"Okay." Mom said, "Every week."

"Dad promises you too." Dad said, "Every week, we'll eat together."

"..." Qianyun's eyes reddened again.

She lowered her head, buried her face in her bowl, and smiled secretly.

That night, Qianyun lay in bed, unable to fall asleep.

She stared at the ceiling, the corners of her mouth rising the whole time.

"The father and mother in the drawing..." she said in a low voice, "thank you."

"I think... I really did draw the family back."

She turned over and looked at that "family portrait" on her desk.

Moonlight shone quietly on the drawing.

In the drawing, a family of three smiled brightly under the phoenix tree.

"Tomorrow..." she said softly, "I want to redraw that drawing."

"Draw one... that really happened."

Because, starting today, the scene in that drawing was no longer "imagination."

It was... something they had truly done.

That Saturday, Qianyun drew another picture.

This time, she didn't draw a phoenix tree or a family portrait.

She drew the scene of the family sitting around the dining table eating that night.

In the picture, the sheen of the braised pork, the golden of the tomato scrambled eggs, the steam rising from the soup bowl... all were drawn by her in careful detail.

Beside the dining table sat three people.

Her father, her mother, and her.

Everyone was smiling so happily.

"This drawing," Qianyun looked at it and smiled softly, "shall be called 'Our Friday.'"

She thought for a moment, then added: "In the future... every Friday, there will be a new drawing."

On Monday, Qianyun walked into the classroom.

"Morning..." she lowered her head, her voice small.

"Morning!" A-Cheng, sitting in the first row, smiled in reply.

"..." Qianyun was stunned.

This was the first time since sixth grade began that someone had said "morning" to her when she entered the classroom.

"Qianyun," A-Cheng spoke again, "you... seemed to be in a good mood yesterday."

"Did... did I?" Qianyun's face flushed slightly.

"Yes!" A-Cheng said, "The way you walked yesterday was a little different from usual. It seemed like..." she thought for a moment, "like you had something happy in your heart."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and smiled softly.

"Mhm." she said in a small voice, "Yesterday... I had a meal with my father and mother."

"Really?" A-Cheng's eyes lit up, "That's great!"

"Mhm." Qianyun nodded, "We... hadn't had a meal together in a long time."

"Then... you should have meals together more often from now on!" A-Cheng said happily.

"..." Qianyun looked at A-Cheng, and her eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"Mhm." she nodded hard, "I will."

That day, Qianyun felt for the first time that the sunlight in the classroom seemed... a little warmer than before.

Perhaps because the door in her heart had finally opened a tiny crack.

A month later, a new drawing appeared in Qianyun's sketchbook.

That drawing looked a lot like the previous one.

A phoenix tree. Fiery red flowers. Under the tree, three figures holding hands.

But it was also different.

Because the "us" in this drawing smiled more genuinely.

Because this drawing was painted by Qianyun one warm Sunday, watching her father and mother take a stroll under the phoenix tree, and drawing their backs with her own hands.

"This drawing," Qianyun gently touched the paper, "is 'real.'"

"It's not the home I imagined."

"It's... the home we drew back together."

She carefully tucked the drawing into her sketchbook.

Then she looked up at the sky.

"The father and mother in the drawing..." she smiled, "thank you for teaching me."

"So mending a broken heart..." she said softly, "isn't about gluing it back together."

"It's about..." she looked down at her hands, "using a little bit of care, to warm it back up."

The wind blew gently.

The leaves of the phoenix tree rustled.

—As if to say: "Yes, you did it."

> **Handmade Tip**: You can try it too! Draw a "picture of our home"—draw the warmest scene you most wish would happen, and give it to your family; or make a little "thank-you card" and write down what you want to say to them. Perhaps a small gesture of care can warm a heart all over again.
