# The Handmade Girl's Fantastic Journey

## Story 2: The Star for a Misunderstanding

There was a sixth-grade girl at Luyang Elementary named Liu Qianyun. Her hands were clever—drawing, molding clay, folding paper, nothing was too hard for her; but the thing she was worst at was "opening her mouth to speak." Rumor had it, whenever she finished a piece of work poured with genuine emotion, that work would carry her away to another world. And in that world, she would always see certain "hearts" she couldn't see in everyday life.

In November, at Luyang Elementary, the leaves of the phoenix tree had all fallen.

The bare branches swayed gently in the wind.

Inside the classroom of Class 6-1, the students were chattering away about next month's "school fair."

"Teacher said the theme for this fair is 'the starry sky'!"

"I'm going to draw a shooting star!"

"I'm going to make a starry-sky jar!"

"Then I'll make origami stars!"

Everyone was talking excitedly. Only Qianyun sat quietly in her corner by the window.

On her desk lay a sketch.

In the sketch, there was a night sky.

In the night sky, there were stars all over, and a bright, shining Milky Way.

"For the art class grouping," the teacher announced from the podium, "you may work alone, or you may find a partner."

"Yay!"

"I'm with Xiaoyun!"

"I'm with A-Qiang!"

The students swarmed to find partners.

Only Qianyun kept her head down, continuing to draw her sketch.

Anyway... she was used to being alone.

"Um... excuse me..."

Suddenly, a voice sounded in front of her.

Qianyun looked up.

Standing before her was a girl with a ponytail.

It was Xia Zhixing.

Xia Zhixing, who had just transferred to this school this semester.

The day Zhixing first transferred here was in September.

She sat alone in the last row.

Teacher said, "Let's welcome our new classmate, Xia Zhixing!"

She stood up, blushing, and bowed to everyone.

The classmates applauded.

But after class, no one talked to her.

At lunch, she sat alone in her seat, eating her lunchbox.

After school, she walked out the school gate alone, carrying her backpack.

"..." Qianyun had actually always noticed her.

Because Zhixing was just like her.

One of those people who were... often alone.

Back then, Qianyun thought: she must... be lonely too.

But Qianyun didn't walk over either.

Because she herself didn't know how to start a conversation.

Come to think of it...

If she had walked over back then...

Would the story have been different?

"Zhixing," Qianyun said, "the starry sky you drew... it's beautiful."

"...Thank you." Qianyun replied blankly, two words.

"Um... I... can I be in a group with you?"

Zhixing's fingers twisted together nervously.

"I..." Qianyun was stunned.

She hadn't expected anyone to want to be in a group with her.

"I..." she stammered, "I... I've always drawn alone..."

"..." Zhixing's smile froze.

"Th-then that's fine!" she said quickly, "I'll be a group by myself! S-sorry, sorry to bother you!"

She turned around and hurried back to her seat.

"..." Qianyun watched her back, her mouth opening slightly.

But she didn't say anything.

She didn't know what to say.

"...Sorry." In the end, she only said those two words, quietly, in her heart.

Actually, Qianyun didn't not want to be in a group with Zhixing.

It was just...

She didn't know how to say "okay."

She was afraid of saying the wrong thing.

She was afraid that after Zhixing was grouped with her, she would find Qianyun boring, and regret it.

So she chose... to say nothing at all.

And that "saying nothing at all" looked, in Zhixing's eyes, like—"I don't want to."

For the next few days, Zhixing didn't come to talk to her again.

Zhixing worked by herself, making a huge starry-sky poster.

On the poster, she had drawn stars, dense and countless.

"So many stars..." the classmates gathered around to look.

"Zhixing, you drew so many stars!"

"Mhm..." Zhixing smiled, "Because... I heard that if you make a wish when you see a shooting star, it comes true."

"What did you wish for?"

"Secret." Zhixing said.

She stole a glance at Qianyun.

Qianyun was looking down, drawing her night sky.

Zhixing thought: if only... we could draw this together.

Qianyun thought: Zhixing's poster is so pretty.

But neither sentence was ever spoken out loud.

It was lunch break.

Qianyun was coming back from the restroom. As she passed the back door of the classroom, she heard voices from a few classmates.

"Hey, did you know? Xia Zhixing said Liu Qianyun is really arrogant."

"Really?"

"Really! She said she wanted to be in a group with Qianyun, but got rejected. She said Qianyun seemed like she 'didn't want to deal with her.'"

"Oh... but Qianyun is always like that anyway, she ignores everyone."

"Yeah, she barely even talks to the class president."

"..."

Qianyun stopped at the back door of the classroom.

Her hands clenched.

"...Arrogant."

"...Doesn't want to deal with her."

Those words pricked her ears like little needles.

"..." She lowered her head and walked away quickly.

Back at her seat, she opened her sketchbook, but couldn't draw a single stroke.

Her mind was full of the words she had just heard.

"So that's it..." she said in a low voice, "even Zhixing thinks that way."

"She thinks... I'm arrogant too."

"She thinks... I don't want to deal with her."

She looked down at her own fingers.

"I... I'm not arrogant."

"I just... don't know how to talk."

"I just... am afraid of saying the wrong thing."

"Why... does everyone look at me that way?"

Her eyes reddened.

She sniffled and closed her sketchbook.

"Forget it." she said, "Anyway... drawing is simpler."

"Talking... is so hard."

That day after school, Qianyun ran into Zhixing in the hallway.

They both saw each other.

"..." Zhixing's mouth opened, as if she wanted to say something.

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and quickened her pace, walking past her.

Zhixing's outstretched hand stopped in midair.

"..." She watched Qianyun's back and slowly lowered her hand.

"So that's it..." she said in a low voice, "she hates me."

She didn't know that Qianyun had also stopped at the corner.

Qianyun leaned against the wall and closed her eyes.

"I... I don't hate you." she said in her heart.

"I just... don't know how to start talking."

"I'm sorry... did I... hurt someone again?"

Two hearts, separated by a wall.

Neither dared to speak first.

Both believed the other hated them.

That night, Qianyun sat at her desk and opened her art class folder.

Inside, besides the sketch of her night sky, there was also a small note.

On the note were crooked words:

"Qianyun: The starry sky you drew is really beautiful. —Zhixing"

It was from two months ago, when Zhixing had just transferred here, secretly tucked into her textbook.

"..." Qianyun held the note and was silent for a long time.

"Zhixing..." she said in a low voice, "why... did you give me this?"

"You clearly... don't hate me at all."

"Then... those words..."

She shook her head and put the note back into the folder.

"Forget it." she said, "Anyway... I'm just not good with people."

"Anyway... everyone thinks I'm weird."

"Anyway..." she paused, "I'm... used to it."

But she couldn't fool herself.

Because that night, she had a dream.

In the dream, there was a huge, huge night sky.

In the night sky, there were stars everywhere.

The stars were all so close to one another.

Only two stars, separated by a Milky Way, stood all alone on their two sides.

They kept shining.

But neither of them flew toward the other.

"..." In the dream, Qianyun watched those two stars.

"Why... don't you go over?" she asked.

The stars didn't answer.

They just shone, quietly.

When Qianyun woke from the dream, a small corner of her pillow was wet.

The next day, Qianyun made a decision.

She was going to make something.

Not a drawing.

Handmade.

She was going to make a star.

A little clay star.

She took out from her backpack that pack of yellow clay she had been saving for a long time.

"I'm going to make a star..." she said, "a round, chubby star with a smile."

"Just like... Zhixing when she laughs."

But why a star?

She wanted to give it to Zhixing.

She had wanted to give it to her for a long time.

Ever since Zhixing first smiled at her, she had wanted to give Zhixing something.

But she never knew how to give it.

How to start.

How to say: "Thank you for smiling at me."

How to say: "I actually... really want to be friends with you."

How to say: "I'm not arrogant, I'm just... scared."

She kneaded the clay, kneading all those words she couldn't say, little by little, into the star.

As she kneaded, her tears fell.

"Zhixing..." she said in a low voice, "I'm sorry."

"I didn't mean to ignore you."

"I'm just... too clumsy."

"I couldn't even say one sentence: 'Okay, let's work together.'"

"I clearly... really wanted to be in your group."

"I clearly... really wanted to be your friend."

She cried and kneaded.

The clay slowly took the shape of a star in her hands.

A round belly.

Chubby points.

And at the corner of its mouth, a tiny, curved smile.

"Done." She wiped her tears and placed the star on the desk.

"Now... I can give it to you."

She looked at that star.

For a very, very long time.

Then she shook her head again.

"No." she said, "Zhixing must... not want to receive anything from me anymore."

"She must... also think I'm arrogant."

"She must... not want to deal with me either."

She put the star into her drawer.

Closed the drawer.

Then lay back down on the desk.

"..." She buried her face in her arms, "forget it."

"Anyway... I'm used to being alone."

But at that very moment—

"Woom—!"

A warm, pale golden light suddenly surged up from her drawer!

"This..." Qianyun was startled and quickly pulled open the drawer.

Inside the drawer, that little clay star was glowing with a soft, gentle light!

"This is..." her eyes went wide, "this is... my star!"

The light gently wrapped around her.

"How..." she murmured, "didn't I... not finish it?"

"No." She lowered her head and looked at the little shining star.

"I finished it."

"I already... finished it while I was kneading it."

"Because..." she gently cupped the star in her hands, "my feelings... were already overflowing."

"So..."

"Swish—!"

The whole world spun before her eyes.

When she came back to her senses—

She found herself standing—

In a night sky.

"This place is..." Qianyun looked around, stunned.

This was a night sky, so beautiful, so beautiful.

The sky was deep blackish blue.

Like a whole piece of soft, fluffy velvet.

The sky was full of stars.

Big and small, shining bright.

Some stars blinked, as if winking.

Some stars dragged long tails, slowly gliding across the sky.

And there was a wide, bright Milky Way, stretching across the entire sky.

"This world..." Qianyun gently reached out her hand, "is exactly like the night sky I drew."

"No." She looked at her fingertips, "even more beautiful than the one I drew."

She looked down at herself.

She was still wearing her school uniform.

But the "ground" beneath her feet was a whole stretch of soft, glowing cloud.

"So soft..." she stepped lightly, "like stepping on cotton candy."

"Here..." She looked up at the stars all over the sky, "is this the place my clay star brought me?"

"Then..." she asked in a low voice, "where should I go?"

"Here..."

Suddenly, a voice sounded softly.

"Is someone there?"

"..." Qianyun was startled and turned around.

"Who is it?"

"It's me."

The voice was soft and bright.

Like... a star speaking.

"Look up."

Qianyun looked up.

Right above her, there was a star.

A star that was especially bright, especially big.

Its light was warm and yellow, like a little light bulb.

"You..." Qianyun looked at it, stunned, "are you... a star?"

"Mhm!" That star swayed gently before her, "I'm a star! I'm 'that star'!"

"That star?" Qianyun asked, "Which one?"

"The one you made!" The star's voice carried a smile, "The star you kneaded from clay corresponds to 'a heart.'"

"A heart?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." The star said, "A heart that is connected to you—a very important heart."

"A heart... connected to me?" Qianyun repeated softly.

She suddenly thought of someone.

"Is it... Zhixing?" she asked.

"..." The star didn't answer.

It just shone, quietly.

Then the star said: "Follow me."

The star flew slowly ahead.

Qianyun followed behind, walking on the soft cloud.

As she walked, she saw—

A Milky Way stretched out before her.

The water of the Milky Way sparkled, like a river flowing with broken stars.

On this side of the Milky Way stood a star.

A small, round, bright star.

"This star..." Qianyun looked at it, "is so cute."

"Who is it?" she asked.

"It," the leading star said softly, "is a 'lonely star.'"

"A lonely star?"

"Mhm." The star said, "It tried very hard to get close to another star. But every time, it failed."

"It failed?"

"It gathered up courage so many times," the star said, "wanting to talk to that other star. But that star never responded."

"..." Qianyun was silent for a moment.

"So... it thought that star didn't like it." The star continued.

"It thought it wasn't good enough."

"It thought it was too annoying."

"So it began to..." the star paused, "hide away."

"Hide away?" Qianyun asked.

"It didn't dare get close anymore." The star said, "It was afraid that if it was rejected one more time, it would shatter."

"..." Qianyun's heart suddenly ached.

"But," the star said again, "it still wanted to get close so badly."

"It secretly watched that star."

"It secretly made so many little stars."

"It secretly wrote down, one by one, the things that star liked."

"It secretly..." the star looked at Qianyun, "made a wish."

"What wish?" Qianyun asked.

"It wished..." the star said, "that one day, that star would fly over first, and say one sentence to it."

"Just one sentence."

"That would be enough."

"..." Qianyun's eyes reddened.

"Because," the star said softly, "it has no strength left to be the one to get close anymore."

"It's afraid of being left behind again, all by itself."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head.

Tears fell, drop by drop.

"It..." she choked, "it's Zhixing, isn't it?"

"..." The star shone, quietly.

"Look over there." the star said.

Qianyun looked up, toward the other side of the Milky Way.

There, another star stood.

A quiet, small star.

"What about that one?" Qianyun asked.

"That one," the star said, "is a 'scared star.'"

"A scared star?"

"It," the star said, "has also been watching that star across from it."

"It also wants to fly over."

"But every time, it stops."

"Why?" Qianyun asked.

"Because..." the star said, "it feels it isn't good enough."

"It feels it can't talk."

"It feels it will definitely mess things up."

"It feels..." the star paused, "that the star across from it must not want to deal with it."

"..." Qianyun's heart suddenly skipped a beat.

"So it chose..." the star said, "to stay where it was."

"It thought that way it wouldn't get hurt."

"But..." the star looked at her, "it didn't know that the way it stood there, in that other star's eyes, looked just like..."

"Like what?" Qianyun's voice trembled slightly.

"Like..." the star said softly, "not wanting to get close."

"..." Qianyun's nose suddenly stung.

"Then..." she choked, "that scared star... is me?"

"..." The star didn't answer.

It just shone, quietly.

"Two stars," the leading star said gently, "both think the other doesn't like them."

"Both think they aren't good enough."

"Both wait in place, for the other."

"Waiting and waiting..." the star said, "until it becomes a misunderstanding."

"A misunderstanding..." Qianyun lowered her head and repeated those two words.

"Mhm." The star said, "A misunderstanding is: 'two stars, both thinking the other doesn't want to get close.'"

"But they actually... both want to get close."

"They just... are both too afraid."

"..." Qianyun's tears flowed without stopping.

"Then... what should I do?" she asked.

"I... I don't dare fly over."

"I'm afraid... she really does hate me."

"I'm afraid... the moment I fly over, she'll run away."

"I'm afraid..." she lowered her head, "I'll be rejected by her."

"..." The star looked at her, quietly.

"You know?" the star said, "that star across the Milky Way has actually been waiting too."

"Waiting for you to fly over first."

"It's been waiting..." the star said softly, "for so, so long."

"But it doesn't dare say it."

"It only dares..." the star said, "secretly watch you."

"Secretly..." the star's tone suddenly grew gentle, "fold the words it wants to say to you into stars, one by one, and put them into a jar."

"Fold them into stars?" Qianyun was stunned, "A jar?"

"Mhm." The star said, "Haven't you... received a jar before?"

"A jar... full of origami stars?"

"..." Qianyun's heart suddenly skipped a beat.

"A jar..." she murmured, "I... I think I've... received one..."

"Where is it?" the star asked.

"It's..." Qianyun tried hard to remember, "in my locker."

"In my locker, one day, there was an extra little clear jar."

"In the jar, it was full of... origami stars."

"So many, so many of them."

"I... I thought someone put it in the wrong locker."

"I thought..." she said in a low voice, "it was a prank."

"So I put the jar..." she choked, "back where it was."

"..." The star was silent for a moment.

"That... that wasn't a misplaced jar." the star said softly.

"That was..." the star looked at her, "the 'feelings' that a star gathered up its courage to send out."

"In that jar of origami stars, every single one has a sentence written on it."

"What sentence?" Qianyun asked.

"'I want to be your friend.'" the star said.

"..." Qianyun froze completely.

"Every single one." the star said.

"She folded them for many days."

"She wrote them over and over."

"She carefully put them into the jar."

"Then she put them..." the star said softly, "into your locker."

"She was so nervous."

"She hid at the stairway corner, secretly watching."

"She thought: 'When she sees the jar, will she know it was me who put it there?'"

"She thought: 'Will she... not like it?'"

"She saw you put the jar back where it was."

"She thought..." the star said, "you didn't like it."

"That day when she got home," the star said, "she cried."

"She cried, and threw away, one by one, all the origami stars that were left."

"She said: 'I'll never fold stars again.'"

"..." Qianyun covered her mouth.

Tears, like beads on a broken string, fell one after another.

"I'm sorry..." she cried, "I'm sorry... I didn't know..."

"I... I thought it was a prank..."

"I didn't know... it was you..."

"I'm sorry..." she crouched down, burying her face in her knees, "I'm sorry..."

"..." The leading star flew quietly to her side.

"Don't cry." the star said softly.

"Now that you know, it's not too late."

"But..." Qianyun lifted her head, her face covered in tears, "what... what can I still do?"

"I've already... made her cry."

"I've already... put her jar back."

"She must... hate me to death."

"..." The star shook its head.

"She doesn't hate you." the star said.

"She's just..." the star said softly, "sad."

"Sad?" Qianyun asked.

"Mhm." The star said, "She's sad because she thinks you don't want to be her friend."

"But didn't you just say, you want to be her friend?"

"..." Qianyun was stunned.

"I..." she lowered her head, "I do want..."

"I want... to be her friend."

"I've wanted to... for so, so long."

"From the very first time she smiled at me, I've wanted to."

"From the time she picked up my colored pencil that fell on the floor, I've wanted to."

"From the time she tucked that note into my textbook, I've wanted to."

"But I didn't dare say it."

"I was afraid..." she choked, "that if I said it, I'd be rejected."

"I was afraid..." she said, "that even this little bit of closeness would annoy her."

"..." The star looked at her, quietly.

"Look." the star said.

Qianyun looked up.

She saw that on this side of the Milky Way, the "scared star" was gently shining.

And on the other side of the Milky Way, the "lonely star" was also gently shining.

"They..." Qianyun said, "are both shining."

"Mhm." The star said, "They've always been shining."

"They've always..." the star said softly, "wanted to let the other know: 'I'm here.'"

"It's just... no one knows how to fly over."

"..." Qianyun watched those two stars.

"I..." she lowered her head and said softly, "I want... to fly over."

"I want..." she said, "to say to that lonely star: 'I'm here.'"

"I want to say to her: 'I've also always... wanted to be your friend.'"

"I want to say to her: 'I'm sorry, I kept you waiting so long.'"

"..." The star listened quietly.

"But..." Qianyun lifted her head, her eyes blurry with tears, "I... I can't fly over."

"This Milky Way... is so, so wide."

"I... I don't dare."

"..." The star smiled (if a star could have an expression).

"You don't need to fly across the whole Milky Way." the star said.

"You just..." the star said softly, "need to take one step."

"Take one step?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." The star said, "Flying starts with a single step."

"You just..." the star said gently, "take one step first, and let that lonely star see."

"Tell it: 'I'm here.'"

"Then..." the star said, "you can fly the rest of the way together."

"..." Qianyun's eyes reddened again.

"Really?" she asked.

"Really." the star said.

"But..." Qianyun said in a low voice, "I... I don't know how to say it."

"I'm afraid... the moment I open my mouth, I'll say the wrong thing again."

"I'm afraid..." she said, "just like last time, I won't be able to say anything at all."

"..." The star looked at her, quietly.

"It's okay." the star said.

"Words you can't say..." the star said softly, "can be said in other ways."

"Other ways?" Qianyun asked.

"Mhm." The star said, "Just like that star you kneaded."

"That star," the star looked at her, "hasn't it already kneaded in all the words you wanted to say?"

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and looked at her own hands.

"That star..." she murmured, "is the one I made."

"I kneaded it..." she said, "so round, so round."

"I kneaded in..." she said, "so many, so many words."

"'I'm sorry.'"

"'I didn't mean to ignore you.'"

"'I just... didn't know how to say it.'"

"'I actually... really want to be your friend.'"

"..." she said in a low voice, "those words... are all inside it."

"Then that's enough." the star said.

"Take it, and go back."

"Put it into the hands of that lonely star."

"Then..." the star shone gently, "you don't need to say anything."

"That star will understand."

"It's been waiting for this moment..." the star said, "for so, so long."

"..." Qianyun stood up and looked at that stretch of starry sky.

"Okay." she said softly, "I will."

"I will..." she said, "put that star into her hands."

"I will..." she choked, "say to her: 'I'm here.'"

"..." The star happily spun around in place.

"Then..." the star said, "it's about time."

"Time?" Qianyun was stunned.

"Mhm." The star said, "In the real world, it's almost dawn."

"Almost dawn?" Qianyun murmured, "But... I only just got here..."

"..." The star laughed softly.

"Time here is different from the real world." the star said, "You've been here for a long, long time; but in the real world, maybe only a moment has passed."

"So..." the star said, "go back."

"Take that star, and go back."

"..." Qianyun looked at that bright, bright star, and her eyes grew wet again.

"Thank you." she said.

"Thank you... for bringing me here to see all this."

"Thank you..." she choked, "for letting me know what's in Zhixing's heart."

"..." The star shone, quietly.

"You're welcome." the star said.

"Because..." the star said gently, "that star you kneaded was always a star 'made of feelings.'"

"A star made of feelings..." Qianyun repeated.

"Mhm." The star said, "A star kneaded with a true heart will find its own way home."

"And it will also..." the star looked at her, "help you find that lonely heart."

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and looked at her own hands.

"That star..." she said softly, with a gentle smile, "is in my drawer right now."

"I will..." she said, "take it out."

"I will..." she said, "put it into her hands."

"..." The star happily circled around her, round and round.

"Then hurry back!" the star said, "She's waiting for you!"

"She's waiting for you..." the star repeated softly, "for so, so long."

"..." Qianyun took a deep breath.

"Okay." she said.

"Woom—!"

The warm light surged up once more.

Qianyun closed her eyes.

When she opened her eyes again—

She found herself lying on her desk.

The sky outside the window had already brightened.

It was dawn.

That little clay star lay quietly on the desk.

A round belly.

Chubby points.

And at the corner of its mouth, a tiny, curved smile.

"..." Qianyun looked at that star.

She reached out and gently cupped it in her hands.

"Star..." she said in a low voice, "thank you for taking me to see."

"Thank you..." she choked, "for letting me know she's been waiting for me."

She took a deep breath and held the star tightly in her palm.

"Zhixing..." she said, "I'm going... to put that star into your hands."

That morning, Qianyun arrived at school earlier than usual.

She didn't go to the classroom.

She went to her locker first.

She opened the locker door and searched inside carefully.

"The jar..." she murmured, "that jar..."

"There it is!"

In the corner of the locker, a little clear jar lay quietly.

The jar was empty.

"..." Qianyun took the jar out and looked at it for a long time.

"You..." she said in a low voice, "threw them... all away?"

"..." She lowered her head and gently touched the jar.

"I'm sorry." she said, "I'm sorry... I didn't open it and look sooner."

"I'm sorry..." she choked, "I didn't discover it was you sooner."

She thought for a moment, and gently placed the clay star into the jar.

"Now..." she looked at the star in the jar, "it's my turn to give you a star."

"This star..." she said in a low voice, "is the one I made."

"Inside it..." she said, "are kneaded the words I want to say to you."

She held the jar and walked toward the classroom.

In the classroom, the classmates had already been arriving one after another.

Zhixing sat in her seat, head down, organizing her backpack.

"..." Qianyun stood at the classroom door.

Her heart was thumping, thumping, so fast.

"I..." she took a deep breath, "I can do this."

"That star said..." she said in a low voice, "I just need to take one step."

She gripped the jar and walked, step by step, to Zhixing's desk.

"..." Zhixing looked up, stunned.

"Qian... Qianyun?" she stammered, "you... why are you..."

"This..." Qianyun held out the jar to Zhixing.

"..." Zhixing looked at that jar, and her eyes suddenly went wide.

"Th-this is..." her voice trembled.

"I know..." Qianyun's voice was also very small, "that jar... was from you."

"Those origami stars..." she choked, "were folded by you."

"..." Zhixing's lips trembled.

"I... I never opened it to look." Qianyun lowered her head, "I thought... it was put in the wrong place."

"I put it... back."

"I'm sorry..." she said, "I'm sorry... I didn't know... it was you..."

"..." Zhixing's eyes reddened instantly.

"You..." she said, trembling, "how do you know..."

"I..." Qianyun said, "I made a star."

"A clay star."

"As I kneaded it..." she said in a low voice, "I... saw it."

"I saw..." she said, "that there was a star waiting for me."

"I saw..." she choked, "it had waited... for so, so long."

"..." Zhixing's tears fell.

"I... I thought..." she cried, "I thought you hated me..."

"I don't!" Qianyun shook her head, tears falling too, "I don't hate you!"

"I just..." she cried, "I just... didn't know how to say it."

"I... I also really wanted to be in your group."

"I... I also really wanted to be your friend."

"I..." she lowered her head, "I didn't dare say it."

"I was afraid... that if I said it, I'd be rejected."

"..." Zhixing was stunned.

"You... you wanted to too?" she asked.

"Mhm." Qianyun nodded, "I've wanted to... for so, so long."

"From the very first time... you smiled at me, I've wanted to."

"From the time you picked up my colored pencil, I've wanted to."

"From the time you tucked that note into my textbook..." she choked, "I've wanted to."

"..." Zhixing's tears couldn't stop flowing.

"I..." she choked, "I folded so many stars."

"Every single one..." she said, "had a sentence written on it."

"'I want to be your friend.'"

"I..." she cried, "I put them into a jar and placed it in your locker."

"I hid at the stairway corner, secretly watching."

"I saw... you put it back."

"I thought..." she lowered her head, "you didn't like it."

"So... I threw them... all away."

"..." After hearing this, Qianyun's tears couldn't stop flowing.

"I'm sorry..." The two of them said it at the same time.

Then, both of them froze.

"Pfft—"

One of them laughed first, somehow.

"Haha..." Qianyun laughed, tears still flowing.

"Hahaha..." Zhixing laughed too, tears also still flowing.

"We... we're so silly." Zhixing said.

"Mhm." Qianyun nodded, "we... we really are silly."

"We both clearly... wanted to be friends with the other."

"Yet we both..." Qianyun said, "stood still, waiting for the other to come first."

"..." Zhixing looked down at the jar in her hands.

Inside the jar, that clay star was round and chubby, with a tiny smile at the corner of its mouth.

"This star..." Zhixing gently touched it, "you made it?"

"Mhm." Qianyun nodded, "When I was kneading it..." she said in a low voice, "I kneaded so many words into it."

"What words?" Zhixing asked.

"'I'm sorry.'" Qianyun said.

"'I didn't mean to ignore you.'"

"'I just... didn't know how to say it.'"

"'I actually... really want to be your friend.'"

"..." Zhixing listened, and her eyes reddened again.

"I..." she choked, hugging the jar to her chest, "I love it."

"..." Qianyun looked at her and smiled gently.

"Then..." she asked, "can we... make that 'starry sky' together?"

"We..." she said in a low voice, "together."

"..." Zhixing looked up at her.

Then Zhixing smiled.

"Okay." she said, "Let's... do it together."

"..." Qianyun smiled too.

At that moment, the sunlight in the classroom seemed... especially warm.

"Hey! You two made up?" The classmate sitting in front turned around to ask.

"We weren't fighting!" Zhixing said, blushing.

"That's right!" Qianyun followed, "we... it was just a misunderstanding!"

"Then it's good that the misunderstanding is cleared up!" the classmate said happily, "So... do you want to make the starry-sky exhibit piece together?"

"We've already decided!" Zhixing raised the jar in her hand, "we're going to make a 'starry-sky jar'!"

"Starry-sky jar?" The classmates gathered around.

"Mhm!" Zhixing said, "A clear bottle, filled with stars!"

"I'll fold paper stars!" she said.

"I'll make clay stars!" Qianyun said.

"Then..." the two of them said in unison, "we'll put all the stars into the bottle!"

"Wow! That sounds so cool!"

"Then can we make them too?"

"Let's all make them together!"

The classroom suddenly became lively.

"..." Qianyun watched all this.

Her heart felt warm.

"So this..." she said in a low voice, "is what it feels like to take that step."

"..." She looked down at the clay left in her hands.

"I think..." she said softly, with a gentle smile, "I've learned it."

For the next two weeks, every day after school, Qianyun and Zhixing stayed together in the art classroom.

Zhixing folded paper stars.

Qianyun kneaded clay stars.

"Your star's belly is so round!" Zhixing teased her.

"Your paper star's points aren't even sharp!" Qianyun teased her back.

"Haha!"

"Hahaha!"

Laughter rose and fell in the art classroom.

"..." The teacher passed by the door of the art classroom, watched them, and smiled softly.

On the day of the school fair, Class 6-1's "starry sky" piece won the Best Popularity Award.

On the exhibit table sat a huge, clear bottle.

The bottle was filled with stars.

Paper stars.

Clay stars.

Every single one was different.

Every single one was shining.

"So beautiful..."

"These stars... are all the ones we made!"

"Our class is amazing!"

The classmates crowded around the bottle, happily taking photos.

"..." Qianyun stood outside the crowd, watching that bottle.

"Qianyun!" Zhixing ran over and grabbed her hand, "come look too!"

"Ah... okay..." Qianyun was pulled by her into the crowd.

"Look!" Zhixing pointed at a clay star in the bottle, "that one! That's the first star you made!"

"Huh?" Qianyun looked closely, "it really is!"

"Its belly... is so round!" Zhixing said.

"Haha!" Qianyun laughed, "Because... at that time, I kneaded so many words into it!"

"What words?" Zhixing asked on purpose.

"..." Qianyun lowered her head and smiled.

"Secret." she said.

"Hey!" Zhixing puffed out her cheeks, "tell me!"

"No!" Qianyun shook her head, laughing and dodging away.

"Tell me, tell me!"

"No way!"

The two girls ran and laughed through the exhibition hall.

Sunlight poured through the window onto them.

"..." Qianyun ran, and suddenly stopped.

She turned back and looked at that bottle full of stars.

"Zhixing." she said softly.

"Mhm?" Zhixing turned around.

"..." Qianyun looked at her and smiled.

"Thank you." she said.

"Thank you..." she said in a low voice, "for being willing to wait for me."

"..." Zhixing was stunned for a moment.

Then Zhixing smiled.

"I should thank you too." she said.

"Thank you..." Zhixing said, "for being willing to take that step."

"..." The two of them looked at each other.

Then, together, they smiled.

That night, Qianyun went home and set down her backpack.

She opened her drawer and took out that little clay star.

Wait, no.

That star had already been given to Zhixing.

The one in her drawer was another one.

A star she had made later.

Small.

Shining.

With a smile at the corner of its mouth.

"This one..." she said in a low voice, "is the one I made for myself."

"Kneading into it..." she gently touched it, "'I can also take that step.'"

She put the star back into the drawer.

Then she walked to the window and looked at the night sky.

In the night sky, there were stars everywhere.

"..." She smiled.

"I wonder..." she said in a low voice, "tonight, are there two stars, leaning close together?"

The wind blew gently.

The leaves outside the window rustled.

—As if to say: "Yes, there are."

"They..."

"have been close together for a long time."
