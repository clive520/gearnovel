# Volume II: Letters of Empathy for Others
## Chapter 08: The Broken Half-Paper Umbrella Beneath the Eaves

#### 1

In the deep autumn of the mountain town, the night winds gusting through the river gorge carried a bone-chilling cold and the icy vapor of rushing mountain waters.

In the backyard of the Overdue Post Office stood a century-old chinaberry tree, its withered yellow leaves blanketing the wet bluestone ground in a thick layer. When Lin Ziyang pushed open the wooden door and walked through the rear veranda, he saw Old Gu standing alone beneath the dripping eaves with hands clasped behind his back, gazing for a long time at a curious object suspended from the rafter beam of the corridor pillar.

It was an oil-paper umbrella that had been battered into utter ruin, its original form scarcely recognizable.

The natural tung oil on the canopy paper had long dried and blackened, displaying dark brown mottling scoured by decades of weather; fully half of the cotton paper had rotted away across the years, exposing the bamboo ribs aligned in neat yet jaggedly fractured rows; on the sole surviving half of the torn canopy, one could still faintly discern pale ink strokes hand-painted with Southeast Asian natural mineral pigments, depicting distant mountains and withered lotus blossoms braving the rain.

Tightly bound to the neck joint where the handle met the ribs was an aged red cotton cord faded into pale pink.

Suspended from the lower end of the cord was a bundle of letters tightly wrapped in military-grade waterproof oilcloth, alongside an immaculate stack of bank receipts for overseas debt settlements, thin as cicada wings and bound with hemp twine.

"Old Gu, can this umbrella... still be called a letter?" Lin Ziyang stepped forward, his eyes falling upon those exposed bamboo ribs.

The ribs were carved with extraordinary delicacy and roundness, yet upon close inspection, the edges of each bamboo slat bore curious, rugged indentations—resembling marks left by a blunt rasp or teeth gnawing relentlessly over and over, radiating an artisan spirit of harrowing tragedy.

"A letter resides not in its outer appearance, but in the weight of devotion it carries." Old Gu extended a withered finger, lightly nudging the red cotton cord hanging beneath the handle, releasing a long sigh that pierced through decades of time. "This umbrella has hung upon the rafters of our post office for twenty-five whole years. Its sender was named Lu Chongshan, once a grandmaster of the famed 'Chongshan Oil-Paper Umbrellas' across the province; the recipient is named Luo Changsheng, who lives in an abandoned boatyard in the deepest recess of the river gorge downstream."

Lin Ziyang's heart leaped: "Luo Changsheng? You mean that old mute with the limp who spends his years patching fishing nets and doing rough carpentry down by the water, who hasn't uttered a single word in thirty years?"

"He is no mute; he bit his own tongue to pieces, not daring to speak a single complete sentence before another living soul for thirty years." Old Gu turned around, his eyes as fathomless as a thousand-fathom pool at the bottom of the gorge. "Thirty-five years ago, a catastrophic inferno burned the lives of both sworn brothers into charred black ash. Today marks the anniversary of Lu Chongshan's passing upon the Southern Seas. Go on, Ziyang. Take this broken half-umbrella to him. Tell Luo Changsheng that no matter how long the rains fall from heaven, there comes an hour when the sky clears; after serving thirty-five years of a life sentence in one's own heart, the time of pardon has finally arrived."

#### 2

"The inferno thirty-five years ago... what truly happened?" Lin Ziyang took the heavy, battered umbrella, feeling a bone-chilling coldness seeping into his palm.

"It was a living hell forged from greed, gambling addiction, and folly," Old Gu's voice was low and gravelly, pulling Lin Ziyang back to that horrifying midnight engulfed by fire.

"Thirty-five years ago, Lu Chongshan and Luo Changsheng were sworn brothers who grew up together in the mountain town. The Lu family had crafted natural tung-oil paper umbrellas for generations; by Lu Chongshan's era, his mastery had attained the sublime, with dignitaries and overseas Chinese across the province counting it an honor to collect a 'Chongshan Umbrella.' Luo Changsheng was a carpenter of singular talent, specializing in selecting deep-mountain aged moso bamboo and shaving the most delicate umbrella ribs for the Lu workshop."

"The two brothers were as close as flesh and blood, sharing hardships and joys alike. However, in the year Luo Changsheng turned thirty, he went astray and became addicted to the underground gambling dens. Within two short years, he gambled away his entire savings, sinking into catastrophic debts with loan sharks large enough to crush several lives. Underworld enforcers severed his little finger, threatening that if he failed to produce the money before New Year's Eve, they would hamstring him and sell his young wife and infant son into ruin."

"Driven to desperation, Luo Changsheng set his mad eyes upon the massive fire insurance policy the Lu workshop had just signed with a foreign trading firm. That night, he snuck into the storehouse packed with dozens of barrels of century-old tung oil and tens of thousands of aged bamboo canes, intending to start a localized fire to help his brother claim the insurance payout and take a cut to settle his debts."

An expression of extreme shock and pain surfaced in Old Gu's eyes:

"Yet he had no inkling that a mountain gale of rare ferocity would suddenly howl through the valley that night! A spark fell into an oil barrel, and the entire storehouse detonated in an apocalyptic series of explosions! Roaring like a demonic dragon, the flames shot into the clouds, staining half the mountain sky blood-red! The inferno raged for three days and three nights, reducing the centenary heritage of three generations and tens of thousands of masterwork umbrellas to cinders!"

"To salvage the ancestral handcrafted templates and rare manuals, Lu Chongshan ignored the screams and restraints of the villagers, plunging madly three times into the roaring sea of fire! When he was dragged from beneath the collapsing, burning rafters by the townsfolk, his face was unrecognizable from smoke, and those peerless hands that had carved countless legendary ribs were charred to the bone, the nerves in all ten fingers dead and carbonized, never again able to hold a carving knife!"

Lin Ziyang drew in a sharp breath of cold air, feeling as though his heart had been seized violently by an iron claw.

"And what of Luo Changsheng?"

"The day after the flames died down, unable to withstand the pressure of the debt collectors and his own mental collapse, Luo Changsheng turned himself in," Old Gu closed his eyes. "Upon learning that her husband had burned down the home of his sworn benefactor, Luo Changsheng's wife, consumed by despair and shame, jumped into Black Dragon Pool downstream with their two-year-old son; the child drowned, and the wife perished. In court, Luo Changsheng was sentenced to twelve years of hard imprisonment; while Lu Chongshan liquidated his ruined assets to compensate every neighbor affected, boarded a freighter bound for Southeast Asia with crippled hands and a chest full of bitter rancor, vanishing completely into the Taiwan Strait."

#### 3

"Twelve years later, Luo Changsheng was released from prison," Old Gu's voice echoed like the dying wind of a frigid night. "He did not return to his ancestral home, for it had long since become rubble. Limping on a shattered right leg, he wandered alone to this mud-choked boatyard downstream. He loathed himself to his very marrow, feeling that every breath he drew was a crime. With a pair of shears, he severed his own tongue, living for thirty years as a filthy, reeking mute, scraping by in the pitch-black muck."

"Go on, Ziyang," Old Gu patted the youth's shoulder. "Take this umbrella to him. Tell him that it is time for this fire to be extinguished."

The path leading down to the river gorge was steeper and far more desolate than any stone stairway.

Fine, cold rain gathered into murky yellow streams along the muddy trail; on either side, withered reeds grew taller than a man, whispering in the howling wind like weeping phantoms. Holding his black umbrella, Lin Ziyang picked his way with uneven steps until he entered that abandoned wooden boatyard forgotten by the world.

It was a dilapidated flat-bottomed wooden barge half-sunken in the silt of the riverbank.

The top of the cabin was haphazardly roofed with rusted sheets of corrugated iron and blackened asphalt felt, surrounded by rotting timber and frayed, rusted cables, the air thick with the sour stench of decaying mud and oxidized iron.

Cold rainwater dripped continuously from the edges of the corrugated iron, striking rusted iron drums with a hollow, monotonous *thump... thump...*, like a water clock counting down the seconds in the depths of a condemned cell.

Beneath the drafty, low-slung shelter of the hull, an emaciated old man, his frame little more than skin and bone, crouched in tattered rags upon the muddy ground.

His hair was disheveled like dried thatch, his face smeared with grime and carved with deep, jagged ravines, his right leg crippled and curled inward; his withered, blackened hand held a dull rasp, numbly, mechanically, and silently scraping a broken oar salvaged from the river.

Lin Ziyang folded his umbrella, stood at the edge of the battered barge, and spoke softly:

"Excuse me... are you Mr. Luo Changsheng?"

#### 4

The harsh, rasping scrape of metal against wood halted abruptly in that split second.

The old man's back stiffened with violent rigidity.

He did not turn around; like a dying animal targeted by a shotgun in a downpour, he arched his withered spine with agonizing slowness, his throat emitting short, ragged gasps—"huk... huk..."

Lin Ziyang stepped into the drafty shelter, laying the broken half-umbrella, the letter tied with red cotton cord, and that thick sheaf of remittance receipts neatly upon the only clean scrap of plank before the elder.

"I am Lin Ziyang, a courier from the Overdue Post Office," the youth crouched down, meeting the old man's profile. "This is a letter that Mr. Lu Chongshan entrusted us to deliver to you."

The instant the name "Lu Chongshan" fell upon his ears, Luo Changsheng shuddered as if struck by ten thousand volts of electricity!

*Clatter!*

The blunt rasp slipped from the carpenter's grip, plunging into the sludge.

As if possessed by madness, the old man snapped his head around; the moment his sunken, cloudy eyes collided with that broken half-umbrella and the red cotton cord, his pupils contracted to the size of pinpricks!

"Huk... AHH... AAAAAHHH!!"

An inhuman, shrill, and agonizing shriek erupted from the depths of the elder's throat!

Frantically clawing at his own face, he scrambled backward through the mud on hands and knees, overturning a battered wooden bucket, his spine slamming violently against the hard, cold timber of the hull!

Curling into a tight ball, his hands clutching his head, his forehead pressed against his knees, his entire frame shook in violent tremors. Spitting out frothy saliva and mud, he unleashed jagged, broken screams with his mangled tongue:

"No... don't kill me... don't find me... I am a sinner... I am the beast who burned my family to death!!"

For thirty years, he had disguised himself as a mute, but in this moment, a bone-deep guilt and terror tore his final facade to ribbons!

"Mr. Luo, look clearly: Mr. Chongshan passed away peacefully in Southeast Asia ten years ago," Lin Ziyang reached out, attempting to support the elder. "No one has come to kill you; no one has come to capture you!"

"He hates me... he must hate me to death!" Luo Changsheng tore at his gray, matted hair, his nails digging into his scalp until blood mingled with mud and streamed down his brow, weeping as though his chest were being ripped apart:

"Why did he send this broken umbrella?! He refuses to let me go even in hell, doesn't he?! He is reminding me of how I burned his hands into charcoal! He is reminding me that in this entire lifetime, I can never repay the half-life I owe him!"

#### 5

"You are mistaken, Mr. Luo Changsheng."

Lin Ziyang stood up, extending both hands, and untied that faded red cotton cord bound to the umbrella handle before the old man's eyes.

The youth gently unfastened the thick stack of documents bound with hemp twine, and along the broken plank, laid them out one by one before the elder.

There were exactly eighty-eight overseas bank remittance receipts.

The paper had long yellowed and grown brittle, yet atop every single receipt was stamped the dark blue official seal of the United Overseas Bank of Singapore or the Oversea-Chinese Banking Corporation of Kuala Lumpur. Spanning from the seventy-fifth year of the Republic to the ninety-fifth year—twenty full years, unbroken!

In the recipient column of every receipt were clearly written the names of all the neighbors whose homes were destroyed in the blaze, the creditors hounded by loan sharks, and all the victims ruined by Luo Changsheng's gambling debts!

In the remarks column, written in clumsy, crooked fountain pen script, was the exact same line:

"'Trustee: Lu Chongshan. Principal and interest settled on behalf of Luo Changsheng; debt cleared.'"

And on the back of the final receipt, eight bold characters were brushed with heavy black ink—"Debts Cleared, All Grievances Extinguished."

Luo Changsheng's wailing seized in his throat. His bloodshot, cloudy eyes stared transfixed at those eighty-eight stamped remittance slips, frozen like a statue of stone, his hands hovering over the mud in wild, uncontrollable trembling.

Lin Ziyang unfurled the letter wrapped immaculately within the waterproof oilcloth.

It was the dying testament that Lu Chongshan had penned from his sickbed in Southeast Asia, using those hands burned into stumps and deformed bone to grip the pen with agonizing effort.

The calligraphy was crooked and halting, yet radiated a majestic grace and compassion that saw through life and death:

"'Luo Changsheng, my younger brother:'

"'When you see this letter and this umbrella, your elder brother will have likely dissolved into a wisp of blue smoke over the Southern Seas.'

"'The inferno thirty-five years ago indeed burned my entire life into charcoal. My hands were crippled, my ancestral business eradicated; on my sickbed, I hated you so fiercely I longed to drink your blood and chew your bones! For five full years, I lived in bitter malice and despair; every time I looked upon these ruined hands, the venomous snake in my heart bit into my soul. Back then, I felt the entire world owed Lu Chongshan a life.'

"'Until the year I turned forty-five, when a catastrophic tsunami and typhoon struck the Southern Seas. Sitting in my wooden hut by the shore, I saw colossal waves flatten thousands of homes in an instant, and saw countless souls weeping amidst the rubble for their lost kin. That night, watching the roaring tempests, a divine revelation struck my soul, and I awoke completely—'

"'Is not hatred itself an unquenchable inferno?! Your fire back then burned my workshop and my flesh; but if I tormented myself with hatred for a lifetime, I would be casting my own soul into the eternal fire with my own hands!'"

#### 6

Lin Ziyang's voice was resonant and deep, every word accompanied by the fine patter of rain outside, knocking heavily upon the chamber of Luo Changsheng's heart that had lain dead for thirty years:

"'Changsheng, I let it go. I no longer hate you. I forgive you, not because your sins can be erased, but because your brother refuses to remain a prisoner of hatred any longer.'

"'When I learned that after leaving prison you hid in an abandoned boatyard in the gorge to mutilate yourself and live as a mute, enduring a living death, my heart bled as though sliced by knives. You thought you were atoning, but that was merely locking yourself cowardly inside a prison of agony to indulge your own grief!'

"'The debts you owed those people back then—over these twenty years, I opened workshops and took apprentices in Southeast Asia, guiding them with these crippled hands, repaying every cent of principal and interest on your behalf! Eighty-eight remittance slips: the law punished you for twelve years, the victims received their hard-earned money back; what you owed this world was settled long ago!'

"'And what you owed your brother... Changsheng, today I return it to you along with this broken half-umbrella.'

"'This umbrella is something I crafted with these crippled hands over three full months, shaving it stroke by stroke with a rasp. The world loves a whole umbrella because it shields them from wind and rain; yet I insist on leaving you this broken half-umbrella. Look closely—'"

Lin Ziyang held aloft that broken half-umbrella with both hands, raising it above Luo Changsheng's head:

"'Even though half the canopy is missing and the rain drenches your clothes, as long as you straighten your spine and lift your head, through these bare bamboo ribs, you can still behold the vast, boundless blue sky above!'

"'Brother, in walking this mortal life, who among us never errs? Tormenting yourself is not atonement; standing tall and living well is! Your brother ceased hating you thirty years ago. Rise up from the dark, moldering mud! The rain has stopped long ago; step out of the boat shelter, and go out into the sunshine!'"

#### 7

The reading of the letter came to an end.

A tomb-like stillness fell over the dilapidated boat shelter, broken only by the drip of rain striking the iron drums, sounding one by one like the morning bells of a sanctuary.

Luo Changsheng remained frozen in place, giant tears splashing heavily into the mud, blurring the five characters "I no longer hate you" into warm pools of moisture.

Thirty-five years.

More than twelve thousand days and nights.

He had hidden like a maggot in this sunless boatyard, eating moldy scraps, drinking icy mud, letting his broken leg fester, deliberately biting his tongue to speak no human word. He had punished himself with the cruelest physical and mental tortures, believing this was atonement, believing his life deserved to rot away in this filth.

Yet that very elder brother whose hands he had burned away, who had been robbed of everything, had used his crippled hands across the distant ocean to clear every worldly debt on his behalf, and at the end of his days, had hand-carved this broken half-umbrella, placing into his hands the key to the light!

"Brother... BROTHER CHONGSHAN——!!!"

An agonizing wail suppressed for thirty-five years, sundering every chain binding his soul, finally tore from the old carpenter's throat!

Falling to his knees, Luo Changsheng lifted the broken half-umbrella with trembling hands, pressing the cold, rough bamboo ribs desperately against his forehead, weeping inconsolably!

The bamboo ribs bore no scent of charred timber, only the pure botanical perfume of aged moso bamboo seasoned by decades, alongside the elder brother's final magnanimity and boundless mercy left behind in this mortal world.

Forgiveness is never a casual brushing away of blame, much less an indulgence of evil.

Forgiveness is a divine miracle whereby the wounded, through a boundless breadth of spirit beyond ordinary men, severs the venomous chains of hatred with his own hands, delivering both himself and his tormentor from an endless hell of agony.

To hate another is to drink poison while hoping the other will perish; only forgiveness allows the wasteland scorched into charred ruins by fire to blossom once more into a flourishing forest.

A long time passed before the weeping gradually subsided.

With his mud-stained, rough sleeve, Luo Changsheng wiped away the tears and grime from his face.

Clutching the handle of that broken half-umbrella in both hands, using it as a cane to support the right leg that had been crippled for thirty years, with agonizing effort yet unyielding resolve, he slowly, slowly straightened the spine that had been bowed for half a lifetime!

For the first time in thirty years, he took a step forward and walked out of that dark, moldering boat shelter.

At some point, the autumn drizzle over the river gorge had ceased completely.

A crisp, clean mountain breeze blew away the mist over the water, and tens of thousands of brilliant golden sunbeams burst through the heavy cloud cover, cascading down upon the rushing creek and the lush expanse of reeds!

The old man lifted his head, holding aloft that broken half-umbrella with both hands.

The warm, pure sunlight streamed through the bare bamboo ribs without reservation, spilling onto his furrowed face, turning the tear tracks at the corners of his eyes into radiant, sparkling gold.

Standing upon the stone bridge in the distance, Lin Ziyang watched that figure reborn under the sun, his eyes brimming with tears.

The young postman turned around, stepping onto the bluestone path back to the mountain town. Behind him, the autumn skies stretched high and clear; the courier's path of Volume II drew to a triumphant close beneath the golden breeze, and the youth's soul stood indestructible, baptized in the sacred grace of forgiveness.

### 【信差迴響 / Messenger's Echo】
> **本章品德力量：【寬恕（Forgiveness）】**
> **Virtue of this Chapter: 【Forgiveness】**
>
> 仇恨是一柄雙刃的毒刃，在刺穿他人的同時，更將自己的靈魂囚禁於暗無天日的煉獄；而寬恕則是神聖的救贖之光，它解開的是束縛在雙方心靈上的沉重枷鎖。
> Hatred is a double-edged venomous blade; while piercing another, it imprisons one's own soul within a sunless purgatory. Forgiveness, however, is the sacred light of redemption, unlocking the heavy shackles binding both hearts.
>
> 真正的寬恕，並非忘記受過的傷痛，而是選擇不再讓過去的灰燼定義未來的生命；它不是軟弱的妥協，而是唯有極致強大與慈悲的靈魂，才能施予這世間的最高禮物。
> True forgiveness does not mean forgetting the wounds endured, but choosing to no longer let the ashes of the past define the life ahead; it is not feeble compromise, but the supreme gift that only an extraordinarily strong and merciful soul can bestow upon this mortal realm.
>
> 當我們學會放下仇恨、以寬恕之心看待人生的殘缺時，心靈的焦土便能迎來破曉的晨曦；縱使命運如半把殘傘，也能迎著陽光，看見整片無垠的晴空。
> When we learn to lay down hatred and gaze upon the imperfections of life with a forgiving heart, the scorched earth of the soul can welcome the dawn of daybreak; even if destiny resembles a broken half-umbrella, one can face the sunlight and behold the entire boundless sky.
