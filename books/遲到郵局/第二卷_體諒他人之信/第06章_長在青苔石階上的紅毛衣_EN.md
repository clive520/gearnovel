# Volume II: Letters of Empathy for Others
## Chapter 06: The Red Sweater Grown Upon Mossy Stone Steps

#### 1

The autumn rains of the mountain town always carried a dampness that seeped into one's very marrow, the fine, icy drizzle resembling countless transparent silk threads weaving the tiered black-tiled roofs, the mottled stone retaining walls, and the steep, narrow stone stairways into an enormous, airtight gray net.

At six in the morning, the standing water in the courtyard of the Overdue Post Office caught a faint, leaden-gray daylight. Lin Ziyang stood before the camphor sorting counter; aside from the aroma of vintage printing ink and humid paper, an extraordinarily distinct fragrance—a slightly bitter perfume mingling deep mountain camphor with aged pure wool—hung in the air, faintly eclipsing the scent of ink.

Old Gu sat upon that aged rattan armchair polished smooth by decades, gold-rimmed reading spectacles perched upon the bridge of his nose, holding in hand a delicate pair of brass pruning shears, meticulously tending to a pot of dendrobium orchids braving the cold drizzle beside the window lattice.

The old postmaster did not raise his head, merely speaking in a voice as gravelly and deep as tumbling mountain stones: "Ziyang, go and slide open drawer number twelve. The cast-iron box at the very bottom, wrapped in three layers of tung-oiled yellow waxed cloth—today is the day it should be unsealed."

Lin Ziyang complied, releasing the rusted latches of the iron box. Peeling back the oil-stained, hardened layers of yellow waxed cloth, what lay inside was not an ordinary envelope, but a fist-sized ball of vintage knitting yarn faded into dark crimson.

The wool fibers had long since become felted and slightly frayed, yet into the central core of this yarn ball were inserted two smooth, jade-like bamboo knitting needles. At the blunt ends of the needles, the character "Xiu" was carved with a fine graver, its edges worn soft and round by time.

Tightly wedged into the deep crevices between the bamboo needles and the wound yarn rested a sheet of yellowed paper folded into the size of a tofu square, its four corners frayed and fringed.

Holding his breath, Lin Ziyang gingerly extracted the paper. Unfurling it beneath the warm glow of the desk lamp, he saw that it was a memorandum written in rich black ink with a stiff-bristled calligraphy brush. The brushstrokes, though forceful and upright, harbored an uncontrollable, violent tremor, and at the very top stood an alarming, startling title—"Little Sister, I Will Not Sign the Property Division Agreement."

The dated signature read the late autumn of the seventy-second year of the Republic, exactly forty-three years ago.

"Old Gu, is this a letter refusing to divide estate properties?" Lin Ziyang looked at the icy words, his brows furrowing slightly. "I have seen far too many tragedies in the outside world where siblings turn against one another over ancestral inheritances. Why would a memorandum refusing to divide a household, filled with bitter strife, be deposited in this post office?"

Setting down the brass shears, Old Gu slowly rose and walked over to the sorting counter. His withered fingers gently brushed over the dark red ball of yarn, his eyes revealing a profound, fathomless sorrow.

"Worldly eyes looking upon this slip of paper see only greed, refusal, and contention; yet beneath the eaves of an ancestral home, certain stubborn refusals as unyielding as cast iron are, in truth, an elder sister's lifelong forbearance and magnanimous concession, swallowed deep into the very earth," Old Gu said softly. "Go on. The address on the letter is No. 37 Xinan Lane; the recipient is named Su Yujiao. Bring rain gear; those moss-covered stone stairs are slicker than the blade of a knife."

#### 2

Slung with the heavy green canvas mailbag, Lin Ziyang hoisted a large black cloth umbrella and set foot upon the Xinan Lane stone steps leading into the mountain's deepest recesses.

Half a month of continuous drizzle had coated both flanks of the steep stone stairway with slick, dark-green moss. With every step forward, the rubber treads of his boots had to grip the wet stone surface with utmost care, for a single misstep would send a traveler tumbling into the gorge below. Most of the stone dwellings along the way kept their doors and windows shuttered tight, their mottled rammed-earth walls and dark roof tiles standing like silent, decrepit elders in the misty rain.

No. 37 Xinan Lane was a two-story vintage red-brick residence nestled against the sheer bluestone cliffside. Scoured by decades of mountain rains, the red bricks bore black patches of moss stains, and the exterior walls of the courtyard were overgrown with wild, lush climbing figs and creeping ivy.

The wooden lattice gate, fashioned from cypress slats, was left ajar, its hinges emitting sour, faint groans of "creak, creak" in the damp, cold mountain breeze.

Lin Ziyang pushed open the wooden gate and entered the courtyard. Several pots of petunias blooming in dark purple desolation rested on the bluestone pavement; beneath the long, sheltering overhang of the porch hallway, a silver-haired woman with a slightly stooped figure sat poised on an old bamboo rocking chair.

Across her lap rested a washed-pale, thin woolen blanket with frayed hems, gold-rimmed reading spectacles resting upon her thin, aquiline nose.

In her hands, the elderly woman held a pair of slender steel knitting needles, working stitch by stitch by the faint, cold daylight filtering under the eaves, knitting a light-gray gentleman's scarf with agonizing slowness. Her movements were sluggish, her knuckle joints swollen and deformed from rheumatism, pausing for half a second with every stitch she threaded.

Folding his umbrella and shaking off the raindrops, Lin Ziyang broke the courtyard's hush in a quiet voice: "Excuse me... are you Teacher Su Yujiao?"

The steel needles in the old woman's hands jolted violently.

Slowly she raised her head, revealing through her spectacles a pair of eyes steeped in vicissitudes and stern reserve. Though well past seventy years of age, her brow still preserved the unmistakable pride, aloofness, and inviolable coldness of a retired elementary school teacher.

"I am," her voice was icy, bristling with a defensive guard that held visitors at arm's length. "Young man, are you from the post office? Our family hasn't received a registered parcel requiring hand-delivery in many years."

Stepping forward, Lin Ziyang retrieved the cast-iron box from his canvas satchel, lifted away the waxed cloth, and presented the dark red yarn ball, the two bamboo knitting needles, and the tucked-in note in both hands before the elder.

"This is a letter that has been overdue for forty-three whole years," Lin Ziyang spoke with utmost calm and solemn reverence. "The sender is... your elder sister, Ms. Su Xiufen."

#### 3

The moment the three syllables "Su Xiufen" drifted into the air, the elderly woman froze as though struck by an invisible bolt of lightning, her very breath seizing in her throat.

*Clack.*

The ball of gray yarn resting upon her lap rolled off, tumbling across the slick bluestone pavement into a mud-splattered puddle between the stones, instantly soiled with grime.

Su Yujiao stared immovably at the dark red yarn ball in the iron box, stared at the two bamboo knitting needles carved with the character "Xiu." Her withered, pale lips began to twitch and tremble violently, every trace of color draining from her countenance within mere seconds!

"Take it away... GET IT AWAY FROM ME!"

Like a hedgehog whose tail had been viciously trampled, Su Yujiao sprang up from the bamboo rocking chair! Her hands gripped the bamboo armrests with white-knuckled frenzy, her voice skyrocketing into a near-hysterical shriek:

"I don't have an elder sister! I ceased having an elder sister decades ago! That thoroughly selfish, stone-hearted woman, who watched her own sister on the verge of destruction without lifting a single finger, severed all bonds with me forty-three years ago!"

The old woman's chest heaved frantically, angry red veins webbing her eyes, plunged into uncontrollable fury and hysterical defense:

"She died ten years ago! Died alone in this house without a child to see her into the earth—that was her retribution! What is she sending this rubbish to me for now? To gloat that she guarded this wretched house until her dying day? Take it away! Don't soil my sight!"

Confronted by the old woman's venomous tirade, Lin Ziyang neither retreated nor pulled his hands back. He simply held the iron box steady, standing upright beneath the drafty, rain-swept hallway, his gaze tranquil and deep as he watched Su Yujiao.

"Teacher Su, if this were truly a lump of cold, selfish rubbish, it would not have been hidden in the deepest recess of an elder's drawer, wrapped in three layers of waxed cloth, unraveled, and stroked over and over for forty-three whole years."

Lin Ziyang's voice was resonant and anchored: "This ball of yarn was originally a garment. You grew up in this ancestral residence as well; do you truly fail to recognize what this yarn is?"

Su Yujiao was pinned to the spot by Lin Ziyang's words.

As if guided by some irresistible force, her gaze was compelled to fall upon that dark red ball of yarn once again.

With a single glance, the old woman's soul was shattered as though by a thunderbolt.

That was no ordinary crimson yarn. That was the pure wool yarn from the heavy crimson sweater that her elder sister, Su Xiufen, had knitted with her own hands beneath the dim light of a kerosene lamp in the winter of the early sixties, when she passed the entrance exams for the Taipei Women's Normal Junior College with top honors!

#### 4

Those memories of her youth, deliberately entombed for half a century, carved open the old woman's chest like a rusted, blunt knife.

Back then, their household was so destitute they could barely afford sweet potato scraps to fill their bellies. Their father had suffered a catastrophic roof-fall in the auriferous copper mines, his spine shattered by boulders, leaving him paraplegic and bedridden for years; their mother had passed away early from overwork. As the elder sister, Su Xiufen was only twenty at the time, yet she broke off her own betrothal without hesitation to shoulder the broken family on her own.

Before dawn, her elder sister would balance two heavy wooden tubs of homemade pickled vegetables on her shoulders, trudging step by treacherous step down the slick stone steps to peddle them at the morning market; by day, she washed grease-blackened bedding and sheets for wealthy households at the tailor shop; by night, beneath the choking fumes of a kerosene lamp, she piece-worked sweaters for textile factories, working until her eyes burned red and her fingers were encrusted in thick calluses.

Cent by cent, her elder sister gathered copper coins to pay for her junior and senior high school tuition.

The day the acceptance letter reached the mountain town, the entire village rejoiced. The winter of that year was unimaginably frigid, with even the stone stairways glazed in thick frost. Her elder sister retrieved every cent of the private savings she had secretly hoarded for two whole years, combed through every import merchant in Keelung, and bought this pound of the costliest, warmest pure Australian wool yarn.

Beneath the dim oil lamp, using the bamboo needles hand-whittled by their father, her elder sister worked stitch by stitch for three full weeks, crafting this smooth, thick, flame-red sweater.

Stroking her little sister's frost-nipped cheeks, her elder sister had smiled proudly to the neighbors: "Our Yujiao is going to Taipei to become a teacher; she will stand before the blackboard to receive bows of respect from hundreds of pupils. She cannot wear patched rags to be looked down upon. My sister must be dressed with utter dignity and warmth."

That scene had once been the proudest, warmest foundation of Su Yujiao's life.

Yet all of that beauty was mercilessly crushed to dust on that stormy late-autumn night forty-three years ago.

"Yes... I recognize this yarn," Su Yujiao trembled like an autumn leaf in the gale, tears bursting from her eyes, yet laced with bone-deep rancor and grievance. "And precisely because I recognize it, I despise her all the more!"

"Forty-three years ago, my husband in Taipei was swindled after standing as a guarantor for a business venture. Our trading firm collapsed into bankruptcy, loan sharks splattered red paint across our doorway, the court moved to foreclose on our apartment, and we couldn't even pay next semester's tuition for my two children... Our family had reached the end of the road. I knelt in the pouring rain at Taipei Main Station with my two terrified children, on the very verge of swallowing pesticide to end our lives!"

Tears carved shining rivers along the old woman's deep furrows, her voice choked and broken with sobs:

"I boarded the overnight train back to the mountain town, threw myself onto these stone steps, and begged her! I begged her to sell this two-story brick house left by our father so we two sisters could split the proceeds! As long as I received the three hundred thousand dollars of my share, I could save my family, save my two children!"

"And what did she do? She stood before this very door, in front of dozens of neighbors from the entire village, and tore the property division agreement to shreds! Pointing her finger in my face, she cursed me as an ungrateful wretch selling ancestral roots, screaming that I had no regard for family heritage!"

"That night, the rain was ten times fiercer than today! She shoved me out of this gate with her own hands and bolted the heavy crossbar! In the muddy floodwaters outside, I knelt for three whole hours with my shivering children, bashing my forehead bloody against the stones, begging her to crack the door open even a sliver... But until daybreak, that door never budged so much as a fraction of an inch!"

Clutching her chest, Su Yujiao slumped limply against the bamboo chair: "That night, my heart died forever! I borrowed from loan sharks, took on multiple tutoring jobs, and scraped through the bankruptcy like a dog. I swore to heaven that if I, Su Yujiao, ever recognized her as my sister again, may I die without a peaceful death!"

#### 5

"You knelt for three hours and hated her for forty-three years. But do you know what Ms. Su Xiufen was doing behind that closed door that night?"

Lin Ziyang stepped forward, extending both hands, and slowly unfurled the reverse side of that folded memorandum before the old woman's eyes.

Densely brushed across the back were faded lines of regular script—a testament of blood and tears penned on the reverse of that torn agreement, never once mailed.

Lifting his head, Lin Ziyang looked calmly upon the weeping Su Yujiao, and amidst the sighs of wind and rain, read aloud the words with ringing clarity, syllable by syllable:

"'Little sister, I will not sign the property division agreement.'

"'The neighbors are stabbing my spine with their tongues, cursing me as cold-hearted, saying I hoard this pile of crumbling bricks rather than helping my own sister survive her trial. Yujiao, go ahead and hate me. Every bitter curse you shouted at me in the storm tonight, I have driven into my heart with an awl.'

"'Yet there is something you do not know. The night our father took his final breath, his withered hand gripped my arm, reminding me three full times from his sickbed: Yujiao has been brilliant and proud since childhood, her spirit fierce and her heart too direct; plunging into that swirling cesspool and money pit of Taipei, she is bound to suffer deception and cruelty. No matter how dazzling the world out there may be, it cannot compare to the grounded shelter of this ancestral soil.'

"'Father said: Xiufen, this old house, even if we are reduced to begging for scraps on the street, must never, ever be sold! As long as this old house stands, Yujiao will always have her roots in this mountain town! Should there come a day when she shatters her head out there, swindled out of everything, with nowhere left to turn... as long as she climbs these mossy stone steps, there will always be a sunlit eastern wing waiting for her, always a sunned quilt to warm her, always a hot meal waiting on the table!'

"'If the old house is divided and sold, and those few hundred thousand dollars are poured into that bottomless hole of debt in Taipei only to be swallowed whole, where would you go when life collapses upon you next? In this vast world under heaven, who else would hold open a door to shield you from the wind and rain?!'"

Lin Ziyang's voice was deep and powerful, each word striking like a sledgehammer upon Su Yujiao's heart.

The old woman's sobs ceased abruptly. She stared blankly at Lin Ziyang, her hands frozen mid-air.

Taking a steady breath, Lin Ziyang continued reading:

"'You curse me for being greedy and selfish; I take all of that blame upon my shoulders. But this old house—your sister never hoarded a single inch for herself! This very afternoon, before you departed for Taipei, I walked on my crutches to the township office and notarized the deed of ownership—the title of this entire house was registered completely and solely in your name, Su Yujiao! The day I close my eyes forever, the entire ancestral home will belong to you alone; your sister will not take a single copper coin with her!'

"'That night, while you knelt outside in the downpour for three hours, your sister sat slumped on the cold earthen floor behind the door, weeping for three hours. All ten of my fingernails dug into the wood until they bled... Your sister longed to open that door and hold you, but I knew that if my heart softened and I signed that deed, your very last lifeline would be severed by my own hands!'

"'I will not sign the agreement; I will bear the shame. As long as you can endure in Taipei, as long as this house still stands, your sister will keep vigil for you atop these stone steps, guarding it until the day I turn to dust...'"

#### 6

"The deed... the deed was in my name all along?" Su Yujiao muttered in a daze, her lips drained of every ounce of blood.

"Ten years ago when your sister passed away and the township office transferred the property directly into your name, did you always believe it was her deathbed guilt and compensation?" Lin Ziyang looked at her, his eyes laden with boundless sorrow. "You were mistaken. That was no compensation; that was what she arranged for you on that very stormy afternoon forty-three years ago, before she shoved you out the door!"

Lin Ziyang reached out and lifted that dark red ball of yarn from the iron box.

Beneath the elder's gaze, the youth gently tugged at the loose strand of yarn. As the coils slowly unraveled, three tiny folded paper slips drifted down from the middle layers and core of the yarn ball!

The first slip of paper was pale yellow, its brushwork dated the seventy-eighth year of the Republic:

"'Yujiao, word reached me that your trading debts have finally been paid off. The weather is growing cold; standing to teach at school all day will make your knees ache. Your sister unraveled the crimson sweater I knitted for you back then, wanting to remake it into thick woolen knee-warmers to mail to Taipei. But halfway through knitting, I remembered your vow never to see me again... Fearing you would grow angry seeing this yarn, I unraveled it once more.'"

The second slip was dated the eighty-third year of the Republic:

"'Yujiao, your eldest boy must have entered university this year, hasn't he? Your sister was so overjoyed she burned incense before our father's grave. This red yarn has been unraveled and knitted, knitted and unraveled by your sister so many times the fiber is turning soft as down. Your sister is old now, her eyes too dim to thread a needle, but I still want to knit it into a long scarf. If you ever return to the mountain town one day, wrapping it around you, you won't feel cold.'"

The third slip was written six months before her elder sister's death:

"'Yujiao, your sister may not live to see the day you push open this door. I leave the yarn for you in the drawer; the two bamboo needles were whittled by our father's own hands. Little sister, in this entire life, your sister never did anything to wrong you. I didn't sign the agreement because... your sister loved you so dearly...'"

The paper slips lay scattered across the bluestone pavement.

Su Yujiao's entire frame convulsed in violent spasms. Reaching out with withered, trembling hands, she frantically clutched those three tiny slips of paper, clutched that ball of dark red yarn that had been unraveled dozens of times and softened by decades of tears, pressing them desperately against her chest!

"SISTER——!!!"

An agonizing wail suppressed for forty-three years, tearing through flesh and soul, finally shattered all the defense and pride deep within the old woman's throat, exploding into the rain-swept courtyard!

#### 7

The old woman collapsed upon the cold bluestone ground, kneeling in the puddles, clutching that ball of crimson yarn, weeping until the heavens and earth seemed to crumble around her.

"Sister! How could I have been so blind... How could I have been so vicious?!"

"Forty-three years... I hated you for forty-three years; I cursed you for forty-three years! I thought I was the victim, thought I had suffered all the grievances under heaven... when all along, the one protected best in this world was me!"

"You guarded my roots, you guarded my hearth, you bore the black blame for an entire lifetime... yet I never even served you a bowl of warm water; I didn't even return to see you laid into the earth!"

Burying her silver head deep into the dark red wool, Su Yujiao wept until her shoulders shook with violent tremors. That ball of yarn exuded the dry camphor fragrance unique to the ancestral home, piercing through forty years of dust and time, still carrying the lingering warmth of her elder sister's palms.

Magnanimous concession is never cowardly compromise; tolerance is by no means feeble surrender.

It is the supreme love that prefers to be misunderstood by those held dearest, prefers to be sliced by ten thousand blades, yet grits its teeth to shield the ultimate dignity and survival of one's kin to the very end.

A long time passed before the weeping gradually subsided.

Supported by Lin Ziyang, Su Yujiao struggled to her feet on trembling legs. Without wiping the grime and tears from her face, she clutched the two warm bamboo needles in both hands, turned around, and walked step by agonizing step toward the wooden door at the easternmost end of the hallway.

It was the door to the eastern wing that had been locked for ten whole years.

Ever since moving back to the ancestral home ten years ago, consumed by lingering rancor, she had locked this door tight, never setting foot inside for a decade.

*Creak——*

The heavy wooden door was slowly pushed open by the elder's trembling hands.

Natural daylight streamed into the room from the courtyard, dust motes dancing in quiet grace within the golden shaft of light.

The room was kept exceptionally pristine, the sun-facing windowsill wiped spotless. Upon the wide wooden bed rested a neatly folded, thick cotton quilt smelling cleanly of sun-baked cotton; on the bedside cabinet sat a pair of red embroidered shoes that Su Yujiao had worn as a six-year-old girl, alongside a set of pure-wool red scarves and red mittens knitted but never mailed.

Everything here, just as on that stormy night forty-three years ago, stood immaculate, warm, and bright—waiting in gentle silence for that headstrong, petulant sister to push open the door and come home.

Stepping into the chamber, Su Yujiao touched the warm bedding, tears cascading silently once more; yet this time, a peaceful, unburdened smile finally surfaced upon the elder's face.

Lin Ziyang quietly retreated from the courtyard, gently drawing the wooden gate closed.

At some point, the overcast drizzle had quietly ceased. A brilliant beam of pale golden sunlight broke through the heavy clouds, piercing the mist, bathing the slick stone steps of Xinan Lane in warmth.

The dark green moss in the crevices sparkled with crystal dew under the sun, like time washed clean of all dust and sorrow.

Touching the weighty green mailbag at his hip, the youth strode toward the old street against the breeze, feeling an unprecedented expanse of peace and breadth surging within his chest.

> **本章品德力量：【謙讓與包容（Generosity & Tolerance）】**
> **Virtue of this Chapter: 【Generosity & Tolerance】**
>
> 世人常將「寸土必爭」視為剛強，把「得理不饒」當作尊嚴；然而世間最深厚、最崇高的情感，往往隱藏在看似頑固的退讓與無聲的承受之中。
> The world often mistakes litigious contention for strength and relentless self-assertion for dignity; yet the most profound and sublime affections under heaven are often concealed within seemingly stubborn concessions and silent endurance.
>
> 真正的包容，不是盲目的順從與苟同，而是在面對至親骨肉的不解、怨懟甚至詛咒時，寧願自己背負一身罵名，也要護其一世周全的堅忍；真正的謙讓，是不計個人得失榮辱，用自己粗糙厚實的肩膀，為他人死死守住最後一扇避風港灣的深情。
> Genuine tolerance is not blind submission or feeble surrender, but the fortitude that willingly bears a lifetime of curses and blame when faced with misunderstanding, resentment, and even curses from one's own flesh and blood, solely to keep them safe and whole for a lifetime; genuine generosity is looking past personal loss and glory, using one's rough, broad shoulders to safeguard the final sanctuary for another.
>
> 愛的至高境界，是化爭奪為成全，在漫長誤解的歲月長河中，始終為你保留一間曬透了棉被的向陽廂房。
> The supreme realm of love is to transform contention into fulfillment, and through the long decades of misunderstanding, to always keep a sunlit wing, warm with sun-baked quilts, waiting for you to return home.
