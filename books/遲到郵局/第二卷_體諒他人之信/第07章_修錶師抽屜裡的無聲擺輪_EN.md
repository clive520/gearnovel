# Volume II: Letters of Empathy for Others
## Chapter 07: The Silent Balance Wheel in the Watchmaker's Drawer

#### 1

The mist over the mountain town gradually thickened in the late autumn afternoon, murky and humid gray vapor drifting lazily along the winding flagstone lanes, submerging the entire vintage street hanging halfway up the mountainside as though steeped in a bowl of bitter, cold tea.

The back room of the Overdue Post Office was exceptionally quiet—so quiet that all that remained was an exceedingly faint, almost ruthlessly rhythmic "tick, tock." It was not the hollow, dull strike of the aged pendulum clock in the front hall, but emanated from a dark walnut wood case lined with black velvet upon Old Gu's desk.

A slender, high-magnification monocular loupe was clamped to the bridge of Old Gu's nose, his right eye squinting slightly, holding in his hand a soft-bristled brush specially fashioned from camel hair, meticulously and with agonizing gentleness dusting an antique silver-cased pocket watch within the wooden casket.

To the right of the pocket watch quietly lay a pair of ultra-fine micro-adjusting tweezers whose tips had worn slightly dull, their steel frame gleaming with a warm, somber luster; while beneath the watch was pressed a sheet of manuscript tissue soaked in translucent horological oil, thin as cicada wings and gleaming with an amber patina.

"Old Gu, today's parcel... is a broken watch?" Lin Ziyang stepped forward, his eyes falling upon that pocket watch refracting a frigid metallic gleam.

The silver rim of the case was engraved with intricate, antique scrollwork using exquisite techniques, yet behind the spotless, curved crystal glass, those two tempered blued steel hands were frozen motionless at three-fifteen in the afternoon, as though paralyzed by an invisible, lethal hex.

"This is no broken watch; this is an artisan's heart that chose to cease beating for forty-one whole years at the very zenith of its brilliance, solely to complete another soul."

Old Gu gently dislodged the monocular loupe from his eye and rubbed his bloodshot eyelids, his tone steeped in a vast, fathomless sorrow spanning half a century: "Forty-one years ago, two master watchmakers famed throughout the entire province emerged from the old street of this mountain town. One was named Tang Bingzheng, and the other was Fang Jingting. They were apprenticed together under the same master horologist, Master Lu, since the age of eight—sharing from the same bowl, sleeping on the same communal cot, and learning the craft stroke by stroke beneath the same kerosene lamp."

"The elder apprentice, Fang Jingting, was as steady as a deep mountain pool, meticulous in thought, humble and generous to all; his junior brother, Tang Bingzheng, was a prodigy of peerless talent, yet born with an unbending, arrogant fire that held the entire world in contempt. Forty-one years ago, the provincial capital hosted the province's inaugural Swiss Horological Exhibition trials; grandmasters gathered from every corner, yet there was only a single sponsored fellowship to pursue a Master Horologist degree in Geneva."

Old Gu closed the walnut box, his withered fingers resting upon the deep grain of the timber: "Go on, Ziyang. Take this watch, these tweezers, and the memoirs penned by Fang Jingting before his death, and place them personally into the hands of Tang Bingzheng at 'Precision Horology' at the end of the old street. Tang Bingzheng turns seventy-nine this year; go tell him that what ceased beating forty-one years ago was never Fang Jingting's craftsmanship, but his own heart that has been blinded by conceit for an entire lifetime."

#### 2

"Precision Horology" at the far end of the old street lay nestled in the depths of a row of weathered, vintage colonnade shophouses.

Pushing open the wooden-lattice glass door hung with a brass wind chime, what rushed over him was a cold metallic aroma mingling horological lubricating oil, cleaning naphtha, dry file shavings, and aged timber.

Across the four walls of the shop hung over a hundred antique mechanical wall clocks of varying sizes, hailing from diverse eras. Bronze pendulums swung in counterpoint under the dim daylight, and hundreds of microscopic gears meshed furiously within their movements, interweaving into a torrential, suffocating downpour of "tick-tick-tick-tick" time.

Before a raised workbench lined with a dark green anti-static rubber mat, an elderly man with hair and beard frosted with white sat bent over his task.

A black elastic headband held a ten-power binocular magnifier against his forehead; his left hand gripped an ultra-fine hairspring stud with pure silver tweezers, while his right held an oiling needle finer than an embroidery pin, applying a microscopic droplet of synthetic lubricant into the jewel cup of a ruby bearing with razor precision.

His hands were astonishingly steady, resembling mountain bedrock weathered by storms, without the slightest tremor of muscle.

Lin Ziyang stepped forward, the wooden floorboards beneath his soles emitting a faint creak.

"If you're neither buying nor repairing, get out. Don't block my light at the door."

The old man did not even lift his head, his voice cold as cast iron, bristling with the imperious arrogance of one secluded in isolation for decades: "My hands, the hands of Tang Bingzheng, do not touch trash under a hundred dollars, nor do they repair electronic quartz garbage. Beat it. Don't soil my hairsprings with your coarse breath."

Lin Ziyang did not retreat. With serene composure, he walked to the edge of the workbench, rested the weighty dark walnut box gently on the green rubber mat, and opened the lid.

"Master Tang, I have not come to buy a watch, nor to repair one," Lin Ziyang said in an anchored tone. "I am a courier from the Overdue Post Office, come to deliver a letter that has been overdue for forty-one whole years."

#### 3

"The Overdue Post Office?"

His brows knit tight, Tang Bingzheng lifted his head with manifest annoyance and disdain. Yet, when his gaze pierced through the binocular magnifier and brushed past the silver pocket watch and the micro-adjusting tweezers resting upon the black velvet within the wooden box, his entire frame jolted violently!

The old man's right hand shook with a violent spasm; the oiling needle clattered onto the green rubber mat, and that precious droplet of clear Swiss lubricant bloomed slowly across the surface.

Tang Bingzheng's pupils contracted sharply, his withered fingers instantly locking onto the edge of the desk with a white-knuckled grip, his breathing seizing entirely in a split second.

"This watch... why is it here? And these tweezers..."

"These are relics left at the Overdue Post Office by Mr. Fang Jingting," Lin Ziyang looked directly into the elder's shocked, pale eyes. "He entrusted us to return them into your hands today, forty years later."

"Fang Jingting..."

Masticating the name, the shock on Tang Bingzheng's countenance rapidly curdled into a manic sneer of blistering fury. Tearing the binocular magnifier from his brow, he slammed it down onto the desk with a thunderous *BANG*!

"That loser! That miserable coward who lost his entire life!"

The old man's voice skyrocketed, shrieking across the dense ticking of the clocks, his chest heaving frantically, his eyes gleaming with the manic triumph and deep-seated terror of an insecure victor:

"He has been dead for three years! Did he still have to send you ghost-chasing couriers to disgust me with this pile of scrap iron before his grave? Is he trying to boast, or is he begging for my pity?!"

Tang Bingzheng surged to his feet, his withered finger pointing at the silver pocket watch in the box, roaring furiously:

"Forty-one years ago! The provincial capital trials for the Swiss Exhibition! Thirty grandmaster judges gathered in the grand auditorium, with only a single fellowship to Geneva! He and I were fellow apprentices under the same master since boyhood; everyone praised him for being grounded, claiming Fang Jingting was the orthodox successor and that I was too proud, too sharp, destined never to achieve greatness!"

"In that final showdown, each of us presented a double-axis tourbillon pocket watch forged with three years of blood and devotion! Before the trials, every expert and judge fawned over him like stars revolving around the moon; everyone believed the ticket to Europe was undeniably his!"

The old man's lips curled into a vicious, near-savage smirk, his eyes spiderwebbed with red veins:

"And what was the outcome?! Right as the judging panel conducted the final twelve-hour limit test of precision, his balance wheel jammed dead at three-fifteen in the afternoon! Motionless! He couldn't even maintain basic timekeeping! Collapsing before the provincial masters—a supreme humiliation of the highest order for any watchmaker!"

"Whereas my creation didn't deviate by a single fraction of a second, clinching the championship with perfection! When I walked toward him holding the trophy, he hung his head like a stray dog, not daring to look me in the eye! From that day forth, my name echoed under heaven, with dignitaries queuing with gold bars to beg for my services; while he was thoroughly disgraced, hiding in a squalid alley for a lifetime, fixing five-dollar spring alarm clocks for laborers to scrape by!"

"Inferior skill is inferior skill! And he still had the gall to keep this shameful piece of trash that stopped at three-fifteen? Take it away! These hands of Tang Bingzheng, accustomed to top-grade Swiss calibers, will never look upon a loser's refuse!"

#### 4

Facing the old man's hysterical tirade and towering arrogance, Lin Ziyang felt no anger, only quietly watching Tang Bingzheng's hands that were trembling uncontrollably with agitation.

"Master Tang, do you truly believe that you won back then because your craftsmanship was vastly superior to his?"

Lin Ziyang's voice was very soft, yet like an icy razor blade, it sliced cleanly through the roaring torrent of ticking time within the shop.

"What did you say?!" Tang Bingzheng's eyes flared with icy menace.

Lin Ziyang did not answer; instead, reaching out, he lifted that silver-cased pocket watch from the walnut box.

Beneath the elder's stare, the youth used his fingernail to trip the hidden spring catch along the rim. With a crisp *clack*, the ornately engraved silver caseback sprang open, revealing the intricate movement hermetically sealed beneath crystal glass.

Taking those micro-adjusting tweezers whose tips had worn dull, Lin Ziyang gently loosened the microscopic screws anchoring the protective bridge, revealing the brass plate within the movement completely beneath the dim lamplight.

There, upon the brass plate polished as mirror-flat as still water, a line of disciplined regular script had been deeply, immaculately carved with an ultra-fine graver:

"'To my best rival of a lifetime—My brother Bingzheng. May this hairspring bear you to the summit.'"

The instant Tang Bingzheng's eyes fell upon that inscription, his entire soul was struck as though by thunder!

The old man's breath died completely in his throat; his body swayed violently, nearly collapsing to the floor, his hands clutching the workbench as all color washed from his face, turning from red to blue, and from blue to a ghastly, deadened white like a skeleton weathered by half a century!

"This... these words..."

Lin Ziyang gave him no quarter to retreat. Reaching into the bottom of the walnut box, he drew out that sheet of manuscript tissue paper soaked in translucent horological oil.

Facing the elder's quivering, vacant stare, Lin Ziyang read aloud the truth of that night forty years ago with a measured, resonant, and solemn voice amidst the ticking of a hundred clocks:

"'Late autumn of the seventy-first year of the Republic, the eve of the finals.'

"'At eleven at night, Bingzheng brought his assembled double-axis tourbillon to my workshop for one final comparative calibration. Young and vigorous, taking absolute pride in his unmatched genius, his countenance radiated unrestrained conceit and triumph. Yet after he departed in high spirits, I placed his movement beneath the twenty-power loupe, and my heart plummeted into a bottomless chasm of ice.'

"'In his rush during final assembly, fueled by impatience and anxious haste, Bingzheng had exerted excessive force with his tweezers, bending the double-layered pure titanium blued-steel hairspring—the very soul that governed the tourbillon's equilibrium—into a hairline micro-crack near the outer stud.'

"'Such a fatal, microscopic crack showed no defect while static or during brief tests, keeping time with immaculate precision; yet under continuous high-frequency oscillation exceeding six hours, metal fatigue would inevitably erupt at the fracture, causing the hairspring to snap and curl, collapsing the entire intricate escapement system instantly before the judges.'

"'That night, I sat beneath the kerosene lamp, motionless for four whole hours.'

"'Had I pretended not to know, had I kept silent, tomorrow's provincial gold medal and the sponsored fellowship to Geneva were mine for the taking. Bingzheng's genius was a miracle seen once in a century, but his pride could brook no defeat. Had his spring snapped and his watch collapsed before the provincial grandmasters and foreign judges, his spirit would have been pulverized by public condemnation, and his path as an artisan would have been destroyed forever.'"

#### 5

Every single word on that manuscript turned into molten iron, poured mercilessly into Tang Bingzheng's chest.

A terrifying crimson flush crept into the old man's eyes, his frame trembling uncontrollably.

Lin Ziyang turned the oil-stained tissue over and read on:

"'At three in the morning, I took up these micro-adjusting tweezers bequeathed to me by our master before his passing.'

"'Master gripped my hand before he departed, saying: Jingting, your nature is grounded and you understand empathy; I leave these most delicate micro-adjusting tweezers to you. Bingzheng is restless; though his skill is peerless, his temperament lacks seasoning. In the days ahead, you must shelter him and fulfill him.'

"'That night, using these tweezers, I personally detached from my own pocket watch that flawless pure titanium blued-steel hairspring, which I had polished for eight months, bearing my handwritten micro-dot lacquer counterweight at its tip. Holding my breath, I transplanted it into Bingzheng's tourbillon movement; while his fatally cracked hairspring was installed into my own watch.'

"'At three-fifteen the following afternoon, when my watch let out a faint wail and its balance wheel jammed dead before the judging panel, I heard the sighs and sneers of the hall, and I saw the intoxicating, wild triumph in my junior brother's eyes.'

"'Watching him hold aloft that trophy, bathing in the cheers of ten thousand souls, I stood in the dim shadows with no grievance in my heart, only profound peace. Junior brother, you won the contest, you ventured to Geneva, you beheld a greater world; while I remained in this mountain town, keeping vigil over this broken pocket watch frozen at three-fifteen.'

"'In these forty years, the world called you the premier artisan of the mountain town, and dubbed me a ruined tinkerer. Whenever I saw you mock my stagnant isolation in newspapers, I felt happy for you. For only one possessed of absolute arrogance can compel his art to the absolute pinnacle; yet only on the day when you learn to lower your head and see the truth will your artisan heart truly reach completion.'

"'These tweezers kept me company for fifty years; today, I return them to you along with this watch. Bingzheng, never look down upon your rival, and never allow arrogance to blind your empathy. The pinnacle of craftsmanship is never defeating another, but completing one another.'"

#### 6

Every word was a razor blade; every syllable drew blood.

Throughout the shop, the ticking of a hundred clocks remained dense as rain, yet in Tang Bingzheng's ears, those sounds exploded like tens of thousands of deafening thunderclaps!

"No... impossible... this is a fabrication! A lie he concocted before his death to humiliate me!"

Tang Bingzheng shrieked like a madman. Slamming the binocular magnifier over his eyes, he snatched the micro-adjusting tweezers from Lin Ziyang's hand, his withered fingers shaking like reeds in the gale.

Inserting the trembling tweezers deep into the balance wheel of the silver pocket watch, he lifted the broken hairspring.

Through the ten-power lens, at the fractured root of the hairspring, he saw with absolute clarity a tear of metal fatigue forced open by blunt leverage—the exact hairline bend he had made forty-one years ago in his youthful arrogance during midnight assembly!

The angle, the curvature of that bend—it was identical to his own unique technique from forty years ago down to the micron!

The old man's heart convulsed violently. Pivoting around in frenzy, he lunged toward the safe behind him, threw open the heavy door, and dragged out the rosewood casket that had been enshrined for forty years—the gold medal masterpiece that had made him famous across the province.

With a crisp *clack*, the back of the gold-medal watch was violently pried open.

His hands shaking violently, Tang Bingzheng aligned the high-power magnifier onto the heart of the timepiece he had cherished his entire life—there, above the exquisite tourbillon balance wheel that had run without deviating a second for forty years, on the tip of the gracefully unfurled blued-steel hairspring, rested a microscopic speck of white lacquer visible only under ten-power magnification!

That was the signature counterweight mark of his elder brother, Fang Jingting—a secret calibration technique unique to the man across the province!

That was the medal of genius he had boasted of for forty years; yet the very core of this medal was forged from his elder brother's willing ruin and thirty years of silent sacrifice!

*Clatter.*

The priceless rosewood casket and the gold-medal pocket watch slammed hard onto the floorboards, delicate gears scattering across the floor.

#### 7

"Brother... ELDER BROTHER——!!!"

An ancient, agonizing, soul-shattering cry finally tore from the depths of Tang Bingzheng's chest!

The old man's knees buckled, collapsing heavily onto the cold, damp wooden floorboards, clutching that silver pocket watch frozen at three-fifteen in both hands, slamming his forehead against the oil-stained edge of the workbench, weeping without restraint!

"So... forty-one years ago, I had already lost..."

Tang Bingzheng wept until his entire frame shook in violent spasms, turbid tears cascading like a burst dam, soaking the dark green work mat into a mottled mess:

"I stepped upon your shoulders and swaggered in arrogance for an entire lifetime! The man I mocked for forty years was the very savior of my life!"

"You shielded me from catastrophic ruin and preserved my honor, yet I trampled upon your dignity in the press, at banquets, using your poverty to prop up my pathetic vanity!"

"What kind of master am I? I am nothing more than a conceited, ungrateful, despicable clown!"

The old man hammered his chest frantically with his fist. Throughout the shop, only his heartbreaking wails mingled with the ceaseless ticking of a hundred wall clocks.

That was the tearing agony of arrogance pulverized to dust, the violent tremor of a soul beholding the light after wandering in pitch-black delusion for half a century.

Humility is never base weakness, but the awe born of beholding the vastness of heaven and earth and the sacrifices of others; empathy is by no means cheap pity, but the noble mercy that willingly veils its own radiance to endure the dark on another's behalf.

A long time passed before the weeping gradually subsided.

With his coarse sleeve, Tang Bingzheng wiped away the tears from his face. Taking a deep breath, he struggled to his feet with agonizing slowness yet immense reverence.

He did not glance at the scattered gold-medal watch upon the floor; instead, with both hands, he lifted the stopped silver pocket watch with devout grace, picking up those micro-adjusting tweezers bequeathed by their master to his brother, and from his brother to him.

The old man donned his reading spectacles. This time, his eyes held neither arrogance nor fury—only pristine clarity and reverence forged through tribulation.

Sitting beneath the lamp, extending those hands that trembled yet gathered inexhaustible power, he took up the tweezers, held his breath, and began to repair that hairspring broken for forty-one years, stroke by microscopic stroke.

Time flowed by, second by second.

The golden rays of dusk pierced the stained glass window, falling gently upon the old man's silver hair and glistening brass parts.

*Tick... tock...*

An exceedingly faint yet crystal-clear heartbeat, like the dawn of daybreak, suddenly resonated from the depths of the movement frozen for forty-one years!

Immediately following:

"Tick-tock... tick-tock... tick-tock..."

The second hand that had rested at three-fifteen for forty-one years took its first light, unyielding step forward beneath the amber sun!

Gazing upon that beating second hand, a pure, unburdened smile like that of a child blossomed across Tang Bingzheng's face, two warm tears tracing silently down the wrinkles at the corners of his mouth.

"Brother... this time, we walk together."

Lin Ziyang quietly stepped out of the shop, gently sliding the glass door shut.

The mist over the old street had dissipated, the setting sun bathing the flagstone path in brilliant gold. The distant laughter of schoolchildren on their way home echoed through the air; lifting his head to the chiming clocks, the young postman strode forward toward the sunset-draped ridges.

### 【信差迴響 / Messenger's Echo】
> **本章品德力量：【謙遜與同理（Humility & Empathy）】**
> **Virtue of this Chapter: 【Humility & Empathy】**
>
> 傲慢是心靈最沉重的盲杖，它讓人沉溺於勝者通吃的虛妄幻象，卻看不見對手在暗處為自己承受的深淵；而謙遜則是智者擦亮的明鏡，讓人懂得敬畏技藝、尊重同儕、放空狹隘的自我。
> Arrogance is the heaviest blind cane of the soul; it intoxicates one with the hollow illusion of winner-takes-all, yet renders one blind to the chasm one's rival endured in the shadows on one's behalf. Humility, however, is the clear mirror polished by the wise, teaching reverence for craftsmanship, deep respect for peers, and the emptying of the narrow ego.
>
> 真正的至高境界，從來不是將他人踩在腳下的孤絕巔峰，而是在登臨高處時，依然能以同理之心體恤他人的隱忍與不易，甚至甘願收斂鋒芒，成全大局。
> The true pinnacle of mastery is never an isolated summit achieved by trampling others underfoot, but standing upon the heights while preserving the empathy to feel another's silent forbearance, and even willingly veiling one's radiance to fulfill a greater purpose.
>
> 當我們放下居高臨下的勝負執念，學會以謙卑的姿態傾聽、以慈悲的胸懷體諒時，人生的擺輪才能擺脫偏狹的桎梏，在歲月的長河中奏出最和諧優美的生命樂章。
> When we lay down our condescending obsession with victory and learn to listen with humility and understand with compassion, the balance wheel of life can finally break free from the shackles of obstinacy, playing the most harmonious and magnificent melody of life through the river of time.
