# Volume III: Letters of Promise to the Future

## Chapter 11: Before the Rain Stops, Please Deliver My Heart

### A Stormy Night and the Final Appointment of the Messenger

Late in the dead of winter night, a furious torrential typhoon storm—unprecedented in decades—swept across the mountain town that hung solitary on the edge of the Black Ditch.

The downpour was so savage it seemed as though the vault of heaven had fractured. The piercingly cold gale, laden with freezing rain, violently whipped against the flimsy, aged cypress window lattices of the mountain town health clinic. The panes of glass shuddered violently in the ferocious air pressure, giving off shrill, heart-wrenching shrieks that sounded on the verge of splintering at any moment. Mountain torrents laden with snapped branches and yellow mud cascaded down the steep and narrow stone staircases. Following the collapse of the power grid, the entire mountain town was plunged into a deathly stillness of darkness, save for a single incandescent bulb in the emergency observation room barely sustained by a diesel generator, casting a faint, flickering amber halo amid the humming flutter of unstable voltage.

Old Gu lay upon a narrow hospital bed. The elder's countenance resembled a stone sculpture weathered by half a century of storms, with sunken eye sockets, prominent cheekbones, and the joints of his fingers already faintly tinged with the cyanotic bluish-purple of oxygen deprivation. The faintly undulating line across the cardiac monitor beat at an extraordinarily sluggish, erratic cadence, its warning chime like the muffled weeping of a dying soul, echoing intermittently through the cavernous, chilled ward.

Lin Ziyang knelt by the bedside, clutching the elder's withered, ice-cold hands with all his might. His eyes were crimson, and scalding tears, mingled with cold sweat trickling from his brow, fell drop by drop onto the coarse bedsheet.

"Old Gu... the emergency team from Keelung Chang Gung has braved the storm and made it up the mountain; the doctors are right outside the door... please hold on, you promised me you'd watch the skies over the mountain town clear..." Lin Ziyang's voice carried a broken sob bordering on desperation, his chest heaving violently.

Old Gu slowly turned his turbid yet still profoundly deep eyes, resting his gaze upon him with immense effort. The corners of the elder's lips tugged with painful difficulty, forming a gentle smile of tranquil release that had seen through life and death.

"Silly child... don't weep." Old Gu's voice was faint, like gossamer trembling in the wind, every vibration of his dry throat accompanied by a heavy, rasping breath. "After being a mail carrier for sixty years... a man knows in his heart, clearer than anyone else, which day his shift ends, and which day he completes his postal run."

Summoning every ounce of his remaining strength, the old man lifted a trembling finger, pointing toward a document pouch wrapped tightly in military-green waterproof canvas resting atop the bedside nightstand.

Gritting his teeth and fighting back the agonizing grief tearing at his heart, Lin Ziyang reached out with shaking hands, retrieved the pouch, and gently untied the twine before the elder.

Inside the canvas pouch lay three items arranged with solemn precision.

Resting on top was an official document, yellowed with time yet smooth as new. Written in regular brush script at the header was *Notice of Passing the 108th Formal Mail Carrier Examination of The Overdue Post Office*, stamped at the lower right corner with the ancient, heavy octagonal cast-iron postmark of the post office. At the line designated for the appointed messenger were three bold, vigorous characters: Lin Ziyang.

The second item was a large cast-iron bicycle bell paddle strung on a red cotton cord, its surface worn to reveal the warm golden brass beneath, alongside the heavy pure brass key capable of opening every hidden drawer of the cast-iron safe in The Overdue Post Office.

And the third item was a legally notarized *Voluntary Gratuitous Organ and Human Tissue Donation Pledge* bearing the steel seal of the Provincial Medical Center, accompanied by an urgent cross-match fax just transmitted by the emergency dispatch center before communications with downtown Keelung were severed.

The print on the fax sheet glared starkly under the dim bulb:
*Keelung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital Pediatric Intensive Care Unit: A nine-year-old girl named "Xiao Qing" (ward of the Mountain Town Hope Orphanage) suffering from terminal congenital dilated cardiomyopathy has suffered acute cardiogenic shock; ECMO life support has reached its absolute operational limit, with cardiac arrest imminent at any moment.*
*Urgent cross-matching via the Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network reveals that all tissue-typing indicators of Mr. Gu Yannian of The Overdue Post Office match the patient with 100% compatibility! Should the implantation be completed within the 6-hour cold ischemia tolerance limit following cardiac arrest, the child's survival probability exceeds 85%...*

Lin Ziyang's pupils contracted violently, his chest feeling as if struck by a thousand-pound boulder.

"Xiao Qing... it's that little girl at the orphanage who always hid behind the wooden door watching us..." Lin Ziyang's voice shook uncontrollably.

"This old heart of mine... has beat inside me for eighty-six years." Old Gu looked at Lin Ziyang, and within his dimming eyes there suddenly erupted an incandescent, soul-stirring glow. "It froze in the snowy night of Huaihai, it pitched in the stormy waves of the Black Ditch, and it endured sixty years through the muddy tracks of this mountain town... but I know that down to its marrow it is forged of steel; it can still beat, and it still yearns to beat!"

"Ziyang, in this life of mine... failing to raise Nianping with my own hands is the deepest sorrow I owe to this mortal world." The old man's fingers suddenly gripped Lin Ziyang's wrist with astonishing force, and the turbid tears finally streamed down the deep crevices of his face. "But I cannot let that child... lose her life in this stormy night."

"This is... the final piece of mail in my entire life. This time, the letter is not written with ink on paper—it is my heart, the last vestige of warmth from this old life of mine!" The elder's breathing surged erratically, his chest heaving violently, his words carrying the weight of thunder amid the howling storm outside: "The road is washed out... no vehicle can descend... You use your feet, use the unyielding spine of a mail messenger, and deliver it down for me!"

"Before daybreak... before the rain stops... deliver it into the chest of that little girl!"

"Ziyang, tell her... on behalf of Grandpa Gu... to take a good, long look at how beautiful this mountain town looks when the skies clear..."

Before the final syllable could fully fade, Old Gu's gaunt fingers clutching Lin Ziyang's wrist suddenly went limp.

With a countenance of absolute serenity and peace, bearing at the corners of his lips that tranquil smile of one who had witnessed all of life's turbulence, the elder slowly closed his eyes.

Across the cardiac monitor, the faintly undulating line trembled briefly, then collapsed completely into an unyielding, piercing flatline chime: "Beep——————"

---

### Organ Procurement and the Impasse of Nature

"Old Gu————!"

A tear-rending cry of pure agony pierced through the howling storm outside the window. Lin Ziyang collapsed against the bedside, burying his face deep into the elder's chest that was slowly losing its warmth. His broad shoulders heaved convulsively as tears poured like an unleashed flood, soaking the rough hospital linens.

Yet, barely ten seconds passed.

The door to the emergency room was thrust open. The lead transplant surgeon from Keelung Chang Gung Hospital, accompanied by two critical care nurses, strode in with heavy solemnity. The surgeon placed a gloved hand gently upon Lin Ziyang's trembling shoulder.

"Mr. Lin... my condolences," the surgeon's voice was raspy and taut. "The elder passed away in peace. But we have less than six hours of golden cold ischemia time. For every minute delayed, a fraction of the myocardial tissue will perish, and that little girl's hope of survival will diminish."

Lin Ziyang drew a fierce, ragged breath, raising the back of his hand to violently wipe away the tears and cold sweat from his face. In an instant, every trace of fragility and sorrow vanished from his eyes, replaced by an iron resolve that was cold, unyielding, and absolute.

"Proceed, Doctor." Lin Ziyang rose to his feet, took a step back, and bowed deeply toward the elder upon the bed. "I will escort Old Gu on his very last postal run."

Amid the dull rumble of the emergency diesel generator, the surgical suite in the local clinic was bathed in sterile lamps. For over an hour, the air was saturated with the cold, medicinal tang of povidone-iodine and chilled preservation saline. The surgeons worked with solemn reverence, completing the cold perfusion and organ preservation with immaculate precision.

"Click."

As the heavy latches of the silver-gray medical organ transport container snapped shut with airtight finality, the LCD timer atop the box illuminated with a cold, pale-green luminescence: [04:45:00].

That was the final countdown of viability after the heart departed the human vessel.

However, the very instant the team rushed toward the clinic's main entrance carrying the transport cooler, a terrifying, earth-shaking roar tore through the stormy skies!

"Rumble—————!"

It was the sound of hundreds of tons of rock, saturated mud, and uprooted forest giants shearing away from the cliffs of the rear mountain and crashing downward. The rampaging mudslide, like a ravenous black dragon, surged across the mountain hollow, smashing the only four-wheel-drive emergency ambulance parked outside against the cliff face, crumpling its reinforced chassis into scrap metal in the blink of an eye!

"The road is gone! The entire asphalt road leading down the mountain has been pulverized by hundreds of tons of rockfall!" The scout who had sprinted out to check the road tumbled back inside covered in mud, his face deathly pale. "The ambulance is totaled, and heavy clearing excavators from the base cannot make it up! We are completely cut off—it will take until noon tomorrow at the earliest to clear a path!"

With a countdown of barely four hours, waiting until tomorrow noon was an absolute impossibility.

The transplant surgeon stared at the pulverized highway and the roaring mud torrents, frozen in his tracks, his eyes glazed with an ashen despair: "Heaven's will... is this truly the cruel irony of fate? Xiao Qing is already lying open upon the operating table..."

"There is no fate."

A voice—quiet, hoarse, yet heavy as solid iron—abruptly pierced through the fury of the gale.

The crowd turned in astonishment. Lin Ziyang had already donned Old Gu's dark olive-green vintage mail carrier overcoat. Using heavy climbing canvas and coarse hemp rope, he had lashed the weighty medical transport cooler firmly against his chest, wrapping layer upon layer until it was anchored flush against his own beating heart.

Then, from beneath the rain shelter in the rear courtyard, the young man wheeled out that olive-green "Yongjiu" vintage bicycle.

---

### The Green Iron Steed and the Chasm on the Cliff

"Mr. Lin! Have you lost your mind?!" The lead surgeon turned pale with horror, lunging forward to seize Lin Ziyang's arm. "Outside is a torrential storm unseen in decades! Mudslides are raging down the stone steps, rockfalls are everywhere! Riding a bicycle down this mountain is nothing short of suicide!"

"Old Gu told me: for a mail messenger, his own two feet are the postal road." Lin Ziyang pulled the green hood over his head, fastening the wind clasp at his collar against the screaming gale. His gaze was crystal-clear and freezing cold, without the slightest tremor of doubt. "Vehicles cannot traverse this mountain road, but this old bicycle and my legs can."

The youth looked up, meeting the eyes of the surgeon and the medical team: "Doctors, contact the operating theater at Keelung Chang Gung immediately and tell them to prepare for sternotomy. Tell them that before dawn breaks, Old Gu's heart will arrive on time!"

Before anyone could react, Lin Ziyang swung his leg over the heavy double crossbar, his right boot slamming down onto the iron pedal!

"Creak—"

The heavy cast-iron chain bit into the sprockets with a crisp metallic crunch. Like an arrow loosed from a bow, the olive-green vintage bicycle shattered the curtains of rain, plunging headlong into the pitch-black abyss of the storm-ravaged mountain!

The deluge poured relentlessly, striking his face like countless shards of ice, stinging so fiercely that Lin Ziyang could barely keep his eyes open.

The descending flagstone footpath had long since transformed into a thundering torrent of yellow slurry. The bicycle tires slipped and fishtailed wildly in the rushing mud, while ferocious crosswinds howled through the ravines on both sides like invisible walls of steel, battering against the young man's body, trying to heave both rider and steed into the bottomless abyss.

"Hold steady... hold steady for me!"

Lin Ziyang ground his teeth until they nearly cracked, the muscles in his forearms bulging as his hands locked with an iron grip around the handlebars wrapped in black friction tape. Lowering his center of gravity, with his chest shielding the organ cooler, his legs pumped the heavy pedals with an almost frantic cadence.

The bottle dynamo on the front hub hummed at furious speed in the rain, and the amber beam from the convex headlamp carved a straight, piercing spear of light through the churning mud and rain.

"Ding-ling—! Ding-ling—!"

With every blind hairpin turn and sunken pit of mud, Lin Ziyang's thumb flicked the cast-iron paddle on the handlebar. The chime—crisp, resonant, piercing through the storm—reverberated fiercely across the pitch-black valley!

At that moment, Lin Ziyang did not feel alone. He felt as though behind him, a broad, warm, callus-worn hand was firmly steadying his rear cargo rack, shielding him from the howling crosswinds. Amid the shrieking gale in his ears, he seemed to hear the steady, rhythmic breathing of the elder who had traversed these mountain tracks for fifty years.

The old messenger had not left; he was pushing his apprentice, hurtling forward along the postal route between life and death!

However, just as the bicycle tore onto the Black Dragon Gorge cliffside trestle midway down the mountain, an earth-shattering roar detonated once more in the darkness ahead!

A fatal landslide had sheared away the center of the trestle. Nearly a hundred meters of roadway and safety barriers had been obliterated and hurled into the bottomless depths of Black Dragon Gorge. Ahead of him lay a gaping, sheer chasm over ten meters wide!

Lin Ziyang's pupils contracted violently as his hands slammed the handbrakes to the grips!

"Screeech————!"

The rear tire carved a deep trench through the slurry, screeching to a halt barely half a meter from the precipice. Loose pebbles tumbled over the rim into the abyss, vanishing without even an echo.

The road was utterly severed.

---

### Bidding Farewell to the Steed and Crossing the Chasm on Iron Cables

The gale howled mournfully across the void, and freezing rain lashed mercilessly against Lin Ziyang's frame.

The digital countdown on the cooler flickered urgently in the dark: [02:15:20].

Barely two hours remained. Across this ten-meter chasm, the terrain sloped gently downward, where the mountain road was said to meet the only point accessible by city ambulances.

Yet the abyss yawned between them, devoid of any path.

Under the dim illumination of the headlamp, Lin Ziyang cast his frantic gaze in all directions. Suddenly, his eyes locked onto the cliffside above—where two wrist-thick raw-iron cables, once used for coal slurry discharge and runoff decades ago, spanned the void.

The cables had been abandoned for half a century, covered in thick red rust, groaning with a sickening metallic creak in the violent crosswinds.

That was the only "bridge" remaining across this chasm.

To cross, the only possible way was to abandon the bicycle and traverse the void hand over hand, suspended by his own flesh and blood across those two rusted cables!

Lin Ziyang turned his head to look at the dark green vintage bicycle beside him—the faithful companion that had shared Old Gu's life for fifty years, now splattered with mud and scratched by rocks.

The young man's eyes welled with warmth once more. He reached out a trembling right hand, softly caressing the cold leather saddle and that vintage iron bell that had just rung so proudly in the storm.

"Old partner... thank you for bringing me this far." Lin Ziyang wheeled the bicycle beside an ancient, sturdy camphor tree, locking it firmly against the trunk with a steel cable lock, before bowing deeply to it. "The rest of the road... Old Gu's heartbeat, I will carry on my own two feet!"

Turning around, Lin Ziyang tightened the canvas straps securing the cooler three times more, ensuring it was anchored immovably against his chest, protected from any impact. Then, rubbing his palms hard against coarse grit to enhance friction, he leaped upward, both hands clamping tightly onto the icy raw-iron cable above!

"Whoosh————!"

The ferocious wind instantly whipped his body like a ragged banner, tossing him violently across the yawning abyss!

Beneath his boots were the roaring torrents of mountain floodwater and jagged boulders; a single slip of his fingers meant being shattered into dust.

The jagged edges of rust bit deeply into his palms, sending sharp agony shooting through his nerves. Blood welled between his fingers, only to be washed clean by the frigid deluge.

"One, two... one, two...!"

Lin Ziyang roared the rhythm in his heart, the veins in his arms bulging like coiled serpents. Locking his legs tightly around a lower guide cable, he crawled forward hand over hand. With every inch of progress, the rough rust sheared away a layer of skin, leaving bloody smears upon the iron.

Rainwater surged into his mouth and nostrils; suffocation and sheer exhaustion washed over him in waves. As he reached the center of the chasm—the point of maximum sway—a monstrous gust of crosswind struck from the flank, and his left hand slipped!

His entire body dropped into empty air, suspended solely by the four bleeding fingers of his right hand clutching the cable!

"Arrrrgh————!"

His finger joints popped under the strain, his body spinning wildly in the void, on the verge of total exhaustion.

At that razor's edge of life and death, more than a dozen piercing, dazzling beams of light suddenly erupted through the dense forest on the opposite bank!

"Over there! We see him! Messenger Xiao Lin is on the cable!"

"Hold on! Throw the safety line over!"

It was the voice of the mountain town!

Old miner Uncle Ah-Rong, familiar shopkeepers, and seven or eight young men from the mountain town, holding high-powered spotlights and explosion-proof lanterns, were shouting frantically in the mud! A thick climbing rope fitted with a carabiner sliced through the rain, dropping squarely across Lin Ziyang's shoulder!

"Grip the line! Xiao Lin! We're pulling you across!"

The townsfolk roared in unison, digging their boots deep into the mire, throwing their entire weight into heaving the rope!

With his remaining strength, Lin Ziyang looped his left arm through the harness. Like a bluebird braving the storm, he was wrenched back from the jaws of the abyss by dozens of warm, powerful hands on the opposite bank!

"Thud!"

Lin Ziyang crashed onto the muddy slope, covered in blood and mud, gasping raggedly for air.

Yet his very first motion upon falling was to lock both arms protectively over his chest.

The medical cooler was completely intact, its green digital display steadily ticking down: [01:12:08].

"Son... you did it... you truly did it!" Uncle Ah-Rong, his face streaked with tears, shakily removed his dry poncho and wrapped it around Lin Ziyang.

Behind them, an ambulance from downtown Keelung, its engine humming and blue beacon lights flashing, tore through the final shadows of night, waiting at the end of the accessible road!

### The Resumption of the Heartbeat and the Dawning Light

At five-forty in the morning.

Outside Operating Room 3 of Keelung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, the crimson surgical lamp had burned continuously for five full hours.

Upon the bench in the corridor, Lin Ziyang, caked in mud and dried blood, leaned against the cold wall in complete exhaustion. His hands were bound in thick white gauze newly dressed by the trauma nurses, with dark crimson stains faintly seeping through the wraps.

Beside him, Director Lin of the orphanage knelt on the floor with palms pressed together in silent prayer, her tears long since dried.

Twenty minutes earlier, with barely fifteen minutes remaining on the cold ischemia clock, Lin Ziyang had personally handed that frost-rimmed cooler firmly into the hands of the lead transplant surgeon.

At this very moment, within the operating theater, the ultimate duel against death was nearing its culmination.

The lead surgeon delicately completed the microvascular anastomosis of the final aortic connection. That heart of the veteran mail messenger—which had endured eighty-six years of turbulent history and rested in freezing cold—was precisely nestled within the petite, delicate chest cavity of nine-year-old Xiao Qing.

"Release the vascular cross-clamps," the surgeon ordered in a raspy, solemn whisper.

The clamps released. Warm, oxygenated blood surged like a roaring spring flood, bathing the dormant myocardial tissues.

Ten seconds passed...

Twenty seconds passed...

The heart remained still, without a beat.

"Prepare the defibrillator, twenty joules, charged... clear!"

"Thump!"

The child's body shuddered slightly on the table, yet the wave across the monitor remained a faint, flat tremor.

"Again! Thirty joules... charged... clear!"

"Thump!"

In that split second when every surgeon and nurse held their breath and hearts leaped to throats, that quiet heart deep within the chest suddenly delivered a fierce, powerful spontaneous contraction!

Then came a second beat, and a third!

The long-dormant line across the monitor surged upward into an elegant, triumphant crest of emerald green!

"Thump—thump—thump—!"

The steady, surging, majestic pulse—brimming with boundless vitality and unyielding resilience—rang out distinctly through the speaker of the cardiac monitor, piercing through the heavy lead doors to reverberate in the quiet, chilled corridor outside!

It was Old Gu's heartbeat.

It was the heart of the mail messenger that had crossed the snowy night of Huaihai, braved the towering swells of the Black Ditch, and run sixty years through the muddy tracks of the mountain town!

It had not ceased; within the body of a brand-new, innocent young life, it had rekindled its rhythm with the proudest and most majestic vigor!

In the hallway, Lin Ziyang slowly lifted his head, listening to that thumping resonance echoing through the wall, hot tears once again slipping silently down his cheeks. Yet this time, there was no grief in his eyes—only boundless release and reverence.

At that very instant, a crisp chorus of birdsong drifted in from outside the window.

Lin Ziyang turned his head toward the expansive floor-to-ceiling glass window at the far end of the corridor.

The ferocious typhoon storm that had rampaged through the night had quietly ceased at some unknown moment. The heavy, dark thunderclouds were parting toward the horizon, and a breathtaking, pure dawn of gold and rose burst forth between the ocean and the distant hills of Keelung Port.

Golden sunlight bathed the rain-washed city, illuminating the polished brass bell paddle resting upon Lin Ziyang's chest with a warm, brilliant radiance.

The rain had stopped.

The mountain town and the mortal world had finally welcomed that long-awaited, immaculately clear sky.
