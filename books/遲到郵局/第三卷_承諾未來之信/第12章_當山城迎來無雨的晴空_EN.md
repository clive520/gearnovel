# Volume III: Letters of Promise to the Future

## Chapter 12: When the Mountain Town Welcomes a Rainless Blue Sky

### The New Bluestone Tomb beneath the Ancient Camphor and the First Clear Spring in Thirty Years

Winter departed and spring arrived. The ceaseless drizzle that had shrouded the mountain town for more than three decades miraculously ceased completely following a light morning mist in March.

The dense, leaden-gray clouds that had clung stubbornly to the mountain ridges and stone stairways for decades were quietly dispersed by warm southern breezes sweeping in over the Black Ditch. The sky was washed a pure, crystalline azure, immaculate as an uncut, unblemished sapphire. Wild lilies, white dendrobiums, and mountain cherry blossoms bursting across the terraced cliffs bloomed simultaneously overnight, adorning the ancient, slate-colored mountain settlement in vibrant emerald and flourishing spring vigor.

This was the mountain town's first clear spring in thirty years without a single thread of drifting rain.

Beside the century-old camphor tree on the rear mountain, upon a sun-drenched knoll boasting an expansive view of the sea, stood a new tomb laid with local bluestone slabs. Upon the headstone there were no ostentatious titles or worldly accolades, only a single line of calligraphy chiseled with dignified precision: *Tomb of Gu Yannian, Veteran Mail Messenger of The Overdue Post Office*.

Lin Ziyang stood before the headstone in solemn reverence, dressed in an olive-green vintage mail carrier overcoat—faded from many washings yet ironed immaculately smooth.

The young man bent low, placing a bouquet of wild white lilies freshly gathered at dawn from the cliffside, their petals still glistening with morning dew, gently and squarely upon the tomb's pedestal.

A morning breeze brushed past, rustling the tender new emerald shoots of the ancient camphor tree in the warm sunshine. Dappled light spilled across the bluestone headstone like shattered gold, resembling the wise, kind-hearted elder squinting his eyes in bright, comforting laughter.

"Old Gu, the sun has come out over the mountain town." Lin Ziyang reached out, his fingertips gently tracing the cool, smooth stone grain of the headstone, a serene and tender smile gracing the corners of his eyes. "The sky is deep blue, the clouds are pure white, and blossoms have opened across the entire mountain. You guarded this post office for a lifetime, running through wind, rain, and mud for sixty years... today, it is my turn to set out in your stead."

Straightening his posture, the young man raised his right hand with crisp solemnity, delivering a textbook mail messenger's salute toward the bluestone tomb.

The morning sun stretched his upright shadow long across the grass, intertwining seamlessly with the resolute silhouette of the ancient camphor, like a bridge spanning life and death, eternally fused beneath the vast azure vault of heaven.

### The Visit of Reborn Lives and the Reunion of Two Generations' Heartbeats

When he returned to The Overdue Post Office, the golden morning sun bathed the entire antique courtyard in unrestrained radiance.

The dust upon the long cypress sorting desk had been wiped clean to a pristine sheen. In the air there was no longer the bone-chilling, suffocating damp of mold from the past; in its place was the mellow, comforting fragrance of ancient cypress warmed thoroughly by the sun.

Suddenly, from the courtyard gate came the joyful patter of running footsteps and laughter as clear as silver chimes.

"Uncle Lin! Uncle Lin!"

A little girl with neat pigtails and a soft pink knit sweater bounded into the courtyard like a frolicking fawn. Her cheeks were flushed with healthy rosiness and her eyes sparkled with lively innocence—it was none other than Xiao Qing, who had hovered between life and death months ago and was now entirely recovered.

Close on her heels was eighteen-year-old Ah-Mu. Once blinded by corneal burns from coal dust in a mining accident, the youth had now discarded his heavy light-shielding goggles. His newly restored eyes were crystal-clear, looking upon the sunlit courtyard with awe, wonder, and boundless gratitude.

"Brother Lin!" Ah-Mu said, his eyes moist with emotion as he hurried toward the sorting desk, bowing deeply to Lin Ziyang. "The doctor said my eyes have made a full recovery! I can see the sunshine, I can see the eagles soaring in the sky, I can see the little floral patterns on Xiao Qing's dress... I can finally see with my own eyes what this post office that Grandpa Gu and Brother Lin guarded looks like!"

Lin Ziyang smiled, patting the youth's shoulder with warm, comforting pride: "It is wonderful that you can see clearly now. The road of life ahead is long; keep a good, watchful eye on this beautiful world on Grandpa Gu's behalf."

At that moment, Xiao Qing nestled into Lin Ziyang's embrace. Reaching out with her soft little hands, she took Lin Ziyang's large hand—callused and scarred from work—and gently pressed it flat against her tender left chest.

"Uncle Lin, feel it quickly!" The little girl looked up with an adorable, porcelain smile, speaking with innocent pride, "Grandpa Gu's heart beats so fast and so strong! Thump-thump-thump, thump-thump-thump! Mother Director says Grandpa Gu is just living inside my chest in a different way. Every night he smiles at me in my dreams, pats my head, and tells me to eat well, grow up fast, and become someone who delivers warmth to others just like him!"

Through the thin fabric of her sweater, Lin Ziyang's fingertips felt with unmistakable clarity the rhythmic, powerful pulse beneath his palm.

"Thump—thump—thump—"

Every beat was so firm, so resolute, brimming with inexhaustible vitality. Every pulse felt like that old mail messenger—who had lost his own flesh and blood on the snowy night of Huaihai, yet poured his whole heart out for humanity without reservation—breathing his most tender sigh across the river of mortality.

Scalding tears silently escaped the corners of Lin Ziyang's eyes, splashing onto the worn brass bell paddle gleaming upon his chest. Yet this time, there was not the slightest sorrow in his tears—only the immaculate purity and boundless gratitude of a soul forged by fate.

### Three Letters Facing the Self: Responsibility, Self-Examination, and Promise

Having sent off the two children, Lin Ziyang sat alone before the sorting desk.

In the center of the long desk draped in clean green felt, three unsealed, brand-new white envelopes were laid out with solemn precision.

These were no longer archival relics delayed for decades, bearing historical fragments and ancestral regrets; these were the "Number One New Letters" written by Lin Ziyang's own hand as the 108th formal mail messenger of The Overdue Post Office.

Lin Ziyang picked up the first letter.

The recipient's address was an old apartment in Da'an District, Taipei City, inscribed to: "Mrs. Su Ruohan, Widow of the Late Mr. Chen Weimin, for Her Respectful Perusal."

Inside the envelope, accompanying the handwritten letter, was a thick legal dossier co-notarized by the District Court and the trust bank: *Agreement on the Mandatory Liquidation and Trust Settlement of All Overseas Assets and Fund Holdings in the Name of the Undersigned*.

Three years ago, Lin Ziyang had manipulated the Taipei capital markets, consumed by the speculative game of wealth and metrics. Because of his ruthless short-selling and market manipulation, his partner Chen Weimin had suffered instantaneous insolvency and credit default, leaping to his death from a skyscraper rooftop in utter despair, leaving behind a young wife and a one-year-old child. That tragedy was the original sin that had driven Lin Ziyang to flee southward in terror and malice into this mountain town.

Across the pages that had taken him three sleepless nights to compose, Lin Ziyang laid bare his past guilt and remorse in unyielding, disciplined brush calligraphy:

'To Sister-in-Law and My Nephew:'

'Three years ago, blinded by greed and swollen with hubris, I treated the families, lives, and trust of others as cold bargaining chips. Brother Weimin's death rests squarely on my head; even a myriad of deaths cannot expiate my crime.'

'The wind and rain of the mountain town taught me the meaning of "responsibility"; the postal run of a messenger washed clean the avarice and cowardice deep within my soul. Attached to this letter is the trust fund derived from the liquidation of all my legitimate assets, dedicated entirely to nephew's upbringing and education until he reaches adulthood. For the remainder of my days, I shall remit all necessary expenses monthly, and before the court of law and conscience, I shall await your summons and judgment at any time, never fleeing again.'

'The Sinner, Lin Ziyang, Bows in Penance One Hundred Times.'

Taking a deep breath, Lin Ziyang ignited the red sealing wax and pressed the heavy octagonal cast-iron postmark of The Overdue Post Office firmly into the molten wax.

The impression struck like a blade severing the shadows of his selfish flight, fulfilling his soul's most profound [Self-Examination and Responsibility].

Next, Lin Ziyang picked up the second letter.

The recipient's address was a modest fishing village in Pingtung, southern Taiwan, inscribed with five characters: "To Mother, Lin Chen Xiuyu."

Ever since his bankruptcy and flight three years ago, terrified of creditors and ashamed to face the mother who had raised him in hardship, Lin Ziyang had severed all contact with his hometown, leaving his aging mother to wait in grief amid the salty sea breeze for nearly a thousand agonizing days.

Upon this single sheet of stationery, Lin Ziyang poured out his repentance, tracing every step of redemption he had experienced in this mountain sanctuary:

'At Mother's Knees:'

'Your unfilial son has caused you torment and dread for more than a thousand days, leaving you to fear that I had perished in a distant land.'

'Your son survived in this mountain town and met the greatest mentor of my life, Old Gu. With a bowl of cold longevity noodles, a mended sweater, a broken paper umbrella, and a beating heart, he hauled your son out of the abyss step by step. The rain in the mountain town has stopped, and cherry blossoms cover the entire valley. Mother, choose a clear day and come visit your son in this mountain town. In the courtyard, your son will personally cook you a piping-hot bowl of pig trotter longevity noodles.'

Folding the letter neatly, the young man affixed a commemorative postage stamp bearing the motif "The Thread in a Loving Mother's Hand."

Finally, Lin Ziyang picked up the third letter.

This envelope bore no specific recipient or address; written in bold, hopeful brush script across the top was a declaration full of strength: *To the Future 109th Messenger*.

It was Lin Ziyang's solemn pledge to the future, dedicated to this post office a century hence:

'To the Messenger of the Future:'

'When you unseal this letter, I may well have returned to dust beneath the roots of the ancient camphor tree, or the tide of time may long since have replaced heavy parchment with invisible fiber-optic threads.'

'Yet remember always: what a mail messenger delivers has never been mere paper, but that breath of life enabling a person to survive in despair. Integrity is our backbone, empathy is our vision, forgiveness is our embrace, and altruistic love is our unceasing heartbeat. So long as there remains a single suffering, lonely heart waiting in this mortal world, never let the chime of this post office fall silent.'

### Mother's Arrival and a Piping-Hot Bowl of Longevity Noodles

Three weeks after the letters were dispatched, on a warm and sunlit afternoon in April.

An early summer breeze stirred the bamboo blinds in the rear courtyard, and the flagstone steps of the old mountain street were warmed pleasantly by the sun.

As Lin Ziyang was sorting seasonal mail at the desk, he suddenly heard the hearty voice of old miner Uncle Ah-Rong guiding someone outside: "Careful with the step, Grandma! Ah, this is The Overdue Post Office! Ziyang is right inside!"

Lin Ziyang froze, the postmark in his hand pausing mid-air.

Hurrying past the threshold, he saw an elderly woman standing upon the bluestone courtyard—diminutive, gaunt, her hair completely silvered with age. She wore a washed-out navy calico tunic and carried in her hands a heavy woven bamboo hamper filled with dried whitebait and silver-stripe round herring just cured in the southern fishing port, exuding the rich, savory aroma of sea salt.

Deep furrows etched by years and salty sea winds lined her face, her clouded eyes squinting in the bright sunlight. The moment her gaze met Lin Ziyang's, the old woman's frail frame shuddered violently, and the bamboo basket crashed to the ground with a soft thud.

"Ziyang... is it my Ziyang...?" The mother's voice trembled with hoarse, disbelieving wonder as tears spilled over her cheeks.

Lin Ziyang stood rooted to the spot, his vision overwhelmed in an instant by a surging torrent of hot tears.

"Thud!"

Without a moment's hesitation, the young man dropped heavily to both knees upon the unyielding bluestone courtyard, pressing his forehead hard against the stone floor, releasing an agonizing, long-suppressed cry that had been choked back for three years:

"Mother————! Your unfilial son, Lin Ziyang... kowtows before you!"

The elderly mother stumbled forward, extending her rough, callused hands that had spent decades soaked in brine and fish scales, pulling Lin Ziyang fiercely against her bosom as both wept so hard their tears drenched each other's clothes.

"Silly child... you are alive, and that is all that matters... that is all that matters!" The old mother wept, caressing her son's weathered, resolute face. "Every day I burned incense at the Guanyin shrine praying for your safety... Mother doesn't blame you. As long as I see you walking upright on the righteous path, Mother's heart is at peace for this life..."

Lin Ziyang helped his mother sit upon a bamboo armchair in the courtyard, gently dabbing away her tears. Then, turning toward the kitchen, he rolled up his sleeves and stoked the firewood stove with practiced familiarity.

Twenty minutes later, Lin Ziyang carried a large blue-and-white porcelain bowl with steady reverence, placing it gently before his mother.

Within the bowl, two large pork hind trotters, braised until tender, glistening, and rich with gelatin, steamed invitingly. Beneath them lay handmade thin longevity noodles thoroughly steeped in a rich, simmering broth of old stewing hen, garnished with vibrant scallions and golden wolfberries, releasing clouds of fragrant, piping-hot steam in the warm sunlight.

This bowl was no longer that cold, spoiled bowl beneath the steamer in Chapter 5, congealed with the agonizing regret of filial devotion delayed until parents are no more;

This bowl was piping-hot, freshly cooked, and blossoming with radiant warmth under the sun and in the loving presence of family—the soup of filial reconciliation fully realized!

"Mother, please have some noodles," Lin Ziyang handed her the bamboo chopsticks with both hands, his voice trembling yet firm. "Your son cooked this for you with his own hands. On this day every year to come, your son will always cook it for you."

Taking the chopsticks, the old mother lifted a steaming mouthful of noodles to her lips, savoring that warmth and sweetness she had longed for across three years. The deep furrows on her face softened in her tears, blooming into a smile as bright and comforting as the mountain town's rainless blue sky.

### The Restored Iron Steed and the Unending Route of the Messenger

As dusk approached, the entrance of The Overdue Post Office bustled with joyful activity.

Old miner Uncle Ah-Rong, Master Chen from the mountain town blacksmith forge, and seven or eight neighbors who had received Old Gu's kindness over the years pushed a gleaming, thoroughly restored heavy vintage bicycle into the center of the courtyard.

It was the "Yongjiu" vintage iron steed that had performed such heroic service along the precipice of Black Dragon Gorge during the torrential storm months before, narrowly escaping a plunge into the abyss.

The townsfolk had applied the finest craftsmanship of the forge, hammering true the bent cast-iron frame, replacing thirty-six broken wheel spokes, relubricating the drive chain with fresh tallow, and coating the entire frame in a durable coat of olive-green rustproof enamel. Welded to the left handlebar, the vintage brass bell that had served Old Gu for half a century had been buffed until it gleamed like pure gold, radiating brilliant luster in the sunset glow.

"Ziyang, take the handlebars!" Master Chen wiped the sweat from his brow, handing the solid grips solemnly into Lin Ziyang's hands. "Old Gu's iron steed is good as new! Its steel skeleton is forged tough—it can easily run another fifty years without coming apart!"

Lin Ziyang took the handlebars with deep emotion, his fingers brushing over the smooth, thick friction tape, a surge of reverence and exhilaration washing through his chest.

Under the proud gaze of the townsfolk, his mother, Ah-Mu, and Xiao Qing, Lin Ziyang stepped back before the sorting desk.

He solemnly donned his dark green mail carrier overcoat, fastening every brass button, and slipped the heavy brass key to the safe securely into his breast pocket. Then, lifting the weighty octagonal cast-iron postmark symbolizing duty and honor, he pressed it into the crimson cinnabar inkpad, stamping a bold, blood-red mark onto The Overdue Post Office's brand-new dispatch log!

Slumping the two heavy green cowhide mailbags over his shoulders, Lin Ziyang wheeled the restored iron steed through the post office gate with his head held high.

Outside, mountain cherry blossoms bloomed in blazing glory across the slopes, their pink-and-white petals drifting lazily on the breeze, carpeting the ancient bluestone path.

The sun shone brilliantly beneath a boundless azure sky; the entire mountain town was bathed in serene, pure golden light.

With practiced ease, Lin Ziyang swung his leg over the sturdy double crossbar, planting his boots firmly upon the iron pedals.

"Ding-ling—! Ding-ling—!"

The chime—crisp, melodious, brimming with boundless strength and hope—rang out jubilantly across the spring sky and mountain hollows, like a hymn of life welcoming the dawn after a long night!

"Uncle Lin is heading out! Uncle Lin is delivering the mail!"

Xiao Qing took Ah-Mu's hand, jumping and skipping along the stone steps, the children's clear, joyful cheers harmonizing with the chime of the vintage bell, echoing long under the blue sky and white clouds.

Facing the breeze and the swirling rain of petals, Lin Ziyang looked ahead along the winding mountain path extending into the distance, his eyes resolute and tender. The wheels rolled forward, traversing bluestone, traversing the years, traversing joys and sorrows, carrying letters of love and hope to every corner of the mortal realm.

The rain over the mountain town had truly stopped.

And the mail messenger's route had only just begun its magnificent journey, heading directly into the most brilliant morning sun.
