# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 11: Liu-Liu's Abnormal Detection

In the week after the beach cleanup ended, a small "anomaly" appeared in the classroom of Class 6-1—Liu-Liu had gone missing.

Not truly missing. It still showed up in the classroom on time every day, but its movements had become mysterious: when no one was watching, it would slip out of the classroom, only to dart back through the ventilation duct when the bell rang, data dancing densely in its electronic eyes.

"Liu-Liu, what have you been busy with lately?" A-Xin leaned in to ask.

"Beep beep! Nothing! Routine patrols!" Liu-Liu puffed out its chest with righteous confidence, "I'm the captain of the 'Intel & Publicity Raid Squad'! Patrolling is my duty!"

"But you used to bring back promotional posters from your patrols," A-Xin stared at it suspiciously, "now you come back with nothing?"

"Beep... well..." Liu-Liu's gaze drifted, "I-I've been focusing more on 'internal security' lately! Yes! Internal security!"

A-Xin frowned, feeling something was off but unable to put his finger on it.

Of course, he didn't know—Liu-Liu had actually been doing one thing these past days: flying up the drainage ditch behind the school, bit by bit, toward the upstream.

"Beep beep... November 12th, drainage ditch odor index: fifty-three. Up nine percent from last week."

"November 15th, odor index: sixty-one. Water beginning to turn turbid."

"November 18th, odor index: seventy. White foam appearing on the water surface..."

"November 20th," Liu-Liu hovered above the drainage ditch, data crazily jumping in its electronic eyes, "odor index: eighty-two. Water color: grayish-green. pH value: 4.7—this is strong acid!"

Liu-Liu's tail bristled, hair by hair.

"This ditch water can't even be called 'water' anymore!" Liu-Liu lowered its voice, unprecedented gravity in its tone, "And the discharge is still ongoing!"

The next day, Liu-Liu flew to the river mouth again.

"Beep beep... odor index: fifty-eight. Water color: starting to turn turbid." Liu-Liu recorded the data and was about to turn back when its electronic eyes suddenly caught an anomaly on the water's surface—"That's..."

On the shallows near the river mouth lay several little fish, bellies up, completely still.

"Fish... dead?" Liu-Liu flew over and carefully scanned, "No external wounds... inside... oh no, their gills are gray!"

"This color..." A dreaded keyword jumped out of Liu-Liu's database, "heavy metal poisoning?"

It refused to give up and flew another circle along the shallows.

The more it flew, the colder its heart grew.

In the corner of the shallows lay even more little fish with bellies up, mixed with dead shrimp. They lay quietly in the grayish-yellow water, as if quietly forgotten by the world.

"This isn't natural death," Liu-Liu said in a low voice, its electronic eyes full of reluctance, "this is... death by poisoning."

It flew along the drainage ditch to the spot three kilometers away, the place it had marked on the map.

It was a pipe hidden among the weeds, extending from the direction of the industrial zone, plunging straight into the drainage ditch.

At the end of the pipe, a grayish-green liquid was pouring out endlessly.

"...It's gotten faster again." Liu-Liu recorded the data, "This pipe's discharge rate is up forty percent from last week. Just who is dumping these things into our sea?"

That evening, after school.

Liu-Liu didn't go home. Instead, it carried a data report in its mouth and slipped into the sports equipment room—the Secret Alliance's "command center."

Qingqing was already waiting there.

"Liu-Liu," Qingqing said in a low voice, "how did the reconnaissance go these past few days?"

Liu-Liu spread the data report out on the equipment box.

"Bad." Liu-Liu's voice was unusually heavy, "The drainage ditch's water quality is getting worse every day. The odor index has shot up from fifty-three all the way to eighty-two. The water has turned turbid, foam has appeared, and the pH has dropped to 4.7."

"pH 4.7..." Qingqing gasped, "That's strongly acidic! If this water flows into the sea, the sea creatures..."

"They'll die." Liu-Liu said, "I detected that the seawater near the ditch's river mouth has already started to change color. If this keeps up, the first thing to suffer will be that beach we just cleaned."

Qingqing fell silent.

She remembered that beach, and the smiles on the children's faces when they picked up seventy-six kilograms of trash.

"...Whose pipe is this, anyway?" Qingqing asked.

"I've looked into it." Liu-Liu shook its head, "That pipe isn't registered on any map. It's hidden in the weeds, extending from the industrial zone. I followed the pipe into the industrial zone and smelled..." Liu-Liu paused, "a very strong chemical smell. And a metallic smell."

"Metallic?" Qingqing frowned.

"Mhm." Liu-Liu nodded, "Like heavy metals. Electroplating plants, dyeing factories—places like that often have this kind of smell."

"Heavy metals..." Qingqing's heart sank.

"Class President," Liu-Liu's voice carried unprecedented seriousness, "should we tell everyone?"

Qingqing thought for a long time.

"Not yet." She finally shook her head, "Weixi said that until we find solid evidence, we shouldn't alarm everyone yet. And..." she paused, "she said she seems to... know when this pipe started."

"Weixi knows?" Liu-Liu's eyes went wide.

"Mhm." Qingqing lowered her head, her voice soft, "She only told me one sentence—'It's exactly the same as the time I remember.'"

Liu-Liu fell silent.

"Then what do we do now..." Liu-Liu asked in a low voice, "just keep reconnoitering?"

"Keep reconnoitering." Qingqing nodded, "But we need to start preparing. I have a feeling this thing... is about to blow wide open."

In fact, Dean Zhou had also noticed the school's anomaly.

"Abnormal water quality in the drinking fountains these past two weeks?" In the principal's office, Dean Zhou flipped through the health room records, brows tightly knit.

"Mhm." Principal Qiu held his tea cup, his expression uncommonly grave, "Over a dozen children have had stomach discomfort, yet the water company says the water quality passes. There's definitely something wrong in between."

"I suspect the problem isn't the drinking fountains." Dean Zhou said.

"Oh?"

"The drainage ditch behind the school." Dean Zhou put down the records, his voice low, "These past few days, I noticed the color of that ditch water isn't right. And at the river mouth..." he paused, "I went to look. There are dead fish."

"Dead fish?" Principal Qiu's hand trembled slightly.

"Principal," Dean Zhou stood up, "I want to investigate where exactly the upstream of that drainage ditch leads."

Principal Qiu was silent for a long time, then nodded heavily: "...Go investigate. Not one of those responsible can be let off."

In the days that followed, strange changes appeared in the school, one after another.

The first to notice was Old Wu.

"Hey! Don't you guys feel it," one day at noon, Old Wu held up his water bottle, wrinkling his nose, "the water from the school drinking fountain tastes... weird?"

"Weird?" A-Xin leaned in and took a sip too, "Does it? Isn't it just regular water?"

"No!" Old Wu shook his head, "There's a... hard-to-describe taste. Like disinfectant, but not quite. Anyway, it's different from before!"

"It's your nose that's broken!" A-Xin laughed heartily, "Didn't you just eat two bowls of braised pork rice? That flavor's covering everything!"

"I'm not broken!" Old Wu refused to concede, "Even our dog can smell it!"

"You don't have a dog!"

"I meant 'the dog next door'!"

The children laughed together.

Only Weixi, sitting by the window, didn't laugh.

She held her own water bottle, quietly looking toward the drainage ditch outside the window.

"...It's begun." she said in a barely audible voice.

"Weixi, what did you say?" Lin Yunyun turned around to ask.

"Nothing." Weixi shook her head and capped her water bottle, "I was just thinking... maybe we should get everyone to pay attention to the school's water."

"The school's water?" Lin Yunyun was puzzled, "What about the water?"

"Nothing." Weixi shook her head again, "I was just... talking casually."

However, the words Weixi "said casually" came true two days later.

That morning, a flurry of frightened screams erupted in the classroom of Class 6-1.

"Ah—! Someone threw up!"

"What's going on?!"

"It's Ada! Chen Ada from Class 6-2! He just threw up at the classroom door!"

The children buzzed with discussion. Even Chen Ada from the neighboring class was sent to the health room, his face pale.

"The doctor said it might be gastroenteritis." Qingqing's mother told her over the phone, "The school has had several students with stomach trouble lately. The health room teacher says it seems to start after drinking the drinking fountain water."

"The drinking fountain water?" Qingqing's heart leaped.

"Yeah," Qingqing's mother continued, "I heard the school has asked someone to check the water quality. Hope it's nothing serious..."

Qingqing hung up and looked out the window.

In the direction of the drainage ditch, everything was gray and hazy.

An uneasy premonition rose in her heart.

That afternoon, in the health room.

"Qingqing," the health room teacher looked at the records in her hand, frowning, "over the past two weeks, more than a dozen students across the school have come to me with stomach trouble."

"A dozen or more?" Qingqing asked in surprise.

"Mhm." The health room teacher nodded, "The symptoms are all similar: after drinking the drinking fountain water, stomach pain and nausea begin. The school has already asked the water company to inspect, but..." she sighed, "the inspection result says the school's water quality meets the standards."

"Meets the standards?" Qingqing frowned.

"Yes," the teacher said helplessly, "the water company said all the water quality tests passed. So... for now, the school can only keep observing."

"But what if the problem isn't the drinking fountains?" A soft voice came from the health room doorway.

It was Weixi.

"Not the drinking fountains?" The health room teacher was puzzled, "Then what?"

"The school's drinking fountains use tap water." Weixi walked in, her voice soft yet clear, "But the drainage ditch behind the school, and the seawater at the river mouth—their water quality has already changed."

"The drainage ditch?" The health room teacher blinked, "What does that have to do with the school drinking fountains?"

"Nothing for now." Weixi shook her head, "But if we let it go, one day it will have something to do with our lives. Because..." she looked out the window, "the water we drink and the things we throw away are all connected in the end."

The health room teacher was silent for a moment.

"You transfer student," she looked at Weixi, curiosity in her voice, "why do you talk like an adult?"

Weixi didn't answer, only smiled faintly.

Walking out of the health room, Qingqing caught up to Weixi.

"Weixi, do you... know something?" Qingqing asked in a low voice.

Weixi stopped.

"Qingqing," she turned around, a nameless complexity in her eyes, "that pipe... I've actually... known about it all along."

"What?!" Qingqing's eyes went wide, "You knew all along?! Then why—"

"Because..." Weixi lowered her head, "in my era, I heard this story. A river was polluted by wastewater discharged from nearby factories. The river changed color, and the fish died. It took the adults decades to restore that river. But..."

"But what?"

"But the people of that era," Weixi's voice was light as a sigh, "had already forgotten what that river originally looked like."

And that same day, Liu-Liu brought back even worse news.

"Class President! Urgent report!" Liu-Liu burst out of the ventilation duct, its voice trembling, "I just flew along the ditch to the river mouth and found—the seawater has changed color!"

"What?!" Qingqing's eyes went wide.

"The seawater near the river mouth," Liu-Liu's electronic eyes displayed a shocking stream of data, "has changed from its original blue-green to an... indescribable grayish-yellow! And I detected fish floating belly-up nearby!"

"Fish..." Qingqing's face turned white.

"That pipe," Liu-Liu's voice grew more and more urgent, "its discharge rate has increased again! Double what it was last week!"

"Doubled?!" Qingqing almost shouted, "Why would it suddenly—"

"I don't know!" Liu-Liu spun around frantically, "But at this rate, the beach we cleaned will soon be flooded by polluted seawater!"

Qingqing took a deep breath, forcing herself to calm down.

"Liu-Liu," she lowered her voice, "when exactly did this pipe start discharging?"

"I checked the earlier data!" Liu-Liu opened its reconnaissance diary, "The ditch's odor was first detected on..."

Liu-Liu's voice suddenly stopped.

"When was it?" Qingqing pressed.

"It was on..." Liu-Liu trembled as it read out the record, "October 12th."

"October 12th..." Qingqing repeated softly, "isn't that..."

"Isn't that the day Weixi transferred to our school?" Liu-Liu's voice was light as a feather.

The equipment room suddenly fell into a terrifying silence.

Qingqing and Liu-Liu stared at each other.

"This can't be a coincidence, right?" Liu-Liu asked in a low voice.

"..." Qingqing didn't answer, but her hand, holding the data report, trembled slightly.

If all of this wasn't a coincidence...

Then what else was Weixi hiding?

After school, A-Xin did something even he found a little "creepy"—he began following Liu-Liu.

"Liu-Liu's been too strange lately," A-Xin leaned on the hallway railing, watching Liu-Liu dart with a "swish" in the direction of the drainage ditch, "it must be hiding something from us! I'm going to find out what it's sneaking around doing every day!"

A-Xin crept along behind Liu-Liu, following it all the way to the seawall behind the school.

Then he saw this—

Liu-Liu crouched at the river mouth of the drainage ditch, its electronic eyes shooting blue scanning beams back and forth across the water's surface, muttering to itself:

"Odor index... ninety-one. Heavy metal concentration... six times over the limit. Water color... grayish-yellow. This is bad, this is really bad..."

Actually, before jumping out, A-Xin had already been hiding in the grass, watching for a very, very long time.

He saw the grayish-yellow drainage water flowing all the way into the river mouth.

He saw the dead fish with bellies up on the shallows.

He saw the ring of discolored seawater, expanding little by little.

"This..." A-Xin hid in the grass, his palms covered in cold sweat, "what's going on? This ditch... wasn't like this before..."

He remembered that months ago, everyone had simply called it "that stinky ditch behind the school"; he remembered the deathly pale face Weixi made the first time she heard the words "drainage ditch."

"So that's how it is," A-Xin murmured to himself, "Weixi... she knew this day would come."

He clenched his fist, took a deep breath, and then—

"Liu-Liu!" A-Xin jumped out of the grass, "What are you doing here?!"

"Waaah—!" Liu-Liu's tail exploded in fright, terror in its electronic eyes, "A-A-Xin?! How did you get here?!"

"Of course I'm here!" A-Xin planted his hands on his hips, "You've been sneaking around every day, either in the classroom or running here! What exactly are you doing?!"

"I... I'm..." Liu-Liu stammered nervously, "I'm... observing the ecosystem! Yes! Observing the ecosystem! Nature is so beautiful!"

"Tell that to the ghosts!" A-Xin pointed at the grayish-yellow water at the river mouth, "This water has turned like this, and you're telling me 'nature is so beautiful'?! What did you mean by 'odor index ninety-one' and 'heavy metals six times over the limit'?!"

Liu-Liu fell silent.

It lowered its head, tail drooping.

"A-Xin..." Liu-Liu's voice was full of difficulty, "this matter... I really can't say. At least not yet."

"That phrase again!" A-Xin couldn't help shouting, "Last time you said 'telling would harm Weixi'! What about this time?! Does it have something to do with Weixi?!"

Liu-Liu's electronic eyes flashed suddenly.

"...You guessed it?" Liu-Liu asked in a low voice.

"I guessed it long ago!" A-Xin crouched down, looking straight into Liu-Liu's eyes, "Liu-Liu, I'm not stupid! Weixi has secrets, you have secrets, I know all that! I just..."

"Just what?"

"Just want to know," A-Xin's voice carried a plea for the first time, "are these secrets connected to our class's 'Campaign'? Connected to this stinking ditch and this dirtied sea?"

Liu-Liu was silent for a very, very long time.

Finally, it spoke softly:

"A-Xin, have you heard a saying?"

"What saying?"

"'The more you know, the heavier the burden on your shoulders.'" Liu-Liu said, "Qingqing and Weixi aren't unwilling to tell you—they're afraid of... dragging you into it too."

"Dragging me in?" A-Xin blinked, "Dragged into what?"

"..." Liu-Liu didn't answer, only lowered its head.

A-Xin looked at Liu-Liu's hesitant expression and was silent for a long time.

"Liu-Liu," he finally said, "listen to me. No matter what secret you're guarding, no matter how scary it is—I, A-Xin, am not someone who gets 'dragged into' things. I'm someone who 'jumps in' of his own accord!"

"You guard your secret, and I'll help you guard it together!"

"But," A-Xin looked at Liu-Liu seriously, "if one day Weixi needs someone standing by her side—you absolutely must not leave me out! That's our promise!"

A complex light flickered in Liu-Liu's electronic eyes.

"...A-Xin," it said softly, "you really are a true friend."

"Of course!" A-Xin puffed out his chest, "I'm the captain of the 'Tree Adoption Raid Squad'!"

Late that night, Liu-Liu made one last flight to the top of that pipe.

In the moonlight, the pipe was still spewing grayish-green liquid.

Liu-Liu recorded the final data point, then flew back to school.

"Class President," Liu-Liu slipped into the sports equipment room, its voice unusually calm, yet making Qingqing's hairs stand on end, "they've... sped up."

"What?"

"That pipe's discharge rate increased another fifty percent tonight." Liu-Liu said, "At this rate, within a month at most... the entire sea behind the school will turn gray."

The equipment room was silent for a long time.

"...One month." Qingqing repeated softly.

She lowered her head and looked at the watch on her wrist.

Then she remembered the countdown number on Weixi's bracelet.

"Weixi's time is almost up too." Qingqing murmured, "One month... what can we do with one month?"

"Enough to do a lot of things." Liu-Liu said, "As long as we want to."

"...Yeah." Qingqing looked up, light returning to her eyes, "As long as we want to."

That night, Weixi wasn't asleep either.

She sat by the window, looking at the bracelet on her wrist.

The countdown number on the inside of the bracelet flickered quietly in the moonlight.

—Thirty-nine.

"Thirty-nine days left," she said in a low voice, "but... they've already begun."

She remembered the news she had heard today at the health room—the students falling ill one after another; the seawater at the river mouth starting to change color.

"Exactly as Grandma said," she murmured, "Grandma said that in that era, the adults were the same: first a small smell, then a small discomfort, and then..."

She didn't finish.

She just gently pressed the bracelet against her chest, as if protecting something precious.

"If... I could be given a little more time," she said softly, "enough for these children to save this world."

The moonlight shone quietly on her.

The drainage ditch outside the window still flowed quietly in the darkness.

Outside the window, moonlight shone on the drainage ditch.

That grayish-green water was still flowing silently.

Flowing toward that beach, which the children had just cleaned.
