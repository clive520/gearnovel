# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 12: The Schoolwide Green Fair

"We need money."

That day after school, Qingqing stood at the podium and said those words with a serious tone.

"Money?" A-Xin's eyes went wide, "Class President, are you short on cash?"

"It's not me who's short on cash," Qingqing shook her head, "it's 'Protect Earth' that's short on cash. Think about it—to investigate the drainage ditch's pipe, we need tools; to clean the beach every month, we need gloves and trash bags; to plant trees, we need saplings and fertilizer... all of that costs money."

"That's right!" Old Wu suddenly understood, "We can't keep asking the principal for money forever!"

"So," Qingqing took a deep breath, "I came up with an idea—let's hold a fair!"

"A fair?!" The whole class's eyes lit up instantly.

"Yes! A 'Green Fair'!" Qingqing announced loudly, "With environmental protection as the theme! We'll use the fair to promote environmental protection and raise funds, and invite the whole school and all the parents!"

"This idea is amazing!" A-Xin jumped up excitedly, "A fair! I'm the best at playing!"

"It's not just playing!" Qingqing added, "We'll make everyone learn environmental protection while they play! So every booth has to be connected to environmental protection!"

"Then what booths should we set up?" The children buzzed with discussion.

"Everyone, think in your groups!" Qingqing said, "Each raid squad is in charge of one booth!"

And so, for the next week, the lights in the classroom of Class 6-1 stayed on every evening.

The children buzzed about like little bees, designing posters, making props, and preparing materials.

"My 'Plastic-Free Sharpshooter' went through precision calculations!" A-Xin proudly displayed his design drawing, covered with trajectory calculations, "The arrow's range, the hanging height of the plastic bag target, the effect of wind direction... all taken into account!"

"You're even considering wind direction?" Wang Zhuankai's eyes went wide, "Are you shooting, or landing on the moon?"

"Landing on the moon also requires ballistic calculations!" A-Xin declared with full confidence.

"A-Xin," Qingqing leaned in for a look at the design drawing and couldn't help holding her forehead, "your target... is hanging two meters high? How are little kids supposed to hit it?"

"So..." A-Xin mysteriously pulled a small stool from behind his back, "I prepared a 'shorty ladder'!"

"You call that a 'shorty ladder,'" Old Wu leaned in, "isn't that just a stool?"

"It's an 'auxiliary shooting platform'!" A-Xin corrected with great ceremony.

"Hahaha!" The children burst into laughter.

"Everyone, come look at this!" Lin Yunyun came running over holding a crookedly drawn poster, "My 'Trash-Sorting Nine-Grid' poster! What do you think?"

On the poster, nine squares contained various kinds of trash: PET bottles, tissue, batteries, old books, broken toys, food waste...

"Lin Yunyun," A-Xin pointed at one of the squares, "that 'old book' you drew looks like a turtle!"

"Ah!" Lin Yunyun blushed, "I... I don't draw very well..."

"No, no!" Qingqing quickly comforted her, "It's very creative! A turtle is also... a kind of book! I mean, it's very recognizable!"

"Pfft!" Wang Zhuankai couldn't help laughing.

"You guys..." Lin Yunyun puffed up angrily, "I'm not talking to you anymore!"

"Hahaha!"

The children's laughter echoed through the classroom.

"Trash-Sorting Raid Squad!" Lin Yunyun spread out a design drawing, "Our booth is called 'Trash-Sorting Nine-Grid'! Throw a soft ball at a nine-grid board; whichever square you hit, you have to answer how that square's trash should be sorted! Answer right, win a small prize!"

"Good! This is educational!" Qingqing nodded.

"The Plastic-Free Sharpshooter!" A-Xin proudly raised a "eco-bow" made of cardboard, "Our booth uses paper arrows to shoot targets made of plastic bags! Hit one, and you've 'destroyed' one plastic bag!"

"Your..." Qingqing laughed helplessly, "the bow and arrow won't hurt anyone, right?"

"Impossible!" A-Xin patted his chest in assurance, "The arrowheads are soft! And the target is three meters away! I calculated the trajectory!"

"The Electricity-Saving Challenge!" Wang Zhuankai brought out a thick stack of question cards, "We'll quiz everyone: what temperature should the AC be set to? How much electricity does a TV in standby mode use? When is the best time to run the washing machine? Answer right, get a stamp!"

"Tree Adoption Raid Squad!" Old Wu carried over a pile of small potted plants, "We're selling 'seed pots'! Fifty dollars each! Take one home, plant it, and when it sprouts, that's one more little tree!"

"These seeds are..." Qingqing asked.

"They're seeds we picked!" Old Wu patted his chest, "From the trees behind the school! We picked them up one by one and planted them in little pots! Every pot sold is a 'future little tree'!"

"The Zero-Waste Snack Bar!" The girls of a neighboring group raised their hands, "We're making snacks from 'ugly fruits and vegetables'! The market auntie said that all the produce that looks bad and can't be sold gets thrown away! We're turning them into fruit tea and vegetable pancakes! So everyone knows that 'ugly' things can be delicious too!"

"I like this one!" Old Wu was nearly drooling.

"Intel & Publicity Raid Squad!" Liu-Liu snapped to attention, "Our booth is called 'Protect Earth Intel Station'! I'll display the drainage ditch's water quality data, the beach cleanup photos, and host an eco-knowledge quiz!"

"And!" A-Xin added mysteriously, "we're setting up the most special booth—'A Letter to the Future'!"

"To the future?" The children looked at each other.

"Yes!" A-Xin brought out a thick stack of postcards, "On fair day, we'll have everyone write a postcard, addressed to 'the future forty years from now'! Write down what you want the Earth of the future to be like! Twenty dollars each, for fundraising!"

"To the future forty years from now..." The children fell silent for a moment.

"Then... where do those postcards get mailed to?" Lin Yunyun asked softly.

"To..." A-Xin thought for a moment, "to 'the future Earth'! The Earth will always be there, so the letters will definitely be received!"

The children laughed.

Only Weixi, looking at those postcards, had a complex light flicker in her eyes.

On fair day, the weather was unexpectedly beautiful.

Sunlight spilled across the playground of Luyang Elementary, making every flag sparkle.

"The Green Fair officially begins—!"

Principal Qiu Shouren, holding his thermos cup of tea, smiling, cut the ribbon for the fair.

"Dear parents, dear students!" Principal Qiu's voice carried through the microphone across the whole playground, "Today's fair was organized by the children of Class 6-1 themselves! The theme is environmental protection! Please come and support them!"

"Okay—!" A wave of cheers rose from the playground.

The fair's booths lined up in a row across the playground.

In front of the "Trash-Sorting Nine-Grid" booth, a long line had formed.

"Throw here! Throw here!" Lin Yunyun directed nervously, "PET bottles go in 'recyclables'!"

"Correct! Stamp!"

"Next question!" A-Xin grabbed the soft ball, "Hehe, this one I wrote—where does 'used tissue' go?"

"Tissue?!" The waiting kids were stunned, "Doesn't tissue go... in the toilet?"

"Wrong!" A-Xin wagged his finger smugly, "Used tissue goes in 'general waste'! Flushing it clogs the pipes, and recycling it contaminates the whole recycling bag! The correct answer—'general waste'!"

"Ah—!" The kids wailed, "That's too hard!"

"If it were easy, it wouldn't be called a 'Campaign'!" A-Xin laughed heartily.

In front of the "Plastic-Free Sharpshooter," A-Xin shouted loudly: "Come, come! Shoot the plastic bags! Destroy one with each arrow!"

"Boss! I want to shoot the biggest one!" A young boy from the lower grades pointed at the largest plastic bag target.

"Alright! Go for it!" A-Xin handed him the "eco-bow."

"Swish—!" The paper arrow flew crookedly and "thwacked" onto the spot beside the target.

"Oh! So close!" A-Xin lamented dramatically, "The plastic bag escaped! Try again!"

In front of the "Electricity-Saving Challenge," Wang Zhuankai worked up a sweat: "What temperature for the AC?! Twenty-six degrees! Correct!"

"When is the best time to use the washing machine?! Off-peak hours!"

"What kind of light bulb should you use?! LED!"

"Wow! How do you know all this?" A mother marveled.

"Because... because our class gets quizzed every day!" Wang Zhuankai scratched his head sheepishly.

In front of the "Seed Pots" booth, Old Wu's voice was the loudest: "Fifty dollars a pot! Plant a future little tree! Take one home, and when it sprouts, remember to take a photo and show us!"

"Boss, how do I take care of this pot?" An elderly woman asked.

"Water it every day and put it in a sunny spot!" Old Wu patted his chest, "I guarantee it will sprout! If it doesn't, come find me, and I'll give you ten pots!"

"If you give away ten pots, you'll run out of saplings!" A-Xin teased from the side.

"Then I'll go pick more!" Old Wu declared with full confidence.

"Hahaha!" Laughter rose around the booth.

In front of the "Zero-Waste Snack Bar," there was an even longer line.

"This is fruit tea made from 'ugly apples'! These are pancakes made from 'crooked carrots'!" The girls greeted everyone enthusiastically, "Just because it's ugly doesn't mean it isn't delicious! Supporting ugly produce means supporting zero waste!"

"So delicious!" A mother exclaimed, "This is really made from apples that 'couldn't be sold'?!"

"Yes! The market auntie gave them to us!" The girls puffed out their chests proudly.

"Then I want to support ugly produce too!" "Me too!" "Auntie, one more cup!"

"Line up! Line up!" Old Wu dashed over from the neighboring booth to keep order, "Ugly produce snacks for everyone!"

"Aren't you supposed to be watching your pots?" Qingqing laughed helplessly.

"The pots are sold out!" Old Wu announced proudly, "All eighty-seven pots, completely sold out!"

"Wow!" Qingqing's eyes went wide.

"And," Old Wu lowered his voice mysteriously, "I secretly saw Dean Zhou just buy two pots!"

"The Dean bought pots?!" Qingqing was stunned.

"Yes!" Old Wu nodded, "And after buying them, he stared at them for a long time, then quietly told Lin Yunyun: 'When the seeds sprout, remember to take them to the principal's office to show him.'"

A warm feeling welled up in Qingqing's heart.

While in the corner of the playground, at the "A Letter to the Future" booth, children lined up, picked up pens, and wrote seriously.

"I'm writing—'I hope the Earth of the future has a clean sea!'"

"I'm writing—'I hope the people of the future won't throw trash into the sea anymore!'"

"I'm writing—'I hope the phoenix trees will still be here in the future!'"

One postcard after another was dropped into a big mailbox painted with the Earth's image.

"Weixi! Weixi! Look what I wrote!" A-Xin came running over triumphantly, holding up his postcard.

Weixi took it and looked—

"To the future Earth: Hello! I'm A-Xin! I'm twelve years old! I promise you, when I grow up, I will pick up every piece of trash on the beach!—A-Xin"

"Well written... full of spirit!" Weixi couldn't help laughing.

"Of course!" A-Xin puffed out his chest proudly, "What I wrote is a 'letter of guarantee'!"

"Then I want to see mine too!" Old Wu squeezed over, "I wrote—'To the future Earth: Hello, I'm Old Wu! I promise not to waste food from now on! But I still want to eat pork buns!—Old Wu'"

"Why are you still thinking about pork buns!" A-Xin teased.

"Pork buns and environmental protection don't conflict!" Old Wu declared with full confidence.

"Hahaha!" The children burst into laughter.

"Me! Me!" Lin Yunyun said softly, "I wrote... 'To the future Earth: I hope you can be well. I hope the trees there can live long, long lives.—Yunyun'"

"Well written!" Qingqing gave a thumbs up.

"And mine!" Wang Zhuankai held up his postcard, on which was written crookedly: "To the people of the future: Please remember, there are kids right now working very, very hard so that you can have clean water to drink!—Wang Zhuankai"

The children wrote, letter after letter.

One after another, they dropped them into that mailbox painted with the Earth.

Weixi stood to the side, quietly watching.

She saw that every sentence the children wrote was about clean seas, green trees, and endless drinking water.

"...What everyone wrote," she said in a low voice, "is the future I want most to see."

"Weixi," Qingqing walked over and said softly, "I saw the one you wrote too."

"Huh?" Weixi blinked.

"'Please remember, the people here once worked hard to protect the Earth.'" Qingqing repeated those words, "Weixi, actually... you don't need to worry. Even if one day you return to your era and can't see the letters we wrote—we will still remember that we once worked hard."

Weixi's nose suddenly tingled.

"Mhm." She lowered her head, softly, "I will remember."

Weixi stood by the booth, watching those postcards, her eyes slightly reddened.

"Weixi," Qingqing walked over and asked softly, "do you want to write one too?"

"I..." Weixi blinked, "Can I?"

"Of course!" Qingqing handed her a postcard, "You're one of us!"

Weixi took the postcard, picked up a pen, and thought for a very, very long time.

Then she wrote down a line.

Qingqing peeked at it—the line read:

"To myself, forty years from now: If you receive this letter one day, please remember—the people here once worked hard to protect the Earth."

After writing, Weixi gently dropped the postcard into the mailbox.

"Will it get delivered?" Qingqing asked.

"I don't know." Weixi shook her head and smiled faintly, "But I will remember that I sent this letter."

Halfway through the fair, Liu-Liu suddenly disappeared.

A-Xin caught it with his sharp eyes, seeing Liu-Liu sneak off toward the drainage ditch behind the school.

"Liu-Liu!" A-Xin chased after it in a low voice, "The fair is in full swing! Why are you coming here?"

"Beep beep... A-Xin..." Liu-Liu crouched by the ditch, gravity in its electronic eyes, "I came to check... whether that pipe is discharging again today."

"It's fair day!" A-Xin said, "Does that pipe keep a calendar too?"

"Pollution doesn't keep calendars." Liu-Liu shook its head, flew to the river mouth, scanned a circle, and then its face fell dramatically, "Beep beep! Bad news! A-Xin!"

"What is it?!"

"The sea!" Liu-Liu's voice was trembling, "The seawater at the river mouth—the discolored area has expanded again! Twice as large as yesterday!"

"Twice as large?!" A-Xin's heart sank heavily.

"That pipe," Liu-Liu said in a low voice, "its discharge speed is getting faster every day. At this rate..."

"At this rate, our fair was all for nothing." A-Xin said in a low voice, "Even if we teach everyone at the fair to 'love the Earth' a hundred times, that sea will still get dirtied."

Liu-Liu fell silent.

"Liu-Liu," A-Xin suddenly spoke, his tone more serious than ever before, "what are we using the money raised at the fair for?"

"The Class President said to buy water quality testing tools."

"That's too slow." A-Xin shook his head, "We should use that money to investigate the truth about that pipe."

"A-Xin..." Liu-Liu froze.

"I know you're still guarding your secrets," A-Xin crouched down, looking straight into Liu-Liu's eyes, "but guarding secrets and finding out the truth are two different things! What we want to investigate is the truth about that pipe! That's allowed, right?"

Liu-Liu thought for a long time, then nodded softly:

"...Fine. That money, we'll use to find out the truth."

"Great!" A-Xin stood up and clenched his fist, "Then once the fair is over, we officially take action!"

At dusk, the booths were packed up one after another.

The children, though exhausted and swaying on their feet, all had sparkling eyes.

"A-Xin," Weixi walked over to A-Xin, who was packing up his booth, and asked softly, "were you... happy today?"

"Happy!" A-Xin answered without a second thought, "I've never been this tired, but I've also never been this happy!"

"Why are you happy?" Weixi asked.

"Because..." A-Xin thought for a moment, then suddenly turned serious, "today I saw so many people smiling because of the things we made. Some people learned how to sort trash, some bought seed pots and said they'd plant them at home, some wrote letters to the future... Weixi, today I suddenly felt that the things we're doing really do make a difference."

Weixi froze.

"A-Xin," she smiled softly, "do you know? What you just said is the thing I most wanted to hear."

"What thing?"

"'The things we're doing really do make a difference.'" Weixi said, "As long as one person, because of today's fair, is willing to use one less plastic bag, plant one more tree, sort their trash properly... then today's fair wasn't held in vain."

"Right!" A-Xin nodded hard, and then he suddenly blurted out, "Weixi, when I grow up, I'm going to open a shop!"

"Open a shop?" Weixi asked curiously, "What kind of shop?"

"A shop that 'sells eco-friendly stuff'!" A-Xin's eyes sparkled, "Selling eco-bags, seeds, solar-powered little toys! I want everyone to know that environmental protection can be fun too!"

Looking at A-Xin's radiant face, Weixi's eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"A-Xin," she said softly, "I believe you'll definitely do it."

"Of course!" A-Xin patted his chest, "I'm a member of the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit'!"

The fair came to a perfect close at dusk.

The children cleaned up the booths, sat in a circle on the playground, and waited for the final "settlement."

"Final tally!" Liu-Liu carried a big account sheet and jumped onto the stage, "For this 'Green Fair,' total revenue—thirty-eight thousand six hundred fifty dollars!"

"Over thirty-eight thousand?!" The children gasped.

"Of which, 'A Letter to the Future' raised five thousand two hundred! 'Seed Pots' sold eighty-seven pots! The 'Zero-Waste Snack Bar'... completely sold out!"

"Yay—!" The children erupted in thunderous cheers.

"This money," Qingqing stood up and announced loudly, "we're going to use it to—buy water quality testing tools, and investigate the truth behind the pollution of the drainage ditch behind our school!"

"Investigate the truth!" The children shouted in unison.

"Alright! 'Protect Earth Operations Unit,' next mission—'Track the Pipe'!"

"Okay—!"

The children's cheers echoed across the playground at dusk for a long time.

And Dean Zhou, standing at the edge of the playground, quietly tucked the "Letter to the Future" in his pocket properly.

The letter read:

"To the Dean of the future: Thank you for believing in us once."

Dean Zhou said nothing.

He simply tucked the letter into the pocket over his chest and quietly turned to leave.

"Dean!" A soft voice called out to him.

Dean Zhou stopped and turned around—it was Weixi.

"Dean," Weixi walked up to him and bowed slightly, "thank you."

"Thank me for what?" Dean Zhou's tone was flat, "I just... cleaned up the fair's trash."

"No." Weixi shook her head, "I heard from Lin Yunyun that you bought two seed pots; and I heard that you kept reading the letters the children wrote."

Dean Zhou fell silent.

"Dean," Weixi's voice was soft, "you've always known that what the children are doing is right, haven't you?"

Dean Zhou looked at her, for a very, very long time.

"...I don't know what counts as 'right,'" he finally spoke, his voice low, "I only know that in my twenty years as dean, this is the first time I've seen a group of children fight this hard over a stinking ditch behind the school."

"So," he looked at Weixi, a gentleness in his tone that even he hadn't noticed, "since they're fighting this hard, this adult can't afford to lose to a bunch of kids."

Weixi froze.

Then she smiled, her eyes reddening.

"Dean," she said softly, "the children will be very happy."

"Hmph," Dean Zhou put on a stern face and turned to leave, "Save it! I just... think those two pots look nice in front of the principal's office!"

"Dean, the pots will get shy!"

"..." Dean Zhou didn't turn back, but Weixi saw his shoulders tremble slightly.

Under the setting sun, the Black-Faced Judge's silhouette didn't seem so "black" anymore.

The sun was setting.

Weixi sat alone on the seawall, looking at that stretch of sea in the distance.

The seawater, under the setting sun, carried a nameless gray.

She gently touched the bracelet on her wrist.

The countdown number on the inside of the bracelet flickered faintly in the setting sun.

—Thirty-eight.

"The letters the children wrote," she said in a low voice, "will they reach my era?"

"If they do... will the me of forty years from now get to see them?"

The sea wind blew across her cheeks.

She lowered her head and smiled softly.

"...I really hope I can see them."
