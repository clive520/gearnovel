# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 9: Bringing Environmental Protection Home

In the first week after the "Inspect the Drainage Ditch" mission began, Liu-Liu submitted its first investigation report.

"Beep beep! Report!" Liu-Liu spread out a map covered in markings, data dancing in its electronic eyes, "After seven days of reconnaissance, I found that the smell of the drainage ditch behind the school doesn't start near the school—it comes from much further upstream! I flew three kilometers up the ditch, and the spot where the smell is strongest is over there!"

Liu-Liu drew a big circle on the map with its tail.

"Over there..." Qingqing leaned in for a closer look and frowned, "isn't that toward the industrial zone?"

"Mhm." Weixi walked over, looked at the map, and said softly, "But with just our little group, we don't have enough strength to investigate the entire ditch. And..."

She paused, sweeping her gaze across the whole class: "the pollution isn't only in that one ditch."

"Not in the ditch?" A-Xin asked in confusion, "Then where is it?"

"In our everyday lives." Weixi said, "Every plastic bag someone throws away, every cleaner someone rinses down the drain, every PET bottle someone uses—they all flow down the ditches, into the rivers, into the sea. The stink of that one ditch is something all of us 'contributed' to, bit by bit."

The classroom fell quiet for a moment.

"So," Qingqing stood up and announced loudly, "the next operation of 'Protect Earth' isn't at school—it's in each of our homes!"

"At home?" The children looked at each other.

"Yes!" Qingqing said, "We're going to bring the 'Campaign' home! Starting today, everyone goes home and rallies their parents, grandparents—to sort trash, save electricity, and cut down on plastic together with us!"

"That... that's way too hard!" A-Xin wailed, "My dad can't even sort his own socks!"

"That's exactly why you're going!" Qingqing planted her hands on her hips, "You're members of the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit'! The battlefield extends from the classroom to every home! This is called—'bringing environmental protection home'!"

"Okay—!" The children shouted in unison.

And so, starting the next day, the twenty-six children of Class 6-1 each launched their own "home campaign."

The first to surrender was A-Xin's house.

"Dad! Why did you buy plastic bags again!" A-Xin stood at the front door, hands on his hips like a little police officer, "Didn't we agree to bring our own shopping bags?"

"Huh? Those two plastic bags cost two dollars!" A-Xin's father held up his grocery basket, utterly confused, "Since when did you become an environmental inspection team?"

"Starting today!" A-Xin declared with full confidence, "Our class is carrying out 'Protect Earth'! Every plastic bag used is one less on Earth—throw it in the sea and it won't disappear for a hundred years!"

"A hundred years?" His father frowned, "Then the plastic bags I've used my whole life would pile up into a mountain!"

"So you have to use fewer!" A-Xin shouted, "Dad! You have to support me! I'm the leader of the 'Tree Adoption Raid Squad'!"

"Fine, fine, I support you, I support you!" His father shook his head helplessly, "Then tell me, if not plastic bags, what do I use to carry groceries?"

"Use these!" A-Xin produced, like magic, a neat stack of reusable shopping bags from behind his back, "I bought five with my allowance at school! One for each of us!"

"..." His father stared at the shopping bags and fell silent for two seconds, "...You even prepared for that?"

"I did my homework thoroughly!" A-Xin puffed out his chest proudly.

That evening, for the first time, no plastic bags appeared on the A-Xin family's dinner table.

Old Wu's house, meanwhile, was a different picture.

"Mom! Starting today, I'm in charge of sorting our household trash!" Old Wu rolled up his sleeves, grand and heroic, "Food waste goes in this bucket! Recyclables go in that bag! General waste goes in the trash can!"

"You?" Old Wu's mother stared wide-eyed and reached out to feel her son's forehead, "Are you running a fever? You can't even keep your socks together—now you're sorting trash?"

"Never judge a book by its cover!" Old Wu tilted his head up proudly, "I'm the leader of the 'Zero-Leftover Lunch Raid Squad'! Our class manages the whole school's leftover food—a bit of household trash is nothing!"

"Fine, fine," his mother laughed heartily, "then let's see how many days you last!"

"Forever!" Old Wu announced loudly.

"Hahaha!" His mother laughed until tears came out.

Lin Yunyun's house, however, didn't go so smoothly.

"Yunyun, what are you doing again?" Lin Yunyun's mother frowned at her daughter, who was crouching on the balcony, carefully folding old newspapers one by one.

"I'm organizing recyclables." Lin Yunyun said quietly, "Our class is carrying out 'Protect Earth,' and the teacher... I mean, my classmates said we should start at home."

"What campaign?" His mother's tone carried impatience, "Have you finished your homework? Isn't the monthly exam next week? Messing around with all this stuff all day—what if your grades drop?"

"I... I finished my homework..." Lin Yunyun lowered her head, her voice growing smaller and smaller.

"Then go review!" Her mother sighed, "Environmental protection is something adults should worry about. Why does a little kid like you need to meddle in it?"

Lin Yunyun said nothing more.

She silently placed the folded newspapers into the recycling bag, returned to her desk, and opened her textbook.

But her eyes kept sneaking, again and again, toward that bag of recyclables on the balcony.

The same thing happened at Wang Zhuankai's house.

"A-Kai, your grandmother says you told her to separate the food waste?" Wang Zhuankai's mother asked, holding a bowl of rice, puzzled.

"Yeah!" Wang Zhuankai nodded, "Food waste can be composted and used to grow vegetables! Otherwise it gets burned with the trash and produces lots of dirty air!"

"Why does an elementary school kid need to worry about these things?" His grandmother chimed in from the side, "We never separated it before, and the garbage truck still came!"

"But Grandma," Wang Zhuankai said with unusual seriousness, "people in the old days didn't have this much trash. One person today throws away more trash in a day than a whole family used to throw away in a month! If we don't sort it, there really won't be anywhere left for the trash to go."

His grandmother froze for a moment, glanced at her grandson, and said nothing more.

But that night, Wang Zhuankai noticed that his grandmother had quietly poured the food waste into a different bucket.

And the most ferocious battlefield of all was Qingqing's house.

"Dad! You can't just pour that waste oil down the ditch!" Qingqing dashed out of the house, grabbing her father's arm just as he was about to pour it out.

"Huh?" Her father held up the pitch-black oil can, face full of question marks, "Where else would I pour it? This is how I've always done it!"

"Always doing it that way doesn't mean it's right!" Qingqing jumped anxiously, "Waste oil poured down the ditch flows into the rivers and the sea, polluting the water sources! It's all over the news!"

"News?" Her father froze, "Since when did you start watching the news?"

"Since... I made a certain friend!" Qingqing said, "Anyway, waste oil has to be collected and taken to a proper recycling point! Dad, you've been fixing cars for twenty years—you must know where they collect waste oil, right?"

"I do know..." Her father scratched the back of his head, "but that's a hassle."

"Dad!" Qingqing planted her hands on her hips and deployed her trump card, "I, your daughter, am the 'Operations Commander' of the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit'! You wouldn't want your daughter's unit's first lost battlefield to be her own home, would you!"

"..." Her father was silent for three seconds, then laughed, "Alright, alright, Commander! From now on, I'll collect the waste oil and take it for recycling!"

"That's my good dad!" Qingqing smiled in satisfaction.

However, not every household was "conquered" as smoothly as Qingqing's.

A week later, Qingqing's mother, with a grave expression, handed a phone screenshot to Qingqing.

"Xiaoqing," her mother's voice carried worry, "in your class's parent group... a parent is complaining about you."

Qingqing took the phone and looked—

In the parent group chat, a message lay quietly at the very top:

"Dear parents, I'm Ke Zhiyuan's mother. I'd like to ask, what exactly is Class 6-1's 'Protect Earth Campaign'? Our Zhiyuan has come home these past two weeks, messing around with trash sorting, saving electricity, and planting trees all day; he scribbles through his homework and runs out to the balcony to look at his tree! I want to know—is this a school-mandated activity, or was it 'invented' by some teacher or student?"

Below it followed several supporting comments:

"Our kid is doing the same! Says they're 'bringing environmental protection home,' and ends up nagging at us all day!"

"Right! The monthly exam is coming up—isn't this a waste of study time?"

"Kids doing environmental stuff is just playing, isn't it? Why isn't the teacher doing something about it?"

Qingqing read those messages, and her face slowly sank.

"Mom..." she lowered her head, her voice dry, "do these parents think... what we're doing is wrong?"

"Xiaoqing," her mother crouched down and gently stroked her head, "Mom doesn't think what you're doing is wrong. But adults have their own worries. You're busy with all this all day—whether you've kept up with your schoolwork is what they naturally worry about."

"But we've kept up with our schoolwork too!" Qingqing's voice carried grievance, "Our class's grades even improved last semester! Why don't they look at that, and only look at us 'doing environmental stuff all day'?"

"Because..." her mother sighed, "adults always see only the side they worry about."

That night, Qingqing couldn't sleep.

She tossed and turned in bed, unable to fall asleep at all.

In her mind echoed those words from the parent group:

"Nagging at us all day..."

"Isn't this a waste of study time?"

"Kids doing environmental stuff is just playing, isn't it?"

She quietly got up, walked to the window, and looked at the five smokestacks faintly glowing in the distance through the night.

"Are we... really wrong?" she whispered.

The next morning, Qingqing walked into the classroom with two dark circles under her eyes.

"Qingqing, what's wrong?" Weixi noticed her change at once, "You don't look like you slept well."

"Weixi..." Qingqing hesitated, then handed her the phone, "Take a look at this."

Weixi took the phone and quietly read through those messages in the group, one by one.

Then she put down the phone and said softly:

"What they said isn't entirely unreasonable."

"What?!" Qingqing's eyes went wide.

"The adults worry about schoolwork because they think 'environmental protection' and 'studying' are two unrelated things." Weixi's voice was still soft, "But we all know they're actually the same thing—protecting the Earth is protecting the place where we study and grow up."

"Then how do we make the adults understand?" Qingqing asked.

Weixi thought for a moment, then said: "Adults don't believe what they hear 'with their mouths,' but they believe what they see 'with their eyes.' Instead of arguing with them, let's... let them see for themselves."

"See for themselves?"

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "Let's hold an event. Invite the fathers, mothers, grandfathers, grandmothers to come to school and see with their own eyes what we've done. Let the data speak, let the trees speak, and let..."

She paused, then smiled softly:

"...let the children speak."

And so, the next operation of "Protect Earth"—the "Green Family Day"—began in earnest.

Qingqing gathered her courage, personally drafted an invitation, and sent it to the parent group:

"Dear fathers, mothers, grandfathers, and grandmothers:
We are the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit' of Class 6-1.
These past two months, we've done many things at school: trash sorting, zero leftover lunches, saving electricity, planting trees.
We'd like to invite you to come to school this Friday afternoon to see what we've done.
Perhaps you'll find that what your children are doing isn't 'playing'—it's genuinely protecting our home.
—Sincerely, all twenty-six students of Class 6-1"

After the invitation went out, the group fell silent for an entire afternoon.

Qingqing stared at her phone anxiously, checking again and again.

"Will anyone come?" she asked quietly.

"I don't know." Weixi shook her head, "But as long as one person comes, we've already succeeded."

Friday afternoon.

Two o'clock.

At the classroom door of Class 6-1, Luyang Elementary, the first parent appeared—it was A-Xin's father.

"Dad! You came!" A-Xin was both surprised and delighted.

"Didn't you keep asking me to come?" His father coughed awkwardly, "I want to see for myself what this 'Campaign' you keep nagging about really is!"

Then, Old Wu's mother, Wang Zhuankai's grandmother, Qingqing's father...

One by one, they appeared at the classroom door.

Finally, even Ke Zhiyuan's mother came.

"Zhiyuan kept begging me to come," Ke's mother stood at the classroom door with her arms crossed, her tone reserved, "I want to see what activity is so good that Zhiyuan doesn't even want to play video games anymore."

"Welcome, welcome!" Qingqing took a deep breath, stepped up to the podium, and said loudly, "Dear parents, welcome to Class 6-1's 'Green Family Day'!"

And so, a special "tour" began.

"First, please look here!" Qingqing pointed at the row of sorting bins at the back of the classroom, "In these two months, our class has correctly sorted three hundred forty-two kilograms of recyclables!"

"Three hundred forty-two kilograms?!" A-Xin's father's eyes went wide.

"This is my daughter..." He stared at the neat row of recycling bins, lost for words.

"Please look here!" Old Wu stuck out his belly and pointed at the statistics chart on the wall, "Our class's lunch went from throwing away 7.8 kilograms a day to 0.5 kilograms! The food we saved could feed three hundred people!"

"Wow..." Old Wu's mother looked at her son, moved, "This glutton of mine is actually 'saving food'?"

"Please look here!" Wang Zhuankai pointed at the sapling lot outside the window, "We planted one hundred twenty-six trees, and every single one survived! Each one was planted by our own hands!"

"That one, 'Eat-Your-Fill,'" Wang Zhuankai's grandmother squinted at the little saplings outside, "was planted by my grandson?"

"Yes, Grandma!" Wang Zhuankai nodded, his eyes slightly reddened, "It'll keep growing, and it'll keep the school company forever!"

His grandmother said nothing.

But the look she gave that little sapling quietly softened.

Finally, Qingqing led the parents to the seawall behind the school.

"Dear parents," Qingqing pointed at that murky gray sea in the distance, "do you know? That drainage ditch behind the school has been getting stinkier these past two months. And the seawater has gained more and more things that shouldn't be there."

"What does that have to do with your 'Campaign'?" Ke's mother asked.

"The connection is," Weixi stepped forward, her voice soft yet clear, "that if we don't do these things now, one day the water we drink, the sea we swim in, and the vegetables we grow will all be dirtied by our own hands. Everything we're doing now is helping the adults hold on to a little more clean."

Ke's mother fell silent.

She looked at this gentle transfer student, then at the sea, and finally asked one question:

"These things... where did you learn them?"

Weixi didn't answer.

She only smiled faintly, then said softly:

"From an era when people hadn't yet forgotten how to love the Earth."

At that moment, the sea breeze blew past.

Ke's mother stood in place, staring at those five smokestacks in the distance, silent for a very, very long time.

"...Zhiyuan," she finally spoke, "what you've been doing—Mom didn't understand before. But now... I think I'm starting to."

"Mom!" Ke Zhiyuan threw himself into his mother's arms, his eyes red.

"Your 'Campaign,'" Ke's mother took a deep breath, her tone carrying unprecedented solemnity, "Mom supports you."

"Wow—!" The children erupted in thunderous cheers!

The parents looked at each other, then laughed along.

"Come on! Let's go back and water the trees for the kids!" A-Xin's father was the first to act.

"Right! From today on, all our household trash gets sorted!" Old Wu's mother announced loudly.

"I'm going to buy reusable bags tomorrow!" Wang Zhuankai's grandmother joined in the excitement.

That day, the setting sun was especially red.

The children walked home holding their parents' hands.

Qingqing held her father's hand and suddenly spoke: "Dad, thank you for coming."

"What's there to thank," her father ruffled her hair, his tone gentle, "I've actually been watching everything you do. It's just... adults have clumsy mouths and don't know how to say it."

"Dad..." Qingqing's eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"In any case," her father coughed twice, "from now on, I'll take the waste oil for recycling. Satisfied?"

"Satisfied!" Qingqing smiled, her eyes squeezed into happy crescents.

That evening, in the living room of Qingqing's house, the phone went "ding-dong" without stop.

Qingqing nervously picked up the phone and opened the parent group—then froze.

Messages popped up one after another:

"I went to the school today, and honestly, I was pretty moved. Those trees the kids planted—each one was watered and grown by their own hands." (A-Xin's father)

"That glutton of mine is actually managing the whole school's leftover food! As his mom, today is the first time I feel he's 'grown up.'" (Old Wu's mother)

"Our Yunyun has been organizing recyclables on the balcony for two months, and I never even noticed. I'm sorry—Mom was wrong before." (Lin Yunyun's mother)

"Grandma says we're switching all our plastic bags to reusable ones tomorrow. Zhiyuan, you win." (Ke's mother)

"Hahaha! My kid says tomorrow he's going to check whether my socks are sorted! (Joke) Anyway, we support the kids!" (Another parent)

One after another.

The words that had once kept Qingqing awake had all changed now.

Qingqing looked at those messages, and her eyes suddenly turned red.

"Mom!" She dashed into the living room and threw herself into her mother's arms, "Look! They... they all..."

"Mom can see." Her mother gently patted her back, her tone soft, "Xiaoqing, you've done something even adults couldn't do."

"What is it?"

"Made adults willing to look back at the things they throw away."

Qingqing lay in her mother's arms, nodding hard, tears falling drop by drop onto her mother's shoulder.

That night, the moonlight was especially bright.

And behind them, Weixi walked alone, quietly, along the seawall.

She watched the children, hand in hand with their parents, laughing and heading home, and the corners of her mouth lifted slightly.

"...This is right." she whispered, "The Earth is saved bit by bit, one small family at a time."

"Beep beep—" A small silver-white figure darted over from the direction of the drainage ditch, panting as it stopped at Weixi's feet.

"Weixi! I found an important clue!" Liu-Liu's electronic eyes carried unusual gravity, "I flew up the ditch and found a 'new' pipe upstream! Completely different from the old ones! It..."

"It's still discharging, isn't it?" Weixi asked.

"Yes!" Liu-Liu nodded, "And what it discharges changes the color of the seawater! I secretly took a drop and scanned it—it contains heavy metals, plus some chemical substances... that not even my database can identify!"

Weixi fell silent.

She looked toward the upstream direction of the ditch and said softly: "Finally... it's begun."

"Begun what?" Liu-Liu asked nervously.

"Nothing." Weixi shook her head, crouched down, and gently patted Liu-Liu's head, "Liu-Liu, don't tell everyone about this yet. Let the children be happy for a few more days."

"Why?" Liu-Liu didn't understand.

"Because..." Weixi looked at the five smokestacks in the distance, her voice light as a feather, "what's about to happen will be hard. I want everyone to at least remember first what 'happy' looks like."

Liu-Liu tilted its head, half understanding.

But looking at Weixi's gentle face, faintly laced with sorrow, it finally nodded.

"Beep beep. Got it." Liu-Liu said quietly, "I'll keep watching that pipe."

"Mhm." Weixi smiled softly, "Thank you, Liu-Liu."

She lowered her head and looked at the bracelet on her wrist.

On the inside of the bracelet, the countdown number flickered faintly in the setting sun.

—Forty.

The wind blew in from the sea.

She gently clenched the bracelet on her wrist, as if holding something precious.

"There's still a little time." she said softly, "Let me stay with them... just a little longer."
