# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 5: Future Projection & the Trash-Sorting Campaign

The morning after the Secret Alliance was formed, Qingqing and Weixi hid in the corner at the back of the classroom during the gap before morning self-study, plotting in hushed voices.

"So, how do we start?" Qingqing asked in a low voice.

Weixi thought for a moment, then said: "I've thought about it a lot. If we want people to care about the environment, we can't start by lecturing them with big principles—nobody wants to listen to that. The best place to start is something... everyone can see, something everyone encounters every day."

"Trash." Qingqing blurted out.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "Every single day, in every classroom, every person makes trash. But most people don't even look at the trash when they throw it away—they toss it and forget. Where I come from, trash is..."

She paused, as if searching for a way to put it that wouldn't frighten anyone: "is something that 'comes back to find you.'"

"Comes back to find us?" Qingqing frowned.

"Mhm." Weixi looked down at the bracelet on her wrist, her voice soft, "I'll show you something tonight, and you'll understand."

That noon, while the whole class was chasing each other around the playground or dozing at their desks, Qingqing and Weixi slipped into the sports equipment room.

"This should be private enough." Qingqing locked the door behind her and double-checked that the curtains were drawn.

Weixi nodded, reached out, and pressed the temporal bracelet on her wrist.

"Woom—!"

A blue-white light lit up, unfolding into a translucent glow in the dim equipment room.

"This is..." Qingqing's breath caught.

The image showed a seaside.

No—that couldn't be called a "seaside." It was a coast completely buried under trash. The beach showed not a single grain of sand; instead, it was densely covered with garbage piled higher than a person: plastic bags, PET bottles, Styrofoam, fishing nets, slippers, toothbrushes, syringes... What the waves washed ashore wasn't clean white foam, but wave after wave of gray-green sewage mixed with plastic scraps.

"This is the sea my grandmother took me to see when I was little." Weixi said, "By then, the coast had already become like this. The adults called it the 'trash coast.' But when my grandmother was young, this was a place where you could walk barefoot on the sand and pick up seashells."

The image shifted again.

On a stretch of sea, a huge whale was beached in the shallows.

As rescue workers cut open its belly, it wasn't full of food—it was packed with plastic bags, fishing line, and all kinds of nameless plastic fragments.

"This news," Weixi said, "actually happened in your era too."

"It did?" Qingqing was stunned.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "In the era I come from, the adults called it 'the first time humanity saw, on the news, the image of the garbage they threw away coming back to them.' After the news aired, the whole world cried. But after the crying..."

"After what?" Qingqing pressed.

"After that..." There was no blame in Weixi's tone, only a hint of bitterness, "after that, people kept throwing things away. Because everyone thought, 'It's okay, what's one more person not doing it?' and 'Everyone else is throwing it anyway.' By the time everyone finally realized something was wrong, it was already too late."

Qingqing fell silent.

She stared at the beached whale in the glow, and a heavy, unfamiliar feeling welled up in her heart.

"So... where should we start?" It was a long moment before Qingqing spoke again.

"Start where you can see the results." Weixi turned off the glow, and the equipment room returned to darkness, yet her eyes were bright, "All the trash our class makes in a whole day, gathered together, is already overflowing the trash can. But if we sort the trash properly, wash what can be recycled, and cut down on wasted food, those 'results' are visible."

"And..." Weixi smiled faintly, "trash sorting is something everyone can do. We don't have to wait for adults, don't have to wait for the government—we kids can start ourselves."

A "let's do this" smile rose to the corner of Qingqing's mouth.

During the break after second period in the afternoon, Qingqing stepped up to the podium and rapped the board eraser twice on the desk.

"Knock! Knock! Attention everyone, the Class President has a major announcement!"

The children, who had been chasing each other, turned around in unison.

"Starting today," Qingqing planted her hands on her hips, her voice ringing with determination, "our Class 6-1 is launching an operation called 'Protect Earth'!"

"'Protect Earth'? What's that? Sounds like a level in a video game!" A-Xin was the first to raise his hand.

"Take a look at the trash can in the back of the classroom," Qingqing pointed at the corner, "Have any of you ever wondered where that trash goes?"

Everyone turned to look. The trash can was indeed stuffed to the brim—half-drunk juice boxes, crumpled tissues, leftover school lunch, broken pens, a rope picked up from who knows where...

"Let me tell you," Qingqing said, "if this trash isn't sorted properly, isn't handled properly, one day it will become a mountain that buries the beach in front of our homes."

"Really?!" Old Wu forgot to even chew his pork bun, "The beach turns into a trash mountain?!"

"Have you seen the news? There's already more trash than fish in the ocean. A whale washed ashore, and when they cut it open, its belly was full of the plastic bags we threw away." Qingqing's voice grew more and more impassioned, "That trash is the trash we threw!"

The classroom fell silent for a second.

Then A-Xin slapped the desk and stood up excitedly: "Alright! So what do we do?!"

"It's simple," Qingqing hauled out a big box from under the podium, "we dump out all the trash in the classroom and sort it again! Into 'paper,' 'plastic,' 'glass,' 'metal,' 'food waste,' and 'general waste'—six bins. The class splits into groups, and each group is in charge of checking one type!"

"Okay—!" The whole class responded with a roar.

"Wait!" From the corner, Lin Yunyun timidly raised her hand, "Class President... if we sort it wrong, will we be punished?"

"No!" Qingqing patted her chest, "Sorting wrong won't get you punished, but Liu-Liu will 'return' it to you! Liu-Liu will bring the wrong-sorted trash back to your desk, and you sort it again!"

On the windowsill, Liu-Liu proudly puffed out its little chest, tail held high.

"Beep beep! I am the 'Trash Sorting Inspector'! Whoever sorts wrong, I deliver the trash to their seat—guaranteed door-to-door service!"

"Hahahaha!" The whole class burst into laughter.

"Alright then—'Protect Earth, Operation One: Trash-Sorting Campaign' begins now!"

At the command, an unprecedented "great battle" unfolded inside the classroom of Class 6-1.

Twenty-six children, plus one little pet, surrounded the piles of trash and started sorting with all their might.

"A-Xin! Juice boxes have to be rinsed clean before they can go into recycling!" Weixi crouched on the ground, her tone gentle but firm, "Otherwise the milk inside dries up and stinks up the whole recycling bag."

"Huh? Isn't a juice box just regular trash?" A-Xin's eyes went wide.

"No," Weixi shook her head, picked up a juice box, and deftly tore it open and flattened it, "A juice box is paper on the outside, aluminum in the middle, and plastic film on the inside—put together, all three can be recycled. But it has to be a used one. A half-drunk one counts as 'general waste.'"

"Wow, you know that too?!" A-Xin clicked his tongue in admiration.

"Old Wu, that's food waste! Food waste goes here!" Qingqing quickly blocked Old Wu, who was about to dump his leftover food into the "paper" bin.

"Ah! I misread it!" Old Wu's face flushed crimson, "But... where does food waste go? Wasn't it always thrown in with the regular trash?"

"That was then, this is now," Weixi walked over and gently scooped the food scraps into the food waste bin, "If food waste gets mixed into general waste and sent to the incinerator, it's too wet to burn cleanly and produces a lot of dirty air. Separating it out means it can be composted, turned into nutrients for growing crops, and grow fresh vegetables for us to eat."

"Oh—!" Old Wu suddenly understood, his eyes lighting up, "So food waste becomes vegetables, and vegetables become the pork buns in my stomach?! That's a total bargain!"

The whole class cracked up again.

"Ada, your PET bottle!" Qingqing called out again, "The cap has to be removed, the bottle crushed flat, and the cap and the bottle go in separate bins!"

"What?! Even the cap has to be separated?" Chen Ada from Class 6-2 poked his head in through the window, "Have you guys in 6-1 all gone crazy? Throwing away trash like you're doing an experiment!"

"You don't get it!" A-Xin shot back, "This is called caring for the Earth! If your PET bottle isn't properly disposed of, it won't decompose for a hundred years and will just lie in the sea!"

"A hundred years, then!" Ada made a face and ran off.

"Is he right?" Lin Yunyun quietly asked Weixi, "Does a PET bottle really take a hundred years to break down?"

"Plastic doesn't 'break down'; it just becomes fragments." Weixi said softly, "A hundred years, five hundred years—it's still there, just getting smaller and smaller, small enough for fish to swallow, small enough for us to stop seeing it. Just because we can't see it doesn't mean it isn't there."

The classroom suddenly fell quiet for a moment.

That sentence, like a fine needle, gently pricked everyone's heart.

The sorting continued.

Wang Zhuankai laughed the whole time he sorted, laughing until tears came out: "Hahahaha! That... that paper bowl! It's been washed too clean! The Class President said to wash it, but A-Xin scrubbed it with a steel wool pad until it nearly fell apart, hahaha!"

"If it's not scrubbed clean, how can it be recycled?" A-Xin defended himself, "Didn't you say if we do it, we should do it best?"

"Scrubbing it best and scrubbing it into holes are two different things!" Qingqing held her forehead.

Weixi crouched among the pile of recyclables, busily tying plastic bags into knots one by one. Her movements were practiced—so practiced they didn't look like a teenager's, as if she had done them a million times over.

Qingqing noticed this and her heart stirred.

"Weixi," she walked over and asked quietly, "where you come from... do people sort trash like this too?"

Weixi paused mid-motion, then said: "Where I come from, sorting isn't 'loving the Earth'; it's 'the rules.' Every household is limited in how many bags of trash they can throw out each day—exceed it and you're fined. Sort wrong and the whole bag gets rejected; you have to take it home and redo it."

"So... do people sort obediently?"

"Not at first." Weixi gave a faint, helpless smile, "Many people find it troublesome, unfair, thinking 'others don't sort, so why should I?' But later, when there was nowhere left to bury trash and the incinerators couldn't burn it all, people had no choice but to sort."

"Had no choice but to sort..." Qingqing repeated the phrase, lost in thought.

"So that's why I want to do it now," Weixi looked up, her eyes resolute, "while trash still has somewhere to be buried, while the beach can still be walked on barefoot, while people still have the 'choice'—to let everyone know: sorting isn't to save yourself trouble, it's to let the Earth live a little longer."

Before school let out that afternoon, the class finally finished organizing their first batch of sorted results.

Six big bags, lined up neatly at the back of the classroom: one bag of paper, one of plastic, one of juice boxes, a box of glass bottles, one bag of metal cans, and a bucket of food waste.

"Beep beep! Inspector's inspection complete!" Liu-Liu popped out of the pile of bags, a bit of shiny foil stuck to its tail, "This round's sorting accuracy rate: ninety-two point three percent! The ones sorted wrong are on their owners' desks—please 'claim' them yourselves!"

"Yay—!" The class high-fived and cheered excitedly.

"Only..." Qingqing frowned at the bags of recyclables, "there aren't any recycling bins at school to put these in, are there?"

"I know!" A-Xin jumped up, "There are two big bins by the wall behind the playground! I saw them before!"

"Then let's carry them over!" Old Wu didn't hesitate, hoisting the heaviest bag of plastic and charging for the door.

"Let's go, let's go!" The children streamed out after him in a swarm.

And so, this was the scene that appeared—

Twenty-six children of Class 6-1, carrying six bags of sorted trash, marched in a grand procession across the playground toward the recycling bins by the wall.

"Left, right, left, right! Sort the trash, love the Earth!" A-Xin led the chanting.

"Sort the trash, love the Earth!"

"Sort the trash, love the Earth!"

The children's synchronized chants echoed across the afternoon campus.

However, just as they were about to reach the recycling bins by the wall—

"Stop!"

A stern bellow, like a bucket of ice water, came down from above.

The children came to a halt in unison, looking at each other.

Stepping out from the corridor corner was Dean Zhou Yan—the school's most feared "Black-Faced Judge," whose name made children tremble.

Dean Zhou held a violation log under his arm, his hawk-like eyes sweeping over the twenty-six children before him, finally resting on the six bags of trash behind them.

"What are you doing?" The Dean's voice was low but cold as winter wind.

"Report, Dean!" Qingqing straightened her back and answered loudly, "We're carrying out the Trash-Sorting Campaign! This is to..."

"Trash sorting?" Dean Zhou cut her off, his brows knotting into a knot, "Not sleeping during lunch break, not going home after school, dragging trash around the whole campus?"

"Dean, this is a good thing!" A-Xin cut in, "We're protecting the Earth!"

"Protecting the Earth?" The Dean laughed coldly, "Have you finished your language homework? Corrected your math tests? Are you ready for next week's midterm?"

The children were suddenly struck speechless.

"Kids should do what kids are supposed to do," Dean Zhou's tone carried an unassailable authority, "study, take exams, do homework. Don't waste your time on all this nonsense!"

"But, Dean," Weixi stepped forward, her voice soft yet unusually firm, "if the Earth gets sick, then no matter how many books we read, no matter how high our scores are... there won't be anywhere left for us to study."

Dean Zhou froze for a moment, his gaze falling on this transfer student standing in the setting sun.

"You're Lin Weixi, right." The Dean's tone softened slightly, but remained hard, "I know you're a transfer student and may not be familiar with our school's rules yet. But let me tell you, at Luyang Elementary, a student's duty is to study. All this trash sorting and Earth protecting, you can talk about it when you're grown up!"

"But by the time we're grown up..." Weixi's voice trembled slightly, "it might be too late."

A gust of wind blew across the playground.

Twenty-six children stood in the setting sun, none of them speaking.

Dean Zhou was silent for several seconds, then left a final sentence and turned away.

"In any case, don't bring this stuff to school again. This is your one and only warning."

He walked off, leaving twenty-six children standing in place, staring at the six bags of trash they had so carefully sorted.

"What the heck..." A-Xin was the first to speak, indignant, "We were doing something good! He talked like we were doing something bad!"

"The Dean has always been strict..." Lin Yunyun lowered her head, her voice full of grievance, "He even records a demerit for secretly reading comics at nap time..."

"So... do we keep going?" Wang Zhuankai asked timidly.

That question plunged the class into silence.

The high spirits of a moment ago, like a popped balloon, drained away completely.

"Of course we keep going." A soft yet utterly unwavering voice broke the silence.

It was Weixi.

She bent down, slung the slipped bag back onto her shoulder, stood up straight, swept her gaze across all twenty-six people, and finally settled it on Qingqing.

"I've dreamed so many times of a world with no phoenix trees, no grass, no clean sea. Every morning I wake up, I tell myself: thank goodness, there's still time."

"If the Dean won't let us do it at school, then we start at home." Weixi's voice was soft, but every word seemed to plant itself in the ground, "Each of us, when we go home, sorts our household trash properly. We don't need anyone's permission to protect the Earth."

One by one, the children's eyes lit up again.

"Right!" A-Xin clenched his fist, "My house can do it too!"

"Mine too!" Old Wu chimed in.

"My house... can try too!" Lin Yunyun blushed but said it firmly, if quietly.

"Then it's settled!" Qingqing announced loudly, "'Protect Earth' Stage One mission: sort at home! Tomorrow morning, everyone brings their sorting results, and we'll tally them up!"

"Okay—!"

Under the setting sun, the shadows of twenty-six children lined up neatly across the playground, like a row of small, stubborn figures.

Standing on Qingqing's shoulder, Liu-Liu suddenly pricked up its ears and glanced toward the area behind the school.

"Beep beep... Class President," Liu-Liu lowered its voice, a hint of unease in its tone, "behind the school... there's a new smell. It's different from yesterday."

Qingqing followed Liu-Liu's gaze. The five smokestacks of the Taichung Thermal Power Plant stood quietly in the direction of the sea, nothing looking out of the ordinary.

But a faint, uneasy premonition rose in her heart.

That night, after finishing her homework, Qingqing lay on her desk, staring at the moon outside the window.

She remembered the images Weixi had projected today—the coast buried under trash, the whale with a belly full of plastic, the people lining up for water.

And she remembered Dean Zhou's words, "You can talk about it when you're grown up."

"But..." Qingqing murmured to herself, "if everyone keeps saying that, then when exactly will the Earth ever get protected?"

She picked up her phone, thought it over, and clicked on today's news.

On the screen was the report the whole world was watching—

"...The World Meteorological Organization issued a warning today that the intensity of the Super El Niño phenomenon could be the strongest in seventy years, with record-breaking heat, storms, and droughts appearing one after another around the globe. Scientists urge that if humanity does not act, the Earth's average temperature rise will breach the 1.5-degree threshold within the next three years..."

Qingqing put down her phone and looked at the pile of old newspapers she had brought home from school today, not yet sorted.

She sighed.

Then, silently, she opened the pile of old newspapers and, one by one, folded, pressed flat, and stacked them neatly.

"Dad! You're home!"

Qingqing's father ran a motorcycle repair shop and didn't drag himself home covered in grease until after nine at night. Seeing the old newspapers spread across the living room floor, the rinsed juice boxes, and the neatly lined-up PET bottles, he was taken aback.

"Xiaoqing, what's all this?"

"I'm doing trash sorting." Qingqing didn't look up, continuing to fold and flatten the newspapers, "Dad, from now on, I'll be in charge of sorting our household trash."

"Sorting?" Her father yawned, walked over to the fridge and grabbed a soda, "Why are you kids messing with this? When the garbage truck comes, you just throw the whole bag out."

"No!" Qingqing looked up sharply and recounted the news she had seen at school in full detail—plastic flooding the ocean, a whale with a belly full of trash, beaches buried under garbage.

"Dad," Qingqing finished, looking seriously at her father, "if every one of us thinks 'it doesn't matter if I don't do it,' then those piles of trash will never be picked up by anyone. But if each of us does a little, at least..." she pointed at the sorting results on the floor, "at least our household can produce a little less trash."

Her father's soda-drinking motion paused.

He looked at his daughter's earnest expression, then at the carefully washed juice boxes on the floor, and was silent for a long while.

Then he put down the soda can, walked into the yard, and picked up the discarded oil barrels, dirty rags, and torn gloves one by one, sorting them into categories.

"Waste oil can't just be dumped," her father said with his back to Qingqing, his voice muffled, "it pollutes the groundwater. Your dad's been fixing cars for twenty years—this much common sense he's got."

Looking at her father's clumsy yet earnest back, Qingqing's eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"Thanks, Dad." she said quietly.

"What's there to thank," her father waved his hand, pretending to be busy, "go do your homework! We'll settle this sorting bill tomorrow!"

The moonlight shone quietly into the room.

And on the other side of the city, Weixi sat alone by the window, watching the faintly flickering countdown number on the inside of her bracelet.

She hadn't told Qingqing.

This morning, the number was still fifty-three.

Now, it had already become fifty-two.
