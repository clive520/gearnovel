# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 2: The Countdown Halo on the Wrist

The next morning, inside the classroom of Class 6-1, the conversation still revolved around that "incredibly accurate transfer student" from yesterday.

"I'm telling you, she's definitely an alien!" A-Xin put one foot on his chair like a detective who had just uncovered a monumental secret, "That move she pulled yesterday, predicting the rain—that's straight-up alien technology! Her backpack is probably hiding a mini flying saucer!"

"A-Xin, since when do flying saucers get 'hidden'? And can a backpack even fit one?" Qingqing didn't look up, measuring yesterday's notes with a ruler as she retorted coolly.

"Then how else does she know things so accurately?" A-Xin refused to concede.

"The natural world is full of patterns," Qingqing pushed up her glasses, her tone calm as if she were lecturing, "For example, ants moving their nests, dragonflies flying low—many creatures react before weather changes. As long as your observation skills are sharp enough, predicting the weather isn't that special."

"Hmph! As if you could do it too!" A-Xin pouted.

Qingqing didn't answer, only silently raised her eyes toward Lin Weixi, who was sitting behind her, quietly flipping through her textbook.

Oddly enough, even though the sky was clear this morning, Lin Weixi had tucked a folding umbrella into her backpack.

Even stranger, her gaze kept drifting, almost involuntarily, toward the seawall behind the school.

"Ring-ring-ring—"

The noon bell rang, and the children surged out of the classroom like water bursting through a sluice gate.

Some went to the playground to play ball, some paired off to the school store, while Old Wu headed straight for the kitchen entrance, crouching there with wide, hopeful eyes waiting to see what was on the menu today.

Only Lin Weixi didn't go play, nor did she go to the school store.

Clutching her lunchbox, she walked quietly to the seat in the very corner of the classroom, sat down alone, and softly opened the lid.

The lunchbox held plain rice, a few simple home-cooked dishes, and one carefully washed orange.

Lin Weixi looked down at her lunch but made no move to pick up her chopsticks.

"...The orange my mom made," she gently touched the orange, her voice so low it was almost inaudible, "after this... I won't be able to eat them anymore."

On the windowsill, Liu-Liu's pointed ears twitched sharply, its two electronic eyes silently locking onto Lin Weixi in the corner.

"Beep beep. Target has entered a low-emotion state. Inference: intense longing for family." Liu-Liu reported quietly to Qingqing, who was resting at her seat, "Class President, should I keep monitoring?"

"No need." Qingqing put down her pen, thought for a moment, then added, "...She's a new student. Maybe she just hasn't adjusted to the environment yet. Let's not disturb her."

Liu-Liu tilted its head, a "?" symbol flashing through its electronic eyes, but obediently withdrew its surveillance beam.

The campus at lunchtime was unusually quiet.

The sun hung high in the sky, and a gentle breeze rustled the treetops behind the classroom.

Just then—

"Beep beep! Beep beep beep! Anomaly! Anomaly!"

Liu-Liu suddenly sprang up from the windowsill, its furry tail exploding into a puffball, electronic eyes frantically flashing with harsh red light, pointing straight at the corner of the classroom!

"Class President! The device on the target's wrist... its energy level has suddenly surged! A thousand times the previous reading! No, ten thousand times!"

Qingqing's head snapped up!

In the corner of the classroom, Lin Weixi had curled up in her seat, both hands pressing hard against the silver-white bracelet on her left wrist, her shoulders trembling slightly, as if enduring some enormous pressure.

And that bracelet was flashing with a blinding blue-white light!

The next second—

"Woom—!"

A beam of light shot out from Lin Weixi's bracelet, unfolding in midair into a huge, translucent image!

In the image was a city submerged in water.

Skyscrapers had only their upper halves poking above the surface, black seawater lapping against shattered glass curtain walls; a grayish-yellow sky hung oppressively low, with sunlight unable to break through; the streets were covered in floating trash, plastic bags bobbing up and down in the murky water like jellyfish...

"What... is this..." Qingqing's pupils suddenly dilated, and she was about to rush over—

"Pop!"

The image burst apart like a soap bubble, vanishing without a trace.

As if all her strength had been drained, Lin Weixi slumped limply over her desk, gasping for breath. Cold sweat dotted her forehead, and her fingers clenched desperately around the bracelet, her knuckles turning white.

"I... I'm fine..." Lin Weixi gasped, forcing a strained smile at the stunned Qingqing, "Just now... it was only... a broken projector light..."

"Projector light?" Qingqing frowned, "There's no projector light in this classroom."

Lin Weixi's body stiffened slightly, then she lowered her head and said in a barely audible voice: "Then... I must have seen wrong. Sorry, I interrupted your lunch break."

She quickly snapped the lunchbox shut, carefully tucked the orange back into her backpack, and fled out of the classroom as if running away.

Qingqing stood in place, watching Lin Weixi disappear around the corner of the hallway, yet the image that had flashed by moments ago refused to leave her mind.

A flooded city.

A grayish-yellow sky.

Streets covered in floating trash.

What exactly... was that?

"Beep beep, Class President," Liu-Liu sidled over, its voice lowered to the extreme, yet carrying an unprecedented weight, "That image just now—I ran a comparison against the database... I can't find a single real city in the world that could be flooded like that."

"You mean..."

"That image might be from the future." Liu-Liu's electronic eyes flickered red, "And... that bracelet's energy dropped another notch in that very instant."

Qingqing's heart sank heavily.

The afternoon classes, Qingqing was almost completely distracted.

She copied notes while stealing glances at Lin Weixi sitting behind her.

Lin Weixi looked like she had returned to calm, an ordinary student through and through. But Qingqing noticed that she hadn't looked out the window for the entire period, nor had she touched the folding umbrella in her backpack.

As if... she was afraid of something.

"Hey, do you guys smell that?" During the break after third period, Old Wu sidled over, sniffing, "Is something behind the school stinking?"

"Yeah! I smell it too!" Lin Yunyun nodded along, "It's coming from that ditch behind the school, a weird smell, hard to describe... like medicine, and also like rotten eggs."

"The ditch?" A-Xin's eyes lit up, "This qualifies as one of the school's Top Ten Mysteries! Let's go exploring after school!"

"I... I'll pass." Lin Yunyun said quietly.

While the children chattered noisily about the strange smell from the drainage ditch behind the school, Qingqing, sitting by the window, suddenly caught a strange sight—

Lin Weixi had heard their words too.

And the moment she heard the words "drainage ditch behind the school," her face suddenly turned deathly pale.

As if... she had heard news she shouldn't have.

"...It's already begun." Lin Weixi murmured in a barely audible voice, "Just like the time I remember... exactly the same."

Qingqing's heart skipped a beat.

In the evening, after school.

Qingqing didn't go home. Instead, she circled around to the hallway behind the classroom and looked from a distance at that infamous "stinking" drainage ditch.

The water in the ditch looked murky, its color slightly off, faintly giving off an unpleasant odor.

Liu-Liu crouched on Qingqing's shoulder, the micro-sensor on its nose emitting a faint "beep beep" as the fur on its tail bristled one by one.

"Class President," Liu-Liu's voice was unusually grave, "the water quality data of this ditch... is abnormal."

"Which part is abnormal?" Qingqing asked.

"Every part is abnormal." A string of unreadable numbers danced through Liu-Liu's electronic eyes, "Color, turbidity, pH value, heavy metal concentration... none of them are within normal range. This kind of water—let alone drinking it, you can't even touch it."

Qingqing crouched down and looked at that unremarkable ditch, suddenly remembering the deathly pale face of Lin Weixi from earlier.

A gentle, quiet transfer student who predicted things with uncanny accuracy.

A bracelet that could project images of the future.

A ditch with abnormal water quality, its upstream connected to who-knows-where.

"These things..." Qingqing said softly, "seem to be all connected."

The sun was setting, and golden afterglow spilled across the campus.

Qingqing stood up and looked toward the sea behind the school, where the five huge red-and-white smokestacks of the Taichung Thermal Power Plant could be faintly seen standing against the horizon.

Meanwhile, in the classroom at the other end of the teaching building, Lin Weixi stood alone by the empty classroom window, gazing at the same setting sun.

She gently turned the silver-white bracelet on her wrist, watching the nearly invisible countdown numbers on its inner side flicker faintly in the afterglow.

"...Fifty-seven more days." she murmured softly, "The timing... is just right."
