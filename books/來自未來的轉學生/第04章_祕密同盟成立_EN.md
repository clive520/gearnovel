# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 4: The Secret Alliance Is Formed

The setting sun stretched the two shadows in the park longer and longer.

Standing in front of Lin Weixi, Qingqing's heart raced, but she forced her voice to stay calm: "I can promise you, whatever you're about to tell me, I won't laugh, and I won't be scared. So... will you tell me?"

Lin Weixi didn't answer right away.

She lowered her head and stayed silent for a very, very long time, so long that Qingqing almost thought she was going to refuse.

Then, she slowly looked up, and a look of resolve surfaced in her eyes.

"Class President," she said, "do you want to hear... a very absurd story?"

"Go ahead."

Lin Weixi reached out and gently pressed the silver-white temporal bracelet on her left wrist.

"Woom—!"

The bracelet emitted a faint vibration, and a translucent blue light rose from its surface, unfolding into a palm-sized glow in the space between the two of them.

"What... is this?" Qingqing's eyes widened.

"What I'm about to show you," Lin Weixi's voice was soft, "is my world."

In the glow, the first image that emerged was a city.

A city submerged in seawater until only its rooftops remained. Skyscrapers were like drowning people, only half their bodies showing, black seawater lapping against shattered glass curtain walls, streets covered in floating trash.

"This city is called Taichung," Lin Weixi said, "In the era I come from, the sea level is much, much higher than it is now. The coastal parts of Taichung—Longjing, Wuqi, the Taichung Harbor... are mostly under the water."

Qingqing's throat suddenly went too dry to speak.

She was from Taichung, her family home in Longjing. Her grandmother's house was close to the sea, and from the window you could see the five big smokestacks of the Taichung Thermal Power Plant.

The glow shifted.

A land under a grayish-yellow sky. No phoenix trees, no grass—the cracked soil was split into pieces, like skin that had been parched for a very long time.

"This place used to be..." Lin Weixi's voice trembled slightly, "a lot of people told me it used to be a field of lush green rice paddies. But it had already become like this before I was born."

"Why did it become like this?" Qingqing asked.

"There are too many reasons." Lin Weixi said softly, "The weather got hotter and hotter, the rain stopped following its seasons—no rain when it should rain, and storms when it shouldn't. On top of that, factories, pesticides, trash... the nutrients in the soil ran out, and slowly... it could no longer grow anything."

The glow shifted again.

The image showed a sea. But in that sea, there was no blue at all—it was densely packed with trash: plastic bags, PET bottles, fishing nets, straws, like a dirty blanket covering the entire ocean surface.

"This is... the sea?" Qingqing gasped.

"It is the sea." Weixi's tone was calm, so calm it was heartbreaking, "Plastic doesn't disappear; it just becomes smaller and smaller fragments. Fish swallow the fragments, and then we eat the fish. The adults say, 'In the end, we always eat back the garbage we threw away.'"

Finally, the glow revealed a simple, bleak scene.

A long line of people, each holding an empty bucket, their faces numb as they waited.

"They're... lining up for water." Weixi said, "Where I live, clean drinking water is rationed. Each household can only collect a little bit every day. Laundry water comes from rainwater, and showers have to be timed."

Qingqing's heart was squeezed tightly by an invisible hand.

She suddenly remembered the noon news today—the port razed to rubble in seven minutes by a glacial mudslide, the flood that dumped a whole year's rainfall in a single day, the "Super El Niño" that had the whole world on edge...

"The news you saw," Weixi said, as if reading her thoughts, "happens several times a month where I live. And that's not all—summers often exceed forty degrees, and winters bring hail instead; some places get three months of straight rain, while other places go three years without a single drop."

"So that's... your 'normal'?" Qingqing's voice was hoarse.

"Mhm." Weixi lowered her head and gently stroked her bracelet, "The adults say it all started in this era. It started while people were still hesitating, still arguing, still saying 'no rush, let's think about it later.'"

"So that's why you came back here?" Qingqing finally understood, "You came back to—"

"To let everyone know," Weixi looked up, her eyes glistening, "that every choice made now becomes our future. As long as we start acting now, it's not too late. But if we keep pretending not to see..."

She didn't finish the sentence. But Qingqing understood.

"But..." Qingqing thought for a long time, then asked a question she had held in her heart, "Why you? Why send a child back?"

"Because..." Weixi smiled faintly, a little bitterly, "because I remember this era. When I was little, my grandmother often told me about the Earth of long ago—with green mountains, clean seas, winters that snowed, and grass you could walk on barefoot. I never saw any of those things, but I wanted to see with my own eyes the proof that they once existed."

"So you transferred to this school," Qingqing's voice grew quiet, "not just for... the 'mission'?"

Weixi lowered her head and didn't answer. But her eyes said it all.

She genuinely wanted to see this world that was still green.

A gust of wind blew through, and the phoenix tree in the park rustled.

"Actually..." Weixi's voice suddenly caught in her throat, "ever since I came back here, I've often thought: if only I could have been born earlier. Born earlier, met this still-living tree earlier, met... this world that still rains normal rain earlier."

"But," she looked up and forced a smile, "I'm also glad I got to see it at least."

Qingqing was silent for a long time.

Then, she took a deep breath and stepped forward.

"Lin Weixi," Qingqing's voice was more determined than ever before, "I'm not a scientist, and I can't change the whole world."

"But I am the Class President of Class 6-1 at Luyang Elementary."

"Our Class 6-1 is the school's most famous, most troublemaking 'Ace Rascal Class.' We may not be good at much else, but when it comes to 'thinking of ways,' our class is the best."

Qingqing reached out her hand toward Weixi:

"If you're alone, maybe you can't make the adults listen."

"But if all twenty-six of us in the class work together... no, if we let the whole school, let every child know that this world is getting sick—"

"Maybe then, the adults would have no choice but to listen?"

Weixi stared blankly at the hand Qingqing extended, and her eyes suddenly turned red.

"You're willing to... help me?" she asked.

"It's not 'helping you.'" Qingqing smiled, "It's us working together. Come on, let's make a pact. This is our secret."

Just then, Qingqing's backpack suddenly zipped open a crack, and a fuzzy little head poked out:

"Beep beep! You two! How could you leave me out of something this big!"

Liu-Liu jumped out entirely, tail raised high, electronic eyes flashing with excited red light: "I'm the school's intelligence king! Surveillance, sending warnings, fetching evidence, standing watch... I'm good at all of it! You need a reliable little partner!"

"Liu-Liu... since when did you follow me here?" Qingqing held her forehead.

"Since the second you said 'stalking,' I've been on standby in your backpack!" Liu-Liu puffed out its chest with pride, "I'm a professional!"

Looking at this pair of live wires before her, the tension Lin Weixi had been holding suddenly eased.

She let out a "pfft" laugh.

It was the first time Qingqing had seen Lin Weixi laugh like that—not that gentle, courteous laugh, but a real, heartfelt laugh.

"Thank you both." Weixi reached out, gently clasping Qingqing's hand and patting Liu-Liu's head, "This is the first time since I came back here that I feel..."

"...not alone anymore."

The sun had set, and night was quietly creeping over the park.

The three members of the "Secret Alliance" high-fived under the phoenix tree, agreeing to start working on a plan together tomorrow.

"Oh, right," on the way home, Qingqing suddenly remembered something and turned back to ask, "the number that keeps running on your bracelet..."

Weixi's steps faltered.

She lowered her head, looking at the almost invisible countdown number on the inside of her bracelet, flickering faintly under the streetlight.

"...That's my 'time to return.'" her voice was very, very soft, "I have to go back to my own time before the energy runs out. Otherwise, the timeline will collapse, and this world... along with all of you, will disappear."

"Your time to return... how much longer?" Qingqing asked.

Weixi didn't answer directly.

She just looked up at the dim yellow streetlight in the distance and said softly:

"So we have to hurry, and make this world better."

Qingqing didn't press further.

But she silently memorized the number that had flashed on the bracelet just now.

—Fifty-three.
