# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 16: The Pollution Alarm

The plan to "make the whole school see" officially launched on the third night.

In the equipment room, the three children, plus Liu-Liu, gathered around a table, working frantically.

"Photos! Printing!" A-Xin pasted the photos Liu-Liu had taken of the pipe, one by one, onto a big poster, "Look everyone! This is the pipe!"

"Test data!" Qingqing neatly copied the test results onto the poster, "pH: 4.5. Heavy metals: six times over the limit!"

"Water sample!" Weixi firmly taped that bottle of grayish-green water to the center of the poster with adhesive tape, "Let everyone see with their own eyes what color this bottle of 'water' is!"

"Beep beep!" Liu-Liu held a pen in its mouth, writing a crooked title in the poster's corner—

"[WARNING] The water we drink is being polluted!"

"That's ugly handwriting." A-Xin teased.

"Beep beep! You just don't understand art!" Liu-Liu shot back.

"Enough, enough!" Qingqing clapped her hands, "Check it over! Tomorrow morning, this poster goes on the school bulletin board!"

"And," Weixi added, "we need to make many copies. Post it on the bulletin board, post it on the playground, post it at the classroom door—make the whole school see!"

"Okay!" The three children, plus Liu-Liu, nodded hard.

That night, the smell of poster ink drifted through the equipment room all night long.

The next day, before dawn, the three children snuck into school.

"Hurry! Before everyone arrives!" A-Xin stood on a chair, sticking the first poster in the most prominent spot on the bulletin board.

"This one goes on the playground!" Qingqing stuck the second poster beside the playground stage.

"This one... goes at the school gate!" Weixi said softly.

"The school gate?" A-Xin froze, "Wouldn't even adults passing by see it?"

"That's exactly the point." Weixi said, "Let the adults know that the children discovered what they didn't."

One by one, the posters were put up.

The three retreated to the other end of the hallway, looking at their "work" from a distance.

"It looks like..." A-Xin swallowed nervously, "something big is really going to happen."

"Mhm." Weixi said softly, "Something big will happen. But... it's the right kind of big."

However, fate didn't give them the chance for "something big."

At 7:30 that morning, when the first student stepped through the school gate—

The poster on the bulletin board was already gone.

"Where's the poster?!" A-Xin rushed to the bulletin board, searching frantically, "I stuck it right here!"

"The one by the stage is gone too!" Qingqing ran back, face pale, "The one at the school gate... was also torn down!"

"How can this be..." A-Xin murmured, "Who tore them down?"

"..." Weixi crouched before the bulletin board, picking up a torn corner of the poster, silent.

"Weixi?" Qingqing noticed her change, "What's wrong?"

"This poster," Weixi said softly, "wasn't torn down by a 'student.'"

"What do you mean?"

"Look here." Weixi pointed at the edge of the fragment, "The tear is very clean. And..." she lowered her head, "it was torn down very 'professionally.' As if... they've done it many times."

"'They'?" A-Xin's heart sank, "Weixi, do you mean..."

"Someone is stopping us." Weixi's voice was light as a feather, "And it's not someone from the school."

On the playground, the morning wind blew past.

The three children stood before the empty bulletin board, unable to say a word.

"How can this be..." A-Xin crouched on the ground, looking at that poster reduced to a torn corner, his voice trembling, "The posters we stayed up three nights to make... how could they..."

"A-Xin," Qingqing crouched down and gripped his shoulder, "let's calm down first."

"I can't calm down!" A-Xin's eyes were red, "We did it for the school! For everyone! What right do they have—"

"Because," Weixi spoke, her voice soft yet carrying a strength, "we discovered something they don't want anyone else to discover."

"Weixi..." Qingqing looked at her.

"The poster was torn down," Weixi said, "which means they're panicking. The more they panic, the more it means... we did the right thing."

"Did the right thing..." A-Xin froze.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "A-Xin, you have to remember: they can tear down posters and bury pipes, but..." she raised the bottle of water sample that had been kept on her person all along, "the evidence is with us."

"The water sample..." A-Xin's eyes slowly lit up again.

"Yes!" Qingqing stood up, "The poster's gone, fine! We have something more important!"

"Then what do we do now?" A-Xin asked.

"We..." Weixi looked toward the principal's office, "hand the evidence to the adults."

"Hand it to who?" A-Xin asked.

"To someone," Weixi said softly, "that they can't snatch away."

That morning, two big things happened at school one after another.

First: the school announced—"Due to abnormal water quality test results, the school's drinking fountains are suspended from use, effective immediately. All teachers and students, please bring your own drinking water."

"The fountains are stopped?" The children buzzed, "Then what do we drink?"

"The school says they'll provide bottled water!" Old Wu carried a box of mineral water, panting as he ran into the classroom, "One bottle each! Limited supply!"

"Bottled water..." Lin Yunyun lowered her head, looking at the mineral water bottle in her hand, whispering, "has something really happened to our school's water?"

Second: the health room's statistics were released.

"This month," Qingqing's mother told her over the phone, her tone grave, "more than fifty children have come to the health room with stomach trouble."

"Fifty?!" Qingqing gasped.

"The school has already notified the education bureau." Qingqing's mother said, "I heard the environmental protection bureau people are coming to the school today."

"Environmental protection bureau..." Qingqing murmured in repetition.

That morning, in the classroom of Class 6-1, a "bottled water campaign" erupted.

"One per person! Limited! No grabbing!" Old Wu hugged a box the size of an exercise book, distributing it while panting, "The school says until the fountains are fixed, everyone drinks this!"

"Mineral water..." Wang Zhuankai took the bottle, looking at it left and right, "This is the first time I've felt mineral water is so precious."

"I always thought the water from the tap would never stop." Lin Yunyun said quietly, "Now... I suddenly feel that being able to open a tap and drink water is a very happy thing."

"..." The children fell silent.

"Hey, you guys," a boy whispered, "has something really happened to the school's water?"

"It should... just be temporary, right?" Another girl said, "Didn't the school say it's just an 'abnormal water quality test'?"

"But..." Old Wu lowered his voice, "I heard from the kitchen auntie that these past few days, the drainage ditch behind the school is so stinky the kitchen windows can't even be opened."

"That drainage ditch..." Wang Zhuankai's face changed slightly, "does it really have something to do with the school's water?"

No one in the classroom answered.

And at noon, Qingqing's phone wouldn't stop ringing.

"Qingqing," her mother's voice carried urgency, "so many parents in your class group are asking! What's going on with the school's water? You're not feeling unwell, are you?"

"I'm not, Mom." Qingqing said.

"That's good..." Her mother breathed a sigh of relief, "By the way, has anyone at your school drunk bad water?"

"..." Qingqing paused, "Mom, do you know the 'Hongyuan Food Processing Factory'?"

"Hongyuan?" Her mother thought for a moment, "Never heard of it. Why?"

"Nothing." Qingqing shook her head, "I was just... asking casually."

Hanging up, Qingqing looked out the window.

In the direction behind the school, a nameless smell could be faintly detected.

"Tomorrow," she said in a low voice, "the environmental protection bureau people are coming. We absolutely have to hand over the evidence."

"Xiaoqing," her mother's voice carried worry, "have you been feeling unwell lately?"

"I haven't." Qingqing said.

"That's good." Her mother breathed a sigh of relief, "If you ever feel unwell, you must tell Mom!"

"Mhm." Qingqing nodded.

But after hanging up, Qingqing's heart couldn't relax at all.

"The environmental protection bureau people are coming..." she said in a low voice, "but where's the evidence?"

"The evidence is buried." Liu-Liu's voice came from her backpack, unusually heavy, "I flew to the industrial zone again this morning... that pipe has been completely buried."

"Buried?!"

"Mhm." Liu-Liu nodded, "Even the traces of the pipe are gone. And at the river mouth... the discolored area of the seawater has expanded again. They buried the pipe, but the pollution is still going on."

"No evidence, but the pollution continues..." Qingqing's heart sank to the bottom.

"Class President," Liu-Liu asked in a low voice, "what do we... do?"

Qingqing fell silent.

"...We still have evidence." After a long moment, she said.

"What evidence?"

"We have photos. We have the water sample. We have the test data." Qingqing's voice grew more and more resolute, "We got the evidence in hand before they 'destroyed' it!"

"But..." Liu-Liu said in a low voice, "that evidence is only in the equipment room. If we take it out... will it be..."

"So," a light flickered in Qingqing's eyes, "we hand the evidence to someone 'they can't snatch away.'"

"Who?"

"Someone," Qingqing said in a low voice, "even the principal respects."

That afternoon, in the principal's office.

"You say you have evidence?" Principal Qiu looked at the three children before him, his expression complex.

"Yes!" A-Xin pulled out a thick stack of materials from his backpack, "Photos of the pipe! The water sample! The test data! It's all here!"

"Principal," Qingqing's voice trembled slightly, yet it was exceptionally resolute, "we know that we obtained this evidence secretly. We also know we shouldn't have investigated on our own."

"But," she looked up, tears glistening in her eyes, "if we hadn't investigated... who would have discovered that the water we drink is being polluted?"

The principal's office fell silent.

The three children stood before the principal, their hearts pounding.

"Principal," A-Xin couldn't help speaking, "we know what we did might be wrong. But..."

"But what?" Principal Qiu asked.

"But," A-Xin took a deep breath, "we're the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit.' We swore to protect the Earth, protect the school, protect the water we drink. So..."

"So you snuck into the industrial zone?" Principal Qiu's tone was unreadable.

"Yes!" A-Xin puffed out his chest, "Even if we get punished, we had to do it!"

"..." Principal Qiu looked at him for a very, very long time.

"A-Xin," he finally spoke, his tone carrying a complex emotion, "do you know how many children have told me in my thirty years as principal, 'even if I get punished, I had to do it'?"

"I don't know..." A-Xin said quietly.

"Not many." Principal Qiu said, "Most children say, 'don't tell the teacher.' Children who say 'even if I get punished, I had to do it'..." he paused, "in these thirty years, I've only heard it from the three of you."

"Principal..." The three children froze.

"Have a seat." Principal Qiu sighed, "Show me your evidence."

"Yes!" The three children hurriedly handed over the materials.

Principal Qiu picked up the stack of materials and flipped through them, page by page.

Photos of the pipe. The grayish-green water sample. The test result of pH 4.5. The data of heavy metals at six times the limit.

"...This bottle of water," Principal Qiu put down the materials, his voice hoarse, "was really taken from the drainage ditch behind our school?"

"Yes." Weixi nodded, "And that pipe connects to the 'Hongyuan Food Processing Factory' in the industrial zone."

"Hongyuan Food Processing Factory..." Principal Qiu murmured in repetition.

"Principal," Dean Zhou suddenly spoke, his voice low, "let me handle this."

"Old Zhou..." Principal Qiu looked at him.

"The children's evidence is real." Dean Zhou said, "I walked the length of the drainage ditch and saw that pipe with my own eyes. Now, they've buried it. But..." he picked up that bottle of grayish-green water sample, "this bottle of water can't be buried."

"What do you plan to do?" Principal Qiu asked.

"The environmental protection bureau people are coming tomorrow." Dean Zhou said, "I'll hand the evidence to them personally. And..." a sharp light flickered in his eyes, "I'll take this bottle of water with me."

"Dean..." The three children stared at him blankly.

"You did very well." Dean Zhou looked at them, a gentleness never seen before in his tone, "Leave the rest to the adults."

"But, Dean!" A-Xin was anxious, "What if the factory people..."

"They won't." Dean Zhou said, "They can tear down posters and bury pipes. But..." he picked up the water sample and gave it a slight shake under the light, "they can't make this bottle of water disappear."

The next day, the environmental protection bureau's inspectors arrived at Luyang Elementary on schedule.

"This is the water sample from the drainage ditch behind our school." Dean Zhou handed that bottle of grayish-green water to the inspector, "pH 4.5. Heavy metals at six times the limit. The test data is all here."

"And these photos." A-Xin added, "They're photos of the pipe! Even though the pipe has been buried, the photos are still here!"

The inspectors examined every photo and every piece of data carefully.

"We waited a whole month," A-Xin clenched his fists nervously, "and finally today has come..."

"Mhm." Qingqing nodded, her voice trembling too, "Photos, water sample, data... all handed over."

"Now," Weixi said in a low voice, "all we can do is wait."

"Wait..." A-Xin murmured in repetition, "waiting is really agonizing."

"But," Weixi smiled faintly, "at least we've done everything we should."

"You children..." the inspector looked at the three children before him, admiration in his tone, "are truly remarkable. Most kids, when they encounter something like this, would probably just hide at home and cry."

"We can't cry," A-Xin said, "because we're the 'Protect Earth Operations Unit'!"

"Hahaha!" The inspector laughed, "Fine! 'Protect Earth Operations Unit'! I'll remember you!"

"...These materials, we'll take back and send to the lab for re-testing." The inspector said, "If confirmed, we'll immediately investigate and deal with it."

"Deal with it?" A-Xin asked nervously, "How?"

"We'll seal off the industrial zone and thoroughly investigate the source of every pipe." The inspector said, "If it really is 'Hongyuan Food Processing Factory'... they'll face suspension, fines, and criminal liability."

"Suspension!" A-Xin's eyes lit up instantly.

"But," the inspector's tone shifted, "re-testing takes time. Until the results come out, the factory won't receive any punishment."

"It still takes time..." Qingqing murmured in repetition, "how long?"

"At the fastest, one week."

"One week..." Weixi lowered her head, looking at the bracelet on her wrist.

The countdown number flickered quietly in the daylight.

—Nineteen.

The days that followed were the hardest week Class 6-1 had ever endured.

Every morning at dawn, Liu-Liu flew to the river mouth to record the color of the seawater.

"Day one: the discolored area of the seawater hasn't expanded. But..."

"Day two: at the factory, the smokestacks are running normally. No movement at the pipe's location."

"Day three: a new group of dead fish appeared at the river mouth."

"Day four: the seawater color is deepening."

"Day five:..." Liu-Liu's voice grew heavier and heavier, "today, I saw an environmental protection bureau inspection vehicle at the factory gate."

"An inspection vehicle?!" A-Xin's eyes lit up, "Is the re-test result out?!"

"I don't know." Liu-Liu shook its head, "I only saw the inspectors get out of the car, and then... the factory gate closed."

"Closed?" Qingqing froze.

"Mhm." Liu-Liu nodded, "And starting that day, the factory's smokestacks... haven't puffed any more smoke."

"Haven't puffed any more smoke..." Weixi repeated softly, and then, her eyes suddenly turned red.

"Weixi?" A-Xin and Qingqing looked at her at the same time.

"Haven't puffed any more smoke..." Weixi's voice trembled slightly, "in the textbooks of our era, it's written... 'Hongyuan Food Processing Factory was ordered to suspend operations for secretly discharging wastewater. From that day on, its smokestacks never puffed smoke again.'"

"..." A-Xin and Qingqing froze.

"The textbook says," Weixi's tears fell drop by drop, "'this was the miracle accomplished by the most remarkable group of children of that era.'"

"A miracle..." Qingqing murmured in repetition.

"Qingqing, A-Xin," Weixi looked up at them, her eyes blurred with tears, "we... did it."

"We," A-Xin's voice was trembling too, "rewrote the textbook?"

"Mhm." Weixi nodded hard, "We rewrote the textbook."

That day, the three children cried together in the equipment room.

"One week," Weixi said softly, "is enough."

"Enough?" A-Xin froze, "Weixi, you..."

"A-Xin," Weixi smiled faintly, "one week is enough for the adults to find out the truth. And one week..." she lowered her head, her voice soft, "is also enough for me to say goodbye to everyone properly."

"Weixi..." A-Xin and Qingqing froze at the same time.

"I've calculated," Weixi said, "my bracelet now has nineteen days left. But... it's moving faster and faster. At this rate..."

"At this rate, what will happen?" A-Xin asked nervously.

"At this rate," Weixi said softly, "in about another week, I'll have to go back."

"Another week?!" A-Xin almost shouted, "But didn't you say there were nineteen days left?!"

"That's at 'current' speed." Weixi said, "Every time I 'see the future,' the bracelet's energy drains faster. These past few days..."

She lowered her head and looked at the bracelet.

"It's already down to nineteen days."

"Weixi," Qingqing's voice trembled slightly, "tell me honestly... how much longer can you hold on?"

Weixi fell silent.

"...I don't know." She finally said, "But I'll try to hold on until that day."

"Until which day?"

"Until..." Weixi looked up, gazing at the sea outside the window, "until the day that drainage ditch becomes clear again. Until..." she smiled softly, "until the day everyone learns to protect the Earth."

Actually, these past few days, Weixi's "flashbacks" had become so severe that even Qingqing could barely hide it.

During class, she would suddenly stare at the blackboard without moving.

At nap time, she would suddenly stand up, walk to the window, and say "I'm sorry" to the view outside.

"Weixi," one day after school, Qingqing finally couldn't help asking, "have you been... seeing more of the 'future' lately?"

Weixi was silent for a moment, then nodded: "Mhm. And... it's getting clearer and clearer."

"Clearer?"

"In the past, the future I saw was blurry." Weixi said, "But now... I see Grandma, the ration station, the grayish-yellow sky... all as if they're really happening before my eyes."

"Then..." Qingqing asked nervously, "does it hurt?"

"It doesn't hurt." Weixi shook her head, "But... it gets hard to tell them apart."

"Hard to tell apart?"

"Hard to tell apart..." Weixi lowered her head, her voice soft, "which is the future, and which is the present."

"Weixi..." Qingqing took her hand, "listen to me. No matter what happens—you're here right now. At Luyang Elementary. By our side."

"Mhm." Weixi nodded softly, "I know. But..." she looked at her bracelet, "I'm worried that one day, I won't be able to 'come back' to the present."

"You will!" Qingqing said loudly, "We'll wake you up! Just like last time!"

"..." Looking at Qingqing's resolute face, Weixi's eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"Mhm." She lowered her head, softly, "Thank you, Qingqing."

That night, Weixi sat alone on the seawall, looking at that murky gray sea.

The number on the bracelet dropped, digit by digit, in the moonlight.

"Twenty, nineteen, eighteen..." Weixi counted softly, "seventeen..."

She gently pressed the bracelet against her chest.

"Grandma," she said softly to the night sky, "give me a little more time. Let me see the rest..." she closed her eyes, "let me see the children save this sea."

The sea wind blew across her cheeks.

The number on the bracelet flickered quietly in the moonlight, stopping at—fifteen.

"Fifteen days..." Weixi smiled softly, "just right."
