# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 7: Saving Electricity & Adopting Trees

On the afternoon the "Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign" came to a triumphant close, Qingqing and Weixi sat side by side in the shade of the tree behind the classroom, letting the cool November sea breeze ruffle their hair.

"We did it." Qingqing looked at the statistics sheet in her hand, unable to keep the corners of her mouth from lifting, "In this one month, the food our whole class saved added up to over forty-five kilograms."

"Mhm." Weixi nodded softly, but her gaze fell on the five smokestacks in the distance, "Next, we're going to deal with that."

"That?" Qingqing followed her gaze.

"Electricity." Weixi said.

"Electricity?" Qingqing blinked, "Is electricity trash too?"

"It's not trash," Weixi shook her head, her voice soft, "but do you know where most of the electricity we use right now comes from?"

"The power plant!" Qingqing said matter-of-factly, "Those big smokestacks behind the school! When my dad watches TV at night, he says it's electricity from the 'Taichung Thermal Power Plant.'"

"Mhm. The Taichung Thermal Power Plant—the largest coal-fired power plant in Taiwan." Weixi's voice was soft, yet it dropped into the calm water of the conversation like a small pebble, "Every time we turn on a light, run an air conditioner, or watch an hour of TV... those smokestacks have to burn a little more coal. The more coal burned, the more smoke released, and the grayer our sky becomes."

"You mean..." Qingqing's eyes went wide, "the electricity we use turns into smoke from the smokestacks?"

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "The adults call this the 'hidden cost of electricity.' We turn on lights and take it for granted, but every second that light is on, coal is burning behind it, smoke is drifting, carbon is being released into the sky. That carbon makes the Earth hotter and the weather stranger."

Qingqing fell silent.

She remembered this summer, when her home was so hot they ran the air conditioner all summer long; she remembered the news saying this year was one of the hottest summers on record; she remembered that terrifying "Super El Niño"...

"So what do we do?" Qingqing asked.

"It's simple." Weixi smiled faintly, "Like saving food—save electricity. Save one unit of electricity, and the smokestacks puff a little less smoke, and the sky gains a little more blue."

"The Save-Electricity Campaign!" Qingqing's eyes lit up, "Sounds even more exciting than saving food!"

The next morning during morning self-study, Qingqing stepped up to the podium again.

"Everyone! 'Protect Earth' Operation Three officially begins!" Qingqing picked up the board eraser and slammed it down hard on the podium, "This operation is called the 'Save-Electricity Campaign'!"

"Save electricity?" A-Xin raised his hand, "Doesn't the teacher always say to turn off the lights? We've known that forever!"

"Knowing is one thing, but do you all actually do it?" Qingqing pointed at the light switches at the back of the classroom, "Yesterday after school, who was the last one to leave the classroom? Who turned off the lights?"

The classroom fell silent for a second.

"It was me!" Old Wu raised his hand, "I was on duty yesterday, so I was the last to leave!"

"Good, Old Wu! But..." Qingqing's tone shifted, "I passed by the class next door yesterday, and their classroom lights were on the entire night! Nobody turned them off until this morning!"

"Wow..." The children gasped.

"There are so many classrooms in this school. If every one of them forgets to turn off the lights, how much coal and electricity gets burned away in a single night?" Qingqing slapped the podium, "So starting today, our class is going to be the school's 'Electricity-Saving Role Model'!"

"How do we do it?" The children asked.

"First, turn off the lights!" Qingqing said, "Second, when the air conditioning is on during nap time, set it to twenty-six degrees—no lower! Third, fans, computers, and projectors must always be turned off after use! Fourth..." she glanced at Weixi, "we're sending Liu-Liu out as the school's 'Electricity Scout'!"

On the windowsill, Liu-Liu snapped to attention, tail held high, electronic eyes flashing with excitement:

"Beep beep! Scout Liu-Liu reporting for duty! My mission is—patrol the whole school, find which class didn't turn off their lights or fans, write down their names, and post them on the bulletin board for the whole school to see!"

"Hahahaha! That's too brutal!" A-Xin laughed, "Liu-Liu, you're going to become the most hated 'light police officer' in the whole school!"

"Serves them right for wasting electricity!" Liu-Liu declared with righteous confidence, "Saving electricity means loving the Earth!"

The children laughed together.

And Weixi, sitting in the corner, suddenly spoke.

"Everyone," Weixi's voice was soft, yet it instantly silenced the boisterous classroom, "do you know what electricity is like... where I come from?"

"Your place?" A-Xin asked, "Isn't electricity just electricity?"

"Where I come from," Weixi lowered her head, a note of indescribable sadness in her voice, "electricity is rationed."

"Rationed?"

"Mhm." Weixi said softly, "Every family has a limited amount of electricity they can use each month. Go over it, and your power gets cut."

"Power cut?!" The children's eyes went wide.

"Yes. Where I come from, blackouts are an everyday thing." Weixi said, "On summer nights, whole cities often go dark. Everyone crowds out on the balconies to cool off, because the fans can't turn and the air conditioners can't run. In winter, many people can't bear to turn on the heater, because the 'electricity bill' is the biggest expense of the whole month."

"Then... isn't that really painful?" Lin Yunyun asked quietly.

"The most painful part isn't not having electricity," Weixi looked up, her eyes slightly reddened, "it's knowing that it's because of ourselves—human beings of this era used up too much electricity, burned too much coal, emitted too much carbon, and made the weather so strange that we couldn't even keep the electricity."

The classroom was dead silent.

"So," Weixi said softly, "while there's still electricity, still air conditioning, still heating—save a little more. Not to save money, but to let the Earth last a little longer."

"Alright!" A-Xin slapped the desk and stood up first, "Then let's get serious! Liu-Liu! Let's go patrol the class next door first! Let's see who didn't turn off their lights yesterday!"

"Beep beep! Moving out!"

For the next two weeks, a peculiar sight appeared on the hallways of Luyang Elementary.

A silver-white little pet, like a gust of wind, darted between every classroom. At each one, it ducked near the light switches, scanned with its electronic eyes for a few seconds, then zipped out with a "swish" and jotted a number down in the little notebook on its chest.

"Class 6-3! Fan not turned off after nap time! Minus one point!"

"Class 5-1! Classroom lights left on after school! Minus two points!"

"Class 4-2! Air conditioner set to twenty-two degrees! Too low, too low! Minus one point!"

Liu-Liu's "Electricity-Saving Leaderboard" updated day by day on the bulletin board.

"Wow, we're number one in the whole school!" A-Xin proudly looked at the bulletin board, "In the Save-Electricity Campaign, our Class 6-1 is the champion!"

"Of course!" Liu-Liu proudly raised its head, "I run a tight ship!"

And A-Xin also began his "crazy idea publicity" campaign.

"Everyone! Look here, look here!" Before school let out, A-Xin mysteriously gathered the whole class at the back of the classroom and pulled a thick stack of poster paper out of his backpack, "I stayed up late designing 'Save-Electricity Campaign' posters! Come on, put them up!"

The children busily plastered the posters all over the classroom. On them were written all sorts of "inspiring" slogans:

"Turn off the lights; make the sky bluer!"
"One unit of electricity = a bit of coal = a cloud of smog!"
"Set the AC to twenty-six degrees—eco-friendly and comfortable!"
"Saving electricity isn't stinginess; it's loving the Earth!"

"Wow, A-Xin, these posters of yours..." Qingqing couldn't help laughing at one of them, "'One unit of electricity = a cloud of smog'? Isn't that a bit much?"

"What's too much about it!" A-Xin argued with conviction, "Doesn't the TV say generating power emits carbon, and carbon makes the Earth run a fever? So me saying one unit of electricity equals a cloud of smog—totally reasonable!"

"I guess... that's kind of right?" Qingqing laughed helplessly.

"And I've thought up an activity too!" A-Xin's eyes sparkled, "Our class is going to elect an 'Electricity-Saving Little Angel'! Every day after school, the Little Angel of the day is in charge of checking whether the classroom lights and fans are off, and signs their name in the duty log!"

"Great! Then I'll go first today!" Old Wu was the first to raise his hand, "I'm the best at turning off lights in the whole class!"

"You're just the best at eating in the whole class!" A-Xin teased.

"The two aren't mutually exclusive!" Old Wu declared with full confidence.

The children burst into laughter again.

And while everyone was busy saving electricity, Weixi did something even Qingqing hadn't expected.

That day after school, Weixi didn't go straight home. She walked alone to the open lot behind the school, near the seawall, crouched down, and looked at that bare patch of land overgrown with weeds.

Qingqing followed her.

"Weixi, what are you doing here?"

"I'm thinking..." Weixi gently stroked the ground, "this place could grow trees."

"Grow trees?" Qingqing crouched beside her, "This empty lot behind the school?"

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "Trees absorb carbon dioxide, block the wind, hold water in the soil, and make summer cooler. One tree is like a little 'Earth air conditioner.' And..." her voice dropped slightly, "trees are something that stays."

"Stays?" Qingqing was puzzled.

"Where I come from, I've never seen a living tree with my own eyes." Weixi lowered her head, "I heard from my grandmother that trees used to bloom, bear fruit, and grow huge canopies where people could sit in the shade. But by the time I was born, many places had already..." she trailed off, gently touching the withered weeds on the ground.

"Then let's plant them!" Qingqing stood up, her tone resolute, "Plant lots and lots of trees, and let them grow strong! Even... even if you go back, these trees will stay here for you, growing bigger and bigger!"

Weixi looked up, staring blankly at Qingqing.

Her eyes suddenly felt a little hot.

"Mhm." She nodded hard, "Then let's adopt these trees!"

And so, the fourth battle of "Protect Earth"—the "Tree Adoption Campaign"—began.

The children applied to the principal for the right to use that empty lot behind the school, then split into four groups, each adopting a small sapling.

"Ours is called 'Little Blue'!" A-Xin proudly named his sapling, "Because its leaves are green! Mhm... and it goes well with the blue sky!"

"Little Blue? A green tree named Little Blue?" Wang Zhuankai laughed until his stomach hurt, "That name of yours is way too creative!"

"Better than yours!" A-Xin shot back, "What's your tree called?"

"Mine's called 'Eat-Your-Fill'!" Wang Zhuankai declared matter-of-factly, "Because I hope it eats its fill of sunshine and rainwater and grows up fast!"

"These names of yours..." Qingqing held her forehead, "Ours is called 'Moon,' because I hope it grows tall and round!"

"Then mine's called 'Iron Egg'!" Old Wu patted his sapling, "Plump, dark, and sturdy as my pork buns!"

The whole class burst into laughter.

Weixi crouched beside these saplings without naming any of them. She simply, carefully, buried the roots of every sapling deep and firm.

"A tree's roots need to be buried deep," she said softly, "so no matter how strong the wind blows, it won't fall."

The children gathered around, watching quietly.

In that moment, everyone suddenly felt that this gentle transfer student wasn't just planting trees.

She was planting some kind of... hope.

After school every day, the children had a new task: taking care of the saplings.

"Little Blue, have you grown taller?" A-Xin crouched before his sapling, leaning in for a close look, "Mhm... looks like no growth? Are you slacking off?"

"Trees don't grow three centimeters a day!" Wang Zhuankai teased from the side, "Why don't you ask your 'Eat-Your-Fill'?"

"It's eaten its fill!" Wang Zhuankai pointed proudly at his own tree, "See, it grew a new leaf yesterday!"

"Weixi," Lin Yunyun crouched beside her and asked, "how long does a tree take to grow big?"

"Some trees take decades, even a hundred years." Weixi gently touched a sapling's leaf, "We plant it now, and we may have to wait a very long time before we see it become a big tree. But that's okay—as long as we take good care of it, it will grow big one day."

"Then if we graduate, who will take care of them?" Old Wu asked.

"The younger students!" A-Xin patted his chest, "We can hand them over! Tell them these are the 'hopes planted by Class 6-1,' and they must take good care of them!"

"Right! That's the plan!" The children discussed it all at once, as if they could already see these saplings growing into towering trees decades later.

"Trees actually need a lot of water." Weixi suddenly said, "Just like we need food. But do you know? Where I come from, water is a very, very precious thing."

"Water precious?" A-Xin blinked, "Isn't water just what comes out of the tap?"

"It is now." Weixi lowered her head, "But if the weather gets hotter and the rain gets more chaotic, some places will stop getting rain altogether. When drought hits, the reservoirs run dry, and the taps stop flowing. Where I come from, many people can only use a tiny bit of water a day—washing faces, brushing teeth, taking showers—all carefully budgeted."

"Then... wouldn't the trees die of thirst?" Lin Yunyun looked worriedly at the sapling before her.

"Right." Weixi said softly, "So we must cherish every drop of water. With enough water, trees survive; with trees, we have clean air. Water, trees, air... they're all connected."

"Just like we're connected to the Earth." Qingqing added.

The children nodded, lost in thought.

On the third day of the Tree Adoption Campaign, an unexpected figure appeared at that empty lot.

"What are you all doing here?" Dean Zhou Yan stood with his hands behind his back, his hawk-like eyes sweeping over the few newly planted saplings.

The children tensed up instantly, looking at each other.

"Report, Dean," Qingqing took a deep breath and stepped forward, "we're planting trees! This is..."

"Planting trees?" Dean Zhou frowned, "Weren't you doing some 'Save-Electricity Campaign'? Now you're planting trees?"

"Saving electricity is saving electricity, planting trees is planting trees!" A-Xin cut in, "Saving electricity makes the sky a little bluer; planting trees makes the Earth a little greener! We need both!"

"Hmph, that sounds all very impressive." Dean Zhou's tone was cold, but his eyes lingered on the saplings for a long time.

"Dean," Weixi spoke softly, "once the trees grow up, they can block the sun in summer and block the wind when it's strong. When they're big, this whole row will become part of the scenery behind the school."

Dean Zhou was silent for a moment.

He didn't scold them. He didn't write any demerits.

He just tossed out a single sentence, flatly: "...Trees need watering. Don't just plant them and abandon them."

Then he turned and walked away.

The children stood frozen, looking at each other, before erupting into a barely contained cheer!

"The Dean didn't scold us!"

"He told us to water them! Does that mean he... approves?"

"Oh my god, the Black-Faced Judge didn't write a single demerit!"

"He must have been moved by our 'Tree Planting Campaign'!" A-Xin declared triumphantly.

"Don't get cocky!" Qingqing clapped her hands, "The Dean said to water them! Come on, split into groups and water!"

"Okay—!"

The children bustled about with buckets, making trip after trip to water the little saplings.

Weixi crouched beside the sapling she was in charge of, gently pushing aside the soil at the roots to let the water seep in slowly.

"Slowly, don't pour too much," she instructed softly, "a tree's roots are afraid of too much water. Just like people—give them too much at once, and they can't breathe."

"Weixi, you know so much." Lin Yunyun crouched beside her, full of admiration.

"Because..." Weixi paused, then smiled softly, "because I've seen too many dead trees. They were all 'loved' to death by us humans, bit by bit."

"Loved to death?" Lin Yunyun's eyes went wide.

"Mhm." There was no blame in Weixi's tone, only gentleness, "Some trees were buried to death under too much trash; some were poisoned to death by too many pesticides; and some trees..." she lowered her head, "were thirsted to death by too much indifference."

Lin Yunyun fell silent.

She looked at that small, green sapling before her, and suddenly felt that it was more than just a tree.

It was a promise for the future.

The next day, a person who surprised and delighted the children appeared at that empty lot.

"Mhm... not badly planted at all." Principal Qiu Shouren held his thermos cup of tea, squinting as he walked two circles around the saplings, "Whose idea were these trees?"

"Report, Principal! Ours, Class 6-1!" A-Xin answered eagerly, "We're carrying out 'Protect Earth · Tree Adoption Campaign'!"

"'Protect Earth'?" Principal Qiu blinked, "That name sounds even more impressive than my 'Campus Beautification Contest'!"

"Principal! Want to plant some with us?" A-Xin pressed his advantage, "We can get the whole school planted with trees!"

"The whole school?" Principal Qiu thought for a moment, holding his tea, smiling until his eyes narrowed into slits, "Alright! I'll contribute too! I'll have the general affairs office buy thirty more saplings! Plant them along the row beside the playground!"

"Yay—!" The children burst into thunderous cheers.

"But," Principal Qiu suddenly lowered his voice and said mysteriously, "you have to remember—once you plant trees, you have to take good care of them. Like taking care of your own little friends. If I find out you planted them and abandoned them..."

"We'll definitely take good care of them!" The children promised in unison.

Principal Qiu nodded with satisfaction, held his tea, and strolled slowly away.

The children stood in place, looking at each other, then burst into a frenzy of joy.

"The Principal is going to plant trees for the whole school!"

"We're turning the whole school green!"

"This is what they call 'spreading from one to ten, ten to a hundred'!" A-Xin declared triumphantly, "Our 'Protect Earth' Campaign has officially taken the whole school!"

In the days that followed, sapling after sapling was planted by the playground, along the corridors, and in the flower beds. Students from other classes saw it and asked curiously: "What are you Class 6-1 kids doing?"

"Planting trees!" The Class 6-1 students always answered with their chests puffed out, "Planting trees means loving the Earth!"

"Then I want to plant too!"

"Our class wants to as well!"

Spreading from one to ten, ten to a hundred, the "tree adoption" trend quietly spread across Luyang Elementary.

A month later.

All the saplings of the "Tree Adoption Campaign" had survived. Some had grown a few centimeters; some had sprouted new shoots. A-Xin's "Little Blue" had even grown two new leaves, making him so proud that he went to "inspect" it every day after school.

And the results of the "Save-Electricity Campaign" shocked the whole school.

"The data is in!" Qingqing stood at the podium, waving the statistics sheet, her voice trembling slightly with excitement, "This month, our class used twenty-eight percent less electricity than last month!"

"Wow—!" The class erupted in cheers.

"And!" Qingqing continued, "Because our class led the way, the whole school's electricity bill this month is a full twenty percent less than the same month last year!"

"Twenty percent?!" A-Xin jumped up, "How much money is that?"

"Enough to buy tons and tons of pork buns!" Old Wu blurted out.

"The electricity money saved by the whole school is enough..." Qingqing smiled, "enough to add an extra fried chicken leg for every single student in the whole school!"

"Chicken legs—!" The children's cheers nearly lifted the roof.

"But we have to keep going!" Qingqing said loudly, "Saving electricity, saving water, planting trees, sorting trash, zero waste—we're going to keep the 'Protect Earth' Campaign going forever!"

"Okay—!"

The children cheered and laughed.

Weixi sat by the window, watching these children, the corners of her mouth lifting slightly.

Suddenly, Liu-Liu burst out of the ventilation duct, a hint of gravity in its electronic eyes. It leaned close to Qingqing's ear and lowered its voice:

"Beep beep... Class President, that drainage ditch behind the school... the smell has gotten stronger again. And," Liu-Liu paused, "I detected something off in the seawater too."

"Seawater?" Qingqing's heart sank, "The sea behind the school?"

"Mhm." Liu-Liu's tail bristled slightly, "That smell flows down the ditch all the way into the sea. The seawater... has gained a lot of things it shouldn't have."

Qingqing whipped her head around, looking in the direction of the sea behind the school.

The November sky should have been cool, yet it was strangely muggy and hot. The five smokestacks of the Taichung Thermal Power Plant stood silently in the sultry haze, puffing out white smoke.

Over the sea hung a layer of gray that was hard to name, hard to describe.

"Weixi," Qingqing lowered her voice, walking over to her, "did you... already know about this?"

Weixi didn't answer.

She just looked at that murky gray sea and said, softly:

"...It's almost here."

In the evening, the school dismissal bell rang.

The children filed out of the school gate in twos and threes. Weixi stood alone before that lot full of saplings, quietly gazing at them.

The setting sun stretched her shadow very, very long.

She gently touched the bracelet on her wrist.

On the inside of the bracelet, the countdown number flickered faintly in the sunset.

—Forty-three.

"Trees," Weixi said softly to the little saplings, "grow up well. One day, someone will sit in your shade, in my place."

The wind blew gently.

The saplings' leaves swayed slightly, as if nodding.
