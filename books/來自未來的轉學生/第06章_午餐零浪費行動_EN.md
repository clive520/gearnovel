# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 6: The Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign

The morning after the first battle of "Protect Earth" concluded, a strange tide of "treasures" poured into the classroom of Class 6-1.

"Class President! All my family's PET bottles are washed clean—eighteen of them!" A-Xin triumphantly dumped a big bag of crushed PET bottles onto the podium, "My dad saw me washing bottles last night and asked if I was planning to sell scrap metal!"

"Ours are sorted too!" Old Wu hauled in a bag of juice boxes, "My mom said from now on, I'm in charge of all our household trash!"

"My house..." Lin Yunyun blushed, carefully holding out a small cardboard box containing neatly folded old newspapers and cartons, "my house only has these..."

"Great, great!" Qingqing stood before the podium like a victorious general, "Everyone, put your household sorting results at the back of the classroom, and we'll deliver them to the recycling station together after school!"

"Yay—!" The children happily hauled their respective "spoils of war," and the back of the classroom was instantly piled into a small mountain of recyclables.

Liu-Liu darted among the recycling mountain, tail held high, "inspecting the goods" with great seriousness:

"Beep beep! PET bottles qualified! Juice boxes qualified! Paper... hmm, this stack of old newspapers smells like newspapers—qualified!"

"You can tell paper by smelling it?!" A-Xin exclaimed, "What kind of nose is that!"

"Professional!" Liu-Liu puffed out its chest with pride.

Just as the children were chattering and celebrating the "great success of Battle One," the lunch bell rang.

"Ring—ring—ring—!"

"Food time! Food time!" Old Wu was the first to dash out, his chubby figure whipping up a whirlwind down the hallway, "There's fried chicken legs today! I passed by the kitchen this morning and saw a whole crate of chicken legs!"

"Really?! Charge!" The children swarmed after him out of the classroom.

Lin Weixi followed the crowd outside, smelling the aroma of food wafting down the hallway, and suddenly stopped in her tracks.

"So fragrant." she said softly, in a voice full of an indescribable cherishing, "This smell... I've smelled it so many times in my dreams."

Qingqing turned back to glance at her, said nothing, and simply took her hand: "Come on, let's go eat!"

Lunchtime in Class 6-1 was always one of the most lively moments in the whole school.

Twenty-six children sat around the classroom, and on the tables sat the school lunch: fried chicken legs, stir-fried cabbage, braised kelp, seaweed and egg drop soup, plus one orange per person.

"Chicken leg! My chicken leg!" Old Wu grabbed the biggest one, nearly melting from happiness, "Dear heavens! Why are you so good to me!"

"Old Wu, calm down!" A-Xin laughed while guarding his own chicken leg, "You've already eaten two bowls of rice!"

"Two bowls is nowhere near enough!" Old Wu's mouth was stuffed full, "One more bowl!"

The children ate with gusto, and the classroom echoed with the clatter of bowls and chopsticks.

But if you looked closely, you'd find that not every child ate everything on their plate.

A boy in the back row picked the stir-fried cabbage out piece by piece, piling it at the edge of his bowl, frowning in disgust: "The vegetables are bitter. I don't like them."

The girl next to him had only eaten half her rice before dumping the rest along with the soup into the food waste bucket: "I'm full. I don't want the rest."

Another person peeled an orange, took a bite, found it a bit sour, and threw the whole thing into the food waste bucket: "It's sour. Not eating it."

Weixi sat by the window, quietly eating her lunch.

She ate very slowly, chewing each bite carefully, as if trying to memorize the taste of this meal in her heart.

But her eyes kept drifting, almost involuntarily, toward the food being dumped into the waste bucket.

"Weixi," Qingqing noticed her unease, leaned over, and asked quietly, "what's wrong?"

"Your school..." Weixi paused, then asked carefully, "does it throw away a lot of food every lunch?"

"Not really," Qingqing thought for a moment, "some people just can't finish, so they dump it. It's normal. Anyway, the food waste gets collected and fed to pigs."

"Fed to pigs..." Weixi repeated those words, lowered her head, and said nothing more.

That afternoon, Weixi did something strange.

During the lunch break, she crouched alone behind the kitchen, watching the kitchen aunties clean up the leftover food.

Bucket after bucket of leftover rice and dishes was carried out of the kitchen.

"Sigh," a kitchen auntie sighed, dumping a full bucket of food waste into the recycling bin, "so much again. Seeing this much food thrown away every day really hurts the heart."

"Auntie," Weixi walked over and asked softly, "how much food does the school... throw away in a day?"

"I don't know," the auntie shook her head, "but look, just our school alone throws away several buckets a day. I hear if you add up all the schools in the whole county..."

The auntie didn't finish. She just sighed again.

That evening, a message arrived on Qingqing's phone from Weixi.

There were no words in the message—just one photo.

It was a photo Weixi had sneaked back to the kitchen to take after school, using Liu-Liu's lens—a photo of that huge food waste bucket, filled to the brim with today's leftover lunch.

The rice and dishes in the bucket were piled so high they were almost overflowing.

The golden-fried chicken legs, the emerald cabbage, the glistening braised kelp... all mixed together, dumped into that bucket.

Qingqing stared at the photo and fell silent.

The next day, at lunchtime.

Weixi didn't start eating right away.

She stood at the front of the classroom, holding up the photo of that food waste bucket (projected from her bracelet), her voice soft yet silencing the entire classroom.

"Everyone," Weixi said, "do you know how much food we threw away at lunch yesterday in total?"

The children looked at each other and shook their heads.

"Yesterday at noon, our class of twenty-six people threw away..." Weixi glanced down at her bracelet, "seven point eight kilograms of food."

"Seven point eight kilograms?" A-Xin's mouth dropped open, "How much is that?"

"About enough... for thirty or forty people to eat a full meal." Weixi said.

The classroom fell instantly silent.

"And that's just our one class, in one day." Weixi's voice remained soft, "Add in the whole school, the whole county, all of Taiwan, the whole world... the food wasted in a single day alone could feed who-knows-how-many people."

"But..." a child said quietly, "we really can't finish it all..."

"I know." Weixi nodded, with not a trace of blame in her voice, "I used to think that way too. But later I learned that in this world, there are people who can't even get one meal."

"Who can't get a meal?" Old Wu asked, puzzled, "Doesn't everyone have money to buy food now?"

Weixi was silent for a moment, then said: "Have you all been watching the news lately?"

"What news?" A-Xin asked.

"A very frightening phenomenon is happening in the world right now, called the 'Super El Niño.'" Weixi said, "It makes the weather very strange—some places keep raining until they flood, other places stay dry and grow nothing. Crops can't grow, so food becomes scarce, and when food becomes scarce, some people can't get meals."

"In fact, even without the Super El Niño, there are already more than six hundred million people going hungry in this world right now." Weixi's voice was soft, "The uncles and aunties at the United Nations announced a number of six hundred forty-five million—roughly one in every fourteen people on Earth can't get enough to eat."

"Six hundred million?!" The whole class gasped.

"But once the Super El Niño strikes, the weather grows more chaotic and crops wither season after season, and that number will only keep ballooning." Weixi's voice was soft, yet heavy, "In my time... that number has already exceeded two billion."

"Two billion?!" Old Wu's mouth hung open, "How many zeros is that?"

"Two with eight zeros behind it." Weixi said, "So many times more than everyone in our whole school that there's no way to count. Back then, many people got only one small bowl of rationed rice for the whole day."

The classroom sank into a silence unlike any before.

Even A-Xin, who was always up to mischief, had gone quiet.

"So," Weixi lowered her head, her voice trembling slightly, "I was thinking... if we reduce the food we can't finish by half, even just a little... then maybe one day, those saved grains could let someone, somewhere, eat one more bite of food."

She looked up, her eyes slightly reddened:

"We always say that those faraway places, those people we don't know, have nothing to do with us. But food is connected, weather is connected—this world... was already connected from the start."

The classroom was quiet for a long time.

Then Old Wu stood up with a "thump."

"I understand!" Old Wu clenched his fist and declared loudly, "I've decided! From today on, I, Wu Chenkai, will be the General Director of the 'Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign'!"

"General Director?" A-Xin blinked, "Isn't that the PE director's job?"

"The PE director handles sports; this time I'm handling food!" Old Wu puffed out his chest, "I eat the most, so I know best how delicious this food is! Anyone who dares to waste food, I'll be the first to stop them!"

"Okay—!" The whole class burst into laughter and applause.

"Then let's make it a competition!" A-Xin's eyes sparkled, "'The Zero-Leftover Lunch Battle'! The class splits into four groups, one table each, and we see which group has the least leftover food! The group with the least gets the 'Zero-Leftover Gold Medal'!"

"And the losing group?" Wang Zhuankai asked.

"The losing group..." A-Xin grinned slyly, "the losing group has to wash the winning group's dishes for a whole month!"

"That's too brutal!" The children wailed, but couldn't help laughing.

"Then it's settled!" Qingqing announced loudly, "'The Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign' starts from today's lunch!"

"Okay—!"

The moment the noon bell rang, the children charged to their seats like a pack of hungry little beasts.

"There's braised pork rice today!" Old Wu stared at his tray, almost drooling, "Classmates! Take as much as you'll eat! And eat everything you take!"

"At your command—!"

However, "zero waste" was easy to say but not so easy to do.

"A-Xin! How did you pick out all the vegetables again?!" Old Wu's sharp eyes caught it, and he pressed down on A-Xin's tray.

"I... I don't like vegetables!" A-Xin grimaced, "They're bitter!"

"Who says vegetables are bitter!" Old Wu put on a stern face, "Close your eyes, pretend they're French fries, and swallow them in one gulp!"

"French fries, my foot!" A-Xin wailed, but he still closed his eyes and stuffed the vegetables into his mouth, "Ugh... okay... it's not that bad...?"

"See?" Old Wu nodded with satisfaction, "You never know until you try!"

"Lin Yunyun! Why did you only eat half?" At another table, Liu-Liu jumped onto the desk, its electronic eyes fixed on Lin Yunyun's bowl, "Inspector spotted! Leftovers! Leftovers!"

"I... I can't finish..." Lin Yunyun blushed and stammered.

"Then take less!" Qingqing walked over, gently adjusting her tray for her, "Next time take half a bowl first, and add more if you're still hungry. Better to make an extra trip than to let food go to waste."

"Mhm!" Lin Yunyun nodded vigorously.

"Wow! Old Wu! Why are you going to get more rice again?!" A-Xin exclaimed.

"I have to wipe out everything you all don't eat!" Old Wu declared grandly, "I'm the 'human food waste processor'!"

"You clearly just want to eat more!" The children burst out laughing.

"Trade! Trade!" At another table, the children had discovered the secret weapon of "zero waste," "I don't like kelp, I'll trade you for your orange!"

"Deal! You give me the kelp, I give you the orange!"

"That's a smart move!" Qingqing's eyes lit up, "If you don't like something, you can trade with others—then nothing gets wasted!"

"Right! Trade it, and everyone eats happily!" The children enthusiastically traded side dishes, and the classroom overflowed with an unprecedented festive atmosphere.

Wang Zhuankai had eaten too much of the traded food and let out a resounding burp: "Burp—! No more, no more, I'm going to die from being stuffed!"

"Don't force yourself!" Lin Yunyun said worriedly.

"It's fine!" Wang Zhuankai rubbed his stomach, grinning, "For the sake of not wasting food, I'll die happily even if I'm stuffed! Burp!"

The whole class laughed again.

Just then, A-Xin suddenly went "Huh?" and stared at Old Wu's tray.

"Old Wu! Your bowl is way too clean!"

Everyone leaned in to look—on Old Wu's tray, not a single grain of rice or drop of braising sauce remained. It was so clean it looked washed, reflecting the classroom lights.

"This is the ultimate state of 'zero waste'!" A-Xin said emotionally, patting Old Wu's shoulder, "Old Wu, you're a legend of our class!"

"A legend, no way," Old Wu patted his belly, utterly content, "I just... don't want these delicious things to be thrown into that stinky bucket."

That sentence made the boisterous classroom suddenly fall quiet for a moment.

Yes. Every one of those foods thrown into the waste bucket had been cooked pot by pot, early in the morning, by the kitchen aunties.

"The aunties work so hard cooking," Lin Yunyun said quietly, "when my parents cook at home, they eat dinner so late..."

"So we have to eat every last bite!" Old Wu said loudly, "That way, we do right by the people who cooked!"

"Right!" The whole class responded in unison.

The first day of the "Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign" came to an end.

Liu-Liu hauled out its electronic scale and conducted the "lunch scout" settlement with great solemnity.

"Beep beep! Settlement begins!" Liu-Liu reported with a serious face, "Today's total leftover food for the class..."

The whole class held their breath.

"...Three point two kilograms!" Liu-Liu announced loudly, "A decrease of fifty-nine percent compared to yesterday's seven point eight kilograms!"

"Yay—!" The class erupted in deafening cheers.

"Just the first day!" A-Xin jumped up excitedly, "At this rate, our class can definitely set the school's 'zero leftover' record!"

"Tomorrow we keep going! Goal: break eighty percent!" Qingqing clenched her fist.

"Okay—!"

In the days that followed, an unprecedented scene appeared on the lunch tables of Class 6-1.

The children no longer wolfed down their food; instead, they began to "plan carefully": those who couldn't finish took less first, those who disliked something traded with others, and anything truly left over was "eliminated" with mutual help.

The kitchen aunties noticed this change and peeked in curiously.

"Oh my! Your Class 6-1 really eats cleanly today!" An auntie said in surprise, "This month, for the first time, the kitchen had an 'empty bucket' of leftovers!"

"Auntie!" Old Wu proudly raised his empty tray, "From now on, our class will have an empty bucket every day!"

"Good, good! Then tomorrow I'll cook a little extra for you all!" The auntie beamed.

The children laughed along with her.

A week later, on Friday at noon, Class 6-1 welcomed the settlement day of the first week of the "Zero-Leftover Lunch Campaign."

"Everyone! Quiet! Settlement begins!" Old Wu stood at the podium, clearing his throat with great ceremony, like a great general, "Inspector Liu-Liu, please report this week's results!"

"Beep beep!" Liu-Liu jumped onto the podium, its tail holding up an electronic display with a string of numbers dancing on it, "Settlement for the five days of this week! Class 6-1's total leftovers: Day one, seven point eight kilograms; Day two, three point two kilograms; Day three, one point nine kilograms; Day four, one point one kilograms... and today! Only zero point five kilograms!"

"Zero point five kilograms?!" The children's eyes went wide.

"That's a decrease of ninety-three percent from Day one!" Liu-Liu announced loudly, "Our class has broken the 'lowest leftover record' in the history of Luyang Elementary!"

"Yay—!" The class burst into thunderous cheers.

"Wait, wait, wait!" A-Xin jumped out, snatched Liu-Liu's display, and gestured excitedly, "Let me do the math for you! In one week, we went from seven point eight kilograms down to zero point five—how much food did we save?"

"Seven point eight minus zero point five..." Wang Zhuankai counted on his fingers, "equals seven point three?"

"Right! Seven point three kilograms! And that's 'every day' saving seven point three!" A-Xin got more and more excited, "Seven point three kilograms of food is enough for the children of a whole village to eat one full meal! What we've saved in one week is enough for them to eat five or six meals!"

"Wow..." The children gasped one after another.

"And do you know?" A-Xin's eyes sparkled, "If we eat like this for a whole year, the food we save could feed..."

"Stop, stop!" Old Wu laughed heartily, cutting him off, "Keep going and I'll cry from being moved! Anyway, we're amazing! Right?"

"Right—!" The whole class shouted in unison.

"Next, let's award the 'Zero-Leftover Gold Medals'!" Old Wu pulled out several medals folded from aluminum foil from his backpack, "First place, Group Two, come up to receive your awards!"

"Whoo—!" The children of Group Two gleefully ran up to the stage, hanging the aluminum foil medals around their necks, chests puffed out like they'd won Olympic gold.

"The losing group has to wash the winning group's dishes for a month," A-Xin grinned mischievously at Group One, "are you all ready?"

"Wash then! A bet's a bet!" Though Group One had lost, they were all grinning, "Anyway, we saved a ton of food too!"

The children laughed together.

Weixi sat in the corner, watching this scene, the corners of her mouth lifting slightly.

"Weixi," Qingqing leaned over and asked quietly, "what are you smiling about?"

"I'm thinking," Weixi said softly, "you all really are amazing. Where I come from, to save food, the adults had to pass laws, impose fines, enforce rationing... but you all, just because you 'don't want to waste,' accomplished it on your own."

"That's because you're here." Qingqing said, "You're the one who made us understand how important these things are."

Weixi was taken aback for a moment, then lowered her head and smiled softly.

"Oh, right," Qingqing suddenly thought of something, "I saw a news report last night that said a full one-third of all food in the world is wasted every year."

"One-third..." Weixi said softly, "the water, land, and labor used to grow all that wasted food are wasted along with it. The adults call it the 'water footprint'—when you save one meal, you're not just saving a meal; you're saving the water used to grow it, the energy consumed, and the carbon emitted."

"Wow, saving one bite of food can even save water?" Qingqing marveled.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "So every clean bite of food you eat is depositing a little more money into the Earth's account."

"Then we need to save more!" Qingqing clenched her fist, her eyes shining, "Fill the Earth's account to the brim!"

"Mhm!" Weixi smiled, a smile carrying an unprecedented peace of mind.

That afternoon, after school.

The children filed out of the classroom one by one, Old Wu the last to leave. He stood in the empty classroom, turned back, and glanced at the table covered with trays, muttering to himself:

"Who would've thought... eating a little less could save the Earth too."

He patted his round belly and grinned naively.

"Then from now on, let me, Old Wu, keep eliminating everyone's leftover food!"

The setting sun cast a long shadow behind his chubby back.

Weixi sat by the window, watching this scene, her eyes suddenly a little hot.

Qingqing noticed her expression, walked over, and asked softly: "What's wrong?"

"Nothing," Weixi shook her head, her voice soft, "I was just... thinking about something."

"Thinking about what?"

"Where I come from," Weixi lowered her head, "every meal is handed out in line. Everyone gets exactly the same amount—no more, no less. Children often can't eat their fill, but the adults can only say: 'Bear with it, we'll see again tomorrow.'"

"So..." Qingqing finally understood, "that's why you cherish every single meal."

"Mhm." Weixi looked up and smiled faintly, "Here, I see you all eating big mouthfuls, trading for the dishes you like, dumping what you can't finish... Part of me is so happy, because this world is still so abundant; part of me is so sad, because..."

"Because this abundance might one day be gone." Qingqing finished for her.

Weixi said nothing, only nodded gently.

That evening, after school, Weixi stood alone in the hallway, watching the kitchen aunties bustling about preparing tomorrow's ingredients.

She remembered the words her grandmother had said.

"Child, being able to eat your fill is the happiest thing in this world. If one day you see someone who can't eat their fill, remember to save the bite of food you can spare, and leave it for them."

Weixi gently touched the bracelet on her wrist.

On the inside of the bracelet, the countdown number had quietly dropped another digit.

"...Fifty." she murmured softly.

Just then, Liu-Liu poked its head out of the ventilation duct, a hint of gravity in its electronic eyes.

"Beep beep, Class President," Liu-Liu ran over to Qingqing and lowered its voice, "that smell behind the school... has gotten stronger again. I detected something flowing down the ditch."

Qingqing's heart sank slightly.

She looked toward the sea behind the school.

In the afterglow of the setting sun, the five huge red-and-white smokestacks in the seaward direction behind Luyang Elementary stood silently on the horizon where sea met sky—the Taichung Thermal Power Plant, the largest coal-fired power plant in Taiwan, its ten coal-fired units sharing five smokestacks, puffing out white smoke day and night. For the people who lived in this little town, those smokestacks had long become part of the sky.

But Qingqing knew that the invisible smoke, and many more invisible things, were drawing closer to them, bit by bit.
