# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 10: Beach Cleanup & the Plastic Crisis

In the second week of the "Inspect the Drainage Ditch" mission, Weixi made a suggestion.

"Instead of just waiting for the truth about the upstream," one day after school, Weixi stood at the back of the classroom, gazing at that murky gray sea outside the window, "let's start with what we can see."

"What we can see?" Qingqing asked.

"That beach." Weixi pointed at the sandy shore beyond the seawall behind the school, "Have you ever looked closely at what that beach looks like now?"

The children looked at each other.

That beach, they saw it every day. But honestly... it seemed no one had ever really looked at it closely.

"Let's go see." Weixi said.

So the next day after school, all twenty-six of Class 6-1, plus Liu-Liu, marched in a grand procession over the seawall and stepped onto that beach for the first time.

Then, everyone froze.

The beach was densely littered with all kinds of trash.

PET bottles, bottle caps, straws, plastic bags, Styrofoam fragments, drink cans, slippers, fishing nets, ropes... stretching from under their feet all the way to the edge of sight, as if someone had deliberately dumped an entire landfill here.

"This..." A-Xin's eyes went wide, "this is way too much! How is there so much trash behind our school?!"

"This trash," Weixi crouched down and picked up a PET bottle bleached white by seawater, her voice soft, "for the most part, didn't come from the school. It floated down from the rivers upstream; it was thrown overboard from ships at sea; it was carried here by ocean currents from the other side of the ocean."

"The other side of the ocean?" Old Wu blinked, "How far is that?"

"Maybe thousands of kilometers away." Weixi said, "The wind blows them out to sea, and the sea brings them here. Every piece of trash we pick up may have traveled halfway around the world."

"Halfway around the world..." Lin Yunyun crouched on the sand, carefully picking up a bottle cap, murmuring in repetition.

"Then what do we do?" Wang Zhuankai asked, "There's so much trash. Can twenty-six of us really pick it all up?"

"We can't pick it all up." Weixi shook her head, but her tone was firm, "But as long as we pick up a handful, this beach gets a little cleaner."

"Right!" Qingqing stood up and said loudly, "'Protect Earth' new mission: 'Beach Cleanup'! Each of us takes responsibility for cleaning the little patch of beach in front of us!"

"Okay—!" The children responded in unison.

The next day was Saturday, overcast, with a salty sea wind that made people shiver.

But at nine in the morning, at the gate of Luyang Elementary, twenty-six children stood in full gear, wrapped in coats, wearing masks.

"Everyone got everything?" Qingqing called the roll.

"Gloves!" "Tongs!" "Trash bags!" "Water bottles!" "Hats!" The children counted off one by one.

"And the most important thing!" A-Xin pulled a small flag made of yellow plastic cloth from his backpack and planted it in the direction of the beach, "'The Beach Cleanup Battle Flag'! Move out!"

"Whoo—!"

The twenty-six children, like a tiny army, marched in a grand procession over the seawall and set out for that beach.

And so, a "Beach Cleanup Campaign" officially began on the beach behind the school.

The children brought gloves, tongs, and trash bags. A-Xin had even somehow gotten hold of a construction hard hat, which he wore on his head as he directed operations with great ceremony.

"Everyone to your posts! Sort the trash! PET bottles go in this bag! Bottle caps in this bag! Other trash in that bag! Sort it wrong, and Liu-Liu will return it!"

"Beep beep! Roger!" Liu-Liu dashed around the beach like a strict overseer, "This beach is our 'battlefield'!"

"Battlefield?" Old Wu picked up a slipper that had rotted in the seawater and held it over his head, "Then this slipper is my 'war trophy'!"

"Yours is nothing!" A-Xin refused to be outdone, digging a fishing line tangled with seaweed out of the sand, "Mine here is a 'sea monster tentacle'!"

"That fishing line of yours," Qingqing walked over, frowning, "if it gets tangled around a sea turtle's neck, the turtle will die! The news reported that sea turtles get entangled in plastic and grow up with deformed shells!"

"Really?!" A-Xin's eyes went wide, and he immediately and carefully put the fishing line into the trash bag, "Then I'm going to 'execute' it! Avenge the sea turtle!"

The children picked as they chattered.

"Hey, look at this!" Wang Zhuankai picked up a straw, "This little straw—if it gets thrown into the sea, how long before it disappears?"

"A hundred years," Weixi said softly, "or longer."

"A hundred years?!" Wang Zhuankai gasped, "A hundred years from now, I'll already be..."

"Already be a grandpa!" A-Xin laughed heartily, "And your straw will still be lying in the sea!"

"That can't be!" Wang Zhuankai clutched the straw, his expression as serious as a declaration of war, "I absolutely refuse to let my straw outlive me!"

"Hahaha!" The children laughed together.

"Oh right, oh right! Everyone come look at this!" Old Wu suddenly shouted, running over holding a strange object, "I dug up an 'antique'!"

"Antique?" The children gathered around to look—it was a rusted iron can, with the word "soda" still faintly legible on it.

"Wow! This is a soda can from at least thirty years ago!" A-Xin's eyes went wide, "Old Wu, you're rich! You can sell this to a museum!"

"Sell it to a museum, my foot!" Qingqing laughed helplessly, "It's trash! Put it in 'metal'!"

"Oh!" Old Wu obediently placed the "antique" into the sorting bag, muttering as he did, "A thirty-year-old soda can... the person who drank it back then surely never imagined that thirty years later, an elementary school student would find it on the beach..."

"So you see," Weixi walked over and said softly, "nothing we throw away ever truly disappears. It stays in this world, waiting to be seen again one day, by someone."

The children fell silent.

"Then we'll pick up more!" A-Xin said loudly, "Bring all those 'waiting-to-be-seen' pieces of trash 'home'!"

"Right!" The children responded in unison.

"Oh! Look what I found!" Lin Yunyun held up a small, faded toy car, "This little car... must have been lost by some child by accident."

"Maybe it drifted over from far out at sea!" Old Wu leaned in, "It might have traveled around the world once!"

"Then let's grant its wish!" A-Xin carefully placed the toy car into the bag, "Let it stay on the beach and wait for its owner to come back and find it!"

"A-Xin, since when did you get so sentimental?" Qingqing laughed helplessly.

"I've always been sentimental!" A-Xin declared with full confidence, "I just don't show it usually!"

"You clearly just can't bear to throw away a toy!" Old Wu teased.

"Hahaha!" The children laughed together.

"Classmates," Weixi suddenly spoke, her voice soft, "did you know? Someone did the statistics—the amount of plastic trash flowing into the ocean every year worldwide is a full eight million tons."

"Eight million tons?!" The children's eyes went wide.

"What does eight million tons even mean?" Weixi thought for a moment, "Roughly... every single minute, one truckload of plastic is dumped into the sea."

"Every minute?!" A-Xin gasped, "Then in the few minutes we've been talking, several more truckloads of plastic have been dumped into the sea?"

"Mhm." Weixi nodded softly, "But the seventy-six kilograms we picked up today were snatched back from those 'every minutes.'"

"Snatched back..." The children looked at those trash bags, and a nameless light suddenly appeared in their eyes.

"So we have to keep snatching!" A-Xin said loudly.

"Keep snatching!" The children responded in unison.

Halfway through the cleanup, Liu-Liu came running back panting, carrying a small bag in its mouth.

"Beep beep! The statistics are out!" Liu-Liu dumped the bag onto the sand, "In the last hour, our class picked up—one hundred forty-seven PET bottles! Two hundred thirty-one bottle caps! Fifty-eight straws! Forty-two plastic bags! Styrofoam... too many to count! Total weight: twenty-three kilograms!"

"Twenty-three kilograms!" The children gasped, "In just one hour?!"

"Mhm." Liu-Liu nodded, "And I scanned it—over eighty percent of this trash is 'plastic.'"

"Plastic..." The children fell silent.

"Plastic never disappears." Weixi crouched on the sand, looking at the trash, her voice soft, "It only becomes smaller and smaller fragments. Small enough to be invisible, small enough to be eaten by fish, small enough to..." she paused, "small enough that, in the end, we eat it back into our own bodies."

The beach suddenly fell quiet.

The sea wind howled past, whipping the children's hair wildly.

"Weixi?" A-Xin was the first to notice something wrong with her, calling out carefully, "Are... are you okay?"

Actually, from the moment she stepped onto this beach, Weixi had been a little unusual.

She walked across the sand unlike the other children, who darted about everywhere; she moved especially slowly, each step as if treading on something very fragile.

She would crouch down, staring for a very long time at the tiny plastic fragments embedded in the sand, then pick them up one by one.

"Weixi, what are you picking up?" Lin Yunyun leaned in curiously.

"These... plastic fragments." Weixi said softly, "They're so small that no one pays attention to them. But they're the biggest problem."

"The biggest problem?" Lin Yunyun didn't understand.

"Big trash gets picked up. But these tiny fragments," Weixi opened her palm, the tiny, colorful plastic fragments glinting in the sunlight, "stay in the sand forever, seep into the water, get eaten by fish, and then..." she didn't finish, just gently put the fragments into her pocket.

"And then?" Lin Yunyun pressed.

"And then," Weixi's voice suddenly grew weak, "they become..."

Her voice stopped abruptly.

Her whole body stiffened in place, as if struck by something.

Then her pupils suddenly dilated.

"Weixi!" Qingqing also noticed something wrong and rushed over, "What's wrong?! Your face..."

"I..." Weixi's voice suddenly began to tremble, "I see..."

"See what?" A-Xin asked nervously.

"I see..." Weixi trembled, reaching out a hand to point at the beach before her, "that beach..."

"What about this beach?" The children looked at each other, "This beach... isn't it just like this?"

"No!" Weixi shook her head violently, her voice choked with tears, "Can't you see it?! That trash... that trash is taller than people! The whole beach is nothing but trash! Black water... and the sky is gray too..."

"Weixi!" Qingqing grabbed her shoulder, "Calm down! There's no trash taller than people here! There's only the twenty-three kilograms we just picked up!"

"But..." Weixi's tears fell without warning, "I saw it... I really saw it... that is... that is our future..."

"Weixi!" A-Xin also rushed over, blocking himself in front of her, shouting, "Look at me! Look at me! There's no trash mountain here! There's only us! Only the twenty-six of us, and this beach!"

Weixi stared blankly at A-Xin.

Her pupils slowly refocused.

"...A-Xin?" she said softly.

"Yes! It's me!" A-Xin breathed a sigh of relief, "Did you just see something scary?"

Weixi fell silent.

She lowered her head, tears falling drop by drop onto the sand.

"...I'm sorry." She sniffled, forcing a smile, "I'm fine. I just... saw some..." She shook her head, "Nothing. Really, nothing."

"But you clearly just—" A-Xin wanted to press further.

"A-Xin!" Qingqing interrupted him, shaking her head, "Don't ask."

A-Xin opened his mouth, but finally closed it.

But looking at Weixi's pale face, still streaked with tears, an unprecedented question welled up in his heart.

An hour later, the beach cleanup came to an end.

The twenty-six children stood in a row on the beach, before them a neat line of trash bags.

"Final tally!" Liu-Liu announced loudly, "For this beach cleanup, our class collected a total of—seventy-six kilograms of trash!"

"Seventy-six kilograms?!" The children exclaimed.

"Three hundred eighty-nine PET bottles!" Liu-Liu continued, "Five hundred seventy-three bottle caps! One hundred twenty-nine straws! Ninety-eight plastic bags! Plus fishing nets, slippers, lighters, toothbrushes... every kind you can imagine!"

"Look," Qingqing pointed at those trash bags, "twenty-six of us, in one afternoon, carried seventy-six kilograms of trash off this beach! If we come every month, in a year..."

"We can pick up over nine hundred kilograms!" A-Xin calculated eagerly, "Nearly one ton!"

"Nearly one ton of trash!" The children looked at each other, light shining in their eyes.

"Starting today," Qingqing announced loudly, "we come to clean the beach every month!"

"Okay—!"

The children cheered and carried the trash bags, one by one, back to the school and handed them to the sanitation crew.

"Today," Qingqing stood on the seawall, looking at the children, and asked loudly, "are you all tired?"

"Tired—!" The children answered in unison, but with laughter in their voices.

"But are you happy?"

"Happy—!"

"Why are you happy?" Qingqing asked.

"Because we made the beach cleaner!" The children shouted in unison.

"Right!" Qingqing said loudly, "This beach is our turf! We won't let trash bully our beach!"

"Never!" The children shouted along.

The sea wind howled, and the children's cries echoed across the sea.

That night, Weixi sat in her room, pulling today's plastic fragments from her pocket, laying them out one by one on the table.

In the moonlight, those fragments glinted with a cold light.

"Plastic never disappears," she said softly, "but I will remember that today, a group of children bent their backs in the sea wind all day just to make this beach a little cleaner."

Gently, she put those fragments into a small glass jar.

"This is the best gift I've received here."

Under the setting sun, that beach finally revealed a little of its original color.

That night, A-Xin lay in bed, tossing and turning, unable to fall asleep.

In his mind, the scene of Weixi this afternoon kept echoing—

"That trash is taller than people! The whole beach is nothing but trash!"

"That is... our future..."

"Our future?" A-Xin sat up and murmured, "How does Weixi... see the 'future'?"

He began to recall all the strange things that had happened since he met Weixi.

On her first day in class, she accurately predicted the heavy rain that afternoon.

She could name the origin of every dish, and say that a pipe would leak three days later.

She wore a bracelet on her wrist that Liu-Liu said "doesn't belong to this world."

In the park, she wept before a withered tree.

She said she came from a "very, very faraway place, a place she could never go back to."

Watching disasters in the news, she would whisper "it's already begun."

Looking at trash, at waste, at the dirtied sea, she would get that look... that look of someone who had seen these things happen with her own eyes.

"Could it be..." A-Xin's heart pounded, "could Weixi really be..."

He didn't say the word.

But he knew that word had already taken root in his heart.

He remembered again—last month, he secretly followed Weixi and saw her crouching alone before the withered tree in the park.

"It hurts, doesn't it." Weixi had said softly to the tree, "The roots can't drink water, the leaves can't stand the sun... I know that feeling."

"In our place, there are also many trees, just like you..."

Back then, A-Xin thought she was just talking to herself.

But thinking about it now... "our place"? Where?

And once, when the whole class was watching the news about a major flood in some country, all the children gasped—only Weixi watched the screen quietly and murmured:

"Just like before..."

"Just like before?" A-Xin hadn't understood at the time.

Now, he thought he might understand, a little.

"Tomorrow, I'm definitely going to ask her!" A-Xin clenched his fist, his mind made up.

Actually, that night, A-Xin did one more thing.

He sneaked back to school and found Liu-Liu on duty in the ventilation duct.

"Liu-Liu! Liu-Liu!" A-Xin lowered his voice, "I want to ask you something, and you have to answer honestly!"

"Beep beep? What question?" Liu-Liu poked its head out.

"That bracelet on Weixi's wrist," A-Xin stared into Liu-Liu's eyes, "didn't you say before that its frequency band 'doesn't belong to this world'? What exactly do you know?"

Liu-Liu fell silent.

"Liu-Liu!" A-Xin pressed, "Are you all... hiding something from us?"

"Beep beep..." Liu-Liu lowered its head and wagged its tail, "A-Xin, some things... it's not that I don't want to tell you, it's that I can't tell you yet."

"Why?"

"Because..." Liu-Liu looked up, unprecedented seriousness in its electronic eyes, "telling you would harm Weixi."

A-Xin was stunned.

"A-Xin," Liu-Liu said softly, "I know you mean well for Weixi. But please, trust us. When it's time to tell, we will definitely tell you."

A-Xin was silent for a long time.

"...Fine." he finally said, "I'll wait."

"But Liu-Liu," he added, "if one day Weixi needs help, you must call me first!"

"Beep beep! It's a deal!" Liu-Liu's tail gently hooked around A-Xin's pinky.

The next morning, A-Xin arrived at school early and planted himself at the classroom door.

"Weixi!" A-Xin blocked her way, "I have something to ask you!"

"A-Xin?" Weixi blinked, "What's wrong?"

"What did you really see on the beach yesterday?" A-Xin looked straight into her eyes, his tone serious in a way unlike his usual self, "You talked about a 'future trash mountain'... how could you see something like that?"

Weixi's smile froze.

"A-Xin," she lowered her head, her voice soft, "that was... my eyes playing tricks on me. Really."

"You're lying!" A-Xin said loudly, "I know you're not seeing things! You... you have so many things about you that are different from us! You..."

"A-Xin." Weixi interrupted him, looked up, and in her eyes was a plea that pained the heart, "Don't ask, okay?"

"Why?"

"Because..." Weixi's voice was so low it was almost inaudible, "some answers, once you know them, you can never go back."

A-Xin was stunned.

He looked at Weixi's slightly reddened eyes and, for the first time, discovered that this transfer student, who always smiled so gently, had a bottomless lake hidden in her eyes.

"...Fine." A-Xin lowered his head and answered with a muffled voice, "I won't ask."

He turned and walked toward the classroom.

After two steps, he stopped again.

"But Weixi," A-Xin said with his back to her, his voice muffled, "whatever you're afraid of, remember this—our 'Protect Earth Operations Unit' has twenty-six people. Your matters are our matters."

Weixi stood in the hallway, watching A-Xin's back.

The wind blew through her hair.

She lowered her head and said softly:

"...Thank you, A-Xin."

After A-Xin left, from the corner of the hallway, Qingqing stepped out quietly.

"You heard all that?" Liu-Liu poked its head out of Qingqing's backpack.

"Mhm." Qingqing watched the back of Weixi as she walked away, and said softly, "A-Xin... is starting to suspect."

"Then what do we do?" Liu-Liu asked nervously, "Should we tell him the truth?"

"Not yet." Qingqing shook her head, "Weixi said the fewer people who know her secret, the better. But..."

"But what?"

"But A-Xin is our friend," Qingqing said in a low voice, "keeping it from him... must be hard for him."

"Beep beep..." Liu-Liu tilted its head, "Class President, do you think A-Xin can keep a secret?"

Qingqing thought for a moment, then suddenly smiled.

"Him," Qingqing said, "he's crazy most of the time, but when it comes to keeping a secret, he's more reliable than anyone."

"Then that's settled!" Liu-Liu breathed a sigh of relief, "Then I'll keep watching that pipe!"

"Mhm." Qingqing nodded, "Weixi's secret—we'll guard it together."

The wind blew across the hallway.

Qingqing looked at the sea outside the window and gently clenched her fist.

"Weixi, don't worry. Before you go back, we'll always be standing by your side."
