# The Transfer Student from the Future

## Chapter 13: Weixi's Future Memories & the Countdown

The "Track the Pipe" operation was unfolding in full swing.

The children used the money raised at the fair to buy water quality testing tools, split into four groups, and investigated along the drainage ditch, bit by bit toward the upstream.

"This stretch of water—odor index eighty-five!" "Mine is ninety-two over here!" "At the river mouth, the discolored area of the seawater has expanded again!"

"Keep going! We're definitely going to find out the truth about that pipe!" Qingqing cheered everyone on loudly.

Everything seemed to be moving in a good direction.

Except... Weixi.

"Weixi, what's wrong?" During morning self-study that day, Lin Yunyun was the first to notice something different about Weixi, "Why is your face so pale?"

"It's nothing." Weixi shook her head, forcing a smile, "I just... didn't sleep well last night."

"Didn't sleep well?" A-Xin leaned in, "Your dark circles have been getting worse lately! You're not secretly reading comics until midnight, are you?"

"I'm not secretly reading comics." Weixi laughed helplessly.

"Then why can't you sleep?" A-Xin pressed.

"Because..." Weixi thought for a moment and said, "I keep having dreams. Dreams about... some very strange places."

"What places?" Lin Yunyun asked curiously.

"A..." Weixi lowered her head, her voice soft, "a gray place."

"Gray?" The children looked at each other.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "No phoenix trees, no green grass. The sky is gray, and so is the sea. The people on the streets all walk with their heads down, as if they're very, very tired."

"That must be a nightmare!" A-Xin patted his chest in assurance, "Don't worry! You're at Luyang Elementary now! There are phoenix trees, green grass, and us!"

"Mhm." Weixi smiled, "With you all here, I'm not afraid."

But Qingqing noticed something.

When Weixi said "not afraid," her hand was gently touching the bracelet on her wrist.

As if touching something... that could disappear at any moment.

Over the next week, Weixi's "nightmares" grew more and more serious.

At first, it was only in the mornings.

Then, it spread to class time.

"Weixi? Weixi!" The natural science teacher called her name three times before she snapped back to reality, "What's wrong? Where were we?"

"Ah? I..." Weixi looked at the blackboard in a daze for several seconds, "I'm sorry, teacher... I just... zoned out."

"Are you feeling unwell?" The natural science teacher asked worriedly, "Do you want to go to the health room?"

"No need, teacher." Weixi shook her head, "I'll be fine after a little rest."

But Qingqing, sitting in front of her, saw an unusual sight—

The moment Weixi lowered her head, the bracelet on her wrist suddenly flashed with a blinding blue light.

That blue light lasted only an instant.

But Qingqing saw it clearly—the countdown number on the inside of the bracelet that had always been there.

Last week, it was still thirty-eight.

Now, it had become... thirty-four.

"In just one week, it dropped four digits?" Qingqing's heart sank heavily.

After school, Qingqing didn't go home.

She waited at the classroom door until everyone had left, then walked to Weixi's seat.

"Weixi," Qingqing lowered her voice, "have you... been going through something lately?"

The hand Weixi was using to pack her backpack paused.

"Nothing." She didn't turn around, but her voice trembled slightly, "I'm fine."

"You're lying." Qingqing walked in front of her, "You zone out during class; your dark circles get deeper every day; and your bracelet—" she pointed at Weixi's wrist, "it was thirty-eight last week, and now it's only thirty-four!"

Weixi's movements froze completely.

"Qingqing..." it was a long moment before she spoke, her voice hoarse, "you... saw it?"

"Mhm." Qingqing nodded, "Weixi, does this number mean... how much time you have left?"

Weixi fell silent.

She lowered her head and looked at the bracelet on her wrist.

After a very, very long moment, she finally said softly:

"...It's gotten faster."

"What's gotten faster?" Qingqing pressed.

"The countdown." Weixi's voice was light as a feather, "When I first arrived, its countdown speed was steady. But lately..." she paused, "it's been moving faster and faster."

"Why would it get faster?"

"I don't know." Weixi shook her head, "Maybe... because I've been using too much 'power of the future.'"

"The power of the future?"

"Mhm." Weixi lowered her head, "I projected the future for you with the bracelet, used future knowledge to help everyone sort trash, predicted the weather... every use consumes a little of the bracelet's energy. Lately..."

"Lately?"

"Lately," Weixi's voice carried a hint of trembling, "I keep accidentally... 'seeing' the future."

"Seeing the future?"

"It's like..." Weixi looked up, a nameless bewilderment in her eyes, "sometimes, I suddenly can't tell whether I'm here or in the future. Just now in natural science class, when I looked up, the blackboard was gone, and in front of me was a grayish-yellow sky. Then the teacher called me, and I..."

She didn't finish.

But Qingqing already understood.

"Weixi..." Qingqing grabbed her hand, "you're in danger like this! If one day, you can't 'come back'..."

"I know." Weixi gently withdrew her hand, her voice so calm it was heartbreaking, "I know. So... I'll try hard not to 'lose myself' for too long."

"But..."

"Qingqing," Weixi interrupted her, a plea in her tone, "don't tell the others about this, okay? I don't want to worry everyone."

Qingqing fell silent.

"...Fine." She finally said, "I'll keep it secret for you. But Weixi," she looked at her seriously, "if you really feel unwell, you must tell me! We'll figure it out together!"

"Mhm." Weixi smiled, "Thank you, Qingqing."

But in the days that followed, Weixi's condition didn't improve; instead, it grew more and more severe.

On the third day, at nap time.

Weixi lay on her desk with her eyes closed, resting.

Suddenly, her eyelids twitched violently. Her body also began to tremble slightly.

"Weixi? Weixi!" Qingqing sensed something wrong and rushed over, shaking her, "Weixi! Wake up!"

Weixi's eyes snapped open.

A grayish-yellow scene still lingered in her pupils.

"I... where am I?" she asked in a daze.

"You're at school! In our class!" Qingqing held her hand, "Weixi, what just happened?!"

"I..." Weixi gasped, her voice choked with tears, "I saw... I saw Grandma."

"Grandma?" Qingqing froze, "Your grandmother?"

"Grandma..." Weixi's eyes suddenly turned red, "she was lining up for water. She was so thin, her hands shaking the whole time. I wanted to call her, but she couldn't hear me..."

"Weixi..." Qingqing's heart twisted into a knot.

"Then I saw..." Weixi's voice grew softer and softer, "she went back to our house and cooked a pot of rice with a tiny bit of water. She gave most of the rice to the kids next door. She only drank a bowl of water herself..."

"She said..." Weixi's tears fell drop by drop, "she said, 'Weixi ah, Grandma can't eat. Give the food to others.'"

"It's not that she couldn't eat." Weixi sobbed, "She couldn't bear to eat. She wanted to save the food for others..."

Qingqing's eyes turned red too.

She held Weixi's hand tightly, unable to say a word.

That night, Weixi's "flashback" appeared again.

This time, what she saw wasn't the future.

It was the "past"—things that had really happened over the past few decades.

"I saw a huge flood," the next morning, Weixi said weakly to Qingqing, "a whole city underwater. The adults said it was the biggest flood in decades."

"Then I saw a glacier on a very high mountain collapsing, blocking a river, and the water flooding the villages below..."

"And a very, very hot summer, the asphalt melting and people fainting on the roads..."

"These..." Weixi's voice trembled, "these really happened. In the textbooks of our era, these are all 'history.'"

"History?" Qingqing's heart leaped.

"Mhm." Weixi lowered her head, "In our textbooks, all of these are called 'the lessons of climate change.' Every page says—'if the people of that time had been willing to act a little earlier...'"

"'If the people of that time had been willing to act a little earlier...'" Qingqing repeated the phrase.

"Qingqing," Weixi looked up at her, "every thing you're doing now is rewriting the 'history' in our textbooks. Do you understand?"

Qingqing fell silent.

"I understand." She finally said, her voice carrying an unprecedented heaviness, "But Weixi... the price you're paying is too great."

That night, Qingqing did something "strange."

She asked Liu-Liu to compile all the extreme weather events that had occurred around the world over the past few decades.

"Beep beep! The amount of data is huge, Class President!" Liu-Liu reported while pulling up data, "Starting with the old news in my memory—"

"The super hurricane of 2005 flooded a whole city."

"In 2010, Russia's heatwave burned up vast forests."

"In 2021, a flood made an entire town disappear overnight."

"In 2025, Europe's heatwave killed tens of thousands of people."

"In 2026..." Liu-Liu's voice suddenly slowed, "the glacier collapse in Nepal destroyed the border crossing; record-breaking torrential rain in southern China dumped a whole year's rain in one day; global sea temperatures hit a new high..."

Qingqing stared at those records, unable to speak for a long time.

"Liu-Liu," she finally spoke, "have you noticed... these 'news' items, seem to..."

"Seem to be increasing?" Liu-Liu continued, "And getting more severe each time?"

"Yes." Qingqing nodded, "Just like Weixi said—'if the people of that time had been willing to act a little earlier.'"

"Class President," Liu-Liu asked in a low voice, "are you saying... these news items are the 'history' in Weixi's textbook?"

"I don't know." Qingqing shook her head, "But if these things keep happening one after another... then forty years from now, the 'lessons' written in Weixi's textbook will come true."

"Then..." Liu-Liu's voice carried a hint of fear, "is what we're doing now... still in time?"

"I don't know." Qingqing finally said, "But... if even we stop doing it, then it really will be too late."

That night, Weixi's flashback was clearer than ever before.

She saw herself standing on a street of the future.

The sky was grayish-yellow, the sun blocked by a thick layer of smog, showing only a blurry outline. The trees on both sides of the street were all dead, leaving only bare branches, like hands reaching toward the sky.

She walked along the street.

On the wall beside the road hung a faded poster: "Save water, everyone's duty. This week's ration: four liters per person per day."

"Four liters..." Weixi lowered her head, looking at the rows of silent people lining up with water buckets by the roadside, "one liter of water can only..."

"Wash a face." The woman in front of her said without turning her head, her voice flat, "Or cook a small pot of rice. Or give it to the kids to drink."

"Auntie," Weixi asked softly, "how long have you... been living like this?"

"How long?" The woman thought for a moment, "When I was little, the river was still clear. My grandmother said that when she was little, you could drink the river water."

"But..." the woman lowered her head, no emotion in her voice, "when my daughter was born, the river had already turned gray."

Weixi's feet suddenly felt nailed to the ground.

She lowered her head and looked at her own hands.

Those hands, in this era, had once picked up the letters the children wrote.

"I..." she murmured, "I think I'm... about to remember."

"Remember what?" The woman asked.

"Remember..." Weixi looked up at the grayish-yellow sky, "that a long, long time ago, a little girl once said to me—"

"'Everything you do is to keep the Earth alive.'"

"Who said that?" The woman asked.

Weixi was silent for a long time.

"...Someone who is the most important person to me." she said softly.

Then the flashback ended.

Weixi snapped back to reality, finding herself sitting in the classroom, covered in cold sweat.

"Weixi!" Qingqing looked at her worriedly, "did you... have an episode again?"

"Mhm." Weixi gasped and nodded gently, "But... I saw something important."

"What?"

"I saw..." Weixi lowered her head, "the future me, standing on a street. And in her heart, she always remembered one sentence."

"What sentence?"

"'Everything you do is to keep the Earth alive.'" Weixi said softly, "That was... what I said to the future me."

"It doesn't matter." Weixi smiled faintly, "As long as... it's worth it."

However, fate didn't give Weixi much time for "worth it."

On the fourth night, a new change appeared on Weixi's bracelet.

That night, Weixi sat by the window, looking at her bracelet.

The number on the inside of the bracelet flickered quietly in the moonlight.

—Thirty-one.

"Three days, three digits..." Weixi said in a low voice, "the speed has gotten faster again."

She gently pressed the bracelet against her chest.

"Grandma," she said softly to the night sky, "I think... I'm about to go back."

"But..." she lowered her head, "I haven't yet seen everyone save the Earth."

"I haven't yet seen the day when the letters the children wrote are delivered to my era."

"I haven't yet..." she closed her eyes gently, "said goodbye to them properly."

Weixi remembered the day she left the future, and the cloth bundle her grandmother had pressed into her arms.

Inside the bundle, besides that yellowed photograph, there was also a note.

On the note, in her grandmother's crooked handwriting, was written:

"Weixi:
Grandma can't write, so I asked Sister Xiaofen next door to write these words for me.
Grandma wants to tell you—no matter where you go, Grandma will be here waiting for you.
Eat well, sleep well, and see that world properly for Grandma.
If one day, you really can't come back... that's okay too.
Grandma will keep waiting for you in her dreams."

"Grandma..." Weixi pressed the note against her chest, tears falling drop by drop, "I will come back. I will definitely come back..."

"But..." she lowered her head, "when I come back, I want to bring good news."

"I want to tell Grandma—" she looked up at the night sky, a resolute light flickering in her eyes, "there's a group of children saving the Earth."

"They..." she smiled softly, "are trying even harder than any adult."

Just then, her vision suddenly turned grayish-yellow again—

"Weixi! Weixi!" Qingqing's voice rang out anxiously.

Weixi snapped back to reality, realizing she had somehow collapsed on the floor of her room.

"I... what happened?" she asked in a daze.

"You fainted!" Qingqing crouched in front of her, panting, "Your mom called me, saying you hadn't replied to messages! I rushed over and found you collapsed here!"

"I..." Weixi lowered her head and looked at the bracelet on her wrist.

The number on the bracelet was jumping from thirty-one to... thirty.

"Dropped another digit." Weixi murmured, "Just now... what did I see?"

"What did you see?" Qingqing asked nervously.

"I saw..." Weixi frowned, trying hard to recall, "a little girl. She was wearing... the same uniform as us. She was under a tree..."

"And then?"

"And then..." Weixi's brow furrowed tighter and tighter, "she looked up and saw a letter. A letter... mailed from a very, very distant past."

"A letter?" Qingqing's heart leaped.

"Mhm." Weixi nodded, "The letter seemed to say... 'To the future Earth: Hello! I'm A-Xin!'"

Qingqing froze.

"A-Xin?" She repeated in disbelief, "That's the letter A-Xin wrote!"

"Yes..." Weixi froze too, "But... how could I see A-Xin's letter?"

"Weixi," Qingqing's voice trembled slightly, "what you saw... could it be 'the future you'?"

"The future me?"

"It's..." Qingqing took a deep breath, "the you of forty years from now! In the future, you received the letters we sent out now!"

Weixi froze.

She lowered her head, looked at her bracelet, then looked up out the window.

"The me of forty years from now..." she murmured, "will receive their letters?"

The moonlight shone quietly on her.

She smiled softly.

"...If that's the case," she said in a low voice, "then that must be the warmest letter in the world."

However, that night, after Qingqing went home, she couldn't sleep at all.

She lay in the darkness, staring at the ceiling, the phrase Weixi had said echoing in her mind—"it's gotten faster."

"The countdown's gotten faster..." she said in a low voice, "that means Weixi has less and less time left."

She got up, opened her phone, and began searching for information.

"Liu-Liu," she asked into the Liu-Liu communicator on her phone, "is it possible for the bracelet's countdown... to suddenly speed up?"

"Beep beep..." Liu-Liu's voice was unusually low, "theoretically... possible. If the user consumes too much energy in a certain period of time, the countdown speed will increase."

"Like..." Qingqing asked, "a phone battery draining too fast?"

"Right." Liu-Liu said, "Like a phone battery—if you keep running the most power-hungry app, the charge drops all at once. Weixi..."

"She's been using the 'power of the future' all along." Qingqing murmured, "Sorting for us, predicting, projecting... she's been spending all her 'battery' on us."

"Class President..." Liu-Liu said in a low voice, "at this rate, Weixi..."

"I know." Qingqing gripped her phone, her voice trembling slightly, "she might... have to go back much earlier than expected."

From that day on, a small notebook appeared in Qingqing's pencil case.

On the first page of the notebook was written: "Weixi's Countdown Record."

Every morning, Qingqing would steal a glance at the bracelet on Weixi's wrist when she wasn't looking, then write down the number in her notebook.

"January 15th: thirty."

"January 18th: twenty-seven."

"January 20th: twenty-five."

The numbers kept dropping, one by one.

"Class President," Liu-Liu looked at those numbers, its voice growing lower and lower, "the countdown speed... seems to have gotten a little faster again."

"I know." Qingqing gripped her pen and said softly.

"Should we... tell everyone?" Liu-Liu asked, "At least let A-Xin know?"

"Not yet." Qingqing shook her head, "Weixi said she wants everyone to do environmental protection happily, without knowing about the countdown. She doesn't want everyone... to worry about her."

"But..." Liu-Liu lowered its head, "at this rate, everyone will find out eventually."

"Then..." Qingqing put down her pen and looked out the window, "we'll deal with that when the day comes. For now, what we can do is... make this world a little better before she goes back."

"..." Liu-Liu fell silent.

Looking at those numbers in Qingqing's notebook, it suddenly felt that those numbers weren't just a "countdown."

They were... one child's gentlest promise to another child.

That night, Qingqing sat until dawn.

She didn't cry.

She just wrote, stroke by stroke, on her notebook, that string of numbers:

"Thirty-eight, thirty-four, thirty-one, thirty..."

"From today on," she said in a low voice, "I'm going to record this number carefully."

"Then..." she looked up, a resolute light flickering in her eyes, "before the number reaches zero, I'm going to make everyone... learn to protect the Earth."

"That way, Weixi..." she wrote the final line softly in her notebook:

"can return to her era with peace of mind."
