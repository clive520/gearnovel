# Chapter 9: The Iron Judge's Diary of Despair

Loop Day Fifteen.

7:50 AM.

Bearing dark, bruised rings under both eyes, Director of Student Affairs Zhou Yan trudged with heavy steps down the central chinaberry-lined avenue of Luyang Elementary.

Having governed campus discipline with an iron fist for three full decades, the "Iron Judge" was celebrated across the county for his incorruptible severity, stable blood pressure, and a will as immovable as granite.

Yet over the past week and a half, Director Zhou had begun to harbor terrifying suspicions that the neural wiring of his brainstem was undergoing irreversible physical decay.

Trembling, he fished a black moleskin notebook from his breast pocket—a "Top Secret Journal" he reserved exclusively for documenting his acute psychological anomalies.

Scrawled frantically across the latest page were dense, red-inked entries written in trembling handwriting:

【July 7th · Memo No. ?】  
*"Witchcraft. Unmitigated, demonic witchcraft. I strongly suspect all twenty-six students of Class 6-1 have collectively awakened quantum mind-reading specifically targeted at my personal biology. Before departing home this morning, the leather lace of my left oxford snapped clean through. I mentioned this catastrophe to not a single living soul. Yet when I stepped into their room at 8:03 AM, that wretched brat Lu Li-Shin smiled with saintly grace and presented me with a brand-new, factory-sealed replacement lace of the exact same shade of brown leather—identical in length down to the millimeter. This is not science. This spits directly in the face of dialectical materialism!"*

Snapping the notebook shut, Director Zhou inhaled a deep lungful of scorched, parched air, willing his hammering heart to steady.

"Compose yourself... Zhou Yan, you are the Director of Student Affairs; you hold the life-and-death administrative authority of this entire school. They are mere fledglings with the scent of mother's milk still upon their lips! Today, you will not let them drag you by the nose!"

Gritting his teeth, Director Zhou glanced down at his wristwatch—7:59 AM.

Following routine, he withdrew the thirty-page test score summary from his briefcase and hoisted his giant stainless-steel thermos filled with scalding chrysanthemum, wolfberry, and ginseng tea.

"This time, I bypass the main South Wing staircase entirely!" A glint of tactical cunning flared in his eyes. "I take the west fire escape! Let's see how you clairvoyant demons predict my route now!"

Like a seasoned old leopard executing a flanking maneuver, Director Zhou slipped silently toward the western fire stairs, hunching low, his padded soles taking silent, agile strides straight up to the third floor.

8:00 AM sharp.

Director Zhou flattened his back against the wall beside the rear door of Class 6-1, holding his breath, poised to launch an unheralded rear-guard blitzkrieg upon these lawless rascals!

Yet the exact millisecond his fingertips grazed the cold brass doorknob—

"Click."

The door swung smoothly inward entirely of its own accord!

Standing on the threshold, clad in a crisp school tracksuit and wearing the professional, crystalline smile of a five-star flight attendant, was Class President Lin Yun-Qing.

"Good morning, Director Zhou. The time is precisely 8:00:08 AM. Welcome to the western rear entrance." Qing-Qing bowed with exquisite poise, her left hand gesturing inwards with practiced grace. "Pursuant to big-data trajectory simulations calculated across your past six cycles, your probability of selecting the western stairs was 87.3 percent. Please enter—the red carpet has been laid."

Director Zhou was petrified in the doorway, his right boot suspended mid-air, unable to step forward, unable to retreat.

Mechanically, he swiveled his gaze toward the interior.

The spectacle before him nearly convinced him he had wandered into the VIP reception lounge of a luxury international hotel—

The chaotic desks had been aligned into a parade-ground phalanx of geometric perfection. The teacher's desk was draped in a pristine white linen cloth, dead center upon which sat a vase crafted from a chalk box holding a spray of crimson flame-tree blossoms. And to the left of the vase rested an opened tin of imported loquat throat lozenges, a pack of fragrance-free pure cotton wet wipes, and an adjustable pipe wrench fitted with a brand-new rubber gasket for domestic plumbing repairs!

"GOOD MORNING, DIRECTOR————!"

All twenty-six students rose in immaculate unison, hands aligned along the seams of their trousers, bowing at a textbook forty-five-degree angle that would shame a state honor guard!

Standing in the second row, Old Wu hadn't even touched a pork bun; instead, he cradled a steaming, folded white hand towel in both palms, beaming with grandmotherly benevolence:

"Director, ascending the western stairs must have been exhausting, wasn't it? The cement on the third riser has weathered slightly; your right ankle bore excessive torque on the way up, correct? Here, wipe your brow with a warm towel. Don't sip your ginseng tea too hastily—today's batch of wolfberries is somewhat heat-inducing, so I took the liberty of floating two dried Hangzhou chrysanthemum petals on the lid to balance your yin."

"Y-you... all of you..."

Director Zhou's jaw went utterly slack. The papers in his grip began to flutter like autumn leaves, his knees turning to bowls of warm congee.

What broke his psychological resolve entirely, however—

Was A-Shin, the undisputed King of Pranks, stepping forward from the back row with an expression of profound, tender empathy, holding a small white sterile bandage, pointing with clinical precision toward Director Zhou's right pinky finger:

"Director, when you swerved around the custodian's trolley at the second-floor landing, did the metal bracket of the fire extinguisher scratch the outer knuckle of your right pinky? The laceration is roughly 0.5 millimeters and has begun to weep capillary blood. Here—waterproof breathable adhesive bandage. I even pre-applied the cooling mint antiseptic ointment for you."

Director Zhou looked down.

Along the outer ridge of his right little finger lay a fresh, hairline scratch weeping a microscopic bead of blood! It was an injury so fresh his own nervous system had not yet registered the pain!

"Monsters..."

A strangled, dream-like wheeze escaped Director Zhou's throat. Slumping against the doorframe, his gaze dissolved into vacant horror:

"What are you creatures... Are you extraterrestrials... or a sleeper cell of future intelligence operatives... How do you know the sensory firing of my own peripheral nerves?!"

"Director, you dwell upon unfounded anxieties."

Standing before the podium, Teacher Gao adjusted his dark spectacles, his three-piece suit without a wrinkle, his tone as placid as an enlightened sage who had peered into the void:

"Pursuant to neurocognitive research, when an organism repeats an identical behavioral loop within an invariant environment exceeding three hundred iterations, its kinematic certainty converges asymptotically toward the absolute determinism of Laplace's Demon. Our students do not possess supernatural foresight; they have merely committed your physiological habits to memory more thoroughly than their multiplication tables."

Teacher Gao extended an elegant hand, relieving Director Zhou of the thirty-page assessment packet and placing it with immaculate alignment upon the podium:

"Furthermore, Director, the 'Detention Disciplinary Monologue' you were slated to deliver at 8:04 AM was transcribed from memory by the entire class during yesterday's study hall. The text spans precisely 1,248 Chinese characters, with an error rate of zero percent. Would you care for an oral recitation?"

"N-no... that won't be necessary..."

Director Zhou waved a feeble hand, two silent streams of tears finally spilling over his coal-black cheeks, trickling through the deep furrows of his weathered skin.

The iron-willed titan who had reigned supreme over county education for thirty years was thoroughly, irreparably broken.

No roaring. No podium-slamming. No threats of disciplinary demerits.

Beneath the warm, compassionate, borderline-parental gazes of twenty-six pairs of eyes, Director Zhou felt less like an administrator punishing juvenile delinquents, and more like an abandoned, frail elder being collectively fostered by a classroom of saintly caregivers.

"I... I am unwell today..." Trembling, Director Zhou accepted the warm towel from Old Wu, dabbing away his tears and clammy perspiration. "Today's morning study hall... is converted to independent recess... Teacher Gao, you... you handle it..."

Cradling the warm towel and the tin of loquat lozenges like an orphaned child, Director Zhou turned on rubbery legs, drifting down the hallway like a deflated ragdoll robbed of its life essence.

From down the corridor came the faint, sniffling murmur of the broken titan:

"Tomorrow... tomorrow I'm calling in sick... No, I'm taking an administrative business trip... I am never setting foot inside this school again..."

"Click."

A-Shin gently, courteously closed the classroom door.

One second of stunned silence passed—

"PFFFT————BAHAHAHAHAHAHAHA!"

The room erupted into the most hysterical, table-pounding, riotous chorus of laughter in the sixty-eight-year history of the school!

Old Wu rolled onto the floor, clutching his belly as it heaved like ocean swells; A-Shin's tears shot sideways as he doubled over the podium, clutching his ribs in agony; several girls clung to one another in shrieks of merriment, nearly upending their desks!

"Did you see that?! Did you see the look Old Zhou gave A-Shin?! He looked like he just saw an alien invasion from outer space!"

"And that warm towel! Old Wu, that expression on your face was so saintly I thought you were Old Zhou's own biological grandmother!"

Tsunamis of ecstatic laughter washed across Class 6-1.

Yet tucked in the corner of the podium, Class President Qing-Qing waved discreetly to A-Shin and Wei-Xi.

Wiping his tears, A-Shin strode over.

Across the glowing screen of Qing-Qing's multimeter, the spatial coordinates for the second spacetime anchor were oscillating violently!

"Following the extraction of the first tent peg, the energy grid of the temporal barrier has shifted underground," Qing-Qing pointed to the deep purple beacon embedded beneath the gymnasium storeroom on the 3D hologram, whispering urgently:

"While Director Zhou spends the next two hours in his office contemplating the meaning of existence, we must move immediately."

"Spacetime Anchor Number Two—the subterranean air-raid shelter. Operation begins now!"
