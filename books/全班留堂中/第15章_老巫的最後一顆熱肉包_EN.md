# Chapter 15: Old Wu's Final Steaming Pork Bun

Loop Day Twenty-Eight.

2:45 PM.

Along the third-floor promenade of the South Academic Wing, a gentle breeze stirred the faded curtains lining the casement windows.

One hour and fifteen minutes remained until the next temporal reset.

Throughout the deserted academic building, it was uncannily serene. No one was floor-surfing on dish soap; no one was firing slingshots at stray birds; no one was huddling before the teacher's desk hatching desperate escape conspiracies.

Along the concrete parapet outside the classroom, twenty-six students sat or leaned shoulder to shoulder, pressed tightly against one another.

Golden afternoon sunlight filtered through the fronds of the king palms, slanting across their shoulders, stretching their shadows long and thin across the concrete, intertwining them into a single, inseparable tapestry of ink.

"Rattle... clatter..."

The rhythmic groan of weathered trolley wheels fractured the corridor's quiet.

Clad in his slightly faded dark-blue school polo, Athletic Officer Wu Chen-Kai pushed a small utility cart with measured, deliberate strides up from the stairwell landing.

Dead center upon the cart sat a colossal, steaming bamboo steamer.

Aromatic white vapor—mingling the slight sourdough tang of traditional yeast fermentation with the rich, savory perfume of shiitake-minced pork filling—drifted lazily across the summer breeze.

It was the very first fresh batch of piping hot pork buns pulled from the cafeteria steamer: precisely twenty-six buns, neither one more nor less, each snowy white, plump, and round, their pleats gathered at the crown like blossoming white chrysanthemums.

Old Wu brought the trolley to a steady halt dead center in the hallway.

He did not cram them into his mouth with wild abandon like Day One, nor did he retch in post-traumatic disgust like Day Seven.

The burly boy who ordinarily possessed the most oblivious, carefree heart of them all appeared solemn and reverent today. Drawing a clean napkin from the lower shelf, he wiped his hands thoroughly, before gently lifting the woven bamboo lid.

"Piping hot mushroom pork buns," Old Wu offered a broad, earnest grin, the flesh at the corners of his eyes crinkling together. "Fresh from the steamer, calibrated to precisely sixty-five degrees Celsius. Paper-thin skin, bursting with filling—one bite and the broth floods your mouth."

Cradling the first bun delicately in both palms, Old Wu presented it to Class President Lin Yun-Qing.

Qing-Qing blinked, staring down at the warm pastry in her fingers with subtle hesitation: "Old Wu... didn't you swear... you never wanted to see another bun for the rest of your life?"

"That was then," Old Wu scratched the back of his neck, lowering his gaze with bashful warmth. "Back then, I thought feeding my own face was the grandest mission in the cosmos. I figured as long as there were infinite buns to eat every day, being trapped in this school forever wouldn't be half bad."

Turning around, Old Wu lifted the second, the third, and the fourth bun, delivering them one by one to every classmate around him:

"Here, Lin Yun-Yun—you always like extra sweet chili sauce, so I asked the work-study intern for two extra packets just for you;"  
"Ang Chuan-Kai, you despise mushrooms, so I took a sterile toothpick and picked out half the diced shiitake for you earlier;"  
"Wei-Xi, you eat slowly; this bun has the thinnest skin, let it cool a breath before taking a bite..."

Twenty-six buns. Old Wu distributed them one by one, casually naming the subtlest culinary quirks of every soul in the room.

By the time he cradled the final two buns and strode before A-Shin, who sat at the far end of the bench, the bamboo steamer was bare.

Old Wu took a seat beside A-Shin, pressing one of the two warm buns firmly into A-Shin's palms.

Looking down at the soft, warm dough cradled in his hands, the soothing heat seeped through A-Shin's skin straight into his chest. His throat convulsed, his eyes reddening as he turned to face the boy beside him:

"Old Wu... what's gotten into you today?"

Lowering his head, Old Wu stroked the white surface of his final bun with his thick thumbs, suddenly sniffling.

A single, scalding tear splashed heavily onto the flour crust.

"A-Shin... I'm terrified."

Old Wu's voice was barely a whisper, carrying the fragile vulnerability of a child laid bare:

"My grades are in the gutter; my athletic times aren't anything special. Apart from having an appetite like a bottomless pit, I don't have a single redeeming talent."

"My dad told me that the second primary school ends, he's shipping me off to boarding school at that strictly regimented private academy in downtown Changhua. You only get to come home once a month, they wake you at six in the morning to drill English flashcards, and if you fail a test, they cane your palms with rattan switches."

Lifting his face, tears streaming down his round cheeks, Old Wu looked at A-Shin, and then at the twenty-odd classmates gathering around them:

"In that school, I won't know a single living soul."

"I'm scared that when lunchtime rolls around, no one will fight me for the chicken drumsticks anymore; I'm scared that when I finish dead last in gym class, no one will be waiting at the finish line smiling with a water bottle for me; and I'm even more scared... that if older kids pick on me, A-Shin won't be there to leap out from the back row and pelt popping candy at their heads..."

Burying his entire face into his massive forearms, Old Wu erupted into a gut-wrenching, broken wail:

"I don't want to go to Changhua! I want to stay with you guys! I want to stay in Class 6-1 for the rest of my life!"

Silence settled over the sunlit corridor.

No one laughed at Old Wu's perceived cowardice, for those words ripped open the deepest, most terrified wound buried within the soul of every child standing there.

Lin Yun-Yun bit her lip, tears trickling silently down her chin; the rowdiest boys in the rear lowered their heads, rubbing fiercely at their eyes.

Growing up was an unthinkably cruel ordeal.

It arrived like an unannounced torrential downpour, ruthlessly scattering children who had huddled tightly beneath a solitary umbrella into the freezing mud and howling gales alone.

Watching the weeping boy beside him, A-Shin inhaled deeply.

Extending his right arm, the boy clamped his hand around Old Wu's broad, fleshy shoulder, hauling him into a fierce embrace.

"Old Wu, you listen to me."

Gritting his teeth, though tears burned behind his own eyelids, A-Shin's gaze held an unprecedented, crystalline resolve:

"You are not some useless glutton who only knows how to stuff his face."

"Last semester at Future Extreme Park, when that rollercoaster was about to slide off the tracks, you were the one who generated over three hundred Newtons of raw force, holding out against the gale to save the lives of all twenty-six of us!"

"And across these past twenty-odd days, every single morning, you were the first to dive and catch Old Zhou's thermos; every single time the rest of us were on the brink of total collapse from Teacher Gao's drills, you were the one pushing this cart down the hall, bringing us hot buns!"

A-Shin hoisted the bun in his hand and took a massive, defiant bite!

Sweet, rich savory broth exploded across his tongue, mingling the earthy aroma of shiitake with the comforting sweetness of traditional dough.

"This is the best damn pork bun I have ever eaten in my life." Mouth crammed with dough, A-Shin spoke with muffled yet sacred conviction:

"So what if you go to Changhua?! So what if they lock you in a strict boarding academy?! Old Wu, you mark my words: the second winter vacation arrives, even if I have to scale perimeter fences, catch freight trains, or pedal a bicycle until my legs fall off, I swear I will show up at your front door, buy out the best pork bun stall in all of Changhua City, and stuff your drawers until they burst!"

"Count me in too!"

Her eyes rimmed with red, Qing-Qing stepped forward, holding her bun aloft:

"My family's motorcycle repair shop is only twenty kilometers from Changhua! The second I master engine tuning, I'm riding a heavy motorcycle to your school gates every single weekend to pick you up!"

"And us!"

Twenty-odd youths swarmed forward, each taking a massive, ravenous bite of their warm buns, chewing through tears and savory broth, before stacking their hands together amidst tearful laughter!

"It's just junior high! What is there to be afraid of?!"  
"Even if we're split across different schools, we are forever Class 6-1 of Luyang Elementary!"  
"Graduation is no final farewell! As long as we hold one another in our hearts, nothing in the universe can ever tear us apart!"

The golden sunset of 3:00 PM washed the long promenade in resplendent crimson light.

Twenty-six young souls leaned against the corridor railing, chewing piping hot mushroom pork buns with fierce devotion, arms wrapped around shoulders, laughter and weeping mingling into a soaring hymn that scattered all twenty-eight days of gloom and despair into the wind.

Tucked within the shadows of the corridor corner, Teacher Gao stood in peaceful stillness.

The evening light caressed the hem of his charcoal-grey suit. Behind his dark spectacle frames, that once-turbulent cascade of chaotic red code miraculously settled into the softest, most stable emerald hue.

The bionic android lowered his gaze, observing the pulsing holographic heart resting within his core processor.

【Telemetry confirms collective fear gravitational field has completely dissipated.】  
【Neural resonance profile transitioned to: Indomitable Shared Bond.】  
【Spacetime barrier stability collapsed to critical threshold: 12 percent.】

Lifting his head, looking upon the twenty-six laughing, weeping youths embracing beneath the golden light, the smile upon Teacher Gao's lips finally curved into the most exquisite, breathtaking human expression:

"The final prompt on the examination... you have already answered with a perfect score."
