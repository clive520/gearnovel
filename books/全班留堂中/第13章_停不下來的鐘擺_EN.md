# Chapter 13: The Unstoppable Pendulum

Loop Day Twenty-Three.

8:00 AM sharp.

Blinding, incandescent summer sunlight sliced once more through the classroom windows of Class 6-1, casting uncompromising shafts of illumination across the immaculate concrete floor.

The four vintage ceiling fans overhead creaked their rhythmic complaints, while outside, the relentless chorus of cicadas droned like chainsaws in the canopy.

The blackboard was scrubbed pristine—no electrical schematics, no crimson skull symbols. The iron gates of the master substation behind the Administration Building remained padlocked by that heavy, rusted brass lock, as if yesterday afternoon's desperate, all-or-nothing blitzkrieg had been nothing more than a shared, feverish hallucination.

The twenty-six students sat slumped in their desks like hollow clay effigies stripped of their vital essence.

No one spoke. No one cracked a joke. Even Old Wu did not reach his hand into his drawer to retrieve the half-eaten cold pork bun that had automatically respawned on cue.

An unprecedented, suffocating abyss of absolute powerlessness gripped every young heart in the room.

Yesterday afternoon at 3:59:50 PM, they had achieved the theoretical pinnacle of what twelve-year-old human children could physically accomplish—

They had executed tactics rivaling elite special forces, neutralized a supreme bionic android with living human armor, blinded his optical array with sudsy chemical fog, and struck his solitary neural damping actuator in the final second, tearing the forty-pound solid copper high-voltage breaker clean out of its cradle!

At that moment, the entire campus had gone pitch black!

Yet why... why did the 4:00 PM bell still ring?!

Why did that blinding wash of white temporal luminescence still tear through the air, ignoring severed cables and collapsed magnetic fields, dumping them back at eight in the morning like yesterday's refuse?!

"Physics... is a lie."

Slumped across her desk, her face buried deep within the crook of her forearms, Class President Lin Yun-Qing let out a muffled whimper choked with tears.

Beside her notebook lay the digital multimeter that had accompanied her through twenty-three days of toil, its LCD panel displaying absolute zero. It was the first time in the twelve years of her life that this proud, unyielding tech prodigy had tasted total, catastrophic, inescapable defeat.

"Resistance, voltage, Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction..." Qing-Qing's shoulders shook violently, her tears soaking into the wood of her desk. "All of it, worthless... We severed every wire, dismantled the energy conduits, but that cursed chime... doesn't need copper wires to travel..."

Slouched in the back row, A-Shin stared blankly up at the spinning ceiling fan blades, his eyes bloodshot and raw.

Both of his palms were wrapped in thick layers of white gauze—painful blisters and lacerations ground into his flesh when he hung his entire weight from the rough cast-iron breaker handle yesterday afternoon.

Though the temporal reset had erased all physical trauma from his skin, the soul-crushing agony of throwing everything he had into the void remained driven into his marrow like a rusted nail.

"We're never getting out of here," A-Shin's voice was raspy, scraped raw like sandpaper. "No matter how hard we fight, no matter how brilliant our schemes are, the moment four o'clock strikes, the cosmos snaps its fingers, and all our blood and sweat turns into a pathetic joke."

Sniffling, Old Wu dropped two heavy tears onto his wooden desk: "A-Shin... I miss my mom... I miss that overcooked, salty duck soup with pickled greens she makes... I swear I'll never be a picky eater again... I'll never steal another pork bun... Please, someone, just let us out of here..."

Grief spread through the quiet room like an airborne contagion.

Several gentler girls buried their faces in their hands, sobbing softly, while even the toughest boys in the rear folded their arms across their desks, their frames trembling with muffled weeping.

Within this endless loop of time, they resembled fireflies trapped inside a sealed glass mason jar—hurling themselves against the transparent glass again and again until battered and broken, only to find the outside world inches away, yet eternally out of reach.

"Tap, tap, tap."

From the front of the room came that familiar, measured cadence of footsteps.

Immaculate in his three-piece charcoal-grey suit, devoid of yesterday's soot or foam, Teacher Gao descended the podium steps with unhurried poise, coming to a halt before the desks of A-Shin and Qing-Qing.

The bionic android looked down at the weeping children.

Behind his dark-rimmed spectacles, pale-blue code streams drifted peacefully—holding no mockery, no cold severity, only a timeless, unshakeable warmth and serenity.

"Class," Teacher Gao spoke, his metallic baritone ringing crystal-clear across the room above the sound of tears. "In cognitive psychology, when an organism endures repeated, high-intensity trauma across immutable conditions, its willpower inevitably slides toward the abyss of 【Learned Helplessness】. Your present despair, grief, and exhaustion represent entirely normative neurochemical responses to extreme environmental stress."

"Teacher Gao..." A-Shin lifted his bloodshot eyes, his voice cracking in a desperate plea, "Can you please stop reading cold statistics at us?! You're an omnipotent machine with infinite computing power—you tell me! Why did that bell still ring yesterday after we cut the main breaker?! Where did our math go wrong?!"

Teacher Gao lowered his gaze slightly, his eyes resting upon A-Shin's bandaged hands.

"Your mathematics were not wrong."

Teacher Gao's answer took every soul in the room by surprise.

The bionic android gently lowered his frame, kneeling on one knee beside A-Shin's desk, bringing his line of sight level with the twelve-year-old boy:

"Viewed purely through the lens of classical electromagnetism, your tactical operation yesterday was executed with breathtaking precision. You successfully severed 99.99 percent of the electrical energy flux throughout this campus at precisely 3:59:50 PM."

"Then why..." Qing-Qing lifted her tear-streaked face.

Teacher Gao extended a large, warm mechanical hand, resting his palm gently upon Qing-Qing's hair:

"Because from the very inception of this ordeal, you defined the Chronos Bangle strictly as an 'instrument suffering mechanical malfunction.'"

A collective jolt rippled through the room. One by one, the students ceased weeping, looking up at their teacher in stunned silence.

"Machines require cables; signals require antennas; physical energy requires conductive media," Teacher Gao stood, turning to face the class, his gaze settling upon Lin Wei-Xi in the front row, whose eyes were heavy with emotion. "However, the formal designation of the apparatus upon Student Lin Wei-Xi's wrist is: 【Spacetime Emotional Resonance Causal Recorder】."

"The ultimate wellspring powering its manifold has never been that hundred-thousand-volt lightning bolt, nor the high-voltage currents coursing through our campus transformer."

Nudging his spectacles, the cascading blue code behind Teacher Gao's lenses gently coalesced into the pulsating shape of a glowing heart:

"That summer thunderstorm was merely the ignition spark. The true perpetual engine sustaining this temporal labyrinth across twenty-three iterations..."

Teacher Gao paused, his gaze sweeping with profound tenderness across all twenty-six young, battered faces:

"...is the colossal gravitational field generated by the terror, sorrow, and fierce longing buried deep within your hearts—the yearning never to be torn apart."

An absolute, awe-inspiring silence blanketed the classroom.

A-Shin froze.

Old Wu froze.

"This pendulum of time is not jammed by a physical circuit breaker," Teacher Gao pointed an elegant finger toward the quartz clock resting peacefully above the blackboard, his voice as gentle as morning light piercing through storm clouds:

"What jams it are your own feet—feet that have not yet found the courage to stride into tomorrow."

"So long as you resist growing up, so long as you fear separation, then even if you level every power plant in this city to rubble..."

Teacher Gao smiled faintly, an unmistakable, deeply human warmth shimmering within his eyes:

"...the 4:00 PM bell will continue to echo across the chambers of your hearts, without end, for all eternity."
