# Chapter 1: The Hellish Summer School Order

In July at Luyang Elementary School, the cicadas shrilled with a racket resembling a thousand chainsaws tearing through tree branches all at once.

Visible heat waves shimmered above the scorching asphalt, while the venerable king palm trees flanking the athletic field drooped listlessly. The entire campus was utterly deserted, the thick air saturated with the parched, stifling breath of high summer.

Under Ministry of Education regulations, this ought to have been prime summer vacation—a glorious window for graduating primary schoolers to revel in leisure, blasting the air conditioning, gaming, and munching on icy fruit popsicles.

Yet at the far end of the third floor in the South Academic Wing—right before the weathered wooden placard of Class 6-1—a suffocating aura of atmospheric low pressure hung heavily in the air.

"Smack!"

A crisp, thunderous impact detonated abruptly, causing the solid cypress teacher's desk to shudder violently.

"Absurd! Shameless! This is unequivocally the darkest, most disgraceful day in the sixty-eight-year history of Luyang Elementary School!"

Director of Student Affairs Zhou Yan slammed the thirty-page test score summary down onto the desk. His complexion was as dark as pig iron freshly hoisted from the depths of a coal mine. Behind the thick black-rimmed spectacles perched on the bridge of his nose, two incandescent glares swept furiously across the twenty-six students sitting pin-straight before him.

Director Zhou yanked out an exam paper at random, his fingers quivering with incandescent rage as he jabbed a stubby finger at the red ink and bellowed:

"Wu Chen-Kai! Get on your feet!"

Seated dead center in the second row, Old Wu—round and doughy like a smiling Maitreya Buddha—shivered from head to toe. In a panic, he hastily crammed half a cold pork bun hidden inside his desk into his mouth, his cheeks bulging as he scrambled shakily to his feet.

"D-Director... present!" Old Wu responded around a mouthful of dough.

"Read it aloud for everyone to hear—what kind of demonic nonsense did you write for Question 17 of the 'Junior High Transition Comprehensive Science Assessment'?" Director Zhou barked, the fleshy folds on his cheeks twitching violently. "The question asked: 'Identify the physical phenomenon produced when heating water to 100 degrees Celsius, and briefly describe the energy transformation process.' What did you write? Read it!"

Old Wu swallowed hard, sneaking a desperate sideways glance at A-Shin, who was keeping his head low and fighting off a fit of snickers. Gritting his teeth, Old Wu muttered softly: "When... when the water boils... you can drop in the pork and cabbage dumplings. The energy transformation process is... turning raw dumplings into cooked dumplings, which then transform into thermal energy in your stomach..."

"Pffft!"

Someone in the back row failed to hold it in, letting out a sharp snort of laughter that instantly triggered an infectious, stifled ripple of giggles across the room.

"Silence! Shut your mouths, every single one of you!" Enraged, Director Zhou seized the chalkboard eraser and pounded the podium, sending clouds of white chalk dust billowing into the air. "And you! Lu Li-Shin! You laughed the loudest—did you think your own score was something to brag about?!"

Having his name called, A-Shin promptly retracted his beaming grin, blinking his wide eyes in feigned innocence while quietly pulling his crossed right leg back beneath the desk.

"History section, open-ended question: 'Discuss the strategic defense measures Zheng Chenggong implemented regarding the Lu'ermen waterway after retreating to Taiwan during the Ming Dynasty.'" Director Zhou pointed to A-Shin's zero-point paper, virtually submerged beneath waves of red ink, reading through gritted teeth: "'Zheng Chenggong ordered the planting of popping candy and water-grass spiders along both flanks of the Lu'ermen channel, intending to bog down the Qing naval cannons in the mud, while dispatching trained squirrel couriers to execute amphibious surprise raids.'—Lu Li-Shin! Were you taking a history exam, or transcribing your disciplinary rap sheet from pranking teachers last semester?!"

"Reporting, Director—that is divergent thinking!" A-Shin puffed out his chest, mounting a grave and earnest defense. "The true essence of an examination lies in inspiring futuristic strategic imagination! If historical figures had possessed our class's nimble deployment of biological warfare and confectionery disruptions, who knows—the map of the Age of Discovery might have been rewritten entirely!"

"You still dare talk back to me?!" Clutched at his chest, Director Zhou staggered half a step backward, nearly choking on his own fury.

Seated in the front row, Class President Lin Yun-Qing pushed her frameless glasses up with practiced elegance. Holding a digital multimeter and a precision screwdriver in her hands, she calibrated a small instrument on her desk while coolly dropping a devastating remark:

"According to big-data analytics performed across all twenty-six class papers, our overall average score for this assessment was 31.4 points. The standard deviation stands at an alarming 42, reflecting an extreme bimodal distribution. Only Lin Wei-Xi and I attained perfect scores; the remaining twenty-four students demonstrated an overlap rate with standard answers of less than 4.2 percent. Objectively speaking, this represents an unmitigated academic catastrophe."

"Lin Yun-Qing! Whose side are you even on?!" A-Shin buried his head in his hands from the back row, wailing in despair.

Seated beside Qing-Qing, transfer student Lin Wei-Xi wore a silver-white 'Chronos Bangle' upon her left wrist, its surface rippling with faint data streams. She turned, looking back at the flustered A-Shin and Old Wu with a mixture of helplessness and quiet sympathy, letting out a soft sigh.

"It's over... it's completely over..." Old Wu groaned in utter ruin, glistening tears welling at the corners of his eyes. "My dad laid down the law just yesterday—if I didn't come home with a passing certificate this summer, he was going to put three padlocks on the kitchen fridge and forbid me from touching a single drop of meat gravy for two whole months..."

Just as the entire classroom devolved into chaotic despair and Director Zhou was about to whip out his telephone-directory-thick master disciplinary registry—

"Tap, tap, tap."

From down the hallway came a cadence of steps, executed with flawless, unshakeable precision.

The interval between each stride was as meticulously calibrated as if gauged by a laser rangefinder—precisely sixty centimeters, neither a millimeter more nor less.

Immediately after, a silver-white figure darted down like lightning from the exterior air-conditioner conduit, slipping nimbly through the classroom transom. It was the class's AI companion pet, 'Liu-Liu.' Its dual obsidian electronic eyes flashed rapid pulses of blue light as it broadcast a hushed digital alert to the room:

"Beep-beep! Proctor entering a three-meter radius! Aura stability: one hundred percent! Attire neatness: ten stars!"

The very next second, the ajar wooden door was nudged open without a sound.

The figure who strode inside wore an immaculate three-piece charcoal-grey suit, the red-and-blue diagonal stripes on his tie deviating from the vertical center by no more than a tenth of a millimeter, his leather shoes polished to a mirror sheen that reflected the fluorescent tubes overhead. Behind the black-rimmed glasses resting on his nose, those clear, profound eyes remained as serene as unrippled water, while pale-blue code progress bars refreshed across the lower fringe of his lenses at blinding speeds.

This was none other than the most formidable, enigmatic legendary educator in the history of Luyang Elementary—Teacher Gao Zhi-Xian (Bionic Android Model: GS-X01).

"Good morning, students, and good morning, Director Zhou. The time is precisely 8:00 AM."

Teacher Gao ascended the podium with textbook strides, bowing with subtle deference toward the livid Director Zhou, his deep, metallic baritone ringing with crystal clarity across the hushed chamber.

"Teacher Gao! You arrived just in time!" Clasping the abysmal stack of papers like a lifeline, Director Zhou thrust them into Teacher Gao's arms. "Take a good look at this circus of a class you've produced! Graduation exams barely concluded, and they butcher the transition assessment to this degree! If word of this reaches the principals of our feeder middle schools, where is the century-old prestige of Luyang Elementary supposed to stand?!"

Teacher Gao extended his long, slender fingers, his fingertip tracing lightly across the topmost exam.

"Hummm—"

A barely perceptible high-frequency electromagnetic scanning tone hummed through the air. All twenty-six exam papers—encompassing over twenty-four thousand Chinese characters and diagrams—were completely digested and parsed in a mere 0.3 seconds.

"According to this machine's... ahem, according to this homeroom teacher's internal computational model," Teacher Gao adjusted his spectacles, his expression earnest and focused, "the answering logic demonstrated by our students indeed exhibits pronounced divergent drift. In particular, Student Wu Chen-Kai's hypothesis synthesizing the First Law of Thermodynamics with the gelatinization of starch molecules yields an outcome that, under conventional grading metrics, statistically aligns with a predictive probability of zero points."

Hearing the verdict, Old Wu collapsed into his chair like a deflated balloon.

"Therefore!" Director Zhou swept his arm with absolute finality, proclaiming the administrative sentence with booming authority. "Pursuant to the 'Implementation Guidelines for Summer Remedial Instruction in Compulsory Education,' this office formally issues a 'Mandatory Summer Detention Order' to all twenty-six students of Class 6-1!"

The decree exploded inside the classroom like a thunderclap from a cloudless sky.

"Detention for how long?!" A-Shin demanded in defiant protest.

"One full week! Daily from eight in the morning until four in the afternoon!" Director Zhou glared daggers at A-Shin. "The central air conditioning in this building has been shut down for electrical retrofitting—your class will be the solitary inhabitants on this entire campus! Eight hours of intensive, locked-door review every single day, until you all pass the retake next Friday!"

The classroom erupted in a chorus of agonized wails:

"No way! In thirty-eight-degree heat with no AC, we'll melt into human jelly!"  
"My streak bonuses in my online games will be ruined!"  
"Seven days of summer vacation down the drain, ahhh!"

Deaf to their desperate pleas, Director Zhou let out a cold snort and turned back to Teacher Gao: "Teacher Gao, you bear full supervisory authority over this week's remedial sessions. If a single student fails the retake next Friday, I will hold this entire den of rascals in detention until the day before the autumn semester commences!"

With that parting threat, Director Zhou tucked his leather dossier under his arm, stomping his heavy leather soles as he slammed the door behind him and departed.

As the heavy footsteps receded down the corridor, only twenty-six mournful, bitter faces remained in the classroom, accompanied by the grating, relentless drone of cicadas outside the windowpane.

Slumped despondently over his desk, A-Shin tilted his head toward the erect figure of Teacher Gao on the podium, sighing feebly: "Teacher Gao... you win. To think we survived all those wild storms, only to be brought down by a measly standardized test. How are we supposed to endure eight hours of this today..."

Standing before the podium, the blue data streams dancing behind Teacher Gao's lenses gently settled into stillness. With consummate grace, he drew a brand-new stick of white chalk from his suit pocket and inscribed eight immaculate characters dead center on the blackboard: 【Spacetime Comprehensive Review: Day One】.

Turning around, Teacher Gao's gaze settled upon A-Shin and Old Wu. The corners of his mouth curved upward slightly, revealing that precision-calibrated smile that unfailingly sent icy chills down the students' spines:

"Students, meteorological monitoring models project a 99.8 percent probability of an extreme supercell thunderstorm striking this sector this afternoon. Please cherish the placid learning environment currently at your disposal. For within the vast river of time, the passage of every fleeting second may prove far more elusive than you could ever fathom."

A-Shin shivered, completely unable to decipher the cryptic omen lingering behind Teacher Gao's words.

At this moment, none of them had the slightest inkling that the "extreme thunderstorm" their teacher spoke of was destined, at precisely 3:59 this very afternoon, to violently plunge their fates, the temporal fabric of this entire building, and this seemingly interminable summer detention straight into an endless, unfathomable temporal abyss...
