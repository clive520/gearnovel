# Chapter 11: The Bangle's Projection: Shadows of the Future

Loop Day Nineteen.

2:30 PM.

Blistering high-summer sunlight cut through the grimy windowpanes, slicing sharp geometric patches of light and shadow across the empty corridor floor outside Class 6-1.

Following the successful dismantling of the second spacetime anchor in the subterranean bunker, the perimeter of temporal distortion generated by the bangle had contracted by nearly a third. Yet as the oppressive atmospheric weight above the campus slowly dissipated, a far more tangible, crushing reality cast its shadow over every young heart.

Inside the natural science laboratory, Qing-Qing was meticulously wiring the secondary diagnostic interface of the Chronos Bangle to an antique classroom projector using two ultra-thin silver-plated leads.

"The third spacetime anchor has been triangulated—it's located right at the century-old clock tower directly above the main entrance of the academic quad," Qing-Qing murmured, her eyes locked upon the fluctuating waveforms on her digital multimeter as she spoke to A-Shin and Wei-Xi. "The electromagnetic barrier around that tower is exceptionally resilient. My strategy is to hijack the bangle's native navigation protocols, firing a counter-frequency decoding pulse to short-circuit the clock tower's resonance from the inside out."

"Is there any danger?" Wei-Xi looked at her wrist with subtle trepidation.

"Theoretically no; this is a controlled bypass discharge." Pulling on her insulated gloves, Qing-Qing inhaled a steady breath. "A-Shin, pin the third filter capacitor for me. Counting down from three—three, two, one, energize!"

"SIZZLE—HUMMMM————!"

The instant Qing-Qing threw her hand-crafted micro-switch, a pale blue arc of current surged along the silver leads straight into the Chronos Bangle!

Yet what transpired in the very next heartbeat bypassed every kinematic calculation Qing-Qing had prepared.

The three purple countdown digits upon the bangle's casing flashed in violent hysterics; the glassy mirror surface emitted an ear-splitting, ultrasonic whine of acute system overload! Immediately following, a pillar of incandescent light as thick as a human forearm erupted from the diamond-shaped processor, slamming dead center into the convex lens of the projector!

"Look out! Impedance backflow! The capacitor is going to blow!" Qing-Qing gasped in terror.

"Get down!" Instinctively throwing his arms wide, A-Shin tackled Qing-Qing and Wei-Xi beneath the heavy wooden lab bench!

"SHWAAAAA————!"

There was no explosion. There was no fire.

The projector's optical assembly scattered the beam outward, blooming into a dream-like, boundless mist of pale-blue holographic light, devouring the gloomy laboratory in an instant!

Trembling, A-Shin poked his head out from beneath the bench.

The sight before his eyes stole the breath straight from the young boy's lungs.

This was no two-dimensional slide show, nor a CGI rendering from a science fiction blockbuster.

It was an impeccably rendered, three-dimensional holographic future world woven from billions of microscopic, shimmering motes of living light!

Rising within the luminous haze stood a majestic, modern junior high school complex framed by dark blue structural steel and floor-to-ceiling acoustic glass. Chiseled in gold across the grand granite entrance gate ran the inscription: 【Municipal Luyang First Senior High School · Junior High Division】.

The holographic projection drifted with astonishing fidelity—

Autumn winds of September swept dried banyan leaves down the broad central promenade.

The campus swarmed with incoming seventh graders clad in crisp sailor suits and stiff mandarin-collar uniforms.

Yet amidst this bustling ocean of unfamiliar faces, A-Shin caught sight of a solitary figure he recognized all too well—

Himself.

The A-Shin in the projection had shot up half a head taller, swallowed in a slightly oversized junior high uniform, a heavy dark-blue backpack slung across his shoulders, head down, picking his way silently through the throng of strangers. The untamed, lawless spark of mischief had vanished from his eyes, replaced by an awkward, cautious melancholy born of stepping into an alien world.

He walked into a vast, sun-drenched classroom humming beneath modern inverter air-conditioning.

Standing before the blackboard was a stern, middle-aged woman wearing wire-rimmed spectacles; seated below were dozens of faces he had never seen before in his life.

Silently, A-Shin navigated to a desk by the window in the very last row and sat down. Subconsciously, he turned his head to glance at the desk to his left.

Sitting there was a scrawny boy with thick coke-bottle glasses, nose buried in advanced academic competition workbooks. There were no greasy stains from mushroom pork buns on the desk corner, no citrus perfume of dish soap in the air, and no one making grotesque faces beneath their hands while he stood at attention in trouble.

The boy in the hologram stared out the window at the overcast sky, letting out a soft, barely audible sigh as his shoulders slumped inward.

The projection rippled like water, shifting seamlessly to the vocational workshop of another school across town.

Wearing a blue canvas work apron, Qing-Qing stood alone before a massive computerized CNC lathe, calibrating gear parameters with razor-sharp focus. Surrounding her were older high school apprentices boasting of their projects, but no one hollered across the room at her, and no boy named Old Wu would ever beg her to pick the padlock on the athletic storeroom for a few extra pork buns.

The imagery rippled once more—

A private academy in downtown Changhua.

Towering Old Wu sat alone on the cold plastic bench of a cafeteria, clutching a soggy, chilled convenience-store sandwich, looking on bewildered as strangers memorized flashcards while shoveling rice into their mouths. A smear of mayonnaise clung to the corner of his lip, but no one smiled warmly to hand him a clean wet wipe.

Twenty-six souls.

The holographic mist mercilessly unveiled one fragment of destiny after another—

Twenty-six children who had lived shoulder-to-shoulder in Class 6-1, who had survived microwave bun miracles, floor-surfing championships, and even a death-defying rescue atop a storm-tossed rollercoaster... Scattered to the four winds like dandelion seeds caught in an autumn gale the moment September arrived, lost in disparate corners of the city.

There were no arguments. There was no betrayal.

They had merely... grown up.

They had merely... graduated.

The holographic projection flickered, dimmed, and finally collapsed into millions of microscopic blue motes, drifting gently back into the Chronos Bangle upon Wei-Xi's wrist.

The laboratory returned to darkness and dead silence.

A-Shin remained sprawled upon the concrete floor, his fingers digging into the cold masonry until his knuckles turned stark white, dirt trapped beneath his nails.

At some point, Old Wu had slipped out from the study hall next door, standing frozen in the doorway having witnessed the final frames of the projection. The chubby boy who usually laughed off being made to stand in the corner stood with bloodshot eyes, two scalding tears rolling without warning down his round cheeks to splash upon his thighs.

"So... that is our future..." Old Wu's voice shivered like a dry leaf in an autumn wind. "Once we reach junior high... everyone really gets split apart..."

Removing her goggles, Qing-Qing lowered her head in silence. The tech genius who feared nothing—who had dared crack open Teacher Gao's bionic circuitry for reverse-engineering—clutched the hem of her white lab coat so hard her knuckles ached.

"Do we... do we really have to grow up?"

A muffled whimper choked with tears broke the tomb-like quiet of the room.

It was A-Shin.

The boy sat up slowly, burying his face between his knees, his slender shoulders shaking violently:

"If the price of growing up means no one will ever stand in the hallway being punished with me; if stepping into junior high means Old Wu has to eat cold sandwiches alone, and Qing-Qing has to fix machines by herself... then I'd rather this stupid summer vacation never end for the rest of my life!"

A-Shin snapped his head up, tears streaming down his face as he looked at Wei-Xi:

"Wei-Xi! This loop was never an accident, was it?! The universe locked us into this day because deep down in our hearts... not a single one of us actually wanted to cross over into a tomorrow without each other!"

That gut-wrenching cry struck like a sledgehammer, shattering the fragile, stoic armor every child in the room had built around themselves.

Indeed.

Why had the entire class deliberately banded together to score zero on the transition assessment with the most absurd answers imaginable?  
Why had not a single one of them fled when that purple lightning storm descended, choosing instead to hold tight to one another's hands?

Looking at the weeping A-Shin and Old Wu, a misty blur clouded Wei-Xi's eyes.

Slowly kneeling down, she extended a warm, gentle hand, covering A-Shin's trembling fingers, her voice as tender as a sigh carried back from the future:

"A-Shin, you're right. The core protocol of the bangle... indeed captured the deepest subconscious yearning of this entire class."

Gently touching the softly glowing metal upon her wrist, Wei-Xi offered a bitter, bittersweet smile:

"The clock froze at 3:59 not because the storm was too powerful..."

"It froze because all of us, in the deepest corners of our souls, were simply terrified of losing the best days of our lives."
